# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 06 Guided Japa Session 05 2023 12 04

## Theme

Mahārāja frames the guided japa session around the prayojana — the soul's highest necessity, which is to awaken love for Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa and abandon the material consciousness that shrinks and diminishes us. He diagnoses the central malady of the conditioned soul: repetition breeds automaticity. The mind, doing things mechanically, stores them as habit and performs them without presence — like the commuter who drives the daily route and arrives remembering nothing of the journey. While such automaticity serves mundane tasks like cooking or travel, in japa it becomes a great obstacle, turning the holy name into mindless, tiring repetition. The remedy is to keep alive the awareness of why we need Kṛṣṇa consciousness, which converts automatic chanting into enthusiastic, mindful, conscious chanting that actively addresses Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa — asking forgiveness for our absent-minded nature and help to turn toward them. He then grounds this attentiveness in the body and the manner of chanting: an upright, aligned posture (even in a chair) widens the breath and supports mindfulness, while, following Rūpa Gosvāmī, japa is properly soft and quiet — not loud, and without the scripturally unsanctioned habit of shaking the body. The arc moves from the goal (love for Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa), through the problem (mindless automaticity), to the practical means (conscious intention, posture, and a peaceful, steady manner of utterance).

## Practical application

Begin each japa session by recalling its purpose — to develop love for Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa — rather than slipping into rounds performed on autopilot. Treat the chanting as a living address: speak to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, asking forgiveness for distraction and inviting their help to turn the mind toward them. Guard against the commuter's mind by keeping alive the question of why you are chanting; this awareness is what lifts the practice out of mechanical, tiring repetition into enthusiastic, attentive chanting. Prepare the body to support attention: sit with a straight, aligned spine — even in a chair — so the breath can open fully. Follow Rūpa Gosvāmī's definition of japa as softly spoken: chant quietly and steadily rather than loudly, and avoid shaking or agitating the body, which has no scriptural basis. The aim throughout is presence — staying consciously connected to the holy name instead of letting habit perform it for you.

## Glossary

- **prayojana** — The ultimate goal or highest necessity of life; here, to develop love for Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa and leave material consciousness behind.
- **japa** — The soft, quiet repetition of the holy names; per Rūpa Gosvāmī, 'softly spoken' — uttered gently rather than loudly.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari); the chanting that is the substance of the practice.
- **Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa** — The divine couple who are the object of the devotee's love and the addressees of the chanting.
- **Kṛṣṇa consciousness** — Awareness centered on Kṛṣṇa; keeping alive the purpose of devotion that turns automatic chanting into mindful chanting.
- **conditioned soul** — The materially bound living being, whose characteristic problem is performing acts mechanically and absent-mindedly.
- **Rūpa Gosvāmī** — A foremost teacher in the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava tradition, cited here for the definition of japa as softly, quietly spoken.
