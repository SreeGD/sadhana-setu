# SNS Seminars Harinaam Cintamani 05 2016 07 09

## Theme

Drawing on Śrīla Haridāsa Ṭhākura's Harināma Cintāmaṇi, this lecture presents attentive chanting of the holy name not as a mechanical ritual but as a fountainhead from which a whole progression of spiritual results unfolds of its own accord. From attentive kīrtana arises the spontaneous enthusiasm to serve Kṛṣṇa; from that, the impulse to offer obeisances and bow in humility; then a hunger to know Kṛṣṇa's plan for oneself (which turns one to the Bhagavad-gītā); and finally the willingness to surrender everything. Because these fruits are natural rather than forced, chanting becomes the standing remedy whenever enthusiasm flags or surrender feels frightening — one simply returns to the holy name. Mahārāja then deepens the practice by moving it inward: rather than mere mechanical japa, one chants in a prayerful mood and, above all, brings the attention into the space of the heart. Through the imagery of the two birds on one tree, the story of the 'secret of life' hidden in the human heart, and the truth that Kṛṣṇa is 'just a thought away,' he shows that the Lord dwells within as the eternal friend and Lord of the heart, discoverable only by turning from an outward-looking (bahiraṅga-mukha) to an inward-looking (antaraṅga-mukha) disposition. The essential technique is to lock out distraction, enter the heart-space, and address the Lord there by His names — for concentration is the prerequisite of real japa. Sanātana Gosvāmī's Dāmodara-līlā image supplies the reason this works: hearing binds Kṛṣṇa with a thread and meditation with a rope (both of which the independent Lord slips), but the holy name binds Him with an iron chain He cannot escape. So the sādhaka, like mother Yaśodā following the yogurt-stained footprints, must actively chase and re-catch the sound vibration each time the mind wanders. The lecture closes on Prabhupāda's clearest order — sixteen offenseless rounds daily — as the reliable engine of this entire progression, culminating in putting Kṛṣṇa in the center by taking oneself out of it: a surrender that, though hard for the conditioned soul, comes naturally to one who simply learns to chant the holy name.

## Practical application

Make japa an inward, prayerful act rather than a mechanical count. Before and during chanting, set the heart's disposition with a traditional prayer — for example the prayer to the japa-mālā, asking the beads to remove obstacles in one's Harināma and to grant service to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. Then bring the attention into the 'space of the heart,' mentally lock the door against outer distraction, and address the Lord who dwells there directly through His holy names, since concentration is the real prerequisite of japa. Treat the practice as actively chasing and catching Kṛṣṇa in the form of His sound vibration: each time the mind wanders and the name is lost, run after it and catch it again — like running after a child who won't go to bed — and let this 'chasing and catching' be your meditation. Do not wait for the restless senses and mind to obey on their own, since inattentiveness is the nature of the conditioned state; instead work to change from the heart, where one's own eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa resides. Above all, keep Prabhupāda's central order — sixteen rounds daily, clearly and without offense — because from clear chanting the natural desire to serve, to offer obeisances and worship, and finally to surrender all develop by themselves. Whenever enthusiasm to serve fades or surrender feels frightening, do not force it: simply return to chanting, the starting point that rekindles willingness and, in time, lets one hear the Lord within and know He is present.

## Glossary

- **Harināma Cintāmaṇi** — A devotional work of instructions on chanting the holy name, spoken by Śrīla Haridāsa Ṭhākura, treating the holy name as a wish-fulfilling touchstone (cintāmaṇi).
- **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** — The great associate of Śrī Caitanya honored as the nāmācārya, the exemplary teacher of chanting the holy name; speaker of the Harināma Cintāmaṇi.
- **Hari-Nāma / Harināma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari); the practice of chanting that name.
- **japa** — The soft, personal, repeated chanting of the holy name, usually counted on beads.
- **japa-mālā** — The string of beads used for counting rounds of chanting; the subject of a preparatory prayer asking it to remove obstacles and grant devotional service.
- **kīrtana** — The chanting or glorification of the holy name and the Lord's qualities; here, attentive chanting from which enthusiasm to serve naturally arises.
- **Kṛṣṇa** — The Supreme Lord, the object of the chanting and the beloved to be served, remembered, and surrendered to.
- **Bhagavad-gītā** — The scripture spoken by Kṛṣṇa; consulted to learn the Lord's eternal orders and His plan for oneself.
- **Bhāgavatam** — The Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam; one of the places where Kṛṣṇa is found present and available.
- **Supersoul** — The Paramātmā, the Lord seated within every heart alongside the individual soul — the eternal friend of the two-birds imagery.
- **two birds (swans) on the same tree** — The Upaniṣadic image of the individual soul and the Supersoul dwelling together on the 'tree' or lake of the heart, expressing their eternal companionship.
- **Dāmodara-līlā** — The pastime in which mother Yaśodā binds baby Kṛṣṇa; source of Sanātana Gosvāmī's image that only the holy name binds the independent Lord.
- **Sanātana Gosvāmī** — A principal follower of Śrī Caitanya whose progressive image of 'binding' Kṛṣṇa — thread, rope, iron chain — illustrates the supremacy of chanting.
- **Yaśodā** — Kṛṣṇa's mother in Vṛndāvana, who followed His yogurt-stained footprints to catch Him — a model for chasing and catching the holy name.
- **sādhaka** — A spiritual practitioner engaged in the disciplined practice (sādhana) of chanting and remembering the Lord.
- **bahiraṅga-mukha jīva** — An outward-looking living entity — one whose attention is turned toward the external world rather than the Lord within.
- **antaraṅga-mukha jīva** — An inward-looking living entity — one who turns attention inward to the heart, where the Lord can be met.
- **Prabhupāda** — Śrīla A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda, who gave all his followers the clear and central order to chant sixteen rounds daily without offense.
- **sixteen rounds** — The daily standard of japa (sixteen rounds of the mantra) that Prabhupāda established; here named the most important of the spiritual master's orders.
- **offenseless / offenses (in chanting)** — Clear chanting free of the faults (aparādha) against the holy name; its clarity is what allows the natural progression toward service and surrender to unfold.
- **surrender** — Placing Kṛṣṇa in the center of life by taking oneself out of the center; the natural culmination of the progression that chanting produces.
