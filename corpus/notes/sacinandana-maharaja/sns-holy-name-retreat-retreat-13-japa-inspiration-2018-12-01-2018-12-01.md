# SNS Holy Name Retreat Retreat 13 Japa Inspiration 2018 12 01 TK

## Theme

This japa-inspiration session reframes chanting from a feat of concentration into an act of loving devotion. Mahārāja opens with a practical diagnosis: when the mind, mood, and attention drift away during chanting, fatigue follows, but a mind that is wholly present keeps the chanter enlivened and untired. The first step, then, is to 'center' or collect oneself — bringing body, mind, and heart to the single place where the holy name is sounding: sitting straight, pronouncing the mantra clearly, and listening to the sound rather than merely speaking it. For a very restless mind, he offers the anchor of fixing attention on just the first 'Hare/Hari' of each mantra until absorption comes easily. But he is emphatic that these focusing techniques are only 'beginner's stuff' and not where the emphasis belongs. Real japa is a process of bhakti — activity done to please Kṛṣṇa, not mere concentration. The heart of the talk is therefore remembrance: chanting while remembering Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, name, form (the deity), or qualities such as His mercy establishes the loving connection that frees japa from the 'prison house' of mechanical, sleep-inducing repetition and makes it suddenly brilliant and alive. He illustrates this with Raghunandana dāsa Gosvāmī's image of relishing Rādhā's name as nectar/honey and Kṛṣṇa's name as condensed milk, blended with the fragrant ice of anurāga and drunk constantly, so that the tongue is captured and the whole life becomes infused with nectar. Centering is thus only the 'launch pad'; the real goal is connection with Kṛṣṇa. He closes by situating the practice in place and community — Ṭera Kadamba, the home of Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī, who systematized Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava philosophy at Mahāprabhu's empowerment — and in sincerity: the true measure of a day of sādhana is the sincerity of one's effort, not its outward results. The retreat even reserves the evening so that those near their target of 64 rounds can complete their vow with steady determination.

## Practical application

Begin each session by centering: sit straight, pronounce the mantra clearly, and consciously listen to the sound rather than just producing it, letting the vibration stay with you. If the mind is scattered, tether it to the sound — and when very absent-minded, narrow your focus to just the first 'Hare' or 'Hari' of each mantra until the mental energy shifts and absorption comes naturally. But treat this only as the launch pad, not the destination. Turn the chanting into bhakti by remembering Kṛṣṇa as you chant — call to mind one of His pastimes, His form as the deity, or a quality such as His mercy — so the name becomes a loving connection rather than a mechanical repetition. Use Raghunandana's meditation as a model: savor Rādhā's name as honey and Kṛṣṇa's name as condensed milk, sweetened by loving affection (anurāga), and 'drink' it continuously. Care for the body so it stays alert — move and breathe deeply when needed — but return to sit, because chanting together with devotees creates a supportive atmosphere; and when you do japa, do only japa, nothing else. Measure the day not by how the chanting felt or what results it produced, but by the sincerity of your effort. And once you are close to your daily vow (e.g. 64 rounds), summon the determination to finish it rather than leaving it incomplete.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Soft, individual repetition of the holy name on beads; here treated not as a concentration exercise but as an act of loving devotional service.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name(s) of God (Hari/Kṛṣṇa); the sacred sound that japa repeats and that the chanter is told to listen to.
- **Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra** — The mahā-mantra whose opening 'Hare/Hari' can serve as an anchor for a restless mind.
- **bhakti** — Loving devotion — doing something with the intent to please Kṛṣṇa; the process that real japa is meant to be.
- **Kṛṣṇa** — The Supreme Person of Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava devotion, whose name, form, pastimes, and qualities are remembered during chanting.
- **anurāga** — Deep loving affection or attachment for Kṛṣṇa; the 'fragrant ice' blended into the relished holy name in Raghunandana's image.
- **Rādhā** — Kṛṣṇa's foremost devotee and beloved; Her name is relished as nectar/honey alongside Kṛṣṇa's name.
- **Raghunandana dāsa Gosvāmī** — Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava teacher cited for the meditation of tasting Rādhā's and Kṛṣṇa's names as honey and condensed milk sweetened with loving affection.
- **sādhana** — Regulated spiritual practice; here its true worth is measured by sincerity of effort rather than outward results.
- **deity** — The worshipable form (mūrti) of Kṛṣṇa that a chanter may remember to make the name vivid and personal.
- **kirtan** — Congregational singing of the holy name; the retreat suspends the evening kirtan program so chanters can complete their rounds.
- **rounds** — Units of japa counted on a 108-bead mālā; devotees here aim at a target of 64 rounds.
- **Ṭera Kadamba** — The sacred place of Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī, from which a practitioner draws strength and direction for chanting.
- **Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī** — Empowered by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu to systematize Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava philosophy; in one sense the founder of Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism.
- **Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — The teacher who empowered Rūpa Gosvāmī to present the philosophy systematically after studying all the scriptures.
- **Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism** — The Bengali Vaiṣṇava tradition of Caitanya Mahāprabhu, systematized by Rūpa Gosvāmī, within which this japa practice stands.
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa; serving sincere, determined Vaiṣṇavas is described as an honor.
