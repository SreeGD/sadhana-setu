# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 06 Learning to Chant with Devotion 03 2024 11 24

## Theme

This lecture traces a single question to its root: how can we chant the holy name not mechanically but with love? Sacīnandana Mahārāja begins with the nature of the name itself — Kṛṣṇa is progressively revealed through it as nāma, rūpa, guṇa, and līlā, and Kṛṣṇa is not contained in the name but is identical with it. Yet at our present stage the material senses catch only the sound while Kṛṣṇa Himself escapes us; He is disclosed only to a heart that loves Him, just as a person is truly known only through affection. From this Mahārāja reframes the seminar away from technique toward awakening dormant affection. He draws the decisive contrast between kaivalya-nāma (mechanical chanting, which at best frees one from hell) and sneha-saṃyukta-nāma (chanting joined with affection, which moves Kṛṣṇa to reveal Himself and grant intimate service). He describes the goal-state — the lover of God who weeps, laughs, calls out, dances — while insisting that genuine bhāva is concealed, never advertised; self-proclaimed purity is suspect. Practically, one must chant 'full' names charged with love (prīti-pūrvakam), since love is the essence without which the name, like a rose without fragrance, is not itself; Kṛṣṇa is interested in love, not flawless performance, and His heart melts only for an offering made with affection (Vidura's meal over Duryodhana's feast). The deeper problem is where such love comes from. Following Jīva Gosvāmī, Mahārāja teaches that rati/rāga is innate and cannot be forced; the natural attraction of the heart is not destroyed but reoriented toward Kṛṣṇa. He distinguishes vaidhī-bhakti — the 'apprenticeship' of regulated, sometimes fear- or image-driven duty — from spontaneous, attraction-driven rāgānugā / parā-bhakti that follows the mood of those who already love Kṛṣṇa. Because the conditioned soul has no organic attachment for Kṛṣṇa, this devotion must be kindled indirectly: by hearing of the rāgātmikā Vrajavāsīs, which awakens lobha, the dormant greed to attain an eternal associate's mood. The transforming agent is meditation not on the Lord alone but on the reciprocal love between Kṛṣṇa and His devotees — the icy heart melts in the fire of their love (Gopāla-campū), and Kṛṣṇa secures the bond by His own vow never to leave those who love Him. Such hearing produces sphoṭa (a burst of self-evident meaning) and sphūrti (brief inner meetings with Kṛṣṇa born of separation). The lecture then turns to practice: a guided meditation (manaḥ-saṃharaṇam, withdrawing the mind; a meditation from Prabhupāda; then resting in the mood), sustained afterward by congregational chanting and dwelling in the dhāma, and grounded in śaraṇāgati — 'My Lord, I am Yours.' With body settled and spine erect, a body-scan returns one to 'I am alive' and then to the soul seated in the heart, where Kṛṣṇa is enthroned on an imagined 'diamond throne' and served inwardly (mānasī-sevā) — a worship that, done sincerely, actually becomes true. Finally Mahārāja addresses how to remain Kṛṣṇa-conscious all day: the work of smaraṇam, third after śravaṇam and kīrtanam. From Jīva Gosvāmī he draws two modes — mantra-upāsana (fixing the mind on one image, like a single lake) and līlā-smaraṇam (flowing through many pastimes, like a river of lakes). Invested with understanding, the deity moves from 'black and white' into colour and life; taste arises, ascending from tongue to mind to soul, and transforms the chanter. The name is the Lord in His most merciful form, opening the lotus of the heart and revealing Himself when tongue and heart serve Him; sphūrta manifestations grow longer over time, following a recognizable order of authentic experience. The whole path reduces to one instruction: always remember Kṛṣṇa and never forget Him — and the questions that words cannot settle are answered only by walking the path itself.

## Practical application

Make love, not technique, the measure of your japa. Stop chanting in a dry, hackneyed, rushed way; pronounce every name fully and feelingly — 'Here is Kṛṣṇa, here is Rādhārāṇī' — chanting prīti-pūrvakam, full names charged with affection rather than empty ones gabbled quickly. Do not be discouraged by imperfection: even a hundred thousand mistakes do not deter Kṛṣṇa, who responds to genuine love, not flawless performance. Because love cannot be manufactured or commanded, kindle it indirectly — feed regularly on hearing the loving exchanges between the Vrajavāsīs and Kṛṣṇa and how Hari reciprocates, since it is this meditation on reciprocal love, not effort to love directly, that melts the icy heart and awakens lobha. Honestly examine why you rise for maṅgala-ārati and sit to chant: duty, fear of criticism, or image-management (the lower, vaidhī motivation that is the necessary 'apprenticeship'), versus spontaneous eagerness for darśana of Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa after a night apart — and lean toward the latter. Settle the body before chanting: a stable posture with an erect spine, on floor or chair; let surfacing tension (coughing and the like) release without judgment; a brief body-scan from the toes upward returns you to 'I am alive' and then inward to the soul in the heart. While chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa, keep Kṛṣṇa within the heart and worship Him there — no costly temple is needed: imagine a diamond throne in your heart, seat Kṛṣṇa upon it (this is not imagination but actually becomes fact when done sincerely), and perform mānasī-sevā in sequence — wash His feet, offer garments and ornaments, then food, even a simple fruit you yourself relish. Pray the self-offering of śaraṇāgati, 'My Lord, I am Yours,' surrendering progressively. Invest your chanting and deity-meditation with understanding rather than performing it mechanically — seek out the meaning of the mantras you already sing (e.g. the 'Veṇuṁ kvaṇantam' verses) as mantra-upāsana, and let remembrance flow through Kṛṣṇa's pastimes as līlā-smaraṇam — for this is how taste arises and transforms a frustrated heart into a joyful one. After chanting, sit in stillness, 'without thinking anything, just be,' resting in the awareness that you are part of Kṛṣṇa and He is your unlimitedly kind master. Sustain the mood through congregational chanting and, where possible, residence in or remembrance of the dhāma. Keep your inner life unadvertised — never display devotional emotion as spectacle or proclaim yourself pure. Treat sphūrta — brief manifestations of the Lord that appear and fade in the beginning and lengthen with time — as a normal stage, neither clinging to them nor doubting them. Above all, hold to the single sufficient instruction: always remember Kṛṣṇa and never forget Him; Kṛṣṇa and the guru seated in the heart will reveal the rest.

## Glossary

- **nāma** — The holy name; the first thing realized of Kṛṣṇa, who is identical with His name.
- **rūpa** — The form of the Lord, revealed after the name in the natural succession of realization.
- **guṇa** — The qualities of the Lord, disclosed after His form.
- **līlā** — The pastimes of the Lord, realized after His qualities; the object of meditative remembrance.
- **kaivalya-nāma** — Chanting the name mechanically; per Jīva Gosvāmī it at best frees one from hell.
- **sneha-saṃyukta-nāma** — Chanting joined with affection (sneha), which moves Kṛṣṇa to reveal Himself and grant intimate service.
- **sneha** — Affection; the loving feeling that joins to chanting and attracts the Lord's notice.
- **bhāva** — Genuine ecstatic love of God; by nature concealed, never advertised.
- **bhāva-āveśa** — Absorption in ecstatic love — crying, laughing, singing — a state saints keep private.
- **prīti-pūrvakam** — 'Preceded by / done with love'; chanting each name fully and feelingly rather than dryly.
- **rati / rāga** — Innate, inborn loving attachment that cannot be forced; redirected toward Kṛṣṇa rather than destroyed.
- **vaidhī-bhakti** — Regulated devotion arising from the injunctions of śāstra; the 'apprenticeship' stage, sometimes driven by duty or fear.
- **rāgānugā-bhakti** — Spontaneous devotion that follows the mood of those who already love Kṛṣṇa; the higher, attraction-driven service.
- **parā-bhakti** — Direct, attraction-driven devotional service of Kṛṣṇa attained when the senses are fully purified; identified with rāgānugā-bhakti.
- **rāgātmikā** — Devotees of Vraja whose devotion is constituted of spontaneous loving attachment; hearing of them awakens the desire for such love.
- **lobha** — Intense greed or eagerness to attain the mood of an eternal associate of Kṛṣṇa; lies dormant and is triggered by hearing of the devotees' love.
- **taṭasthā lobha** — A momentary, spontaneously arising and fading greed (Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura), felt by beginners — distinct from the abiding natural lobha of advanced devotees.
- **Vrajavāsīs / Vraja** — The residents of Vraja (Vṛndāvana) filled with affection for Kṛṣṇa; meditation on their love melts the heart.
- **sac-cid-ānanda** — Eternity, knowledge, and bliss; the nature of Kṛṣṇa's body, owing to which He is never actually hungry and accepts only love.
- **maṅgala-ārati** — The early-morning worship service devotees rise for; rising merely to avoid blame exemplifies forced devotion.
- **brahma-muhūrta** — The auspicious pre-dawn period for spiritual practice; rising for it from fear rather than attraction is vaidhī.
- **darśana** — Sight of the deity (here Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa); the eagerness for it exemplifies spontaneous devotion.
- **śaraṇāgati** — Surrender; the body fully surrendered takes shelter and prays 'My Lord, I am Yours' — a progressive realization.
- **sphoṭa** — An 'explosion of meaning' — sudden self-evident clarity in the heart when scripture is heard from authentic sources among good devotees.
- **sphūrti / sphūrta** — A brief inner meeting with or manifestation of Kṛṣṇa in the mind or heart, often born of separation; fleeting at first, lengthening over time.
- **manaḥ-saṃharaṇam** — Withdrawing and collecting the mind; the first element of the guided meditation.
- **dhāma** — The holy abode (Vṛndāvana); devotees are meant to enter it and congregationally glorify Kṛṣṇa.
- **mānasī-sevā** — Mental service of the Lord — bathing, dressing, decorating, and feeding Kṛṣṇa seated within the heart.
- **śravaṇam** — Hearing; the first process of devotional service.
- **kīrtanam** — Chanting/glorifying; the second process of devotional service.
- **smaraṇam** — Remembering Kṛṣṇa; the third process, the key to remaining Kṛṣṇa-conscious throughout the day.
- **mantra-upāsana** — Meditation fixing the mind on the content of a single mantra — one image, like a single lake (e.g. the Veṇuṁ kvaṇantam verses).
- **līlā-smaraṇam** — Meditation flowing sequentially through many images of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes — like a river through interconnected lakes.
- **niśānta-līlā** — Kṛṣṇa's early-morning (end-of-night) pastimes, which manifest in the pure heart during dawn remembrance.
- **Bhāgavatam** — Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam; cited for its description of the devotee who has attained love of God.
- **Brahma-saṁhitā** — Scripture containing the 'Veṇuṁ kvaṇantam' verses chanted each morning, used as the basis for mantra-upāsana.
- **Caitanya-caritāmṛta** — Scripture prescribing congregational chanting and residence in Vṛndāvana to sustain the devotional mood.
- **Gopāla-campū** — Work by Jīva Gosvāmī; its testimony that only meditation on Vraja's reciprocal love melts the ice-cold heart.
- **Yoga-sāra-stava** — Jīva Gosvāmī's prayer (verse 38) asking that the innate rāga between man and woman be redirected to delight in the Lord.
- **Stavāmṛta-laharī / Anurāga-vallī** — Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's prayer begging for millions of mouths and tongues to glorify Kṛṣṇa's names and qualities.
