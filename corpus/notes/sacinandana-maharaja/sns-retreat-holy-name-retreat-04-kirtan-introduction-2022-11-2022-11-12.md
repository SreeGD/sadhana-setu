# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 04 Kirtan Introduction 2022 11 12

## Theme

This lecture serves as a meditative threshold into kīrtan, framing the act of singing the Holy Name as the natural culmination of hearing and dwelling on Kṛṣṇa's pastimes. Sacīnandana Mahārāja begins with the atmosphere of the evening — the hour when the moon rises, people grow calm, and the cows and animals return to rest — presenting this settled, conducive time as the ideal setting in which the heart can gather itself for absorption in Kṛṣṇa. From there he draws the listeners into Vraja: into Nanda Mahārāja's assembly hall, alive with clowns, musicians, Purāṇic reciters, and acrobats, where amid every conceivable entertainment the Vrajavāsīs' eyes never wander from Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma. This single-pointed fascination amid total distraction, he teaches, is the very nature of love. He then opens the confidential, conjugal heart of Vraja's līlā — Kṛṣṇa, put to bed as a small child by mother Yaśodā, slipping out once the lights go out to steal through the forest and meet the gopīs and Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī — revealing the sweetness toward which the whole tradition of hearing and chanting points. The arc closes by naming the purpose of all this remembrance: kīrtan sung with full attention is not performance but attentive absorption that engraves a deep and lasting impression on the heart. Thus the lecture moves from the outer setting (evening), through the inner disposition (loving, single-pointed attraction), into the confidential object of love (Vraja's pastimes), and arrives at the practice itself (attentive kīrtan that leaves a permanent saṁskāra).

## Practical application

A devotee can carry this teaching directly into daily japa and Hari-Nāma. First, honor the setting: where possible, choose a calm, conducive hour and a settled atmosphere — as the lecture models with the evening — so that the mind is naturally inclined toward absorption rather than agitation. Second, prepare the heart before chanting by hearing and dwelling on Kṛṣṇa's pastimes (Kṛṣṇa-kathā / līlā-smaraṇa); remembering a single sweet pastime — Kṛṣṇa in Nanda's hall, or His nightly forest līlā — softens and opens the heart and turns it toward the Lord, making the chanting that follows a continuation of that remembrance rather than a cold mechanical exercise. Third, cultivate single-pointed attention in imitation of the Vrajavāsīs whose eyes never left Kṛṣṇa: amid the many 'entertainments' and distractions of the mind during japa, keep returning the attention to the sound of the Name and to Kṛṣṇa, treating that focused attraction as the practice of love itself. Finally, chant for absorption, not performance — the measure of the round is not how it sounds or how fast it is finished but how attentively it is heard, because attentive chanting is what engraves a deep, lasting saṁskāra on the heart.

## Glossary

- **kīrtan** — Devotional singing/chanting of the Holy Names and glories of Kṛṣṇa; here presented as the heart's attentive absorption rather than a performance.
- **Kṛṣṇa-kathā** — Hearing and discussion of topics, narratives, and pastimes about Kṛṣṇa, itself a devotional practice that prepares the heart.
- **līlā** — The transcendental pastimes or 'play' of Kṛṣṇa, such as His childhood in Nanda's home and His nightly meetings in the forest.
- **līlā-smaraṇa** — Remembrance and contemplation of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes; dwelling on the līlā as a means of softening and turning the heart toward Him.
- **saṁskāra** — A deep, lasting impression engraved upon the heart/consciousness; attentive kīrtan leaves such a permanent impression.
- **Vrajavāsīs** — The residents of Vraja (Vṛndāvana), whose eyes and hearts remain fixed exclusively on Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma.
- **gopīs** — The cowherd maidens of Vraja, Kṛṣṇa's most intimate devotees, whom He meets in His confidential nightly pastimes.
- **Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī** — The foremost of the gopīs and the embodiment of the highest love for Kṛṣṇa, at the center of Vraja's conjugal pastimes.
- **Nanda Mahārāja** — Kṛṣṇa's foster father in Vraja, in whose assembly hall the entertainments described in the lecture take place.
- **Yaśodā** — Kṛṣṇa's foster mother in Vraja, who puts Him to bed as a small child before His nightly līlā.
- **Balarāma** — Kṛṣṇa's elder brother, beheld together with Kṛṣṇa by the Vrajavāsīs in Nanda's hall.
- **Purāṇic reciters** — Reciters of the Purāṇas (ancient sacred narratives), among the entertainers present in Nanda Mahārāja's assembly hall.
