# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 08 Chanting Prayerfully 2022 11 15

## Theme

This lecture, 'Chanting Prayerfully,' centers on the difference between mechanically completing one's rounds and genuinely chanting from the heart. Mahārāja frames the teaching through the story of a sadhu and a lady: the lady, wholly absorbed in her beloved, rushed past the sadhu without even noticing him, while the sadhu — though he had chanted the Lord's names all night — was merely repeating them absent-mindedly, never actually holding the Lord in his heart. From this worldly woman absorbed in her lover the sadhu received his correction, illustrating the Avadhuta's principle from the Eleventh Canto that instruction can be taken from anyone and any circumstance. The arc moves from this diagnosis of inattentive chanting toward its remedy: chant mindfully, even prayerfully, with the heart fully engaged. Because Krishna is the dearmost of all — our only true friend since time immemorial — the names being chanted are not abstract sounds but the names of the beloved. Mahārāja closes by distinguishing kīrtana from 'therapeutic' self-soothing: chanting is offered to Krishna, sung for Him, and this orientation toward Him is what resets the inner atmosphere, leaving the chanter not tired or disturbed but inspired, enlivened, and freshly ready to continue.

## Practical application

Before and during japa, consciously place Krishna in the heart rather than treating the rounds as a quota to finish. Remember as you chant that the name is the name of your dearmost friend and beloved, not a string of syllables — this single shift turns mechanical repetition into prayerful, attentive chanting. Sing the Holy Name for Krishna, not merely to calm or comfort yourself; let the chanting be an offering directed toward Him. When attention drifts or the mind feels disturbed, use the prayerful, heart-engaged orientation to reset the mental atmosphere and begin again fresh, returning to attentiveness round after round. Practiced this way, chanting does not leave one fatigued but inspired and enlivened. And take the broader lesson of the sadhu and the lady to heart: be willing to receive a lesson in attentiveness from any person or circumstance, however unlikely the source.

## Glossary

- **sadhu** — A holy man or saintly practitioner; in the story, the one who chants all night yet does so inattentively until corrected.
- **japa** — The personal, often quiet repetition of the Holy Name, typically counted on beads as one's daily 'rounds.'
- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name(s) of the Lord; the chanting of the names of Krishna.
- **kīrtana** — The singing of the Holy Name, offered to Krishna — distinguished here from therapeutic self-soothing.
- **Krishna** — The Supreme Lord, addressed here as the beloved and only true friend of all living beings since time immemorial.
- **Avadhuta** — The wandering, liberated mendicant of the Eleventh Canto (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam) who taught that instruction can be taken from anyone and any circumstance, drawing lessons from many gurus including worldly figures.
- **Eleventh Canto** — The portion of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam containing the Avadhuta's teaching on learning from all beings and situations.
