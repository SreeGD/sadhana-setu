# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 02 Guided Japa Session 01 2023 12 04

## Theme

This guided japa session frames effective sādhana around one central principle: intention is the heart of chanting. Mahārāja begins by stressing that the inner orientation of the mind — what he calls amatihi, alongside sankalpa — determines the entire character of one's japa. Sitting down with the conscious resolve 'now I wish to connect with Krishna' produces a wholly different experience and outcome than mechanically rushing to complete sixteen rounds. From this foundation he moves to the body, showing that physical posture supports attentive japa: one sits upright with a straight spine and holds it, recognizing that the body will inevitably slump into inattention so that the mind must be consciously reset and brought back again and again. Finally he gives the devotional mood to chant in — a prayer of surrender drawn from the Caitanya-caritāmṛta (Madhya-līlā): 'My dear Lord Krishna, for so many long years I have been totally forgetful of You; now today I turn to You as Your sincere and serious servant — please accept me.' He applies this honestly to himself, noting that such forgetfulness can persist even within one's ISKCON service. The lecture's arc thus moves from intention, through the supporting discipline of posture, to the surrendered, turning-toward mood that is the orientation to return to repeatedly throughout the chanting.

## Practical application

Before beginning rounds, pause and set a conscious intention — 'now I wish to connect with Krishna' — rather than sitting down only to finish sixteen rounds quickly; let this sankalpa, not mere completion, define the session. Sit upright with the spine straight and hold the posture; when the body slumps and attention drifts (as it inevitably will), consciously reset both body and mind, telling the mind 'I wish to remain attentive now' and bringing it back again and again. Throughout, chant in the mood of surrender: turn toward Krishna with the prayer that despite long forgetfulness — even forgetfulness that can creep into one's devotional service — one now comes as a sincere and serious servant asking to be accepted. Returning the mind to this turning-toward and this plea for acceptance, repeatedly, is the practical work of attentive Hari-Nāma.

## Glossary

- **sādhana** — Regulated spiritual practice; the disciplined effort by which a devotee cultivates devotion, here centered on chanting.
- **japa** — The practice of softly repeating the holy names of God, typically counted on beads.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name(s) of the Lord (Hari/Krishna); the chanting that is the heart of this practice.
- **amatihi** — The inner orientation or disposition of the mind — the intention with which one approaches chanting, treated here as the most important element of sādhana.
- **sankalpa** — A conscious resolve or intention set before practice; the deliberate inner orientation, e.g. 'now I wish to connect with Krishna.'
- **Krishna** — The Supreme Lord toward whom the chanting and surrender are directed.
- **16 rounds** — The standard daily quota of japa (sixteen times around a 108-bead mala) that a practitioner chants; contrasted here with mechanical completion as a goal.
- **Caitanya-caritāmṛta** — A principal Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava scripture recounting the life and teachings of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu; source of the surrender prayer cited.
- **Madhya-līlā** — The 'middle' division of the Caitanya-caritāmṛta, from which the prayer of surrender used in this session is drawn.
- **ISKCON** — The International Society for Krishna Consciousness; the devotional movement within whose service, Mahārāja notes, forgetfulness of Krishna can still persist.
