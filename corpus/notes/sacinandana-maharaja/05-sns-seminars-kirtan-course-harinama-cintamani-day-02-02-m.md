# 05 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 02 02 Morning Session ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This morning session of the Kirtan Course (Harināma Cintāmaṇi, Day 2) develops one unifying arc: the holy name is approached and matured through faith. Mahārāja frames chanting not as a distant goal but as abhidheya — the daily lived process of cultivating a relationship with Krishna, the way one continually loves and serves one's family. Faith (śraddhā) is presented as the strength of the soul that must be actively nourished like an athlete's body or an expert's mind; it stands at the beginning, middle, and end of the journey and must be concrete and constant, directed to śāstra and to the process of devotional service (a clarity he credits to Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura). From faith unfolds a five-stage progression drawn from the Harināma-cintāmaṇi tradition — śraddhā → vīrya (spiritual vigor and enthusiasm) → smṛti (undisturbed remembrance, the fixed 'North Pole') → samādhi (undivided 'Krishna absorption') → prajñā (realization). Faith is acquired chiefly through the association of the faithful and attentive service to a genuine guru, which dissolves anarthas and lifts one from nāmābhāsa (shadow) chanting to the pure name; in ISKCON this is enacted by reading Prabhupāda's books, associating through his vāṇī, and serving him. Devotees are urged to locate the 'stations' in life where faith can be refilled, like a traveler noting gas stations. The session then turns to kirtan practice: the sole criterion for choosing a melody is whether it draws one's devotional emotions out toward Krishna, since the tune is only the sugar coating and the holy name within is the medicine. Finally Mahārāja establishes the philosophical foundation from śāstra — the verse cited across Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Rūpa Gosvāmī's Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, and Bhaktivinoda's Harināma-cintāmaṇi — that the name is cintāmaṇi (wish-fulfilling), nitya-mukta (eternally free of material imperfection), caitanya (fully alive), and, most importantly, non-different from the named (abhinnatvān nāma-nāminoḥ, per the Padma Purāṇa). Through the parable of the poor girl, the messenger, and the prince, he shows that Krishna first comes disguised as the name, unrecognized, until a maturing relationship reveals the 'messenger' to be the prince himself. Haridāsa Ṭhākura is offered as the exemplar of all these symptoms, and Prabhupāda — who chanted even in sleep and under anesthesia — as the model of the name as perpetual presence.

## Practical application

A devotee turns this into daily japa by treating chanting as an ongoing relationship rather than a task to be completed: chant to be with Krishna each day, the way one continually loves one's family. First, tend faith deliberately — notice that faith must be concrete ('how I go about it,' not just 'I believe') and constant, so make it specific and rooted in śāstra and in the process itself. Identify your personal 'refill stations' — the association of faithful devotees, attentive service to guru, and, in ISKCON, reading Prabhupāda's books and serving through his vāṇī — and return to them regularly to replenish faith when it runs low. Watch for the fruits of strong faith in your chanting: an unforced eagerness to rise and chant (vīrya), a mind that stays fixed on Krishna regardless of circumstance (smṛti), deepening into absorption (samādhi) and realization (prajñā); this five-stage ladder can be applied directly to the holy name. In kīrtan, choose whatever melody actually draws your own devotional feeling toward Krishna — personal resonance, not a 'correct' tune, is the standard — while remembering the tune is only sugar coating and the pure name chanted in the mood of glorifying Krishna is the medicine that matters. Chant knowing the name is non-different from Krishna, so that japa is direct association with the person; do not be discouraged when the name at first seems unremarkable — like the messenger who is really the prince, the name reveals its identity as the relationship matures. Seek sustained, even 'forced' immersion (long kīrtans such as Radhadesh Mellows) to carry the name into the subconscious. And build in genuine pauses — after intense study or chanting let the mind rest and do something entirely different, even stepping outside among the trees, so it becomes fresh and elastic to absorb more.

## Glossary

- **Harināma-cintāmaṇi** — A work by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura on the science of the holy name, the source text for the course; it describes how service dissolves anarthas and the pure name comes to dance on the tongue.
- **abhidheya** — The daily process or means of practice — here, chanting understood as the ongoing enactment of one's relationship with Krishna, not merely a future goal.
- **śraddhā** — Faith; the strength of the soul, which in bhakti must be concrete and constant and directed to śāstra and to the process of devotional service.
- **vīrya** — Spiritual vigor, strength, and enthusiasm arising from faith — an unforced eagerness to chant and readiness to do anything in service.
- **smṛti** — Steady remembrance; an undisturbed mind that keeps the goal (Krishna) fixed regardless of circumstance — one's constant 'North Pole.'
- **samādhi** — Undivided concentration or absorption — 'Krishna samādhi' — a deeper stage beyond steady remembrance.
- **prajñā** — Realization; the mature fruit reached at the end of the progression faith → vigor → remembrance → absorption → realization.
- **anartha** — Unwanted material impurities or misgivings in the heart, dissolved through attentive service to a genuine guru.
- **nāmābhāsa** — A 'shadow' or reflected chanting of the name, still mixed with imperfection; one is lifted beyond it to the pure name (śuddha-nāma).
- **jīva** — The individual living soul; when the pure name dances on the tongue, 'the jīva dances, prema dances, and Māyā is forced to flee.'
- **prema / prema-bhakti** — Pure love of Krishna; what the holy name grants to the devotee who desires nothing else.
- **Māyā** — Illusion, the Lord's deluding material energy, which is forced to flee when the pure name manifests.
- **vāṇī** — The instructions and words of a spiritual master; associating with Prabhupāda through his vāṇī (books and teachings) is a means of cultivating faith.
- **cintāmaṇi** — A touchstone or wish-fulfilling gem/tree; the holy name is cintāmaṇi, granting whatever one desires.
- **dharma, artha, kāma, mokṣa** — The four material aims — religiosity, economic gain, sense enjoyment, and liberation — which the name yields to the materialist, as distinct from prema given to the devotee.
- **nitya-mukta** — Eternally liberated; the name, though present in the material world, is entirely free of material imperfection.
- **caitanya** — Fully conscious and alive; the name is eternally full of life force, not a dead material sound.
- **abhinnatvān nāma-nāminoḥ** — 'Because of the non-difference between the name and the named' — the śāstric principle (Padma Purāṇa) that the sound 'Kṛṣṇa' is non-different from Kṛṣṇa the person, making chanting direct association with the Lord.
- **caitanya-rasa-vigraha** — The form full of conscious spiritual mellow; a description of the holy name as Krishna himself — complete, pure, spiritual, and eternally liberated.
- **kīrtan** — Congregational or devotional singing/glorification of Krishna; a devotional act whose melody should awaken and carry devotional feeling toward Him.
- **rajas / tamas** — The material modes of passion and ignorance; melodies for kirtan should be relatively free of them, though Mahārāja treats this as secondary.
- **ācārya** — A spiritual teacher who teaches by example; all the ācāryas repeat the śāstric teaching that the name is non-different from Krishna.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture; faith is meant to rest on its statements, which is why the key verse on the name is studied when placing faith in it.
- **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** — The great exemplar of chanting who manifested all the symptoms of the faith-to-realization progression, offered for further study.
