# 08 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 03 03 Morning Session ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This morning session frames the entire practice of chanting the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra around three interlocking guidelines — chant attentively, chant with quality, and pray for mercy — and shows how each grows out of the last. Mahārāja begins by reorienting the sattvic calm produced by the preceding physical exercises: it is not an end in itself but a settled base to be harnessed for steady chanting. He then offers a concrete entry point drawn from Prabhupāda's purport in 'Teachings of Lord Kapila': the mantra opens by addressing Krishna's internal, pleasure energy — 'Hare' is a personal call to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī, who alone can reconnect the soul with Krishna — so the devotee focuses 'like a laser' on that first 'Hare' in every repetition, aided by cupping the hand at the ear to hear one's own voice. From this technique the talk widens into a vision of the Holy Name as an eternal stream, non-different from Krishna, into which one enters in three stages: observing the mantra from outside, stepping in with concentration on the first Hari, and finally releasing the technique to float, absorbed. Absorption is described as passive and receptive rather than effortful — one lets the sound work on and carry the self — culminating in a prayerful mood of surrender, 'O Krishna, O Radha, please accept me.' Mahārāja then presses the point that quality outweighs quantity: rounds chanted carelessly are themselves a fault, and inattention (pramāda) is the root offense from which all other offenses arise — so grave that even continuous chanting may not yield prema while it persists. Crucially, this offense cannot be conquered by one's own effort; following Haridāsa Ṭhākura, one must chant in solitude with full concentration and beg constantly for the Lord's mercy, since becoming a genuine kīrtanīya depends entirely on grace — which is why 'pray' is the third guideline. The same attentiveness applies equally in congregational kīrtana, where attentive hearing is the whole point of call-and-response, and where taste appears immediately the moment one works on careful hearing. The session closes with method: a text is truly retained only through śravaṇa, manana, and nididhyāsana — always ending in working out how to apply it — and by forming a sankalpa, an inner resolve carried like an inspiring friend and offered before the deities as a prayer to emerge transformed.

## Practical application

In daily japa, use the calm from any preceding exercise or settling as a base rather than a goal, then anchor each new mantra by bringing full attention 'like a laser' to only the first 'Hare' — a personal call to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī — instead of straining to concentrate on every word. Cup a hand near the ear to hear your own chanting more clearly. Treat this 'first Hari' focus as a training aid for the restless mind: once the mind settles, drop the technique and let absorption become effortless, opening the ears, mind, and heart to receive the sound and letting it carry you, ending in a prayer of surrender ('O Krishna, O Radha, please accept me'). Prioritize quality over quantity — never rush rounds carelessly or add rounds for show at the cost of attentiveness. Following Haridāsa Ṭhākura, chant in a solitary place, alone, for a short focused period at first, praying continuously for the ability to chant the clear name with a devotional mind. Because inattention (pramāda) is the root offense that no personal effort can remove, beg for the Lord's mercy while chanting — for example praying before the deities after kīrtana. Carry the same attentiveness into congregational kīrtana by hearing carefully in both the leading and responding turns, and while singing simultaneously pray for the capacity to truly hear the Name. Finally, make a teaching your own by studying it in three steps — reading/hearing it (śravaṇa), reflecting on it, ideally with others (manana), and stopping to work out its application (nididhyāsana) — and form a sankalpa, a resolve offered as a prayer before the deities, to keep what matters in view throughout your practice.

## Glossary

- **sattvic (sattva)** — The peaceful, calm quality of goodness; here the settled state produced by physical exercise, to be harnessed to support steady chanting rather than treated as an end in itself.
- **mahā-mantra (maha-mantra)** — The 'great chant' — the Hare Krishna mantra; described as an eternal stream, non-different from Krishna and sharing his eternality.
- **Hare** — The opening word of the mantra, understood as a personal call to Krishna's internal pleasure energy — 'O Rādhārāṇī, O energy of the Lord' — and treated as the mantra's root or entry point.
- **Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī** — Krishna's pleasure (internal) energy, addressed by 'Hare'; she alone can reconnect the soul with Krishna, and the prayer to her is 'Please engage me in your service.'
- **japa** — Individual, quiet repetition of the mantra, counted in rounds; to be done attentively, in solitude, with a mind concentrated and dependent on the Lord's mercy.
- **saṅkīrtana / kīrtana** — Congregational chanting of the Holy Name, typically call-and-response; requires the same attentiveness as japa, with attentive hearing between leader and group as its central discipline.
- **Nāma-saṅkīrtana** — Congregational chanting specifically of the Holy Name (nāma), in which one strives to chant fully attentively while feeling dependent on the Lord's mercy.
- **Holy Name (Nāma)** — The name of Krishna, non-different from Krishna himself and eternal; chanting it is entering an ever-present divine current rather than merely producing a present-moment sound.
- **pramāda** — The offense of inattention; identified as the root from which all other chanting offenses arise, so that even ceaseless chanting may not yield prema while it persists, and which uniquely requires the Lord's help to overcome.
- **prema** — Pure love of God, the ultimate fruit of chanting, which may fail to appear if the offense of inattention (pramāda) is committed.
- **kīrtanīya** — One who chants — a genuine chanter or devotee; becoming one depends entirely on the Lord's grace rather than on mastering techniques and skills.
- **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** — The great teacher of the Holy Name cited here, who recommends chanting alone in a solitary place with full concentration and teaches that the offense of inattention specifically requires Krishna's help.
- **śravaṇa** — Hearing or reading a text — the first of the three-fold methods of study by which a teaching is truly absorbed.
- **manana** — Reflecting on a text, ideally in a group — the second of the three-fold methods of study.
- **nididhyāsana** — Considering how to apply a text — the final step of study that makes a teaching one's own and prevents forgetting it.
- **sankalpa** — An inner resolve or orientation carried throughout practice like an inspiring friend; brought before the deities and offered as a prayer to emerge transformed, happy, and enlivened in Krishna consciousness.
- **Teachings of Lord Kapila** — Prabhupāda's book (ch. 14, purport to verse 32) from which Mahārāja draws the method of understanding 'Hare' as a call to Krishna's internal energy.
