# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 07 Evening Krishna Katha 2023 08 18 Slovenia

## Theme

Built around the life of Nārada Muni, this lecture traces the path of the Holy Name from first contact to perfection, and locates that perfection not in attainment but in longing. When the boy Nārada lost his mother — his only material protector — the loss revealed a truth that was always so: that he was wholly in the hands of God. This launched his single-pointed quest for the Lord of his heart. Though the Lord is formless (amūrta) to material eyes, He has a form made of sound, the mantra-mūrti; through mantras such as oṁ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya, Nārada approached Him in meditation, and Kṛṣṇa once appeared — heralded by tinkling ankle-bells and fragrance, radiant with beauty (lāvaṇya) and lotus eyes — only to vanish, leaving Nārada crying 'Where are you, Kṛṣṇa?' From this Mahārāja draws the lecture's pivot: no mechanical process compels the Lord to appear; as one cannot make the sun rise, sādhana does not produce the vision but keeps us awake and receptive so we are not asleep when mercy comes. The Lord Himself explained He appeared only once precisely to kindle an eager desire to see Him again — and it is this intense longing for darśana that burns away material attachment. What matters is not how often the Lord shows Himself but that the devotee stays yearning; chanting the Holy Name calls Him into the heart, where He answers 'Here I am.' Nārada, orphaned and yet fearless among the beasts because 'Kṛṣṇa is always with me,' embodies the settled fruit: roaming free, chanting and dancing, satisfied within. The lecture then names this mood through Caitanya Mahāprabhu's secret inner orientation — not 'I have seen Kṛṣṇa' but 'I have not seen Kṛṣṇa, yet I long to see Him' — which is vipralambha, love in separation. The word itself means to attain what lies at a distance: separation is the very means of attainment, and the longing is the guarantee of meeting. Because the soul belongs to Kṛṣṇa as naturally as pollen to the lotus, the fitting mood is helpless dependence. Material life is an ocean where karma tosses us like waves and lust, greed, anger and envy lurk as crocodiles; the partial shelters of karma, jñāna, yoga, family and money are only sea-weeds a drowning man grabs. The only rescue is for Kṛṣṇa or Rādhārāṇī to lift us up — illustrated by the fragrance-less Krishna-cūḍā flower whom Rādhārāṇī's compassion sets in Kṛṣṇa's crown, and by the chariot on Her lotus feet: we cannot travel far on our own strength, but sheltered in the mood of longing we are carried swiftly and can fight the sharks of lust and anger. Reading Rumi's 'Love Dogs,' Mahārāja shows that the crying itself is already the answer: the howling for the master is the connection, and the very longing to chant is not the devotee's achievement but Kṛṣṇa's own gift and investment, which will bring Him to the one who chants. He gathers further proofs of how longing is evoked and rewarded — the Ṣaḍ-gosvāmī prayers crying 'Where is Rādhārāṇī? Where is Govardhana? Where is the Yamunā?'; Madhumaṅgala's 'Madhu's gone' plunging Rādhārāṇī into separation though Kṛṣṇa stands beside her; Kṛṣṇa unable to keep his four-armed Nārāyaṇa disguise before Rādhā's perfect love; and Kṛṣṇa slipping away the moment spiritual pride claims His favor as one's own. Love transcends time, so Mahāprabhu's parting message is to always remain in the mood of longing, expressed through enthusiasm in service and above all in chanting the Holy Name. The lecture closes practically: two things develop this mood — the 'drowning man realization' that one cannot reach Kṛṣṇa by one's own strength but can only cry out, and to 'do something in the meantime' by actively expressing desire through service — together with a formula for quality chanting: intention, sincerity, and attention.

    - cf. Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu
- **[01:01:57.760]** Mahāprabhu's formula for cultivating longing: serve with such full absorption, attention and devotion that you are effectively calling Krishna to come and personally accept your service. Krishna responds to this mood — he comes, accepts, then withdraws again precisely to deepen the desire, returning by his grace and leaving by his own wish.

- **[01:05:30.180]** Hope is the element that keeps longing strong — the gopīs, mad in separation, question the trees and animals because they still believe Krishna can be found. By chanting he has developed prema (jātānurāgaḥ).In great longing to see the Lord, his heart melts like molten gold. “Oh! That thief, the son of Yaśodā, has entered a house to steal the butter. He does not care whether people laugh at him, praise him, respect or disrespect him (loka-bāhyaḥ).

## Practical application

Approach japa as the means of calling Kṛṣṇa into the heart rather than as a technique that earns His appearance: your effort cannot compel the vision any more than it can make the sun rise — its real work is to purify the heart and keep you awake and receptive so that, when mercy comes, you are not too covered or asleep to feel His presence. Before each round, catch the wandering mind by consciously forming an intention to chant; chant with sincerity, from your real self rather than a performed identity (chanting well to please and inspire fellow devotees is a valid motivation, since chanting together generates spiritual energy); and give attention by gently returning the mind to the Holy Name each time it strays — never settling for going through the motions when tired, hungry, or reluctant, which yields no spiritual experience. Above the mechanics, cultivate the mood of vipralambha, longing in separation: do not measure progress by how often Kṛṣṇa is felt, but stay eager and yearning, holding the inner stance 'I have not seen Kṛṣṇa, yet I long to see Him.' Anchor this in the 'drowning man realization' — admit honestly that you cannot reach Kṛṣṇa on your own strength and, like a drowning man, simply cry out for rescue while begging to be considered a particle of dust at His lotus feet — and balance it by 'doing something in the meantime': actively express that desire through absorbed, attentive devotional service, which itself invites His mercy. Take shelter at the lotus feet of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa as the chariot that carries you where your own striving cannot, trusting that the very longing to chant is already Kṛṣṇa's gift to you and will draw Him to you; and guard against spiritual pride, remembering that any sweetness or favor in chanting is His mercy alone, not your achievement.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name of the Lord; chanting it summons Kṛṣṇa's personal presence into the heart, where He responds 'Here I am.'
- **japa** — The practice of chanting the Holy Name on beads; in this lecture, done with intention, sincerity, and attention and in the mood of longing.
- **amūrta** — Formless; the Lord as He appears to material eyes, who nonetheless has a form made of sound.
- **mantra-mūrti** — The Lord's form made of sound — the mantra/Holy Name as the accessible form of the otherwise unmanifest Lord.
- **oṁ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya** — The mantra Nārada received and used as the very means of meditative approach to the Lord.
- **lāvaṇya** — Bodily radiance or luster; the glowing beauty of Kṛṣṇa's form as Nārada beheld it.
- **darśana** — Sight or vision of the Lord; longing for His darśana is what burns away remaining material attachment.
- **sādhana** — Regulated spiritual practice; it does not produce the vision of the Lord but keeps the practitioner awake and receptive for it.
- **vipralambha** — Love in separation; literally to attain that which lies in the distance — separation is the very means of attainment and the perfection of chanting.
- **Paramātmā** — The Supersoul, the Lord within the heart; in Rumi's poem, the one who reveals that the longing itself is the return message.
- **karma, jñāna, yoga** — Paths of fruitive action, knowledge, and mystic discipline; named here as only partial shelters, like sea-weeds a drowning man grabs, unable to give permanent refuge.
- **Krishna-cūḍā** — A fragrance-less flower on a thorny tree; emblem of one who feels useless and far from spiritual life, yet whom Rādhārāṇī's compassion sets in Kṛṣṇa's crown.
- **Nārāyaṇa** — The four-armed form of the Lord in which Kṛṣṇa hid among the gopīs; before Rādhārāṇī's perfect love the two extra arms fall away and He stands revealed as Kṛṣṇa.
- **gopīs** — The cowherd maidens of Vraja, whose maddened questioning of trees and animals in separation exemplifies hope-sustained longing for Kṛṣṇa.
- **Ṣaḍ-gosvāmī** — The six Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana, whose prayers cry out in separation — 'Where is Rādhārāṇī? Where is Govardhana? Where is the Yamunā?' — exemplifying the mood of longing.
- **Rādhārāṇī** — Kṛṣṇa's foremost devotee; her mercy lifts up the desperate and hopeless and carries the soul like a chariot to Kṛṣṇa's service.
