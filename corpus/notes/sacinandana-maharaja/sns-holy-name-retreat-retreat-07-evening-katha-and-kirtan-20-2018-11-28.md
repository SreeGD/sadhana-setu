# SNS Holy Name Retreat Retreat 07 Evening Katha and Kirtan 2018 11 28 GV

## Theme

The lecture moves from a prayer to its application, framing Hari-Nāma as the meeting between a helpless soul and an eager Lord. Mahārāja opens by reading a prayer from Yāmunācārya's Stotra Ratna — one valued enough by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura to merit a purport — using it to soften and attune the heart before chanting. The prayer paints the conditioned soul as drowning since time immemorial in the shoreless ocean of saṃsāra, unable to see its beginning or end and finding no shore by his own effort. Deliverance comes not through strength but through a helpless cry of 'rescue me!': when the soul cries out, the Lord comes, his lotus feet become the shore, and a seed of hope arises. This is the reciprocity of mercy — the surrendered, helpless soul is the 'most appropriate recipient' of the Lord's unlimited mercy, his very helplessness being his qualification. From this devotional foundation the lecture turns practical: the insights of the day are to be carried straight into kīrtana, treated as the practice ground. Mahārāja offers a method that engages the whole person — body (clear pronunciation and attentive hearing), mind (active and passive focus), and heart (an expression of devotion). He then deepens the mood of absorption through a story: a sadhu chanting at night is knocked over by a young woman who never even notices him, because her heart is wholly fixed on a beloved she has been separated from for a year. The sadhu reflects that had he been as absorbed in the Lord as she was in her lover, no disturbance could have registered — and he ought to be far more absorbed, since his beloved is the supreme Lover, not a temporary one. This yields the central instruction: chant in the mood of an abhisārikā, the heroine on an urgent, single-pointed journey to her beloved, letting nothing divert the heart. Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī is held up as the supreme abhisārikā — moving secretly through Vraja's lanes by night, unafraid of hissing snakes whose mouths she gently covers, mistaking a black tamāl tree for Krishna and embracing it so that even the tree bursts into ecstatic horripilation — a measure of the power of pure prema, which awakens ecstasy even in matter. The retreat itself is cast as a 'launchpad' from which devotees, themselves abhisārikās, are launched toward Krishna; having come to the house of God, they must now knock at his door, and that door is opened by chanting the holy name. Citing Bhūrijana Prabhu that the Lord of Vṛndāvana is 'only a prayer away,' the lecture closes by framing the kīrtana as the act of joyful approach — sung loudly, full-voiced, and heartfelt, which is itself the practice and not mere performance.

## Practical application

Begin japa or Hari-Nāma by first softening the heart — read or recite a prayer (as Mahārāja does with Yāmunācārya's verse) to attune yourself before chanting, and let the prayer's mood take hold: feel yourself as the helpless soul who cannot cross saṃsāra alone, and chant as a sincere cry for rescue, since it is precisely your helplessness that qualifies you to receive the Lord's mercy. Carry the day's hearing and insight directly into the chanting rather than leaving them behind. Engage your whole person: the body through clear, careful pronunciation and attentive hearing of each name; the mind through both active and passive focus; and the heart through a genuine expression of devotion. Above all, cultivate the mood of the abhisārikā — chant as one on an urgent, single-pointed journey to meet the person most dear, refusing to let any external distraction divert the heart, just as Rādhārāṇī was so absorbed that even snakes and obstacles became occasions of love rather than fear. Treat the holy name as the door of God's house and your chanting as the knock that opens it; remember that Krishna is 'only a prayer away,' so the name sincerely chanted closes all distance. In congregational kīrtana, sing loudly, full-voiced, and from the heart — this wholehearted participation is itself the practice.

## Glossary

- **Yāmunācārya** — One of the most ancient Vaiṣṇava ācāryas; author of the prayer book Stotra Ratna.
- **ācārya** — A spiritual teacher who instructs by precept and personal example.
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee or worshipper of Viṣṇu/Krishna.
- **Stotra Ratna** — Yāmunācārya's prayer book, the title meaning 'The Jewels of Prayer.'
- **Caitanya-caritāmṛta** — The classic biography of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, which quotes the prayer Mahārāja reads.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — A revered Vaiṣṇava ācārya who so valued the prayer that he wrote a purport (commentary) on it.
- **saṃsāra** — The cycle of repeated birth and death; here pictured as a shoreless ocean the soul cannot cross by its own effort.
- **japa** — The soft, personal chanting of the holy name, usually on beads.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari); its chanting as spiritual practice.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational singing of the holy name; here treated as the practice ground for the day's insights.
- **sadhu** — A holy man or renunciant devoted to spiritual practice.
- **abhisārikā** — The heroine who journeys, urgently and single-mindedly, to meet her beloved — offered as the model mood for chanting.
- **Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī** — Krishna's foremost beloved, held up as the supreme abhisārikā.
- **Krishna** — The Supreme Lord; here the 'greatest Lover' and the object of the chanter's journey.
- **Vṛndāvana** — The sacred forest land of Krishna's pastimes, to which the abhisārikā journeys.
- **tamāl tree** — A dark-barked tree whose blackness resembles Krishna's complexion; Rādhā mistakes one for him in the moonlight.
- **Rūpa Mañjarī** — An intimate maidservant of Rādhā, whose reminder cannot stop Rādhā from embracing the tamāl tree.
- **prema** — Pure love of God, so powerful it awakens ecstasy even in matter.
- **horripilation** — The standing of hairs on end (here, in the tree) as a bodily sign of ecstatic devotion.
- **Bhūrijana Prabhu** — A senior devotee teacher cited for the saying that the Lord of Vṛndāvana is 'only a prayer away.'
