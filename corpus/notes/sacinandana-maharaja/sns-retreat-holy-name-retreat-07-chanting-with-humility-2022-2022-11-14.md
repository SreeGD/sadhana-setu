# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 07 Chanting with Humility 2022 11 14

## Theme

This lecture, the seventh in HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja's Holy Name Retreat series on chanting with humility, traces a single arc: the holy name becomes real only when the chanter turns to Krishna in humble, helpless prayer. Mahārāja opens by naming the retreat's purpose — to recover the inward, 'vertical' movement of spiritual life that the outward mission can crowd out, a danger Śrīla Prabhupāda himself warned of when he cautioned leaders against neglecting the real goal of becoming 'mad for Krishna.' He then confronts the 'most disturbing question': if chanting is the easiest process in Kali-yuga, why is it so hard? The answer, echoing Haridāsa Ṭhākura, is that by our own strength it is simply not possible; victory over the material mind comes only by Krishna's mercy. The prescribed remedy is therefore to add prayer to practice — chant, adhere to the discipline, and pray for the strength to chant — approached in a mood of humble helplessness. Mahārāja grounds this in Krishna's own vows: through Govardhana, Ajāmila, Dhruva, and the Vrajavāsīs, he shows that Krishna binds Himself to protect anyone who surrenders even once, even unconsciously, yet — being 'shy' — He will not impose Himself and waits for the soul to call out. From this flows the lecture's central distinction: chanting 'without nectar' (lip-deep sound while the mind is elsewhere) versus chanting 'with nectar' (the name held in a heart that knows its relationship with Krishna). The mere physical sound vibration is not yet the Holy Name; the Name 'descends' when one chants in realized humility and an established relationship (sambandha). Mahārāja then makes humility concrete across body, mind, and speech — repentant feeling, the neutral 'vision of reality' that corrects the ego's distortions, menial service, and truthful rather than flattering speech — illustrated by Ajāmila's repentance and the queen of Kashmir who traded her ornaments for a broom. He insists that kīrtana must be wedded to hearing (Hari-kathā as the svarūpa-śakti of the name), since hearing — especially from a realized devotee, like a bee gathering honey from the lotus — restores faith and replenishes devotion when it 'dries up.' Finally he resolves the apparent tensions: humility is not opposed to confidence but is its truest source; crying out for mercy is not opposed to joy, for spiritual 'tears' are the highest ecstasy; and prayer offered before acting (śaraṇāgati) — not karma-yoga that brings Krishna in only at the end — is where miracles begin. Using the image of a revving car stuck in neutral, he closes on the one variable left to us: to turn to Krishna, who is already present and has vowed to come to our aid.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this by reframing daily japa as prayer rather than performance. Begin by fixing a regular time (ideally early morning) and a steady place, fulfilling Prabhupāda's vow of sixteen rounds without being trapped in a 'counting game.' Adopt a simple three-step sequence each round: settle the body into an attentive, listening posture; bring the mind to each distinct syllable of the holy name; then 'breathe life' into it by chanting in a prayerful, humble mood — addressing the sound TO Krishna (envisioning Him, His form, or Rādhārāṇī) rather than merely reciting it. When the mind wanders or drifts into mechanical lip-service, catch it and deliberately exchange that state for its opposite — meditate on the name's meaning or rest the gaze on the Deity. Crucially, add a third limb to chanting and discipline: pray for the strength to chant, in a mood of humble helplessness ('I am fallen, I have no strength, I try and fail — please give me strength and accept me'), since progress comes by grace, not effort alone. Cultivate humility in three registers: in the heart, through genuine, felt repentance over inattentive or incomplete chanting; in the mind, by watching oneself dispassionately from a neutral-observer position that bypasses the ego's flattery and self-criticism; and in the body, through concrete menial service to other devotees — washing a devotee's cloth, helping serve prasādam, cleaning, or simply giving someone your full attention. Guard the tongue against idle chatter (jalpa/prajalpa) and quarrelsome argument (vitaṇḍā), offering honest praise and truthful feedback instead of flattery. Wed chanting to hearing: read scripture and hear Hari-kathā — especially from realized devotees — daily, because hearing builds the faith and the relationship (sambandha) that make the name complete and revives devotion when it dries up. Focus less on quantity and more on avoiding offenses, which a Krishna-absorbed, ego-free mind naturally escapes. When energy flags in chanting, do not push harder on your own strength — call out for mercy. And carry the principle beyond japa: before any devotional duty or venture outside your comfort zone, pray first (śaraṇāgati), so that Krishna acts through you rather than being summoned only after the fact.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name of Krishna; the practice of chanting the names of God, central to spiritual life in the present age.
- **japa** — Soft, individual recitation of the Hare Krishna mantra, typically counted on beads as a daily prescribed practice (e.g., sixteen rounds).
- **kīrtana** — Congregational or vocal glorification of Krishna through song and chanting of His names.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present age of quarrel and hypocrisy, in which chanting the holy name is given as the recommended and easiest process of self-realization.
- **sādhana** — Regulated devotional practice undertaken to progress toward love of God.
- **sādhaka** — A practitioner engaged in sādhana.
- **mantra** — A spiritual sound formula; here the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra, understood as a direct address to Krishna and His energy.
- **sambandha** — One's relationship with Krishna; the realized knowledge ('I am the servant of Krishna, His energy') that makes chanting of the name complete and real.
- **śaraṇāgati** — Surrender to Krishna; here specifically praying to Him BEFORE acting, as distinct from doing things on one's own strength.
- **karma-yoga** — Action performed by one's own effort with Krishna brought in only afterward — contrasted here with śaraṇāgati's prior surrender.
- **Krishna-smaraṇa** — Remembrance of Krishna; here remembering and turning to Him before undertaking an activity.
- **śravaṇa** — Hearing about Krishna; the devotional process that enlivens chanting and rebuilds faith.
- **Hari-kathā** — Spiritual discourse and narration about Krishna; described as the intrinsic potency of the holy name and the necessary companion to chanting.
- **svarūpa-śakti** — The intrinsic, internal potency (of Krishna); here, Hari-kathā as the essential energy that completes the Holy Name.
- **akiñcana** — One who possesses nothing materially; the materially exhausted soul who, free of external coverings, can sincerely approach Krishna.
- **prasādam** — Sanctified food offered to Krishna and then distributed; serving it is given as an act of humble devotional service.
- **samādhi** — The tomb or memorial shrine of a great devotee; here the resting place where the queen of Kashmir's broom was laid on her chest.
- **sampradāya** — The disciplic succession or authorized spiritual lineage; here rooted in the teachings of Rūpa Gosvāmī.
- **jalpa / prajalpa** — Idle talk and mundane chatter; speech to be avoided as it corrupts the intelligence.
- **vitaṇḍā** — Quarrelsome argumentation that disputes for and against without aiming at truth; a fault of speech to be given up.
- **vāda** — Speech directed toward truth; the constructive form of discourse to be cultivated in place of jalpa and vitaṇḍā.
- **Vrajavāsīs** — The residents of Vraja (Vṛndāvana), whom Krishna vowed never to forget but to keep always in His heart.
- **Vṛndāvana / Vraja** — Krishna's intimate spiritual abode and the setting of His pastimes; visited as the root-place of the sampradāya and a means of receiving mercy.
- **GBC** — Governing Body Commission, the managerial leadership of ISKCON; referenced in connection with Prabhupāda's 1972 warning against neglecting inner advancement.
- **Govardhana** — The hill Krishna lifted to shelter the residents of Vraja from Indra's deluge, cited as proof that protection comes from crying out to Him.
