# 04 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 02 01 SB Lecture ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam class opens Day 2 of the Kīrtan Course (based on Harināma Cintāmaṇi) and sets the theme for the whole day: śraddhā (faith), and specifically what faith in the process of chanting the holy name means. Taking the verse Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja cites in Caitanya-caritāmṛta Madhya 22.63 as the entry point, Mahārāja explains why a course on the holy name must begin with faith — because Harināma Cintāmaṇi (ch. 2) names faith as the sole qualification for seriously taking up chanting. The Lord has invested the holy name with all His energy and offered it to everyone without restriction, yet only those who have developed śraddhā actually become adhikārīs who take shelter of the name; as Caitanya Mahāprabhu told Kṛṣṇadāsa at Puri, the rate of a devotee's advancement corresponds to the degree of his faith. Mahārāja then defines faith carefully — the turning of consciousness toward bhakti while setting aside all other means, expressed through śaraṇāgati (surrender), following Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura, Jīva Gosvāmī and Patañjali — and shows it as a journey from the mere removal of doubt and a favorable leaning toward Kṛṣṇa, up to complete clarity of heart that protects the devotee like a mother. He grounds this in the verse 'yathā taror mūla-niṣecanena': as watering a tree's root nourishes the whole tree and feeding the stomach satisfies all the senses, worshiping Kṛṣṇa alone automatically discharges every other duty and obligation. Faith bears fruit progressively — vīrya (spiritual vigor), smṛti (a steady, undisturbed mind), samādhi (undivided concentration on Kṛṣṇa) and finally prajñā (direct realization) — and (per Viśvanātha Cakravartī's Mādhurya-kādambinī) rests in two places: the śāstras, which give the eyes of spiritual vision and replace worldly faith, and the process (abhidheya) of devotional service. Using Jīva Gosvāmī's gold-rush analogy, Mahārāja delivers a candid self-criticism of the movement: after 50 years devotees praise 'the gold' (the goal) but must now become 'scientists' of the method, since the missing training in HOW to chant has cost the movement most of its members. He assures that Nāma-kīrtana genuinely works because it is given in śāstra and proven by example; and (with Viśvanātha on Kṛṣṇa's words to the gopīs) that when Kṛṣṇa delays revealing Himself it is instruction, not neglect — the delay cultivates humility and purifies the heart. Finally he analyzes the gradations of faith through the three modes — tāmasa faith that trusts what destroys (intoxication), rājasa faith in one's own endeavor, sāttvika faith in regulated practice — culminating in transcendental (nirguṇa) faith in pure bhakti and the holy name alone. Faith, he concludes, is dynamic and cultivable: sincere immersion in the course will visibly shift one's faith upward.

## Practical application

Treat faith as the working foundation of your japa, not a settled assumption. First, interrogate what you actually mean by 'faith' in the holy name — move consciously from doubting whether the Name is Kṛṣṇa Himself to genuinely liking the Name and being convinced that chanting will bring you to Him. Feed that faith daily as deliberately as you feed the body: read and discuss Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam among devotees, hear of Kṛṣṇa's qualities (savior of the fallen, affectionate to His devotees), and let scriptural conviction gradually loosen your faith in worldly things. Then repose faith in the process itself: don't merely praise the goal — become a 'scientist' of chanting, learning and practicing the actual method attentively, giving your best to your rounds even when no visible result appears, exactly as a gold-seeker keeps digging. Chant in all circumstances of life, relying on the Name alone since the spiritual process needs no external provision. When Kṛṣṇa seems not to respond, read the delay as His personal instruction to deepen humility, recognize your offenses, and purify your heart, so you enter deeper realization pleasing to Him rather than 'disturbed.' Steady the mind (smṛti) with a realistic view of the community — others' imperfections are no reason to be shaken, since everyone is still developing on the path. Watch the quality of your faith and steer it upward through the modes: away from intoxicated (tāmasa) and self-reliant (rājasa) faith, toward regulated sāttvika practice — rising early, chanting your fixed rounds, living simply — and ultimately toward the transcendental conviction that 'if I just chant, the holy name is everything.' As a concrete retention habit, right after hearing a class turn to a neighbor and briefly say what you learned, so it isn't lost by breakfast. Sustained sincere practice is guaranteed to shift your faith over time.

## Glossary

- **śraddhā** — Faith; the turning of consciousness toward bhakti while setting aside other means. Identified as the sole qualification for taking up devotional service and especially the chanting of the holy name.
- **Harināma Cintāmaṇi** — The scripture (by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura) on which the Kīrtan Course is based; its second chapter, 'Taking up the chanting of the holy name,' names faith as the qualification for serious chanting and features Haridāsa Ṭhākura.
- **adhikārī** — A qualified possessor of the name — one who, having developed faith, actually takes shelter of the holy name and is thus fit to make best use of it.
- **bhakti** — Devotional service to Kṛṣṇa; the process for which śraddhā alone is the qualifying entrance.
- **śaraṇāgati** — Surrender; the mode through which faith comes into active expression (definition drawn from Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura and Jīva Gosvāmī).
- **Nāma-kīrtana** — Chanting/glorification of the holy name; the actual process (given in śāstra and proven by example) by which the devotee attains Kṛṣṇa.
- **japa** — The soft, repeated personal chanting of the holy name on beads; part of the daily practice faith strengthens one to sustain.
- **yathā taror mūla-niṣecanena** — The Bhāgavatam verse illustrating faith: as watering a tree's root nourishes trunk, branches and twigs, and feeding the stomach satisfies all the senses, so serving Kṛṣṇa alone accomplishes all other duties.
- **acyuta** — 'The infallible one,' a name of Kṛṣṇa; worshiping Him alone through devotional service automatically fulfills all other obligations.
- **seva** — Devotional service; by rendering it to Kṛṣṇa one is understood to have already discharged all material responsibilities to forefathers, living entities and demigods.
- **vīrya** — Spiritual vigor and energetic endeavor that faith yields — the strength to pursue bhakti and overcome obstacles, including long hours of kīrtana and japa.
- **smṛti** — An undisturbed, steady mind that keeps the goal and śāstric advice in view despite inner and outer disturbances; a fruit of strengthened faith.
- **samādhi** — From 'sama' (equal/same) plus 'dhī' (intelligence): a godly (not God) consciousness of undivided concentration on Kṛṣṇa, to which deepening faith carries the devotee.
- **prajñā** — Direct realization that sees things as they are, distinguishing soul, world and God; the final fruit of matured faith.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture — especially Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam — the first resting place of faith, giving the 'eyes' of spiritual vision that light one's path in this age of forgetfulness.
- **laukika-śraddhā** — Worldly faith; faith placed in mundane things, which a devotee gradually loses as he reposes faith in scripture.
- **abhidheya** — The process or means of attaining Kṛṣṇa (devotional service) — the second resting place of faith; knowing the goal is not enough, one must practice the method.
- **Mādhurya-kādambinī** — Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's work identifying śraddhā as the first stage a candidate reaches, comprising firm belief in the bhakti scriptures and exclusive aspiration to follow their process.
- **guṇas (three modes)** — The three modes of material nature — ignorance, passion and goodness — which produce corresponding gradations of faith.
- **tāmasa** — Faith in the mode of ignorance (tamas): trust invested in what actually destroys the person, e.g. intoxication, drugs and party indulgence mistaken for enjoyment.
- **rājasa** — Faith in the mode of passion: the trust that one will make life successful through one's own endeavors.
- **sāttvika** — Faith in the mode of goodness: trust that regulated spiritual practice — rising early, chanting one's rounds, sāttvic living — will purify consciousness and perfect life.
- **nirguṇa** — Transcendental, beyond the modes: faith in pure bhakti and the holy name alone — the conviction that simply chanting, the holy name is everything.
- **gopīs** — The cowherd maidens of Vṛndāvana; in Kṛṣṇa's words to them (with Viśvanātha's explanation), He delays revealing Himself to surrendered souls precisely to perfect their worship through humility.
- **Caitanya-caritāmṛta (Madhya-līlā 22.63)** — The passage where Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja quotes the entry Bhāgavatam verse within his discussion of faith, serving as this class's opening text.
