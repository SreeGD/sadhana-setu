# 12 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 04 03 Morning Session 02 ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This final morning session of the Kirtan Course centers on chanting the Holy Name in sambandha — from within a conscious, established relationship with Kṛṣṇa rather than as abstract singing. Mahārāja frames the practice with three brief meditations: on oneself ('I am a part of Kṛṣṇa'), on Kṛṣṇa ('my most merciful Lord and Master'), and on one's purpose ('I am in this world only to sing for / make contact with Kṛṣṇa'). He recommends beginning with the meditation on the self, because affirming 'I am a part of Kṛṣṇa' immediately situates the chanter in relationship rather than isolation. Practically, one first becomes peaceful, centers oneself, and deliberately releases the past — a passing moment of joking or tiredness is gone — so the only present concern is singing for Kṛṣṇa. During kīrtana one holds the awareness of being Kṛṣṇa's eternal, tiny servant (a technique some kīrtanīyas use is to go in the mind to a sacred place or before their beloved deities and sing from there), and the awareness that though one may have forgotten Kṛṣṇa, He has never forgotten His servant. This very life, body, and moment is the opportunity to revive a dormant connection. The whole meditation is summarized in one prayer of surrender: though one has forgotten the Lord for long years in the material world, today one surrenders as His sincere and serious servant — and it is this sincere surrender, not the mechanics of chanting, that frees one from māyā. The session closes by grounding these ideas in Haridāsa Ṭhākura's teaching that only two purely spiritual entities exist here — the jīva and the Holy Name — and the entire task of spiritual life is to connect them, establishing sambandha with the Name. Mahārāja notes that of all he teaches, he most cherishes speaking of the Name in devotee-saṅga, and directs the devotees to study the ten offenses not academically but personally, pairing up over the Harinām Cintāmaṇi to grasp each offense and, above all, how to overcome it in their own chanting.

## Practical application

Before you begin your japa or kīrtana, pause to become peaceful and centered, and consciously let go of whatever just happened — a joke, fatigue, a distraction — so that your only present business is to sing for Kṛṣṇa. Then chant in sambandha: hold three simple meditations, starting with 'I am a part of Kṛṣṇa' to place yourself at once in relationship, then 'Kṛṣṇa is my most merciful Lord and Master,' and 'I am here only to make contact with Him.' As you chant each round, remember you are His tiny eternal servant; if it helps, mentally go to a place sacred to you or stand before your beloved deities and sing to Kṛṣṇa from there. Chant with the conviction that although you may have forgotten Him across many lifetimes, He has never forgotten you — and that this very body and lifetime is your chance to make that dormant connection alive again. Let the mood be one of sincere surrender rather than mechanical repetition, echoing the prayer: 'My dear Lord Kṛṣṇa, though I have forgotten You for many long years, today I surrender unto You as Your sincere and serious servant.' Treat the whole of sādhana as the work of connecting the two spiritual entities present — yourself (the jīva) and the Holy Name. And study the ten offenses personally, not academically: take up the Harinām Cintāmaṇi, ideally with other devotees, and for each offense ask concretely how you yourself will recognize and overcome it in your own chanting.

## Glossary

- **sambandha** — One's established relationship or connection with Kṛṣṇa; here, chanting the Holy Name consciously from within that relationship rather than abstractly.
- **kīrtana / kirtan** — The congregational or personal singing of the Holy Names of Kṛṣṇa; the devotional act at the center of this session.
- **Holy Name / Hari-Nāma** — The name of Kṛṣṇa, understood as non-different from Kṛṣṇa Himself and as one of only two purely spiritual entities present in this world.
- **jīva** — The individual living soul or self; per Haridāsa Ṭhākura, one of the two purely spiritual entities to be connected with the Holy Name.
- **māyā** — The material illusory energy that binds the forgetful soul; sincere surrender to Kṛṣṇa frees one from its clutches.
- **kīrtanīya** — One who performs kīrtana; a chanter or leader of the singing of the Holy Name.
- **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** — The great Vaiṣṇava teacher honored as the ācārya (master exemplar) of the Holy Name, cited here for the teaching that spiritual life is the joining of the jīva and the Name.
- **Harinām Cintāmaṇi / Harināma Cintāmaṇi** — The classic text on the glories and offenses of the Holy Name, used by the devotees as a study guide for understanding and overcoming the ten offenses.
- **ten offenses (to the Holy Name)** — The traditional list of offenses (aparādha) in chanting — including inattention — to be studied personally and overcome in one's own practice.
- **saṅga (devotee-saṅga)** — Spiritual association; the company of devotees, in which hearing about and chanting the Holy Name is especially prized.
