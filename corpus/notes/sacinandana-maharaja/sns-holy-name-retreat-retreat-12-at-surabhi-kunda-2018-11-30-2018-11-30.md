# SNS Holy Name Retreat Retreat 12 At Surabhi Kunda 2018 11 30 GV

## Theme

This Holy-Name retreat lecture, delivered at Surabhi Kuṇḍa, turns on the theme of forgiveness for offenses and what it takes for the holy name to truly bring Krishna near. Mahārāja roots the teaching in the place itself: Surabhi Kuṇḍa is where Indra, after assaulting Vraja with apocalyptic monsoon showers, came to beg Krishna's forgiveness once Govardhana was lifted — making it a tīrtha for the repentant. Through Brahmā's counsel to approach Krishna not alone but from behind a cow (so that the offender receives the remnant of Krishna's smile, drawing near to the Lord through what is dear to Him) and the truth that Krishna keeps no place in His heart for His devotees' faults — seeing instead the little service offered — the lecture establishes that the Lord is inclined to forgive even great offenders like Indra. This becomes the diagnosis of our own chanting: when we chant we unwittingly commit nāma-aparādha, which is why the holy name still feels far away, and our root offense is a long, long absent-mindedness — forgetfulness of Krishna. The retreat therefore calls for serious, heartfelt begging of forgiveness, beginning with praṇāms to Prabhupāda, and for worship done with full inward presence, transforming external ritual (the ārati candle offering, the chanting) into a genuine offering of love rather than a mechanical rite. Mahārāja exalts the dhāma — Krishna never takes a single step out of Vṛndāvana — and urges chanting with urgency and longing, crying out so that Krishna's presence does not slip away. He illustrates that the Lord still reveals Himself to sincere, intensely longing devotees through the story of Mādhavendra Purī, whose pure absorption drew Krishna to come in person as the boy at Govinda-kuṇḍa and to disclose the buried, hungry Gopāla/Giridhārī deity now worshipped at Nathdwara. The lecture's central message: the holy name brings Krishna, but only chanting freed from the fog and clouds of offenses powerfully pulls the Lord into the heart. Citing Prabhupāda's Geneva definition of chanting as a means to keep Krishna continually in the heart, Mahārāja makes purification concrete and volitional — stop the nāma-aparādhas (criticizing devotees, disobeying the guru, belittling the chanting) and replace them with a positive desire for immortal life with Krishna. He closes by inviting devotees to internalize the holy place — 'make a photo with your heart' — and to offer a spontaneous, sincere prayer arising in the moment.

## Practical application

Treat your daily japa as the very work this lecture describes: a place to beg forgiveness and to purify chanting until it actually keeps Krishna in your heart. Begin by acknowledging that, like Indra, you have offended — and that your deepest offense is chronic absent-mindedness, forgetfulness of Krishna — then offer praṇāms (starting with Śrīla Prabhupāda) and seriously ask for forgiveness rather than chanting on autopilot. Approach the Lord through what is dear to Him and in a mood of humble repentance, trusting that He looks past your faults to the small service you offer. During every round and during ārati or kīrtana, stay inwardly present: do not let the body go through the motions while the mind wanders; consciously turn the external act into a loving offering from the heart. Work concretely and volitionally to stop the nāma-aparādhas — do not criticize devotees, do not disobey the spiritual master, do not belittle the chanting — and replace them with an active, positive longing for spiritual, immortal life with Krishna. Chant with urgency and feeling, almost crying out for Krishna, so that His presence does not slip away; it is this intensity of longing, as with Mādhavendra Purī, that draws the Lord to respond personally. When on pilgrimage or in the dhāma, internalize it rather than merely photographing it — 'make a photo with your heart' — and let your prayer be your own spontaneous, sincere words of the moment rather than only a prescribed formula. The goal is offenseless chanting, the kind that powerfully pulls Krishna into the heart and keeps Him there continually.

## Glossary

- **Surabhi Kuṇḍa** — A sacred pond near Govardhana, held to be the place where Indra came to beg Krishna's forgiveness after the lifting of Govardhana; honored as a tīrtha specifically for seeking forgiveness for offenses.
- **Govardhana** — The sacred hill of Vraja that Krishna lifted to shelter the residents of Vṛndāvana from Indra's torrential rains.
- **Vraja / Vṛndāvana** — The land of Krishna's pastoral pastimes; the supreme dhāma, which Krishna is said never to leave even by a single step.
- **Indra** — The king of the demigods who, in pride, attacked Vraja with devastating monsoon rains and later repented and was fully forgiven by Krishna.
- **tīrtha** — A holy place of pilgrimage; here, a site whose spiritual potency can free a sincere devotee from the reactions of offenses.
- **dhāma** — A holy abode of the Lord; the sanctified spiritual地 of Vṛndāvana, which retains the power to absolve offenses.
- **nāma-aparādha** — Offenses committed against the holy name (e.g., criticizing devotees, disobeying the spiritual master, belittling the chanting) which obscure the name's potency and keep Krishna feeling distant.
- **aparādha** — An offense or transgression in devotional service that must be recognized and given up.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name(s) of the Lord; the chanting of Krishna's names as the central practice of devotional life.
- **japa** — The soft, attentive personal chanting of the holy name, usually on beads.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational or sung chanting of the holy name; here urged to be done with urgency and crying longing for Krishna.
- **ārati** — A worship ceremony of offering items such as a lit candle/lamp to the Deity, which is meant to be done with full presence of mind as an offering of love.
- **praṇāms** — Respectful obeisances; here, offered first to Śrīla Prabhupāda as one begins to beg forgiveness.
- **parikramā** — Circumambulation of a holy place; Mādhavendra Purī received the Lord's revelation after completing his Govardhana parikramā.
- **Mādhavendra Purī** — A great saint whose intense, loving devotion drew Krishna to appear to him personally and reveal the buried Gopāla deity.
- **Govinda-kuṇḍa** — The pond near Govardhana where, in the Mādhavendra Purī narrative, Krishna came as a cowherd boy bringing milk.
- **Gopāla / Giridhārī** — The Deity form of Krishna revealed to Mādhavendra Purī as buried and hungry in the ground, retrieved and served, now worshipped at Nathdwara.
- **Nathdwara** — The temple town where the Gopāla/Giridhārī Deity discovered by Mādhavendra Purī is now worshipped.
- **Brahmā** — The demigod creator who counseled the offender to approach Krishna from behind a cow, so as to receive the remnant of Krishna's loving smile.
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda** — The founder-ācārya whose Geneva lecture defined chanting as the means to keep Krishna continually present in the heart; obeisances are offered to him first.
