# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 08 2014 09 19 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

Sacīnandana Mahārāja frames the whole talk around a single diagnosis: successful chanting of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra fails not because the Holy Name is weak or absent, but because we cover it. The Name is always fully present like the sun; our offenses (aparādhas) are the clouds. He opens by insisting that spiritual life, unlike material life, cannot be managed by outward appearances — the heart's hidden 'cellar' of secret weaknesses and attachments must actually be cleansed, and one must become an inward-looking practitioner. Drawing on the Harināma-cintāmaṇi (whose fifteen chapters establish faith/śraddhā as the prerequisite and devote ten to the offenses), he argues that rather than fighting all ten offenses branch by branch over many years, one should cut the poisonous tree at its root. That root is inattention — pramāda — the 'Ur-disease' from which every other offense and all the anarthas arise. This emphasis descends from Caitanya Mahāprabhu, who gave elaborate instruction on attentive chanting to Sanātana Gosvāmī at Jagannātha Purī and to Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī in South India, a teaching preserved through Haridāsa Ṭhākura. He explains why inattention is so stubborn: the mind by nature grasps material objects (wealth and sexual enjoyment are its 'perfect material match') and turns away from Kṛṣṇa — the condition of bahir-mukha, being outwardly facing, our original problem. Physical presence without attention yields no real benefit, like sleeping through a lecture or chanting round after round while still offensive and tasting nothing (no nāma-ruci). The cure is to turn back to Kṛṣṇa and chant heart-deep, not lip-deep — 'heart service, not lip service' — as a love affair with God in which the lover continually calls the beloved's names. He breaks inattention into three forms — indifference, inertia, and restlessness of the mind — and prescribes a threefold method of real study (śravaṇa, manana, and applied remedy), warning against rote 'university' learning and urging devotees to restate the teaching in their own words and live it. He notes that devotees can chant sixteen rounds dutifully for years yet remain indifferent and unconnected to the Name, missing the movement's whole purpose: purification of the heart, for which Mahāprabhu has given all the keys. The closing remedies are honest self-recognition of one's own indifference (seen in small choices like checking email over rounds), and association — sitting and chanting for sustained hours with a Vaiṣṇava who has genuine attraction for the Name, so that his taste transmits into one's own heart. The retreat itself is presented as exactly this program, and the lecture as a 'present' to be carried home and re-read.

## Practical application

Stop treating japa as a quota of outer appearances and start cleaning the inner 'cellar' of the heart. Because inattention (pramāda) is the single root of all offenses, a devotee's daily work is not to police ten separate offenses but to chant attentively — turning the mind back to Kṛṣṇa and chanting with a sense of relationship, calling out to Him heart-deep rather than lip-deep. Watch for the three faces of inattention in your own rounds: indifference (not caring), inertia (too lazy to work the relationship), and restlessness (attending to everything except Kṛṣṇa). Honestly catch the small everyday signals of indifference — for example, breaking off rounds to check email for an 'important' message — and recognize that admission as the first remedy. Study the Holy Name's principles the living way, not by rote: hear/read the teaching (śravaṇa), reflect on where it actually applies in your own life (manana), then take up the concrete cure for your particular fault and change how you live; restating it in your own words and explaining it to others fixes it far more deeply. Above all, take association: seek out a Vaiṣṇava who chants properly with real attraction for the Name and chant in his company for substantial, sustained time each day — scaling the practice up (four rounds / 24 minutes, then double or quadruple) so his taste gradually transmits into your heart. Use a retreat as exactly this kind of extended chanting-in-good-association. Finally, carry the instruction home and deliberately re-read it again and again; repeated return to the teaching is itself what wears away inattention over time. Get this one thing — attentiveness to Kṛṣṇa — clear, and kindness to devotees, freedom from 'I and mine,' and growing attachment to chanting follow of themselves.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma / Holy Name** — The names of God, especially as chanted in the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra; said to be always fully present like the sun, obscured only by the chanter's offenses.
- **Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra** — The principal mantra of the tradition, chanted on beads as 'rounds'; chanting it while still committing offenses yields no real benefit.
- **Harināma-cintāmaṇi** — A fifteen-chapter work on the Holy Name (by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura) that establishes faith as the prerequisite for chanting and devotes ten chapters to the offenses against the Name.
- **aparādha** — Offense against the Holy Name; the 'clouds' that cover the ever-present Name like clouds cover the sun and prevent the chanter from tasting it.
- **pramāda** — Inattention, carelessness, or negligence (here distinguished from its common rendering 'craziness'); identified as the 'Ur-disease,' the single root from which all other offenses and anarthas arise.
- **anartha** — Unwanted material desires or impurities in the heart; depicted as the clouds, born of inattention, that block the 'Krishna-sun' from shining on the heart.
- **śraddhā** — Faith; the prerequisite established in the Harināma-cintāmaṇi for chanting the Name successfully.
- **nāma-ruci** — Taste or relish for the Holy Name; its absence — chanting round after round without 'a drop of taste' — signals that offenses are covering the Name.
- **bahir-mukha** — Being outwardly facing — turned away from Kṛṣṇa toward material objects; described as our original problem since coming to this world, most obvious when we try to chant.
- **prema** — Pure love of God, the goal of chanting; may fail to appear even in one who has overcome all other offenses if the offense of inattention (pramāda) remains.
- **śravaṇa** — Hearing or reading the teaching — the first stage of the threefold process of real study.
- **manana** — Reflection — pondering the teaching and locating where it actually applies in one's own life — the second stage of study, preceding the application of remedies.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational or melodic chanting of the Name; even rhythmic, well-voiced kīrtana 'switches off' like over-played music unless it is heart-felt chanting for Kṛṣṇa.
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa; the prescribed association is to chant with a Vaiṣṇava who chants properly and has genuine attraction for the Name, so his taste transmits to one's own heart.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture; among the objects (along with devotees, Lord Viṣṇu, and the guru) one may outwardly respect while still remaining inattentive and turned away from Kṛṣṇa.
- **guru** — The spiritual master; respected by the devotee, yet such respect does not by itself cure the root offense of inattention.
- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — Lord Caitanya, source of the emphasis on attentive, non-negligent chanting, which he gave to Sanātana Gosvāmī at Jagannātha Purī and to Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī in South India.
- **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** — The great chanter through whom Caitanya's teaching on avoiding inattention was preserved; cited that even continuous, otherwise offenseless chanting may not yield prema while pramāda persists.
