# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 13 2014 09 21 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

This Holy-Name retreat lecture guides devotees through a complete arc of inner preparation and absorbed chanting on the banks of the Yamunā. It opens by releasing the chronic, hidden tension most people unknowingly carry, then settles awareness on the breath as a 'bridge to the inside' that carries attention from the surface of life to the inner self. Rather than fighting the restless, protesting mind, the practitioner is taught to ignore its chatter and gently return to the breath, meditating from the heart-space — the seat of feeling and of inner vision. From there one visualizes a sacred, safe setting on the Yamunā and reconnects with one's true identity through the sambandha prayer: recovering the knowledge that one is Kṛṣṇa's eternal servant, distinct from the gross and subtle body, fallen through long inimicality but rescued by contact with Śrīla Prabhupāda's movement. The meditation deepens into a sacred bath in the Yamunā — three successive dips that progressively dissolve identification with the body, mental concepts, and finally the false ego, removing the three coverings before approaching the Lord, accompanied by prayers to Yamunā Devī for purification. Once purified, the practitioner envisions Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Śyāmasundara beneath a kadamba tree, served by Their confidential associates, and turns the chanting into direct, personal prayer to Them. The latter portion concentrates on the inner mood of japa and kīrtana: to chant from the platform of the soul, setting aside body, mind, and every other identity, because at the level of the soul there is only one relationship — the self and Kṛṣṇa. Quieting the self-critical 'mental noise' and learning to be alone with Kṛṣṇa, the devotee gains an inner strength that then flows outward; this is not withdrawal into hermitage but the means of entering all relationships as a grounded, whole 'real person.' The lecture closes by honoring the deep, absorbed inward state that such meditation can produce, treating it as something to be gently honored rather than abruptly broken.

## Practical application

A devotee can use this as a template for daily japa and Hari-Nāma. Before chanting, pause to notice and consciously release the held inner tension in the body, then rest attention briefly on the breath — breathing in, knowing you breathe in; breathing out, knowing you breathe out — using it as a bridge from surface distraction into the heart. When the mind protests or wanders, do not argue with it; simply ignore the chatter and return to presence. From the heart-space, visualize a peaceful, protected setting on the banks of the Yamunā, and recite the sambandha prayer to re-establish your identity as Kṛṣṇa's eternal servant. Take an inner sacred bath in three dips — releasing identification with the body, then mental concepts, then false ego — and pray to Yamunā Devī for purification of the day's reactions. Then bring Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Śyāmasundara before the inner eye beneath the kadamba tree and address the Holy Name to Them directly and personally, singing for Their pleasure rather than mechanically. For the duration of the rounds, set the body aside and put down the mental world of thoughts and worries, chanting from the platform of the soul where the only relationship is you and Kṛṣṇa. Treat this time as being genuinely alone with the Lord — using the Name to quiet self-critical mental noise and to draw strength that you then carry back into all your daily relationships and duties. When the chanting ends in a deeply absorbed state, come out of it gently rather than abruptly, honoring the inwardness it has cultivated.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name of the Lord (Hari/Kṛṣṇa); the practice of chanting it.
- **japa** — Soft, personal repetition of the Holy Name, typically on beads.
- **kīrtana** — Sung or congregational chanting of the Holy Name and the Lord's glories.
- **sambandha prayer** — A prayer of relationship that re-establishes one's true identity as Kṛṣṇa's eternal servant, distinct from the body and mind; 'sambandha' refers to one's constitutional relationship with the Lord.
- **Yamunā** — The sacred river associated with Kṛṣṇa's pastimes; here, the spiritual water that bestows love for Kṛṣṇa and is non-different from the water of the spiritual world.
- **Yamunā Devī** — The personified deity of the Yamunā river, daughter of the sun-god, invoked to wash away sinful reactions and purify the practitioner.
- **Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Śyāmasundara** — The Divine Couple — Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa (Śyāmasundara, the beautiful dark-complexioned Lord) — envisioned beneath a kadamba tree as the object of the chanting.
- **kadamba tree** — A flowering tree associated with Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, beneath which Rādhā-Śyāmasundara are visualized.
- **Nanda Mahārāja** — Kṛṣṇa's foster-father in Vraja; 'the son of Nanda Mahārāja' is Kṛṣṇa, for whom the Yamunā bestows love.
- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — The 16th-century avatar of Kṛṣṇa who inaugurated the congregational chanting of the Holy Name; His words are used in praying to the Yamunā.
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda** — The founder-ācārya of the devotional movement whose contact restores the practitioner's knowledge of being Kṛṣṇa's eternal servant.
- **gross and subtle body** — The two material coverings of the soul — the gross physical body and the subtle body of mind, intelligence, and false ego — from which the self is distinct.
- **false ego** — The mistaken identification of the soul with the body and mind; the third covering dissolved in the third Yamunā dip.
- **threefold miseries** — The three categories of material suffering — those caused by one's own body/mind, by other living beings, and by nature/providence.
