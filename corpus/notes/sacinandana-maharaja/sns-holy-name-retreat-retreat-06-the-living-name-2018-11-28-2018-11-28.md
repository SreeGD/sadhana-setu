# SNS Holy Name Retreat Retreat 06 The Living Name 2018 11 28 GV

## Theme

Sacīnandana Mahārāja opens from a paradox: the holy name is itself fully alive — Krishna present as a divine person — yet our chanting is often lifeless, completed out of habit or duty rather than from a living, tasted experience. Drawing on the analogy of the five prāṇas that animate the body, he frames the whole retreat as five practices, handed down by Prabhupāda, the previous ācāryas, and scripture, that can bring 'dead' chanting back to life. The arc turns on a single word: taste (rasa). Every being craves taste and recoils from mechanical rote activity; the enjoyment people chase through marriage, household and sense gratification actually resides in Krishna, the unlimited reservoir of all rasa. Approaching Krishna directly is pure honey from the comb; worldly objects are honey fallen in the dust — always mixed with disappointment and frustration. Liberation itself, as Haridāsa Ṭhākura tells Lord Caitanya, is a question of taste: the soul abandons the world's mixed pleasures easily only when a superior nectar is present. The name is a budding flower mercifully placed in this world, and chanting unfolds in observable stages — closed bud, then Krishna's form, then His sixty-four qualities, then His pastimes in the eight periods of the day, and finally His bhakti-śakti revealing the chanter's own eternal identity; a bud that never opens rots and must be cut away, so making the practice blossom is necessary for survival, not a luxury. The lecture's foundational truth is then stated bluntly: 'you cannot chant the holy name.' The material senses can never grasp the name, form or pastimes; the real name only appears (sphurati) from the spiritual realm, and only for one who is sevonmukha — turned toward the Lord in service. We cannot climb to Krishna, but He descends into our senses when we chant in a serving mood, felt as a sudden change of atmosphere and of heart. Service is rendered with the sādhana-śarīra — the body and mind we presently have: sit with an aligned spine, pronounce clearly, listen attentively, and engage the mind and heart. From here Mahārāja teaches the mind's three functions — active focus (laser-like concentration placed into the mantra), passive reception (relaxing and letting the name speak to you), and the equilibrium that arises automatically as a by-product of alternating between the two, illustrated by a man entering a river: wading in by effort, then standing as the current comes to him, then being carried to the ocean. Practical refinements follow — first activate the mind's focus-potency on just the first 'Hare,' the optional mantra-trāṭaka of gazing at the written names, focusing internally without raising the voice. But every technique can turn mechanical; the one thing that never does is bhakti, the inclination to serve. So the final movement turns from technique to the heart: chant in a mood of longing and humble separation as Caitanya Mahāprabhu did, offer total surrender (he commends the Pātya-pañcaka prayer), and when one 'doesn't know how to offer the heart,' consciously build a framework of gratitude, since gratitude opens the heart. Because bhakti lives in the hearts of devotees, association is the reliable way to rekindle it — and congregational kīrtana, per the Harināma-cintāmaṇi, most swiftly dissolves offenses, since Krishna pours mercy on the whole assembly. Devotion is ultimately stronger than Krishna Himself, who makes Himself subordinate to His devotee's love; the entire aim is therefore to chant with devotion, sought in the company of devotees.

## Practical application

Treat your daily rounds as service rendered with the body and mind you already have, not as a concentration exercise to be performed perfectly. Before chanting, sit in a stable cross-legged posture with a straight spine (keep the ability to sit on the floor through simple stretching as you age), pronounce each name clearly without slurring, and listen attentively, capturing the sound with the ear. Form the conscious intention 'I am doing this to serve Krishna's holy name' — the same outward act done in a serving mood moves you into another realm, and this holds even when you feel tired, moody or faithless: acknowledge the condition and still offer the chanting. Work the mind's three functions in alternation rather than straining: actively place the mind laser-like into the mantra; when that cramps, relax and passively receive the sound as the body receives water, letting the name speak to you; equilibrium then comes on its own, like being carried by a river's current. If you cannot yet hold the whole mantra, concentrate all mental energy on just the first 'Hare' of each round — internally, without chanting louder — and extend focus from there; optionally gaze at the written mantra and scan each name left-to-right as an aid. Above technique, chant with the heart: become aware that you are an eternal soul who has traveled far and now has a chance to reconnect; let a mood of longing and humble separation arise, crying to be lifted from material existence — this is a valuable devotional mood, not weakness. When you 'can't feel anything,' deliberately construct a framework of gratitude (the rare human birth, like-minded devotees, a holy place) so grateful feeling opens the heart, and consider daily recitation of a surrender prayer such as the Pātya-pañcaka. Whenever you 'catch a whiff' of devotion during chanting, seize it, since the fixed preparatory steps can themselves go mechanical. When chanting goes badly, turn to association: read prayers, hear Prabhupāda's lectures, sit with devotees and reveal your mind — and prefer congregational kīrtana even when solo chanting feels easier to focus in, because Krishna pours mercy on the whole assembly and you are caught in that downpour. Overcome past offenses by patiently continuing to chant (offenses to the name clear with time) and by using the same mouth to praise and serve Vaiṣṇavas you may have offended; the swiftest remedy is to sit in the assembly of Vaiṣṇavas performing kīrtana. During intensive practice, 'park' arising doubts to revisit at a set time so absorption is not broken. Finally, watch for the long-term blossoming in your life over the years; if after many years you experience none of the holy name's miracles, take it as a signal the chanting has not yet come alive, and do everything to make it blossom.

## Glossary

- **holy name (Hari-Nāma)** — The name of Krishna, understood as identical with Krishna Himself — a fully alive divine person, not a mere sound.
- **prāṇa** — Life air; the five prāṇas are the vital airs that animate the body — used as the analogy for the five practices that bring chanting to life.
- **rasa** — Taste; the relishable flavor of an experience. All beings crave rasa; Krishna is the unlimited reservoir of all taste.
- **bhakti** — Devotion; devotional service to Krishna. The inclination to serve, which alone never turns mechanical and which is ultimately stronger than Krishna Himself.
- **bhakti-śakti** — The Lord's devotional potency, which in the final stage of the name's blossoming enters the chanter and reveals his eternal spiritual form and identity.
- **ācārya** — A spiritual preceptor who teaches by example; here, the previous ācāryas in the disciplic line who handed down the practices.
- **sphurati** — 'It appears / manifests' — the technical term for the holy name spontaneously appearing from the spiritual realm rather than being produced by the tongue.
- **sevonmukha** — One who is turned toward the Lord in service, service-inclined; the disposition to which alone the holy name reveals himself.
- **sādhana-śarīra** — The 'body of practice' — the present body and mind with which a practitioner renders service to the holy name.
- **sādhana** — Regulated spiritual practice, such as the daily chanting of prescribed rounds.
- **māyā** — The Lord's illusory material energy that conditions the soul; the soul is released from it only by a superior taste.
- **japa** — Soft, individual chanting of the mantra, typically counted on beads (the 16 rounds).
- **kīrtana** — Congregational, sung chanting of the holy names; the most effective means of dissolving offenses and receiving collective mercy.
- **ājñā cakra** — In the yoga-śāstra, the energy center associated with directed mental focus, to which the speaker links the mind's active focusing function.
- **yoga-śāstra** — The body of scriptural literature on yoga.
- **mantra trāṭaka** — The practice of steady gazing at the written mantra while chanting, scanning each name in sequence as an optional aid to attention.
- **Pātya-pañcaka** — A five-fold prayer of total surrender from the Sanat-kumāra-saṁhitā, whose daily recitation is said to grant direct service to Rādhā and Krishna.
- **dhāma** — The Lord's holy abode, such as Govardhana, where mundane conversation is felt as antithetical to the sacred atmosphere.
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee of Krishna (Viṣṇu); offenses to Vaiṣṇavas are remedied by using the same mouth to praise and serve them.
- **Dīna-dayāl** — 'Merciful to the fallen,' a name/epithet of the Lord, here sung by a devotee on Govardhana.
- **Giriraj-Govardhan** — Govardhana Hill, a supremely sacred place in the holy land of Vraja.
- **sixty-four qualities** — Krishna's complete set of transcendental qualities, revealed as the 'fragrance' in an intermediate stage of the name's blossoming.
- **eight periods of the day (aṣṭa-kāla)** — The eightfold daily cycle of Krishna's eternal pastimes, revealed when the name comes to full bloom.
