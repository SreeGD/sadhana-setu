# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 03 Learning to Chant with Devotion 01 2024 11 21

## Theme

This lecture — the third session of HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja's Holy Name Retreat, titled 'Learning to Chant with Devotion' — frames japa not as a fixed practice but as a living relationship with Kṛṣṇa that matures through three stages: establishing, growing, and deepening one's bond with the Holy Name. Its governing thesis is that devotion (bhakti) is the very life force of the mind, just as prāṇa is the life force of the body; citing Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura (smaraṇa-mananera prāṇa), the speaker teaches that remembrance and mindfulness of Kṛṣṇa keep the practitioner spiritually 'alive' and protected. Where this vital force is absent, the mind becomes 'dry' and the inner enemies — anger, confusion, resurging material desires, and envy — rush in to feast, just as vultures and hyenas devour a body emptied of life. Mechanical, round-counting chanting, though better than nothing and not to be condemned, cannot by itself awaken this life force; it yields only a child's pleasure at moving beads and may breed the illusion of being a great chanter. Drawing on Rūpa Gosvāmī's prayers, the Mahārāja establishes the central image for every chanter: Kṛṣṇa and His name are identical, so the Holy Name is a worshipable divine person — with lotus feet, adored by liberated souls and the Upaniṣads — who removes suffering even when uttered once, inattentively, or disrespectfully, and who bestows the dawning sight of pure devotion. Because the Name is a person, it must be approached with deliberate intent to develop a relationship; affection arises only through cultivation, and chanting while forgetting that Kṛṣṇa Himself is the Name leaves one unfortunate and unrevealed-to. Genuine relationship yields direct experience of Kṛṣṇa (pareśānubhava) and a taste so deep that lesser tastes fall away. The speaker grounds the path in the threefold Gauḍīya structure — sambandha, abhidheya, prayojana — insisting one begin at the beginning: not prema, which is very high, but bhakti rooted in sambandha, the conscious bond of being an eternal soul and Kṛṣṇa's eternal servant. The qualification for this is genuine humility — chanting lower than the straw, understanding (per Prabhupāda) that nothing and no one will save you, divesting oneself of all man-made management and taking refuge in the Name (Rūpa Gosvāmī's nāmāśraya) in a mood of helpless dependence, crying 'Kṛṣṇa, accept me, save me, protect me.' He weaves in supporting teachings: even Īśvara Purī lamented that the Name did not melt his heart while a mango tree melted in prema; Jagadānanda Paṇḍita's Prema-vivarta describes the wandering jīva awakened by contact with a saint; māyā, 'Kṛṣṇa's dog,' withdraws only when Kṛṣṇa, moved by our humble appeal, supplies the strength of His internal potency. He maps Kṛṣṇa's five ascending mercies — creating the world, sending awakening frustration, turning souls to śāstra, providing ācāryas, and descending as avatar — culminating in the Nāma-avatar, Nāma Prabhu, by whom Kṛṣṇa is directly accessible in this age. Before each mantra, the speaker says, two doors stand open — mindless chanting or chanting in relationship — and one must consciously choose the second by meditating on one's eternal identity. The session then turns practical, unfolding Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's six areas of conscious effort in japa (a lineage descending through his Bhajan Rahasya, Hari-Bhakti-Vilāsa, and mantra-śāstra), beginning with the first: mana-saṃharaṇa, the gathering of the scattered, many-branched (bahu-śākhā) mind into one place. He distinguishes nāma-smaraṇa (mindful recollection of the syllables) from līlā-smaraṇa, and explains that inattentive chanting is not merely a mental lapse but a mechanical disturbance of the prāṇa in the heart, as thoughts become churning vṛttis. Two remedies follow: the familiar effort of repeatedly bringing the mind back, and a mechanical tool for a mechanical problem — Sanātana Gosvāmī's prāṇāyāma (pūraka, kumbhaka, rechaka, measured in mātrās, here a sharp 6:12:3 ratio with forceful complete exhalation) to calm an agitated life-air fast — though prāṇāyāma is only an aid, never a requirement; the essential point is concentration. The central exhortation is that it is time to stop mechanical chanting: many devotees no longer keep even their vow of sixteen rounds because the taste is gone. The remedy is to re-establish sambandha, begin with affectionate respect (ādara/ādarāt), and lead the mind gently — never by force, for the mind pushed too hard rebels; as the Gītā teaches, it is subdued by detachment (vairāgya) and steady practice (abhyāsa). One must learn one's own mind, create interest in the Name (through a verse, a fresh insight, or gazing on Śrīla Prabhupāda), and guide it as one guides an understood child. Throughout, the speaker holds open the mystery of why such rare fortune — meeting a pure devotee, like the blind tortoise finding the floating plank — falls on one soul, keeping the devotee in humble, grateful wonder.

## Practical application

A devotee turns these teachings into daily japa by treating each session as the cultivation of a relationship rather than the completion of a quota. Before chanting, first re-establish sambandha: deliberately remember 'I am an eternal soul, a tiny particle of pure consciousness and Kṛṣṇa's eternal servant; Kṛṣṇa is my only Lord and master; and this world exists not to entangle me but to give me the chance to re-establish my forgotten relationship with Him.' Rūpa Gosvāmī's three-step meditation can be used here. Approach the chanting from a position of humility and helpless dependence — understanding that no money, intelligence, possession, or weapon will save you — and offer an opening prayer in the mood of one long separated now turning back: 'For many years I have been removed from You; today I turn to You — please accept me, save me, protect me.' At the start of japa, perform mana-saṃharaṇa: consciously gather the scattered, many-branched mind and rest it in one place. If the mind is too agitated to settle, an optional aid is a few rounds of forceful prāṇāyāma (full inhalation, retention, and complete forceful exhalation, e.g. a 6:12:3 count) to steady the disturbed prāṇa before chanting — but this is only a tool, not a rule; one may equally just breathe fully and simply, or simply keep drawing the wandering mind back again and again. During chanting, practice nāma-smaraṇa by bringing the chant into the mind and contemplating the very syllables of the Name, remembering that Kṛṣṇa is personally present as His Name. Before each mantra, consciously choose the 'door' of relationship over the door of mindless rote. Chant with ādara — affectionate respect for the Name as a worshipable person — following Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's practical points: focus the mind in one place, keep consciousness pure, avoid useless talk, meditate on the meaning of the names, and take shelter of patience. Lead the mind gently rather than forcing it, since force breeds rebellion; subdue it instead through detachment and steady, regular practice. Learn your own mind's temperament and deliberately create interest in the Name — reflect on a verse, dwell on a fresh insight, or gaze on a picture of Śrīla Prabhupāda to bring the chanting alive. Above all, chant as a heartfelt calling-out — not to finish sixteen rounds or sit out a fixed kīrtana, but to cry sincerely for Kṛṣṇa's acceptance — trusting that when one appeals earnestly from genuine helplessness, Kṛṣṇa Himself calls back māyā and supplies the strength, and the taste returns so that time passes unnoticed and one cannot get enough.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name of the Lord (Hari/Kṛṣṇa); the chanting of the Name as spiritual practice.
- **japa** — Personal, soft repetition of the Holy Name, typically counted on beads.
- **bhakti** — Devotion, devotional service to Kṛṣṇa; described here as the life force of the mind and the starting point of the path.
- **prema** — Pure, mature love of God — the highest goal, distinguished from bhakti where one begins.
- **prāṇa** — The life force or vital air that regulates the body's functions; used as an analogy for devotion as the mind's vital force.
- **smaraṇa-mananera prāṇa** — Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura's phrase: remembrance and mindfulness (of Kṛṣṇa) are the very life-breath (prāṇa) of devotion.
- **pareśānubhava** — Direct experience or realization of the Supreme Lord, arising from genuine relationship in chanting.
- **sambandha** — Relationship — knowing one's eternal bond with Kṛṣṇa as soul and servant; the first of the three divisions of Gauḍīya philosophy and the necessary foundation.
- **abhidheya** — The process or means of acting on that relationship; the second division of Gauḍīya philosophy.
- **prayojana** — The ultimate goal (love of God); the third division of Gauḍīya philosophy.
- **nāmāśraya** — Taking shelter of the Holy Name, as termed by Rūpa Gosvāmī; chanting in a mood of helpless dependence on Kṛṣṇa.
- **ādara / ādarāt** — Affectionate respect; reverent regard for the Name as a person, which the speaker identifies as the beginning of relationship.
- **māyā** — Kṛṣṇa's external, illusory energy that binds the soul; likened to Kṛṣṇa's dog, withdrawing only at His call.
- **jīva** — The individual living entity or soul, eternally a servant of Kṛṣṇa, wandering in the material world.
- **nāma-smaraṇa** — Mindful chanting — bringing the chant into the mind and contemplating the syllables of the Name; distinct from līlā-smaraṇa.
- **līlā-smaraṇa** — Remembrance of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, which has its own place but is not what nāma-smaraṇa refers to.
- **mana-saṃharaṇa / manaḥ-saṃharaṇa** — Gathering or collecting the scattered mind into one place; the first of the six practices for chanting with devotion.
- **bahu-śākhā** — 'Many-branched' — the ordinary mind scattered across countless objects, making single-pointed focus difficult.
- **vṛtti** — A mental wave or modification; thoughts that churn the heart and disturb the prāṇa seated there.
- **prāṇāyāma** — Regulation of the life-air through controlled breathing; prescribed by Sanātana Gosvāmī as an aid to calm an agitated mind before chanting.
- **pūraka** — Inhalation phase of prāṇāyāma.
- **kumbhaka** — Retention (holding) of the breath in prāṇāyāma.
- **rechaka** — Exhalation phase of prāṇāyāma; here done forcefully and completely to clear stale air and the heavy mind.
- **mātrā** — A time-unit used to measure the phases of prāṇāyāma (roughly 32 mātrās ≈ 6 seconds).
- **vairāgya** — Detachment; with steady practice, the means by which the Gītā says the restless mind is subdued.
- **abhyāsa** — Steady, regular practice; paired with vairāgya as the means of controlling the mind.
- **Nāma-avatar / Nāma Prabhu** — Kṛṣṇa's descent as His own Holy Name — the form of mercy by which He is directly accessible in this age.
- **avatar** — A descent of the Lord into the world; the fifth and highest of Kṛṣṇa's five ascending mercies.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture, toward which Kṛṣṇa turns awakening souls.
- **ācārya** — A spiritual teacher who lives and teaches the scripture by example; provided by Kṛṣṇa as one of His mercies.
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa.
- **kṛpā** — Mercy (of Kṛṣṇa); one offered explanation for the rare fortune of meeting a pure devotee.
- **ajñāta-sukṛti** — Unintentionally or unknowingly performed devotional credit; another offered explanation for that rare fortune.
- **saṃsāra** — The cycle of repeated birth and death in the material world.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational singing/chanting of the Holy Names.
