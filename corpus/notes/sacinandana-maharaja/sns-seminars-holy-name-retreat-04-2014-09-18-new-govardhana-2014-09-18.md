# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 04 2014 09 18 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

This Holy-Name retreat lecture by HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja frames japa as a practical skill that, like swimming or surfing, is mastered only by doing it — yet the doing must be attentive rather than mechanical. The arc moves from method to disposition to fruit: first, attention is anchored by understanding the holy name as Nāma-Brahma, the worshipable form of the Lord himself present in the very letters of the Mahā-mantra. Second, when the mind is focused and one chants sincerely, from the heart, the holy name reveals himself — revelation is the reward of attentive, heartfelt chanting, not of repetition alone. Third, this is governed by a spiritual law: the Lord reveals himself in exact proportion to one's surrender, so surrender becomes the true measure of how much of the name is disclosed. The lecture closes by making this devotional disposition concrete, recommending the old system of Nāma-Brahma mantra cards as a deliberate way to fold worship and surrender into one's chanting — offered freely as a copyable gift to help devotees deepen their japa.

## Practical application

Treat japa as practice that improves only through doing it with attention — sitting down to chant is itself the lesson, but a distracted round teaches little. Before or during chanting, hold the awareness that the name is Nāma-Brahma: the Lord is personally present in the syllables of the Mahā-mantra, so chanting is a meeting, not a recitation. Chant 'from the heart' — meaning it sincerely — and let the desire for the name's revelation rest on surrender rather than on counting rounds, since the name discloses himself in the measure that one gives oneself to him. To make worship and surrender deliberate rather than accidental, use Nāma-Brahma mantra cards as a structured aid, weaving an attitude of worship into each round so that attentive, surrendered chanting becomes a daily habit.

## Glossary

- **Nāma-Brahma** — The holy name understood as the worshipable form of the Lord himself, fully present in the letters of the Mahā-mantra — not a mere sound or symbol; also the name of the mantra-card system recommended as a chanting aid.
- **Mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' (Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare) chanted in Hari-Nāma practice; the Lord is held to be present within its very letters.
- **japa** — The personal, often repeated practice of softly chanting the holy name, typically counted on beads — the discipline the lecture aims to deepen through attention and surrender.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari); the chanting practice centered on the name.
- **surrender** — The devotee's self-giving to the Lord; in the lecture's spiritual law, the measure by which the holy name reveals himself — the more one surrenders, the more the name is disclosed.
- **mantra cards** — The traditional Nāma-Brahma cards, a practical aid for deliberately adding worship and surrender to chanting, offered as a freely copyable gift.
