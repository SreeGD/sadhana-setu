# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 04 Learning to Chant with Devotion 02 2024 11 22

## Theme

This is the second session of HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja's Holy-Name retreat on learning to chant with devotion, and it builds cumulatively on the first. The Mahārāja opens not with technique but with posture: participants are asked to settle, become receptive, and 'tune in,' then to approach the Name through the paramparā in a mood of reverence — offering obeisances to Śrīla Prabhupāda and the disciplic succession, and reciting the scriptural glories of the Name (harer nāma... and the Namāṣṭakam) so that the established glory of the Name prepares the heart. From Rūpa Gosvāmī's Namāṣṭakam he draws two threads that frame the whole talk: that the holy name's manifestation (sphuraṇa) destroys even prārabdha-karma, and that taste (rati/ruci) — for which Rūpa Gosvāmī prays to increase unlimitedly — is what actually sustains a devotee. This leads to the central, honest question of the retreat: why, after ten or twenty years of chanting, have the promised fruits not yet appeared? The Mahārāja's tentative answer is the heart of the lecture — the 'road of tears.' The almighty Lord will not force His way into a life that does not desperately want Him; the only price for Kṛṣṇa is laulyam, intense crying eagerness, which no amount of external sādhana or heroic discipline can substitute for. He distinguishes three kinds of tears — of the body, of the mind, and the sweet tears of the soul — the last arising when the soul awakens to its one real necessity, the eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa. The obstacle to these tears is not exhaustion but the hardened ego and clever mind; burning up one's cleverness lets a hidden ocean of longing pour out. He frames the 'magic of bhakti' as a polarity of humility (smallness) and intense desire for Kṛṣṇa, like plus and minus poles transferring the Lord's energy, and recalls Prabhupāda's revealed 'secret' that Caitanya Mahāprabhu, though a sannyāsī with no material need, still cries — for Govinda. Turning to the later verses of the Namāṣṭakam, the Mahārāja shows Mahāprabhu addressing Kṛṣṇa directly as the fallen eternal servant begging causeless mercy, expanded by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura into a song of utter helplessness; illustrated by Radhanath Swami's near-drowning in the Puri sea-current, he counsels chanting in the 'drowning man mood' of exclusive dependence, the mood Kṛṣṇa cannot resist. Bhakti itself is then defined concretely as 'crying while remembering Kṛṣṇa's name' — genuine tears, never artificial 'crocodile tears,' a capacity present in everyone. The talk then becomes practical, walking through qualities of proper japa drawn from the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa: mana-saṁharaṇam (repeatedly drawing back the wandering mind, required of every chanter), śaucam (cleanliness of body and space, purity of heart, guarding what enters the mind), maunam (no idle talk that renders japa fruitless), and mantrārtha-cintana (contemplating the meaning of the mantra). He unfolds the meaning of the names — Hari as the Lord who removes ignorance and inauspiciousness by revealing His own form, Hare/Harā as Rādhā who steals Kṛṣṇa's mind — and presents the mahā-mantra as a prayer with a 'heartbeat': the eternal servant-master appeal, 'O Rādhā, O Kṛṣṇa, please engage me in Your service; accept me.' Throughout he insists that praying for taste is not selfish but necessary, that devotees today need direct experience rather than more philosophy (citing high attrition among those who lack sustaining taste), and that humility must be lived on three levels — mind, body, and heart — before its words carry any brilliance. He closes on the mood of loving separation that nourishes chanting, with the reassurance that even in felt absence Kṛṣṇa is 'just around the corner,' the longing itself being proof that He loves us and is drawing near.

## Practical application

For daily japa and Hari-Nāma, the devotee first changes inner posture before quantity: settle, quiet the mind, and 'tune in' rather than begin mechanically, approaching the Name reverently as something received through the paramparā and served, not casually taken up. The core practice is to build one's own 'road of tears' — consciously connecting, while chanting, with the part of oneself that genuinely longs for Kṛṣṇa as its one real necessity, and making the saints' prayers one's own ('maintain me by giving me taste,' 'be merciful and consider me a particle of dust at Your feet'). Praying directly for taste is not a selfish, self-enjoying motive but a necessity, as long as it stays within a mood of service rather than demand. The recommended mood is the 'drowning man mood': chanting from the realized conviction that no one and nothing can deliver me except Kṛṣṇa — this exclusive, helpless dependence is what makes the Name effective and attracts His response; the tears, however, must be genuine, never artificially worked-up 'crocodile tears.' Concretely, the speaker offers qualities from the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa to perfect japa: (1) mana-saṁharaṇam — patiently bring the wandering mind back every time it strays, a discipline for every chanter, not only trained yogīs (prāṇāyāma is an optional aid, never an obligation); (2) śaucam — keep the body and the chanting space clean and uncluttered, since a tamasic/rajasic environment makes chanting impossible, and guard the mind's intake, because scandalous or critical input chokes off taste like garbage in a garden, so cultivate sāttvic thoughts; (3) maunam — do not break a round with idle talk (which renders japa fruitless and slights Kṛṣṇa like taking a phone call mid-conversation); signal an approaching friend and finish your round, while allowing genuinely necessary interruptions; (4) mantrārtha-cintana — fix the mind on the meaning of the names so the Deity stays uninterruptedly present, hearing the mahā-mantra as a prayer with a 'heartbeat': 'O Rādhā, O Kṛṣṇa, please engage me in Your service; please accept me.' Humility is to be practiced, not merely claimed, on three levels — understanding one's real situation (mind), bowing and doing menial service (body), and genuine regret (heart). Finally, the devotee cultivates the ache of separation as itself a form of love, remembering that direct experience — like solitary, absorbed chanting at a holy place — sustains faith through difficulty, and that the felt longing is evidence Kṛṣṇa is near and drawing close.

## Glossary

- **paramparā** — The disciplic succession through which the holy name is received and served; one approaches the Name through it in a mood of reverence rather than taking it up casually on one's own.
- **Namāṣṭakam** — Rūpa Gosvāmī's eight-verse prayer glorifying the holy name, recited to establish the Name's glory before the teaching on chanting with devotion; its verses (including the prayer for taste and the address to Kṛṣṇa as fallen servant) structure much of the lecture.
- **prārabdha-karma** — The karma already manifesting in the present life (e.g., physical illness, limited intelligence), which the Vedas say must be experienced and cannot otherwise be removed — yet is destroyed simply by the manifestation of the holy name.
- **sphuraṇa** — The manifestation or shining-forth of the holy name, by which even prārabdha-karma is destroyed.
- **aparādha** — Offense; for a Vaiṣṇava who already follows the rules, the relevant 'sin' to be crushed (by the name Agha-damana) is offense to the Name and the Lord.
- **mādhurya** — Sweetness; the sweet quality of the Lord's names that the gopīs meditate upon, such as Kamala-nayana and Gopī-candra.
- **rati / ruci** — Taste or relish for the holy name and devotional service; per Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura, taste is what maintains a devotee on the path, making everything easy.
- **laulyam** — Intense eagerness or crying for Kṛṣṇa — the only 'price' by which He is obtained; it cannot be purchased even by millions of pious births.
- **sādhana** — Disciplined devotional practice (chanting, vows, pilgrimage, austerity); the devotee's effort to come before the Lord, which without longing can leave the heart empty.
- **Paramātmā** — The Supersoul, the Lord present within the heart; in the Draupadī episode Kṛṣṇa would not merely reveal Himself as Paramātmā but waited for her helpless exclusive appeal.
- **bhakti** — Devotion; defined concretely in the lecture as crying while remembering Kṛṣṇa's holy name, and glorified as the greatest treasure. It descends as a 'divine investment' through Kṛṣṇa's mercy, the only means of grasping Him.
- **Hari-bhakti-vilāsa** — The Vaiṣṇava scripture on devotional conduct cited for the qualities that perfect one's japa (1.729) and for the rule against idle talk during chanting (17.132).
- **mana-saṁharaṇam / manaḥsaṃhāraṇa** — Concentrating the mind by repeatedly drawing it back whenever it wanders; the first quality of proper japa, required of every chanter.
- **prāṇāyāma** — Yogic breath-control; offered only as an optional aid to concentration since it appears in scripture, never an obligation for chanting.
- **śaucam** — Cleanliness (of the body and chanting space) and purity (of the heart and mind); both support chanting, while a cluttered, tamasic/rajasic environment makes it impossible.
- **sāttvic / rajasic / tamasic** — The three modes of nature (goodness, passion, ignorance); one cultivates sāttvic thoughts and a clean environment, since rajasic/tamasic surroundings and impure mental input choke off taste for spiritual topics.
- **maunam** — Silence during japa — not breaking the chanting with unnecessary talk, which renders it fruitless and slights Kṛṣṇa; necessary interruptions are excepted.
- **mantrārtha-cintana** — Contemplating the meaning of the mantra; the fourth quality of proper chanting (cited from Jīva Gosvāmī), by which the invoked Deity remains uninterruptedly present in the mind.
- **Hari / Hare / Harā** — 'Hari' — the Lord who takes away ignorance, sins, and inauspiciousness by revealing His own spiritual form; 'Hare' (vocative) is also Rādhā as Harā, who steals Kṛṣṇa's mind and is the incarnation of His joy (per Gopāla Guru Gosvāmī).
- **bhavāmbudhi** — The ocean of repeated birth and death (material existence); Mahāprabhu teaches the devotee to pray as the Lord's eternal servant to be lifted out of it.
- **Bhagavān** — Usually 'full of all opulences'; in the context of the Lord's inability to bear His devotees' tears of separation, best read as 'most loving' — the opulence of love.
- **sannyāsī** — A renunciant with no material necessity; Prabhupāda's 'secret' notes that Caitanya Mahāprabhu, though a sannyāsī, still cries — because He cries for Govinda, the one real necessity.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present age, in which (per the chanted verse 'harer nāma...') there is no other way for self-realization than the chanting of the holy name.
