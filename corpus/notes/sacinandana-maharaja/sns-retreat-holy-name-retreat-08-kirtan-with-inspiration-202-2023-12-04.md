# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 08 Kirtan with Inspiration 2023 12 04

## Theme

Speaking at a Holy-Name retreat whose stage is decorated with the first verse of the Śikṣāṣṭaka, Sacīnandana Mahārāja unfolds a single sustained image: the chanting of the holy name as the full moon and the various mantras as its cooling moonbeams, which descend into the lotus of the chanter's heart. Just as the night-blooming lotus opens only under the soft touch of moonlight, the heart opens not by force but by receptivity to the name. He grounds this in the Śikṣāṣṭaka's promise that saṅkīrtana cleanses the mirror of the heart, extinguishes the forest fire of material existence, and spreads the moonbeams of devotion upon the white lotus of good fortune. He illustrates the stakes of this opening with Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, who at the end of his life asked for 'Śrī Rūpa Mañjarī Pade' — a bhajan in which the rays of Śrī Rūpa Mañjarī's lotus feet themselves open the heart — showing that the same softening of the heart is the very goal at the moment of departure. The arc then turns practical: chant feelingly from the heart and do not block the name's work of softening it, avoiding the two failure modes of an over-active restless mind and a dull, switched-off mood. He offers two supporting meditations — visualizing the mahā-mantra descending as moonbeams from the moon into the heart, and moving through the Pañca-tattva one member at a time to beg each for empowerment, letting the mind work like a bumblebee gathering blessings. The lecture closes by returning to its heart: in kīrtan the holy name descends of its own accord; the chanter's only work is to keep the heart open, brought forward, and receptive to what is freely being given.

## Practical application

In daily japa and Hari-Nāma, the devotee's task is reframed from performing to receiving. Chant from the heart and let the holy name do its own work of softening a hardened heart rather than blocking that benefit. Guard against the two ways the heart hides during chanting: becoming overly active and busy in the mind, or sinking into a dull, dampened, switched-off mood — chant feelingly between these extremes. To stay receptive, employ the moonbeam meditation: see the moon, see the mahā-mantra descending from it, and let those cooling beams come down into the heart so it is nourished. Alternately, meditate through the Pañca-tattva one member at a time — bringing the mind to Śrī Caitanya's lotus feet at the center, then Nityānanda, Advaita, Gadādhara, and Śrīvāsa — and beg each to empower the chanting, letting the mind move like a bumblebee gathering blessings rather than repeating mechanically. Above all, especially in kīrtan, do not close or hide the heart; bring it forward and keep it open to receive the holy name that descends of its own accord.

## Glossary

- **Śikṣāṣṭaka** — The eight verses composed by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu; its first verse — describing saṅkīrtana cleansing the mirror of the heart, extinguishing the forest fire of material existence, and spreading moonbeams of devotion upon the white lotus of good fortune — frames the whole lecture.
- **saṅkīrtana** — Congregational chanting of Kṛṣṇa's holy names, glorified in the first verse of the Śikṣāṣṭaka as the supreme cleansing and liberating practice.
- **mahā-mantra** — The great chant of the holy names (Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare), here visualized as descending from the moon as cooling beams into the heart.
- **bhajan** — A devotional song; here 'Śrī Rūpa Mañjarī Pade', requested by Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī at the end of his life.
- **Śrī Rūpa Mañjarī Pade** — A bhajan by Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura in which the rays from Śrī Rūpa Mañjarī's lotus feet open the devotee's heart; cited as the heart-opening goal at the moment of departure.
- **Pañca-tattva** — The five features of the Lord in His Caitanya pastimes — Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, Nityānanda Prabhu, Advaitācārya, Gadādhara Paṇḍita, and Śrīvāsa Ṭhākura — meditated upon one by one and begged for empowerment in chanting.
- **Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura** — A great Vaiṣṇava ācārya who, at the very end of his life, asked a devotee to chant 'Śrī Rūpa Mañjarī Pade', illustrating the heart's opening as the final goal.
- **Nityānanda Prabhu** — Member of the Pañca-tattva, meditated upon to Śrī Caitanya's right (the chanter's left), in bluish garments.
- **Advaitācārya** — Member of the Pañca-tattva included in the one-by-one meditation upon the divine assembly.
- **Gadādhara Paṇḍita** — Member of the Pañca-tattva included in the one-by-one meditation.
- **Śrīvāsa Ṭhākura** — Member of the Pañca-tattva, described as the leader of the devotees.
- **kīrtan** — Melodic chanting of the holy name during which, the teacher says, the name descends of its own accord like moonbeams, the chanter's part being only to stay open and receptive.
- **Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa** — The Divine Couple upon whose lotus feet the chanter meditates so that the moonbeams of good fortune come to bloom the heart.
