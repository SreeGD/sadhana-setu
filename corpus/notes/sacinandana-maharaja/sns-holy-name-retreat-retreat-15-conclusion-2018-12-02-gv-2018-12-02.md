# SNS Holy Name Retreat Retreat 15 Conclusion 2018 12 02 GV

## Theme

In this concluding session of the Holy-Name Retreat, Sacīnandana Mahārāja reflects on what has actually been happening as the participants listened. He frames hearing itself as the decisive spiritual act: when a listener opens the heart, the speaker's own attraction and love for Rādhā-Krishna, for the Dhāma, and for Giriraja are transmitted as living 'sparks' to the hearer — spiritual emotion is contagious through receptive, attentive hearing. He grounds this in the Nectar of Devotion, which teaches that we do not manufacture devotion on our own; we receive the very seeds of attraction for Krishna from those who already possess that attraction, passed heart to heart. The arc then turns from receiving to keeping: the seeds sown during the retreat carry real potential to grow, but only if they are cultivated. Once the program ends, the devotee must remember and 'water' them so the impressions stay alive, continue to guide, and keep supplying ever-fresh inspiration rather than fading away. The conclusion is thus a charge — the retreat is not an endpoint but a planting whose fruit depends on the care that follows.

## Practical application

A devotee carries the retreat into daily japa by treating it as cultivation rather than a finished event. First, approach hearing and chanting with an open, attentive heart, so that the attraction and love carried by realized devotees can be received as sparks rather than merely as information. Second, after the program ends, deliberately 'water' the seeds that were planted: return in memory to the moods, verses, and emotions that touched the heart during the retreat, and let them accompany the daily rounds so each session of Hari-Nāma is fed by fresh inspiration instead of routine. Practically, this means guarding and revisiting the impressions received — remembering Rādhā-Krishna, the Dhāma, and Giriraja while chanting — so the attraction stays, deepens, and continues to guide one's practice over time rather than dimming once the retreat is over.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari); the practice of chanting Krishna's names, the central activity of the retreat.
- **japa** — The personal, often soft or whispered, repetition of the holy name on beads as one's daily chanting practice.
- **Rādhā-Krishna** — The divine couple — Krishna and His foremost devotee Rādhā — who are the object of the attraction and love the speaker seeks to transmit.
- **Dhāma** — The sacred abode or holy land of Krishna (e.g., Vrindavan); a focus of the devotee's love and remembrance.
- **Giriraja** — Govardhana Hill, worshiped as non-different from Krishna; a particular object of devotional attraction named in the lecture.
- **Nectar of Devotion** — Srila Prabhupāda's summary study of Rūpa Gosvāmī's Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, cited for the teaching that the seeds of attraction for Krishna are received from those who already possess them.
