# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 07 2014 09 19 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

Framed by the verse that the heart unmelted by chanting—'steel-framed,' producing no tears or standing of hairs—is the diagnostic of failed chanting, this Holy-Name retreat lecture by HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja maps the whole journey from 'shadow chanting' into the light of pure devotion. The entire process of spiritual culture aims at one thing: changing the heart of the living being so it is restored to its eternal constitutional position as a subordinate, loving servant of Krishna. This change is concretely testable—gradual detachment from sense enjoyment and the urge to lord over the world, paired with an ever-increasing attitude of loving service. Mahārāja identifies the second stage of chanting, nāma-ābhāsa: a shadow or reflection connected to the real name yet lacking its substance, like the shadow of an apple that has the form but neither the color, nourishment, nor taste of the fruit. Such chanting is not useless—gross sinful reactions begin to wane—but it lacks the living 'caitanya-rasa,' and must not be mistaken for the real thing. The neophyte is covered by two things: the clouds of anarthas (hankering for illusory things, weakness of heart, and offenses, chief among them the ten offenses to the holy name) and the fog of bodily identification. Drawing a psychological parallel, Mahārāja describes the 'shadow' as the repressed, hidden self kept in the cellar while a clean external image (dhoti, tilak, smile, good terminology) is displayed; a private face is natural, but the danger is the 'shadow breakthrough' into scandal, and—unlike an Indian shopkeeper hiding poor goods—spiritual life cannot be faked, because Krishna sees the heart and these hidden anarthas are precisely what block advancement. The remedy, from Harināma Cintāmaṇi, is that by providence one meets a bona fide spiritual master who gives sambandha (knowledge of one's eternal relationship with Krishna) and then abhidheya (the devotional service of chanting), planting the seed of bhakti as one's real treasure. Using Haridāsa Ṭhākura's image, the holy name is the sun and material attachments are clouds and fog; when sincere practice blows them away, the sun shines and bestows prema, the goal (prayojana) of chanting. The distinctive treasure of the Gauḍīya school is sambandha—knowing one is the eternal servant of Krishna, not Krishna Himself—and this relational awareness is what gives kīrtana its rasa (the 'sugar' aphorism: one cannot taste the sugar while imagining oneself to be the sugar). Mahārāja closes by warning against mechanical, automatic-pilot chanting and by locating the work in two responsibilities: the individual must become the gardener of his own heart, pulling the weeds that grow beside the bhakti-latā—Krishna consciousness is no 'washing machine'—and must avail himself of devotee association, which acts as a mirror revealing hidden faults and, more importantly, as the source of tasty Kṛṣṇa-kathā ('rasāyana kathā'). When the heart is cleansed, the Lord takes His seat on its throne, and the holy farm becomes a second Vṛndāvana or Māyāpur.

## Practical application

The lecture turns on a single, repeatable practice: chant 'with a sense of relationship.' Before beginning japa, fill the mind with sambandha-jñāna received from Prabhupāda—'I am not this body; I am an eternal servant of Krishna, kṛṣṇa-dāsa not māyā-dāsa'—and chant from that settled understanding for Krishna's pleasure rather than to serve your own senses. Precede chanting with a prayer of relationship in the mood of the Śikṣāṣṭaka: 'I am fallen into this material ocean; kindly accept me again as a particle of dust at Your lotus feet,' remembering that, like pollen-dust naturally belonging to the lotus, the soul's constitutional function is to make the sound 'Hare Krishna' for Krishna (Bhaktivinoda's violin is made for melody, not to hammer nails). Guard actively against 'automatic-pilot' chanting: when the mind switches off because 'I already know it,' tell the mind there is more in the name and the Śikṣāṣṭaka than it perceives, and re-enter the relationship—this small addition of sambandha 'changes everything, like bringing light into a dark room.' Examine your own heart honestly for the two-fold movement—decreasing attachment to sense enjoyment and lording it over, increasing eagerness to serve—and use this as your litmus test rather than measuring external paraphernalia. Stage the work: in the beginning carefully avoid the gross offenses to the holy name; then labor to clear the clouds of anartha (attachment to objects unrelated to Krishna, the propensity to cheat and pose—the 'super japa man' who chants intensely only while watched, envy of others' service, and the 'see me, see me' hunger for recognition) and the fog of bodily identification. Do the gardening yourself—pull these weeds growing beside the creeper of devotion—rather than expecting Krishna consciousness to cleanse you automatically. Descend into the 'cellar' instead of polishing the public image, and take full advantage of the association of advanced devotees in two ways: let them be a mirror that exposes your hidden weaknesses, and seek out tasty Kṛṣṇa-kathā and kīrtana that nourish and attract the soul. Resolve inwardly to give your best even if presently fallen—stay in the kīrtana and read Prabhupāda's books while truly pondering them, letting the teaching enter and remain rather than passing through one ear and out the other. The aim is not that everyone must weep in ecstasy today, but to step out of the shadow into the light, trusting that as the coverings are blown away the sun of the holy name will shine and awaken prema.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Soft, individual repetition of the holy name on beads; the central practice the retreat seeks to deepen from shadow into substance.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of Hari (Krishna); chanting it is the basic principle of devotional practice.
- **nāma-ābhāsa** — The second stage of chanting—a 'shadow' or reflection of the name that is connected to the real name but lacks its taste and nourishment; gross sinful reactions wane, but no real ecstasy yet appears.
- **ābhāsa** — Shadow or reflection; something with the form of the real thing but neither its color, nourishment, nor taste—like the shadow of an apple.
- **rasa** — Transcendental taste or flavor; the living relish of the holy name that shadow chanting lacks.
- **caitanya-rasa** — The living taste of the holy name 'full of spiritual life,' absent in nāma-ābhāsa chanting.
- **bhāva** — The preliminary ecstatic emotion just before prema, marked by signs such as tears in the eyes and standing of the hairs.
- **prema** — Pure, perfected love of God; the goal (prayojana) of chanting, awakened when the holy name reveals its treasure of spiritual love.
- **anartha** — An unnatural, unwanted thing on the spiritual path—hankering for illusory things, weakness of heart, and offenses—that acts like a cloud covering the pure holy name.
- **vidhi** — Regulated devotional engagement of the bodily limbs (eyes, ears, nose, hands, legs) from which one progresses to engaging the mind.
- **sambandha** — Knowledge of one's eternal relationship with Krishna as His servant; the distinctive treasure of the Gauḍīya school and the source of kīrtana's rasa.
- **sambandha-jñāna** — The knowledge of relationship—'I am not this body, I am an eternal servant of Krishna'—with which one fills the mind before chanting.
- **abhidheya** — The prescribed means of devotional service—chanting the holy name—given after sambandha is established.
- **prayojana** — The ultimate goal of the practice, namely prema, pure love for Krishna.
- **kṛṣṇa-dāsa** — Servant of Krishna; the soul's true position, as opposed to māyā-dāsa, servant of illusion.
- **māyā-dāsa** — Servant of illusion (māyā); the false position from which one cannot chant in one's true identity.
- **kṛṣṇa-sevā** — Loving service to Krishna, toward which sambandha-jñāna orients the chanter.
- **kīrtana** — Devotional singing/chanting of the holy name; yields rasa only when performed with relational awareness of being Krishna's servant.
- **Kṛṣṇa-kathā** — Topics and discussions about Krishna; tasty and attractive hearing that nourishes the soul.
- **rasāyana kathā** — Bhāgavatam's term for topics 'full of taste' that inspire and nourish the soul—the chief fruit of devotee association.
- **bhakti-latā** — The creeper of devotion; the good plant in the garden of the heart that must be protected from the weeds of anartha.
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka** — Śrī Caitanya's eight verses; its mood—'kindly accept me again as a particle of dust at Your lotus feet'—is used as the prayer of relationship before chanting.
- **ācārya** — A realized, pure devotee teacher whose example and instruction one must follow; neophytes who ignore the ācāryas cannot taste the name.
- **Māyāvādī** — An impersonalist who identifies himself with the object of his worship and therefore cannot feel devotion or taste the ecstasy of chanting.
- **Gauḍīya** — The Krishna conscious school whose distinctive treasure is sambandha—knowing one is the eternal servant of Krishna, not Krishna Himself.
- **Harināma Cintāmaṇi** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's work (ch. 3 cited) prescribing the remedy for heart-weaknesses: meeting a bona fide guru who gives sambandha and abhidheya.
- **Caitanya-caritāmṛta** — The biography-theology of Śrī Caitanya (Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja) through which Mahārāja signals he will treat the process of dealing with anarthas.
- **Bhāgavatārka-marīci-mālā** — Source cited for the point that offenseless, duplicity-free chanting is still 'shadow chanting' if it lacks knowledge of one's relationship with the Lord.
- **Jaiva Dharma** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's work, source of the violin analogy—an instrument made to make melody, not to hammer nails, as the soul is made to sound 'Hare Krishna.'
- **Vṛndāvana / Māyāpur** — Holy abodes of Krishna and Caitanya; a Krishna conscious farm is described as a 'second' of these—the kingdom of God for one who clears his consciousness.
- **dhoti / tilak** — Traditional devotional cloth and clay markings; here named as elements of the clean external image displayed while the 'shadow' is hidden in the cellar.
