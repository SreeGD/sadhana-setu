# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 01 Welcome 2024 11 20

## Theme

Delivered as the welcome to the Govardhan Holy-Name Retreat (a 5–7 day gathering running some 23–24 years), this lecture frames the retreat as a categorically different undertaking from a class or seminar: a japa anuṣṭhāna in which devotees withdraw from all other activity to concentrate exclusively on the holy name through śravaṇam, kīrtanam, and smaraṇam, performed under the added shelter and potency of Giriraj Govardhan. Mahārāja explains that this exclusive, shared focus generates new energy and harmony, and that the simple practice of chanting and hearing at Giriraj's feet has proven life-changing — so participants should approach it expecting miracles. He grounds the retreat in the glories and mercy of Vrindavan (an expansion of Srimati Radharani, an ocean of mercy whose every object is a person of transcendental bliss), teaching that the proper way to enter is humble, exclusive shelter-taking (ananya-śaraṇa): admitting one is fallen, still controlled by the senses, and incapable of defeating māyā by one's own strength, yet clinging to the dham and the holy name as the only refuge — for it is acceptance by the holy place and name, not prior merit, that elevates even the most fallen. The goal of all this shelter-seeking is absorption: following Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu, to become internally immersed in love for Krishna and the sweetness of Radha's pastimes. Mahārāja then explains why a retreat matters: it moves one off the 'known ground' of habitual routine into a 'liminal space' (drawing on Richard Rohr) — 'Krishna's waiting room' — where the old world can fall away and a bigger one be revealed, and where solitude with Krishna (the 'Alone with Krishna' practice) becomes the site of new discovery. Against the pull of modern life and legitimate duties, he insists that reestablishing one's eternal relationship with Krishna is the most important thing precisely because it is the least visible — like oxygen, unseen yet sustaining, and known by the misery felt in its absence. Krishna is most accessible in His holy name; the depth of chanting is unlimited (the schoolchild and professor share one alphabet but work on different platforms), carrying ever-deeper rasa without fatigue. He closes with practical guidance — four guidelines (willingness/openness to new practices, minimizing distraction especially from phone/internet/news, dedication to the programs, and avoiding gossip to guard the atmosphere), the law that Krishna reciprocates in proportion to what one invests, the call to form a realistic personal resolve, and the reminder that reaching one's spiritual ideals is easy with help and impossible alone — so never forget Giriraj and Krishna, by whose mercy everything is possible.

## Practical application

Treat your own daily japa as a personal 'retreat' rather than a routine to defend as already-perfect. Begin by withdrawing: deliberately fast from the mobile phone, internet, and news for a set period so something new can come, and protect the atmosphere of your practice by serving, speaking only on spiritual subjects, and refusing gossip. Enter chanting in the mood of humble, exclusive shelter — not waiting until you feel qualified or pure, but turning to the holy name precisely from a position of weakness, admitting your faults yet clinging to it as your only refuge. Chant with full attention to each individual name: be wholly present with 'Hare,' then 'Krishna,' drawing in both conscious and subconscious attention. Cultivate willingness to try unfamiliar forms of sādhana (such as solitary 'Alone with Krishna' time) instead of dismissing them as 'not what I usually do.' Remember that what you put in determines what you get out — invest yourself fully, since Krishna reciprocates in proportion to your surrender and service. Form a concrete, realistic resolve (sankalpa): clarify what you want your chanting to bring you and commit to specific, feasible steps to deepen it — avoiding extreme vows that collapse after a day. And throughout, pray to your spiritual master for strength, stay aware that the name carries unlimited depth and ever-fresh rasa, and do not try to do it alone — with Krishna's mercy every obstacle can be crossed.

## Glossary

- **Giriraj Govardhan** — The sacred hill of Govardhan in the Vrindavan area, worshiped as a personality (Giriraj, 'king of mountains') and non-different from Krishna; taking shelter at his lotus feet is said to offer special inspiration and potency.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari/Krishna); the chanting of the name, which is the central practice of the retreat.
- **japa** — The soft, individual chanting of the holy name, typically on beads; here cultivated as attentive, name-by-name absorption.
- **japa anuṣṭhāna** — A dedicated observance or sustained, regulated period of japa in which one withdraws from all other activities to chant, forming new habits and capacities in chanting.
- **śravaṇam** — Hearing about Krishna — hearing the sacred texts and the holy name; one of the principal limbs of devotional service.
- **kīrtanam** — Chanting and glorifying Krishna and His name; a principal limb of devotional service.
- **smaraṇam** — Remembering or meditating on Krishna; the limb of devotional service concentrated, alongside hearing and chanting, at the retreat.
- **sādhana** — Regulated spiritual practice; the disciplines through which one cultivates devotion, openness to new forms of which is the first condition for a breakthrough.
- **ananya-śaraṇa** — Exclusive shelter — surrendering to Krishna and the holy dham with no other refuge, confessing one's offenses yet clinging to the Lord precisely because no alternative exists.
- **māyā** — The Lord's illusory energy, said to be insurmountable by anyone's own power — so that taking exclusive shelter of the Lord and His dham is the only escape.
- **dham** — The holy abode (e.g. Vrindavan); a transcendental place whose mercy, not one's prior merit, elevates the surrendered soul.
- **Vrindavan** — Krishna's transcendental abode, every object of which is a person of transcendental bliss; described as an expansion of Srimati Radharani and an ocean of mercy more soothing than the full moon.
- **ananda** — Transcendental bliss; the essential nature of Krishna and of every person and object in Vrindavan.
- **rasa** — Spiritual mellow or relish; the deepening, ever-sweeter flavor of relationship with Krishna that the holy name can progressively reveal.
- **Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu** — The 16th-century incarnation who exemplified deep internal absorption in and love for Krishna, attracting all who saw him; the model of the absorption the retreat aims at.
- **Srimati Radharani** — Krishna's foremost devotee and feminine counterpart; Vrindavan is described as her expansion, accounting for its special sweetness, and absorption in the sweetness of her pastimes is the prayed-for goal.
- **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī** — A Gaudiya Vaishnava teacher who illustrated the unlimited depth of chanting with the analogy that a schoolchild and a university professor use the same alphabet yet work on entirely different platforms.
- **Bhūrijana Prabhu** — The devotee whose inspiration shaped the retreat — to perform devotional activities at the shelter of Giriraj Govardhan.
- **Srila Prabhupada** — The founder-ācārya of ISKCON, cited as saying that even a non-devotee who comes to Vrindavan will experience separation from Krishna.
- **Raghunatha Dasa Goswami** — One of the Six Goswamis of Vrindavan, cited for the teaching that Vrindavan is an expansion of Srimati Radharani.
- **brahmacari** — A celibate student/monk in the Vaishnava community, whose legitimate duty of preaching can, like any duty, crowd out the invisible essential — the relationship with Krishna.
- **liminal space** — The threshold between the familiar old world (left behind) and a not-yet-grasped new one — described as 'Krishna's waiting room,' the sacred space of openness where transformation occurs (a concept drawn from Richard Rohr).
- **sankalpa** — A personal resolve or vow formed for the retreat — clarifying the gifts one seeks and committing to specific, realistic, feasible steps to deepen one's sadhana.
