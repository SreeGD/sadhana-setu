# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 05 64 Rounds Day Inspiration 2024 11 23

## Theme

Across twenty years of retreats, one question surfaces again and again — and it is a question of life, not merely of retreats: how can a devotee gain a genuine *experience* of Kṛṣṇa consciousness that sets all doubt aside and supplies the strength to continue? Sacīnandana Mahārāja frames this as the difference between belief and realization; where the experience is missing, spiritual life turns half-hearted and disappointed. He shows that this longing is ancient, voicing it through Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's song: the soul has yearned since time immemorial to take complete shelter at the lotus feet of Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa, yet 'this wretched servant is simply waiting,' not yet arrived. The prayer carries urgency, because life's restless current rushes toward the ocean of time and death and the days that pass never return. Crucially, the lotus feet cannot be reached by one's own strength — admitting he is unfortunate, foolish, and ignorant of even a sesame seed of bhakti, the devotee throws himself entirely on grace and surrenders: 'take my soul, make me your own, vanquish my sufferings.' From here the lecture names the inner dynamic of effective chanting — the 'magic of bhakti' that arises when one honestly confronts one's fallen condition with real (not lip-service) humility while *at the same time* intensely desiring Kṛṣṇa. The holy name works precisely between these two opposite poles: deep humility on one side, bold living desire to serve in Vṛndāvana on the other, never slackening into dull, mechanical japa. One approaches Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa like a beggar seeking alms, asking only for sevāmṛta, the nectar of direct personal service. Mahārāja then makes the path concrete with three simple practices — intention, sincerity, attentiveness — and with the discipline of attentive hearing as a form of seeing ('see with your ears'), using Rūpa Gosvāmī's word-painting of Kṛṣṇa to let the Lord's transcendental form arise in the heart. The lasting aim is that this picture remain always in the heart and appear spontaneously the moment one chants Kṛṣṇa's name.

## Practical application

Treat each round not as routine but as a rare opportunity, and enter it through three steps. First, set an intention: resolve before chanting to give your honest best. Second, draw on sincerity — connect with the part of your heart that is no longer satisfied by anything the world can supply and wants only what the Lord alone gives. Third, practice attentiveness: when the mind wanders, as it inevitably will, gently bring it back to the holy name without frustration. Throughout, hold the two poles together — pray like a beggar admitting your fallenness and that time is running short, while keeping a bold, living desire to serve Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa alive; do not let japa go dull, unaware, or mechanical, and do not slip into mere lip-service humility. Lean on grace rather than your own strength: the one charity to ask for is sevāmṛta — 'please accept me in your service.' Finally, chant by 'seeing with your ears': listen so attentively to descriptions of Kṛṣṇa — His monsoon-cloud complexion, peacock crown, flute, the lifting of Govardhana — that His form is visualized in your heart, until that picture arises spontaneously whenever His name is chanted.

## Glossary

- **Kṛṣṇa consciousness** — Awareness of and absorption in Kṛṣṇa; here, the lived spiritual experience (not mere belief) that sustains practice.
- **bhakti** — Loving devotional service to the Lord; the devotee confesses he does not understand even 'a sesame seed' of it.
- **japa** — The practice of softly repeating the holy name on beads; the lecture warns against letting it become dull and mechanical.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name(s) of the Lord; the chanting through which the 'magic' of devotion takes place.
- **Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa** — The Divine Couple at whose lotus feet the soul seeks complete shelter and personal service.
- **Vṛndāvana** — Kṛṣṇa's transcendental abode of loving service; the object of the chanter's bold desire to serve.
- **sevāmṛta** — The 'nectar of service' — direct, personal devotional service, the one charity the devotee begs of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — Vaiṣṇava teacher and poet whose song voices the soul's age-old longing to reach Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet.
- **Rūpa Gosvāmī** — Vaiṣṇava ācārya whose 'word-painting' of Kṛṣṇa's form is heard attentively so the Lord can be visualized in the heart.
- **Bhūrijana Prabhu** — Contemporary teacher whose class on the 'magic of bhakti' — humility joined with intense desire for Kṛṣṇa — is echoed here.
- **Govardhana** — The hill Kṛṣṇa lifted; one of the pastimes recalled in the meditative word-picture of His form.
- **lotus feet** — Idiom for the Lord's feet as the supreme object of shelter and surrender, reached only by mercy, not one's own strength.
