# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 05 SB 11 03 30 Discovery Lecture 02 2023 08 18 Slovenia

## Theme

Mahārāja frames the entire lecture as a graded teaching on the holy name: instruction must be matched to where a practitioner actually stands — a beginning level grounded in Harināma-cintāmaṇi for those starting a serious japa revolution, a medium level for active chanters, and an advanced level (the 'two types of faith' — forced and natural — and nāma-smaraṇam opening into līlā- and guṇa-smaraṇam, as Haridāsa Ṭhākura practiced). These confidential, integrally Gauḍīya subjects were long 'locked away' in the movement; teachers like Śivarāma Mahārāja are now restoring them, and we must enter them carefully within Śrīla Prabhupāda's guidelines rather than seal off a whole compartment of the tradition. From this educational principle the talk turns to its text, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.3.30: by gathering to chant the Lord's glories together (saṅkīrtana), devotees develop loving friendship, mutual satisfaction, and the strength to give up the material sense gratification that is the root of all suffering. Drawing on Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, Mahārāja shows that devotional joy is non-competitive and envy-free — unlike worldly enjoyment, which always boasts of being better — and clarifies that the 'detachment' meant is only from what is unfavorable to bhakti, not from a devotee spouse. He then redefines a disciple as a perpetual learner and lays out a foundation of sambandha-jñāna: knowing oneself as soul and getting to actually know Krishna as a person through śāstra. On this basis he gives the lecture's central architecture — the three S's that supply favorable conditions for the bhakti plant (śāstra, sādhu-saṅga, and sharing/sādhana — giving the holy name away through saṅkīrtana and joyful preaching) — and warns that favorable conditions alone are insufficient: the obstacles, chiefly the ten offenses (nāma-aparādha), must be patiently warded off, or one may chant for lifetimes without bhāva, like pouring water on a fire one is kindling. The practical heart follows: a sequence of aids to japa itself — sit rather than walk, pronounce each syllable clearly, hear oneself chant, let the sound penetrate from ear to heart where it cleanses consciousness, and chant prayerfully, turning even dry, mechanical chanting into a humble appeal ('I am fallen, please give me strength') that brings the heart into the holy name. Mahārāja closes by handling the mind: make it a friend, not an enemy — use the firm, disciplining mood only at set morning and evening times, and otherwise encourage rather than coerce (forcing devotion on a resentful mind or a teenager backfires). Penitential songs that begin 'I am a rascal' always end positively, because bhakti is fundamentally hopeful and meant for the most fallen. He situates Prabhupāda's standard of 16 rounds within tradition and notes that japa, being inward and meditative, is harder than the open, inclusive kīrtana of Caitanya Mahāprabhu, the Nāma Avatāra — yet by simply 'booking one's seat' in the kīrtan and staying there, the holy name itself carries the chanter back home, back to Godhead.

## Practical application

Build your daily japa on the three S's: feed it with śāstra (study the Bhāgavatam so that as you chant Krishna's name you actually know who Krishna is), with sādhu-saṅga (associate with devotees who have faith and taste, in person or through their books and lectures, drawing conclusions that move your sādhana forward rather than merely cataloguing problems), and with sharing — give the holy name away through congregational and street kīrtana and by showing people how happy you are, which reciprocally enlivens you. Guard the practice by patiently warding off the ten offenses. For the rounds themselves, apply the 'seven P's': choose a fixed period (morning and evening), a settled place and seated posture (sit rather than walk so the senses calm and maximum attention is free), pronounce each syllable clearly using lips and tongue, hear your own chanting attentively (use the ear-fold to hear how you really sound), let the vibration penetrate from the ear deep into the heart where it purifies, and chant prayerfully — when the chanting goes dry, turn it into a heartfelt, humble appeal ('I have no qualification, I am trying but failing, please give me strength'), which involves the heart and makes everything sweet. Treat the mind as a friend: at the set morning and evening times, firmly compel it to chant even when it resists (the 'broomstick'), but do not beat yourself up all day with guilt, which only breeds depression and turns the mind against devotion. When the 'monkey mind' chases a distraction, don't fight it — let it 'go alone' while you remain seated in the kīrtan. Keep Prabhupāda's standard of 16 rounds, carry the philosophy you hear into your whole day rather than filing it in a single morning class, and read penitential prayers to their hopeful conclusion, trusting that simply staying seated in the holy name will carry you home.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Soft, individual chanting of the holy name on beads; an inward, meditative discipline — harder than kīrtana but central to daily sādhana.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari/Krishna); chanting it is the central practice discussed.
- **Harināma-cintāmaṇi** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's treatise on the holy name; the grounding text for the beginning level of holy-name instruction.
- **nāma-smaraṇam** — Remembrance/meditation on Krishna combined with chanting the holy name, as practiced by Haridāsa Ṭhākura; an advanced practice.
- **līlā-smaraṇam** — Remembrance of the Lord's pastimes, into which advanced nāma-smaraṇam opens.
- **guṇa-smaraṇam** — Remembrance of the Lord's qualities, likewise opened up by advanced remembrance of the name.
- **Gauḍīya** — Pertaining to the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava tradition of Caitanya Mahāprabhu; the lineage to which these confidential teachings integrally belong.
- **saṅkīrtana** — Congregational chanting of the Lord's glories together; the most purifying process and the means of sharing the holy name.
- **kīrtana** — Open, public chanting of the holy name — inclusive and easy for all, as exemplified by Caitanya Mahāprabhu.
- **bhakti** — Devotional service to the Lord; the 'plant' that grows in the favorable conditions of śāstra, sādhu-saṅga, and sharing.
- **bhāva** — Spiritual emotion / the dawning of love of God; unattainable even after lifetimes of chanting if offenses are not cleared.
- **gṛhastha** — A householder devotee; not asked to renounce a devotional spouse, since detachment means giving up only what is unfavorable to bhakti.
- **sambandha-jñāna** — Foundational knowledge of one's relationship with God — that one is soul, part of Krishna — and of who Krishna is; the orientation underlying spiritual education.
- **śāstra** — The revealed scriptures, especially Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam; the first building block, by which one comes to actually know Krishna.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** — The principal Vaiṣṇava scripture studied to know Krishna; source of the day's text, SB 11.3.30.
- **sādhu-saṅga** — Association with devotees who have faith in and taste for the holy name; per Caitanya Mahāprabhu, the beginning, middle, and end of bhakti.
- **sādhana** — Regulated devotional practice — regular reading, association, and service — that genuine sādhu-saṅga advances.
- **sādhaka** — A practitioner of sādhana; the introspective devotee to whom inward japa is especially suited.
- **nāma-aparādha** — Offenses to the holy name — chiefly the ten offenses — the principal obstacles, best countered by a patient 'ten defenses' approach.
- **mahā-mantra** — The great chant (Hare Krishna...); per Prabhupāda, itself a humble prayer appealing to Krishna and His energy to be engaged in service.
- **Nāma Avatāra** — The incarnation of the holy name — Caitanya Mahāprabhu, famed for publicly dancing and chanting in inclusive congregational kīrtana.
- **prasādam** — Sanctified food offered to the Lord; brought back from gatherings as a means of gently attracting others rather than forcing devotion.
- **maṭha** — A monastery/temple-residence; Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī required its residents to chant 64 rounds.
