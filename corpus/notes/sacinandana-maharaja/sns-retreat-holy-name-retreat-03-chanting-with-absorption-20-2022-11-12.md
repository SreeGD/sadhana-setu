# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 03 Chanting with Absorption 2022 11 12

## Theme

This lecture, the third session of HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja's 2022 Holy-Name Retreat, reframes japa and kīrtana from an exercise in mind-control into one of absorption. Mahārāja opens by asking participants to set aside long-held assumptions and to engage the practical exercises, not merely listen. He diagnoses the central obstacle as the wandering mind, which—after a brief period of focus—'dislodges' like an uncoupled rail car and fills with worldly thoughts. The standard prescription of forcibly dragging the mind back fails to hold, he argues, because the mind is continuously fed by the world through the senses and keeps running on its own momentum like a fan still spinning after the power is cut. The decisive pivot is therefore not to suppress the mind but to lift it out of the borrowed material reality and place it into the spiritual world: we are not 'worldly people' but jantus, eternal souls temporarily operating through a lent body and mind. Chanting with absorption then works like blotting paper drawing out a stain—the mind soaks up Krishna's name and personality until consciousness is recolored by Him, and this, not mechanical recitation, produces spiritual ecstasy. Mahārāja shows how reframing transforms the same activity: through Patañjali's pratipakṣa-bhāvana ('opposite thinking') and a shift of inner narrative from 'I have to chant' to 'I want to,' 'I get to,' and finally 'I love to chant.' Love, he stresses, does not count rounds; mechanical counting betrays its absence. He grounds the absorption method in the tradition—Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's instruction (via Caitanya-Bhāgavata) to keep the vibrated sound within the heart, Rūpa Gosvāmī's image of the Holy Name dancing in the 'courtyard of the heart,' and the heart as the meeting place of ātmā and Paramātmā (the two birds in one tree). The practical core is a guided, breath-led method: withdraw from the world, recall one's eternal identity, hold the Lord before the inner vision (or simply the name itself), bring the vibrated sound into the heart, and each time the mind strays, gently return it—anchoring, on hard or agitated days, on just the first 'Hare' of each mantra. Mahārāja is careful to mark this single-point concentration as only the first, mechanical rung—mind-control, not yet bhakti—with higher stages mapped by Jīva Gosvāmī (the sequential appearance of the Lord in the purified heart) and Viśvanātha Cakravartī (beholding Krishna's pastimes 'through the window' of the letters). He then deepens the mood: chant the Name as a prayer of śaraṇāgati and ātma-nivedana, full dependence on Krishna's will, guided by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's Bhajana-rahasya, Caitanya-śikṣāmṛta, and Śaraṇāgati songs. Supporting principles round out the arc: taste must precede renunciation (do not abandon a lifestyle before developing higher taste); japa and kīrtan are themselves devotional service—śravaṇam, kīrtanam, and smaraṇam—pleasing to Krishna because He delights in the returning soul; the Name must be chanted prīti-pūrvakam, with love, so that the sound pierces the gross and subtle bodies to awaken the soul's dormant love; mental chanting is bona fide (Haridāsa Ṭhākura the precedent); and the practice should be kept pure devotion under Śrīla Prabhupāda's guidance—not blended with yoga (miśra-bhakti) but engaged as yukta-vairāgya, with 'Krishna first.' He closes with retreat logistics: take responsibility for one's pacing, rest (the noon nap), find quiet, and receive the retreat as one continuous whole.

## Practical application

Stop fighting the wandering mind and start relocating it. Before chanting, deliberately withdraw from the world—let surroundings, relationships, responsibilities, messages, and future plans gently slip away—and sit alone with the Lord, feeling safe and connected. Recall who you truly are: an eternal soul, separate from the body and its suffering, only temporarily estranged from Krishna through forgetfulness, and let that remembrance inspire you to take up loving service again. Hold the Lord before your inner vision—Radha-Krishna, your home deities, or Gaura—and if no clear image comes, simply rest in the name itself, since the Name is the Lord's presence. Then bring the vibrated sound of the mantra into the heart, the meeting place of the soul and the Supersoul, and keep your attention there. When the mind inevitably strays to likes, dislikes, or a forgotten message, do not battle it—gently return it to the sound; you cannot hold two things at once, so a Krishna-conscious thought simply displaces the worldly one. On agitated, fearful, or clouded days, simplify radically: give full concentration to just the first 'Hare' of each mantra and keep coming back to that single anchor—even a few minutes of intermittent connection begins to change something inside. Treat this as a starting rung, not the goal: aim gradually to chant the Name as a heartfelt prayer of surrender ('please engage me in Your service,' 'please accept me'), in the mood of the Śaraṇāgati prayers, with love (prīti-pūrvakam) rather than counting rounds against a clock. Transform your inner narrative from 'I have to chant' toward 'I love to chant.' Build supporting conditions—chant in the association of devotees to catch the mood, fill your rooms with light, fragrance, and recorded Hare Krishna melodies (remembering that the bhāva behind the sound matters most)—and let taste, not forced renunciation, lead the way. Keep the practice pure devotion: use techniques and breath only as aids to remember Krishna (yukta-vairāgya), not as ends in themselves, staying in tune with Śrīla Prabhupāda. Mental chanting is valid when needed. Recognize that sitting to chant attentively is itself real service that pleases Krishna—giving Him your time, energy, and focus through śravaṇam, kīrtanam, and smaraṇam.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Soft, often individual repetition of the Hare Krishna mantra, typically on beads; the central practice the lecture addresses.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational or vocal glorification of the Lord through chanting; together with japa, one of the two primary chanting practices.
- **jantu** — A living being or creature; Mahārāja's term for our true status as eternal souls who merely took birth here, as opposed to being 'worldly people.'
- **pratipakṣa-bhāvana** — 'Opposite thinking'; the technique from Patañjali's Yoga Sūtras of deliberately cultivating counteracting positive thoughts when harassed by negative ones.
- **Yoga Sūtras** — Patañjali's foundational text on yoga, cited as the source of pratipakṣa-bhāvana (reframing the mind).
- **bhāva** — Spiritual emotion or devotional mood; an advanced stage of devotion preceding prema, and the inner disposition that matters more than the melody.
- **prema** — Pure love of God; the final, highest stage of devotional development reached after bhāva.
- **ātmā** — The individual soul or self; with the Supersoul, one of the 'two birds in the same tree' meeting in the heart.
- **Paramātmā** — The Supersoul, the Lord's localized expansion residing in the heart alongside the individual soul; the place where spiritual realization occurs.
- **Bhajana-rahasya** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's work laying out a progressive, staged program of chanting that advances the practitioner toward bhāva and prema.
- **śaraṇāgati** — Surrender or taking shelter of Krishna; the mood in which chanting becomes a prayer that the Lord hears and reciprocates.
- **ātma-nivedation** — Full self-surrender—giving up faith in one's own strength and accepting Krishna's lotus feet as the only hope, dependent on His will in all things.
- **Caitanya-śikṣāmṛta** — A work of Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura recommended, with his Śaraṇāgati songs, as a practical guide to surrendered, prayerful chanting.
- **Śaraṇāgati** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's collection of devotional songs expressing the moods of surrender to Krishna.
- **sādhana** — Regulated devotional practice; its stages mirror the sequential appearance of the Lord within the purified heart.
- **bhakti** — Loving devotional service to Krishna; the actual goal, distinct from mere mind-control, which is only a means.
- **miśra-bhakti** — 'Mixed' devotion—blending bhakti with yoga practices (yama, niyama, prāṇāyāma); cautioned against because it shifts attention from Krishna back onto oneself.
- **yukta-vairāgya** — 'Appropriate renunciation'; engaging things or techniques only in order to worship Krishna, rather than getting caught up in the techniques themselves.
- **śravaṇam** — Hearing about the Lord; one of the nine limbs of bhakti, engaged in chanting.
- **kīrtanam** — Glorifying or chanting about the Lord; one of the nine limbs of bhakti, engaged in japa and kīrtan.
- **smaraṇam** — Remembrance of the Lord; the limb of bhakti added when chanting is done mindfully, especially stressed in the Caitanya-caritāmṛta.
- **prīti-pūrvakam** — 'With love' or 'preceded by affection'; the manner in which the holy name must be chanted as a loving offering for it to be true bhakti.
- **sthūla-śarīra** — The gross (physical) body, which the absorbed, loving sound vibration of the Name pierces to reach the soul.
- **liṅga-śarīra** — The subtle body (mind, intelligence, false ego), which the holy name also perforates on its way to the covered soul.
- **Krama-sandarbha** — Jīva Gosvāmī's commentary (on Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 7.5.18) mapping chanting as a progressive realization in which the Lord's form, qualities, and pastimes appear in the purified heart.
- **Caitanya-Bhāgavata** — The biography of Śrī Caitanya cited for Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura's instruction to keep the vibrated sound within the heart while controlling the materially attached mind.
- **Caitanya-caritāmṛta** — The principal biography and theology of Śrī Caitanya, cited as especially stressing remembrance (smaraṇam) in chanting.
- **Ananta-Śeṣa** — The infinite serpent on whom the Lord rests; one of the pastimes an advanced devotee may behold 'through the window' of the Name's letters.
- **Garuḍa Purāṇa** — A Purāṇa cited (with Prabhupāda's own exhortation) to locate the essence of chanting in the love behind it, not in the sound alone.
- **rajasic** — Pertaining to rajas, the mode of passion; the restless, driven atmosphere one must consciously wake out of to become Krishna conscious.
- **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** — The great devotee renowned for his daily chanting, cited as the precedent for the validity of mental chanting (within the mind).
