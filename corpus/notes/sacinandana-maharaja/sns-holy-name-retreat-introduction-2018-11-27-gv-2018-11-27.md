# SNS Holy Name Retreat Introduction 2018 11 27 GV

## Theme

This introductory talk frames the Holy-Name retreat as a deliberate withdrawal from ordinary engagements to enter a sacred place — the valley of Giriraj Govardhan — for the sole purpose of going deeper in Krishna consciousness. Mahārāja's central claim is that the holy dham offers a unique support for spiritual advancement unavailable anywhere else: in its association nearly every sincere searcher receives the sign or hint, however subtle, needed to take the next step, with Govardhan himself granting each person the specific guidance their present stage requires. From there the talk turns to how one must hold this opportunity. Realization, he cautions, is not the goal in itself; participants will leave 'stumbling' under the weight of insights they have gathered, and the real work begins afterward in implementing those insights in daily life. Two conditions make the retreat fruitful. First, the gathering is a sangha — a community of sincere seekers united as one — and this association of like-minded devotees is itself a primary source of help and inspiration. Second, one must come in a receiver's mood, opening heart and mind in humble receptivity rather than sitting as a passive spectator, because what one gains is proportional to the openness one brings. Underlying all of this is the truth that Krishna is the origin and original father of all living beings; while devotees may share a generic, material fatherhood as well, their deeper unity rests on this shared relationship with Krishna — the bond that makes the sangha one.

## Practical application

A devotee carries the retreat's logic into daily Hari-Nāma by first creating the conditions the talk describes. Set aside ordinary distractions before sitting to chant, treating japa time as a small 'retreat' in which other engagements are deliberately dropped so the mind can go deeper. Chant in a receiver's mood — approach the Holy Name with an open, humble heart rather than as a passive routine, expecting that the gain is proportional to the receptivity offered. Watch for the 'sign or hint': remain attentive during and after chanting for the subtle guidance the Name and the dham give about the next step in one's sadhana, and then do the harder work of implementing those realizations in conduct rather than collecting insights and leaving them unused. Lean on sangha — chant in and stay connected to a community of sincere seekers, drawing inspiration from that association. And let the chanting deepen the awareness that Krishna is the original father of all beings, so that the Name is approached as the renewal of a shared, eternal relationship rather than a mechanical practice.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name of Krishna (Hari); the practice of chanting the Name, which is the focus of the retreat.
- **japa** — The personal, meditative repetition of the Holy Name, typically counted on beads — the daily practice to which the retreat's teachings are applied.
- **dham (holy dham)** — A sacred place of pilgrimage manifesting Krishna's presence; here the dham offers a unique support for spiritual advancement not available elsewhere.
- **Giriraj Govardhan** — The sacred hill ('king of mountains', Giriraj) of the Govardhan region, lifted by Krishna; the valley where the retreat is held, whose blessings give each searcher specific guidance.
- **Krishna consciousness** — Awareness of and absorption in Krishna; deepening this is the stated purpose of going on retreat.
- **sangha** — A community of sincere spiritual seekers united as one; such association of like-minded devotees is itself a primary source of help and inspiration on the path.
