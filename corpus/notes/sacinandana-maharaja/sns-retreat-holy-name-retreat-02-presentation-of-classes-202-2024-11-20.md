# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 02 Presentation of Classes 2024 11 20

## Theme

Framed by the opening image of Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's Mādhurya-kādambinī — Caitanya Mahāprabhu as a raincloud of sweetness whose nectar of mercy can revive even a dried-out tree in a barren desert — this presentation sets the aspiration for a Holy-Name retreat held before Giriraj Govardhan in Vṛndāvana. Its central thesis is that the quality of chanting matters more than its quantity. Drawing on Jīva Gosvāmī's distinction between kevala-nāma (mechanical chanting, whose result is merely deliverance from hell) and sneha-saṁyukta-nāma (the name chanted with affection, which draws Kṛṣṇa into close association), and on Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's counsel to chant with extremely clear pronunciation and genuine feeling, Sacīnandana Mahārāja — with additions from Bhūrijana Prabhu — argues that increasing rounds is easy (a matter of time and willpower) while cultivating devotional feeling requires contemplation and cannot be forced. The 'loads of popcorn' analogy underscores that volume offered without love is no real gift to Kṛṣṇa. The deeper aim is a sacred, personal relationship with Nāma Prabhu, beginning with the honest self-inquiry 'Do I actually want to get closer to Kṛṣṇa?' The retreat's radical conditions — silence (mauna-vrata), no phone, no photography, sitting alone before Govardhan — are designed to remove distraction so devotees apply what they hear, confront their own turning away from Kṛṣṇa, and consciously choose to turn back. Worship of Giriraj is presented not as stone-worship but as loving exchange with a soft, welcoming person, approached through self-introduction and the surrendered mood of ātma-nivedana, in a spirit of hope rather than expectation, since mercy is freely given and never earned. The larger purpose is restated as not merely spreading Kṛṣṇa consciousness but actually being Kṛṣṇa conscious — developing love of God, the highest goal of life — and discovering that in essence one belongs to Kṛṣṇa. Practical guidance follows: identifying with the sincere 'devotee-part' of oneself amid the tired, hungry, and distracted parts; entering solitary practice prepared by study of a guidance booklet; and, in marathon chanting, valuing quality of attention over hitting a round-count target. Substantial portions of the session are collective kīrtana of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra — itself treated as the core practice — with instruction on unified sound (holding the beat, gathering at the kīrtana center, staying silent rather than playing offbeat) so that outward coordination supports inward absorption, and the reminder that even good-sounding chanting can deepen further.

## Practical application

Shift the daily focus from how many rounds you finish to how you chant. Before sitting down — nearly every time — ask honestly, 'Do I actually want to get closer to Kṛṣṇa?', recognizing that the real obstacle is usually the absence of that desire rather than technique. Then watch for two things Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura names: extremely clear pronunciation of each name, and genuine feeling toward Kṛṣṇa. Rather than relying on willpower to pile up quantity (which gives only 'loads of popcorn'), give time to contemplation that grows affection. Notice that you contain many 'parts' — tired, hungry, distracted — and deliberately identify with the sincere, determined part that wants to connect with Kṛṣṇa. Treat chanting as a relationship: introduce yourself to Giriraj or to the holy name, telling the story of your life, your pains, pleasures, and hopes, in the surrendered mood of ātma-nivedana. Come with hope, not demand, approaching as a humble beggar, since mercy cannot be earned. Where possible, create retreat-like conditions — set the phone aside, reduce distraction, observe periods of silence — so that time with the holy name is spent applying what you hear and consciously choosing to turn toward Kṛṣṇa rather than away. In marathon or extended sessions, aim for quality of attention and feeling, not just a target count, and prepare for solitary chanting with proper study rather than entering it aimlessly. In group kīrtana, support unified sound — hold the beat, gather together, and stay quiet rather than play offbeat — so outward coordination aids inward absorption.

## Glossary

- **Mādhurya-kādambinī** — A treatise by Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura on the development of devotion; its opening image of Caitanya Mahāprabhu as a raincloud of mercy frames the retreat's aspiration.
- **kevala-nāma** — The holy name chanted only mechanically, as mere discharge of duty; per Jīva Gosvāmī its result is deliverance from hell.
- **sneha-saṁyukta-nāma** — The holy name chanted with affection; per Jīva Gosvāmī it draws Kṛṣṇa to bring the chanter into His close association and direct devotional service.
- **nāma-kīrtana** — Chanting or singing of the holy name; recommended to be performed constantly, with clear pronunciation and genuine feeling.
- **japa** — Soft, often individual recitation of the holy name, typically counted on beads; the retreat aims to make it deep, meaningful, and filled with affection.
- **Nāma Prabhu** — The holy name regarded as a person and Lord, with whom the devotee cultivates a sacred relationship.
- **mauna-vrata** — A vow of silence; observed in the retreat to remove distraction and deepen private time with the holy name.
- **Giriraj Govardhan / Govardhan-śilā** — Govardhan Hill, worshiped as a person non-different from Kṛṣṇa; a Govardhan-śilā is a sacred stone from the hill, honored as loving exchange with Kṛṣṇa, not stone-worship.
- **ātma-nivedana** — Full self-surrender — laying one's whole life bare before Kṛṣṇa or a sādhu; described by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura in Śaraṇāgati and applied here as opening the heart before chanting.
- **Śaraṇāgati** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's work on surrender to Kṛṣṇa, the source for the surrendered mood of ātma-nivedana invoked in the lecture.
- **mahā-mantra** — The Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra (Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma Hare Hare), sung in the retreat's collective kīrtana as the core devotional practice.
- **Vṛndāvana** — The holy land where Kṛṣṇa is always present; chanting there, in the association of devotees and of Giriraj Govardhan, is said to increase one's desire to draw closer to Kṛṣṇa.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational singing of the holy name; treated here as the central practice, requiring unified sound for effective collective absorption.
