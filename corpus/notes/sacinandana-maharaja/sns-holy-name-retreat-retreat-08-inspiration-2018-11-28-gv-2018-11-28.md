# SNS Holy Name Retreat Retreat 08 Inspiration 2018 11 28 GV

## Theme

Mahārāja opens with a story from Govardhan: after walking the same parikramā path for decades, he one day stepped onto a small road leading inward toward Giri-rāja and discovered a quiet, unique area — peacocks, cows, no crowds — that had been waiting beside his familiar route all along. He offers this as the lecture's governing image for spiritual life: we keep treading our well-worn routine, unaware that treasures and unique discoveries lie right next to it, reachable only if we are still and willing to walk the less-trodden path and make a little extra effort. He then frames the retreat itself in this light. Its intensive practice — many hours of kīrtana, eight hours in a day — is not an end in itself but preparation: it readies participants in body and energy to move toward a 64-round chanting day at Ter Kadamba, the place of Rūpa Gosvāmī. The deeper purpose runs through Vṛndāvana, a mysterious place one can truly perceive only when one's consciousness becomes transcendental, full of love for Krishna. All the retreat's elements — chanting, hearing, the teacher's pushing and guidance, the inner path taken with extra effort — are signposts meant to help us at least touch that transcendental platform, so that the transcendental experience and realm become accessible. The arc closes on how simple this touching can be: to call out the holy name — here, Giri-rāja — as loudly as one can, with the full force of heart, mind, and lungs.

## Practical application

Treat your daily japa and Hari-Nāma as that step off the familiar path. Don't let chanting harden into unconscious routine: be still, bring willingness, and make the small extra effort to go inward, trusting that something great waits right beside your usual practice. On intensive chanting days, manage your energies wisely — pace yourself through the long hours of kīrtana rather than burning out — and absorb yourself fully in the holy name, letting the practice prepare you for deeper rounds rather than treating quantity as the goal. Remember that hearing, the guidance and 'pushing' of a teacher, and your own effort are aids, not destinations; their aim is to help you touch the transcendental platform even briefly. And keep the method simple at its core: call out the holy name with your whole heart, mind, and lungs — wholehearted, full-strength calling — rather than chanting mechanically.

## Glossary

- **Govardhan** — The sacred hill near Vṛndāvana lifted by Krishna; site of the parikramā path Mahārāja describes walking.
- **parikramā** — Devotional circumambulation of a sacred place along an established path.
- **Giri-rāja** — "King of mountains" — a name for Govardhan Hill, here also called out as a holy name.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational singing/chanting of the holy names of Krishna.
- **japa** — Individual, often quiet, repetition of the holy name, traditionally counted on beads.
- **round** — A unit of japa — one full cycle of 108 beads; the retreat builds toward a 64-round chanting day.
- **Ter Kadamba** — A sacred place near Vṛndāvana associated with Rūpa Gosvāmī, the destination for the intensive chanting day.
- **Rūpa Gosvāmī** — A foremost disciple of Caitanya Mahāprabhu and a principal teacher of Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava devotion, associated with Ter Kadamba.
- **Vṛndāvana** — Krishna's sacred land; described as mysterious, perceptible in its true nature only to transcendental consciousness.
- **Krishna** — The Supreme Person and object of loving devotion in Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava practice.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari); the chanting of it is the central practice of the retreat.
- **transcendental platform** — A state of consciousness beyond the material, full of love for Krishna, from which the spiritual realm becomes accessible.
