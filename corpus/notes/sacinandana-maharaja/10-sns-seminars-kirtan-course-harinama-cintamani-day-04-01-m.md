# 10 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 04 01 Morning Lecture ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This lecture — Day 04, Morning Session of HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja's Kirtan Course on Haridāsa Ṭhākura's Harināma Cintāmaṇi at ISKCON Radhadesh — traces the movement from 'shadow chanting' (nāmābhāsa) to potent, pure chanting of the Holy Name. Opening at Radhadesh, the Mahārāja establishes that a holy place draws its power from sustained devotional practice — resident Vaiṣṇavas and faithful daily deity worship — not from geography alone. The core obstacle keeping the Name from revealing itself is identified as 'weakness of the heart': attachment to name, fame, and objects unrelated to Kṛṣṇa. The bulk of the lecture then explains, through Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (5.19.27), Caitanya-caritāmṛta, and the example of Dhruva Mahārāja, how Kṛṣṇa deals with such material desire. Rather than forbidding the desirous devotee or fueling his cravings like a drug, Kṛṣṇa welcomes that the devotee approached HIM at all, fulfills the desire in a purifying way, and — unasked — grants shelter at His lotus feet. Thus the sakāma-bhakta (motivated devotee) is gradually transformed into an akāma-bhakta (motiveless devotee) who ultimately desires nothing but service. The Mahārāja illustrates this from his own life, where a youthful, ego-centered craving for stage celebration was dedicated to Kṛṣṇa through kīrtana and eventually purified. Applied to japa, he counsels engaging one's whole personality — even a material desire — fully in the Name, since Kṛṣṇa Himself helps the sincere chanter, while cautioning that removing gross desires is only a beginning; deep saṁskāras remain and must be purified through the association of devotees, whose pure lives awaken admiration and the wish to follow. The remedy for the nāmābhāsa plateau is sambandha-jñāna — understanding Kṛṣṇa — which Haridāsa Ṭhākura opens Harināma Cintāmaṇi by teaching through three subjects: the spiritual manifestation (cit-vaibhava — Kṛṣṇa Himself, His names, forms, qualities, and pastimes), the material manifestation (acit), and the self (jīva), a minute conscious atom driven by the need for happiness (ānanda) who finds it only in Kṛṣṇa, never in temporary matter. The lecture culminates in the Holy Name as the most merciful of Kṛṣṇa's spiritual manifestations — Nāma Prabhu — who, when served rather than merely produced, melts the hardened heart as fire melts gold and drains out its impurities. In the age of Kali the one essential duty is thus remembering the Name (japa) and congregationally chanting it (nāma-saṅkīrtana), and the Mahārāja closes by having students actively reflect on the teaching so that chanting rises above mechanical repetition into a clear grasp of cit, acit, and jīva.

## Practical application

Sit down for japa honestly acknowledging that you come with material desire (sakāma) — do not wait to be 'pure enough' first, because Kṛṣṇa welcomes the devotee who brings his desires to HIM rather than seeking them elsewhere, and He personally helps the sincere chanter. Engage your whole personality, even a material craving, fully in serving the Name, trusting Kṛṣṇa to both fulfill and purify it as He did for Dhruva. Chant with concentration and determination — best in the association of devotees, and, when possible, day and night rather than dwelling on eating and enjoyment — since focused effort drives out material desire quickly. Recognize that dropping gross desires is only the start: the heart is still full of saṁskāras, so keep purifying it so old attachments do not return in new forms. To break past the nāmābhāsa plateau, do not chant blindly; cultivate sambandha-jñāna by regularly reading Prabhupāda's books (Bhagavad-gītā, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, Caitanya-caritāmṛta) so that you come to know Kṛṣṇa's names, forms, qualities, and pastimes — for you cannot have a relationship with someone you do not know. Serve the Holy Name as a person (Nāma Prabhu), not merely produce a mechanical 'na-na-na,' letting the Name melt and cleanse your heart. Seek out and serve pure devotees, holding Śrīla Prabhupāda dear, so that admiration for their lives awakens the desire to follow. And to retain the teaching, reflect on it actively — turn it over, discuss it — keeping clearly in mind the three categories of spiritual manifestation (cit), matter (acit), and the self (jīva) as you chant.

## Glossary

- **nāma / Holy Name (Nāma Prabhu)** — The name of Kṛṣṇa, understood not as a mundane sound but as the Lord Himself personally present; among His spiritual manifestations, the most merciful form, who enters and cleanses the heart when served rather than merely mechanically produced.
- **nāmābhāsa** — 'Shadow' or 'clearing' chanting of the Name — a second level where inattention, lingering anarthas, and material desire cloud the Name like fog over the sun, so its full glory and transformative power are only partially revealed.
- **dhāma** — A holy place or abode of the Lord (e.g. Radhadesh, Vraja, Navadvīpa), whose spiritual potency is built by sustained devotional practice — faithful resident Vaiṣṇavas and regular deity worship — not by location alone.
- **sakāma-bhakta** — A devotee who serves the Lord with a material motive or desire.
- **akāma-bhakta** — A devotee who serves without material motive; the higher stage into which Kṛṣṇa's special mercy transforms the sakāma-bhakta, until he desires nothing but service to the Lord's lotus feet.
- **bhakti / devotional service** — Loving service to the Supreme Lord; the intense practice through which everyone — free of desire, full of desire, or seeking liberation — is enjoined to worship Kṛṣṇa.
- **darśana** — The direct sight or audience of the Lord; on receiving it, Dhruva Mahārāja declared himself fully satisfied and free of material want.
- **saṁskāra** — A deep-seated mental impression or conditioning left in the heart; even after gross desires are given up, these remain and must be purified so renounced desires do not reappear in new forms.
- **anartha** — An unwanted thing or impurity in the heart that obstructs pure devotion and clouds the 'sun' of the Holy Name.
- **sambandha-jñāna** — Knowledge of one's relationship with Kṛṣṇa — understanding who Kṛṣṇa is; the remedy for the nāmābhāsa level, without which chanting stays on the material platform.
- **cit / cit-vaibhava** — The spiritual manifestation born of Kṛṣṇa's superior spiritual energy — His realm and above all Kṛṣṇa Himself: His names, forms, qualities, and transcendental pastimes; one of the three subjects Haridāsa Ṭhākura teaches for understanding the Name.
- **acit** — The material manifestation or material nature; the second of the three subjects to be understood on the path to pure chanting.
- **jīva** — The individual living entity — a minute atom of consciousness with independent will, driven by the need for happiness, who either turns to matter or takes shelter as Kṛṣṇa's eternal servant; the third of the three subjects to be understood.
- **ānanda** — Bliss or happiness; the innate motive driving every jīva, truly fulfilled only in Kṛṣṇa and never in temporary material gratification.
- **nāma-saṅkīrtana** — Congregational chanting of the Holy Name; together with remembering the Name, the essential religious duty for the jīva in the age of Kali.
- **nāmera smaraṇa** — Remembrance of the Name — i.e. japa, the personal recitation and recollection of the Holy Name that is the one essential duty in Kali-yuga.
- **japa** — The individual, meditative chanting/remembrance of the Holy Name, typically on beads.
- **Kali / Kali-yuga** — The present degraded age, in which the Lord descended bringing His Holy Name as the preeminent means of deliverance.
- **suhṛt** — A well-wishing friend; the pure-hearted state of one whose heart the Lord, seated within, has cleansed of impure and inauspicious things — a friend of the saintly.
- **Naṭavara** — 'The best of dancers' — a charming form of Kṛṣṇa as the dancer who plays the flute.
- **rāsa-līlā** — Kṛṣṇa's transcendental dance pastime in Vraja; cited as an example of His spiritual pastimes, wholly spiritual even when appearing in this world.
- **Harināma Cintāmaṇi** — Haridāsa Ṭhākura's treatise on the Holy Name (as presented by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura), whose first chapter explains how to understand Kṛṣṇa through cit, acit, and jīva; the text on which this Kirtan Course is based.
