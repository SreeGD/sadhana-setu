# SNS Holy Name Retreat Retreat 11 The Living Name 2018 11 30 GV

## Theme

Titled 'The Living Name,' this retreat lecture by HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja traces how the holy name, being wholly spiritual, lies beyond our material senses just as a landscape lies beyond a blind man — and so it cannot be seized by technique or effort but reveals itself only by its own mercy. It descends first into the purified heart, where both Paramātmā and the soul already dwell at the border of matter and spirit, and only then springs onto the tongue; where it has truly entered, it brings prema, ecstasy, and tears. The name waits for one thing: sevonmukha, a mood of service. Mahārāja shows how chanting is rendered as service of the body (sit respectfully, vibrate the name, hear it correctly) and of the mind, which is steadied by Krishna's twin instructions to Arjuna — abhyāsa (repeatedly returning to the name) and vairāgya (firmly refusing the mind's distractions). But control of the mind is only the threshold; the deeper work is to chant from the platform of the soul, since the material body and mind have no wish to chant. This raises the lecture's central question — 'Who are you?' — answered not as theory but as lived realization through sustained hearing from a saint, which produces an unshakeable conviction that 'I am not the body but a part of Krishna.' Drawing on Jagadānanda Paṇḍita's Prema-vivarta, Mahārāja describes the soul's awakening on contact with a sādhu and its lament for having served māyā so long, and recommends regular 'infusions' from saintly persons — including daily hearing of Śrīla Prabhupāda — to keep that identity alive. He then maps the dynamics of testing: Krishna, who cannot be cheated, sends māyā with offers tailored to our hidden desires, covering the soul with her āvaraṇātmika-śakti and dragging it by her pushing-pulling potency; the sincere chanter recognizes 'I am being tested,' says 'no, no, no,' and on that refusal Krishna infuses sphūrti-śakti, spiritual strength that revives the weak soul and overpowers māyā. The breakthrough from mechanical chanting to kīrtana-rasa comes only through genuine repentance born of honest self-introspection — even in Vrindavan one may still be self-absorbed and fault-finding — and through the spontaneous tears of one who cries 'O Krishna, what have I done?' Mahārāja then turns to what actually attracts the Lord: not many rounds, austerities, or beauty, but love. He illustrates with the gopīs, whose pride drove Krishna away and whose humble return to chant His name by the Yamuna shows the cure ('I am fortunate' rather than 'I am the best'); with Krishna's open delight in being praised by His devotees; with His promise, like Nārāyaṇa, never to reject a surrendered soul; and with His word to Arjuna in the Ādi Purāṇa that He can be purchased by one who chants His name and cries for Him. Rukmiṇī Devī, who never saw Krishna but heard of Him constantly from Nārada Muni, becomes the lecture's emblem: she rejected all other relationships for Him on the strength of hearing alone, and His reply to Garuḍa — that He is won by love and devotion, not beauty — crystallizes the thesis. Effective chanting, then, is simply turning to Krishna with 'please accept me in Your service'; yet keeping the prescribed minimum of rounds remains good and necessary, for by it ātma-bhāva develops, and chanting one's rounds even without taste is itself a sign of surrender to the spiritual master who requested it. The lecture closes practically: Mahārāja frames japa around three settled meditations — who I am (an atomic particle of consciousness, eternally Krishna's servant), who Krishna is (the infinite Lord and my only master), and what the world is (a 'house of correction' and thus a grand opportunity to return) — and leads a centering breath-awareness practice and a guided meditation on being present in the holy place, situated in the heart-space as the servant of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. He teaches Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's simple meditation ('I am an atomic particle of consciousness, and I am Krishna's eternal servant'), shows how questioning the platform on which a problem stands and then chanting often dissolves the problem, and ends by insisting that one can leave the false world only after coming to know the real one — which grows by ever more hearing from genuine saints and study of Krishna's pastimes.

## Practical application

Approach japa not as a technique to master but as a service rendered in a mood of turning to Krishna (sevonmukha). Begin by settling the body — sit upright and respectfully, breathe out a little more fully to empty and center yourself, let distracting thoughts gently go and return to the breath ('I know that I am breathing in'), and then vibrate the name clearly and hear it correctly. Carry three settled meditations into the mantra: who you are (an atomic particle of consciousness, the eternal servant of Krishna, distinct from and dwelling within body and mind, situated in the heart-space); who Krishna is (the infinite Supreme Lord and your only real master, the one relationship you have forgotten amid countless others); and what this world is (a house of correction, and therefore a grand opportunity to turn back to Him now). Steady the wandering mind with abhyāsa and vairāgya — keep returning to the holy name, and when the mind is pushed and pulled by distractions (the mobile phone, WhatsApp, doubts, tiredness), recognize that the name is still present and you are only momentarily covered by māyā, then refuse to follow it firmly: 'No — I'm not interested, come back when you have something positive to say.' Treat such restlessness as a test Krishna deliberately sends; stand firm in your convictions and be happy to be tested, knowing that on your refusal He infuses spiritual strength (sphūrti-śakti) that weakens māyā's pull. Beyond controlling the mind, offer the heart: open it and pray, 'I am not very qualified, but it is now my time with You — please accept me in Your service,' calling with urgency and sincerity. Cultivate repentance through honest self-introspection — notice where you remain self-absorbed or fault-finding even while chanting — and let the tears of 'O Krishna, what have I done? Without Your mercy I cannot move forward' come spontaneously rather than by willful arrangement. Guard against pride: shift from 'I am the best' to 'I am fortunate,' redirecting credit to guru and Krishna. Keep faithfully to your prescribed minimum of rounds (e.g., sixteen) even when taste is absent — not because numbers impress the Lord, but because that steadiness is itself a sign of surrender to the spiritual master and the means by which ātma-bhāva, your natural attraction to Krishna, develops. Sustain all of this by regular 'infusions' from saintly persons: hear daily from a genuine saint and from Śrīla Prabhupāda's lectures and books, and study Krishna's pastimes — as Rukmiṇī's example shows, hearing alone is powerful enough to awaken real devotion. Finally, when a problem occupies the mind, question the platform on which it stands ('I am a soul, not of this world, I belong to Krishna') and then chant from that conviction; you will often find the problem resolved and solutions arriving on their own, while chanting itself becomes a realistic contact with the eternal home where the soul is invited to dwell.

## Glossary

- **Holy name (Hari-Nāma)** — The wholly spiritual name of the Lord, identical with Him; beyond the reach of unpurified material senses, it reveals itself only by its own mercy, descending first into the heart and then onto the tongue.
- **Prema** — Pure love of God with which the holy name is filled; where the name truly enters the heart, prema moves the chanter to ecstasy and tears.
- **Paramātmā** — The Supersoul, the Lord's expansion who dwells within the heart alongside the individual soul, at the fine border of the material and spiritual; He knows all our pains and pleasures and cannot be cheated.
- **Ātman / ātmā** — The soul, the true self beyond the material body and mind; real chanting must take place on its platform, since only the soul wishes to chant and serve.
- **Sevonmukha** — A service-oriented mood, turning to the Lord with the desire to serve; the one disposition the holy name waits for before revealing itself.
- **Seva** — Devotional service; chanting itself is rendered as seva of the body and of the mind.
- **Abhyāsa** — Practice — repeatedly listening to and returning to the holy name; one of the two means Krishna gives Arjuna for controlling the restless mind.
- **Vairāgya** — Detachment — saying no to and ignoring the mind's distractions rather than being dragged by its likes and dislikes; the second means of steadying the mind in chanting.
- **Kīrtana** — Congregational or vocal glorification of the Lord through singing His names; Krishna becomes pleased when a devotee sincerely sings for Him.
- **Kīrtana-rasa** — The taste or relish of chanting, attained when one rises above mechanical, mental chanting through the desire to sever one's bond with māyā and the force of genuine repentance.
- **Māyā** — The Lord's illusory material energy; sent by Krishna to test the chanter with offers tailored to hidden desires, she covers and drags the soul until the devotee firmly refuses her.
- **Āvaraṇātmika-śakti** — Māyā's covering potency, by which she veils the soul from its true spiritual identity.
- **Sphūrti-śakti** — The spiritual strength Krishna infuses into the weak soul — like reviving the dead — enabling it to make spiritual decisions, advance in devotional service, and say 'no' when māyā tests it.
- **Māyā-sambandha** — One's relationship or entanglement with māyā, which the sincere devotee wishes to sever in favor of a relationship with Krishna.
- **Sambandha** — Relationship — here, the relationship with Krishna that is immediately evoked when one turns to Him sincerely and with urgency.
- **Ātma-bhāva** — The soul's natural, awakened attraction to Krishna; until it develops, the devotee's attraction remains divided between the world and the Lord, which is why keeping the prescribed rounds is necessary.
- **Bhajan** — Devotional worship and practice; Mahārāja notes that even one doing bhajan in Vrindavan may remain self-absorbed without genuine repentance.
- **Bhakti** — Devotion; chanting is infused with its life only when one can turn to Krishna, which comes from hearing about Him repeatedly.
- **Sādhu** — A saintly person; transformative contact with a sādhu awakens the soul to its true identity, as described in the scriptures.
- **Guru / spiritual master** — The bestower of mercy and spiritual guidance to whom the devotee surrenders; chanting the prescribed rounds is a sign of surrender to the spiritual master who requested it.
- **Sankalpa / vow of rounds** — The prescribed minimum number of japa rounds (e.g., sixteen); not meant to impress the Lord, but the means by which ātma-bhāva develops and a sign of genuine desire for devotion.
- **Prasādam** — Sanctified food offered to the Lord; cited as one of the external causes to which we wrongly attribute our misery.
- **Gopīs** — The cowherd maidens of Vrindavan whose love for Krishna is exemplary; their pride drove Him away, and their humble return to chant His name by the Yamuna illustrates the cure of humility.
- **Dharma** — Religious and social duty; Krishna externally invoked it when telling the gopīs to return home, testing their surrender.
- **Prema-vivarta** — The work of Jagadānanda Paṇḍita quoted in the lecture, describing the soul's wandering through birth after birth and its awakening to its true identity on contact with a saintly person.
- **Ādi Purāṇa** — The scripture in which Krishna tells Arjuna that He can be purchased by one who chants His holy name and cries for Him.
- **Nārāyaṇa** — The Lord in His majestic form who never rejects anyone who comes to His lotus feet; cited as the nature to which a surrendered soul can hold Krishna.
- **Rukmiṇī Devī** — Krishna's queen, daughter of Mahārāja Bhīṣmaka, who awakened her love for Krishna purely by hearing Nārada Muni narrate His pastimes — the lecture's emblem of the power of hearing.
- **Nārada Muni** — The traveling sage whose prolonged narrations of Krishna's pastimes, forms, and qualities awakened Rukmiṇī's attraction to the Lord.
- **Garuḍa** — Krishna's eagle carrier; in the Rukmiṇī pastime his praise of her beauty prompts Krishna's reply that He is won by love and devotion, not by beauty.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — The Vaiṣṇava ācārya who gives the easy meditation for chanting with feeling: 'I am an atomic particle of consciousness, and I am Krishna's eternal servant.'
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda** — The founder-ācārya whose lectures and books provide the daily 'infusion' from a saint that changes one's sense of identity; Mahārāja recalls his first contact with him in London.
- **Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa** — The Divine Couple; the soul, situated in the heart-space, is realized as Their eternal servant and a resident of the spiritual world.
