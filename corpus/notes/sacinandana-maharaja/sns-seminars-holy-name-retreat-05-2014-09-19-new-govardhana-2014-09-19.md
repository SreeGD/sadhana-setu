# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 05 2014 09 19 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

Mahārāja frames Holy-Name worship not as passive adoration but as a reciprocal exchange: when one chants in a mood of adoring and glorifying the Name, devotion goes out and Krishna responds in proportion to one's surrender, lifting the worshipper within that circle. Because this knowledge is experiential rather than merely theoretical, the workshop emphasizes singing more and talking less — learning by doing — and then carrying that same concentration into one's daily japa. He offers a set of complementary techniques. First, mantra-trāṭaka, using a card to stay with every name and syllable, trains one to remain 'present-minded' rather than drifting into the surrounding 'dream world.' Three japa principles follow: (1) be aware that the Holy Name is non-different from Krishna, so chanting is direct association with Him — 'Krishna is just a turn away'; (2) chant from humility and longing, where humility means thinking less of oneself and therefore more of Krishna, the mood of Śikṣāṣṭakam verse five — the infinitesimal soul fallen in the ocean of this world begging to be fixed as a particle of dust at Krishna's lotus feet; and (3) mantrārtha-cintana, Jīva Gosvāmī's instruction to reflect on the meaning of the mantra while chanting. The practical core is staying completely present with each name — be with 'Hare' when chanting Hare, with 'Krishna' when chanting Krishna — pronouncing distinctly, without rushing, in a mood of affectionate respect. Mahārāja contrasts saṅkīrtana, the most effective and easiest process in the age of Kali because the collective sound carries everyone, with the harder solitary japa that nonetheless forms a major part of one's sādhana, urging devotees to bring the same principles into individual chanting. He clarifies that the meaning-meditations are aids, not requirements: use them if they help, or simply concentrate on the sound. Finally he diagnoses chronic distraction — being only half-present — as the chief obstacle to the deeper levels of the Name that await us, and stresses building a conducive, undistracted atmosphere, meeting people at their level (children given suitable activity rather than forced into mature practice) so that this protected space yields its evident benefit.

## Practical application

Sit for your daily 16 rounds having first arranged the environment so you can stay undistracted — settle children and other responsibilities elsewhere, since protecting the atmosphere is part of the practice. Optionally use a card (mantra-trāṭaka) to hold your focus, letting your attention move along each name as the eyes move along the lines of an article. Chant every name distinctly and do not accelerate; racing defeats the purpose. Stay fully present with each name — when you are on 'Hare' be with Hare, when on 'Krishna' be with Krishna. Carry a threefold awareness as it helps: remember that the Name is non-different from Krishna, so chanting touches Him directly and He is 'just a turn away'; chant in humility and longing, thinking less of yourself and more of Him, returning from time to time to the prayer of Śikṣāṣṭakam verse five — 'I am Your eternal servant, fallen in the ocean of this world; kindly fix me as a particle of dust at Your lotus feet'; and engage in mantrārtha-cintana, reflecting on the meaning of the mantra, with the inner stance, 'You are identical with this name; I do not fully realize it yet, but I have faith and chant with affectionate respect.' Treat these meaning-meditations as aids, not obligations — if they scatter you, simply concentrate on the pure sound; both are valid. When chanting alone feels harder than congregational singing, deliberately import the principles that carry you in saṅkīrtana into your solo japa. Above all, work against chronic half-presence: the single discipline of staying fully here, name after name, is what opens the next level of the Holy Name.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name of the Lord (Hari/Krishna); the practice of chanting it.
- **japa** — Soft, individual chanting of the mantra, typically counted on beads; harder than congregational chanting but a major part of one's daily sādhana.
- **saṅkīrtana** — Congregational chanting in which all sing together in one voice; described as the most effective process in the age of Kali because the collective sound vibration carries everyone.
- **sādhana** — Regulated daily spiritual practice; here, the daily rounds of chanting.
- **mantra-trāṭaka** — Concentration practice of focusing on the mantra with the help of a card, reading and staying with every name and syllable to remain present-minded.
- **mantrārtha-cintana** — Reflecting on the meaning of the mantra while chanting (cintana = to think, artha = meaning, mantra = the mantra); an instruction of Jīva Gosvāmī that deepens one's connection with the Name.
- **mantra-cintana** — Reflecting on or meditating within the mantra during chanting, e.g., as the prayer of the fallen soul asking to be accepted again in the Lord's service.
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka(m)** — The eight verses (aṣṭaka) of instruction (śikṣā) by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu; verse five expresses the humble longing of the eternal servant fallen in the ocean of this world, praying to be fixed as a particle of dust at Krishna's lotus feet.
- **Janmāṣṭamī** — The annual celebration of Krishna's appearance day; referenced via Prabhupāda's Hamburg lecture on touching Krishna with the tongue through the Name.
- **Kali (age of Kali / Kali-yuga)** — The present age of quarrel and degradation, in which congregational chanting of the Holy Name is the recommended and most effective spiritual process.
- **Jīva Gosvāmī** — One of the six Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana; cited here for the instruction to think on the meaning of the mantra while chanting.
- **Prabhupāda** — Śrīla A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda; his Hamburg Janmāṣṭamī lecture is recalled for the teaching that in chanting one touches Krishna with the tongue and 'Krishna is just a turn away.'
