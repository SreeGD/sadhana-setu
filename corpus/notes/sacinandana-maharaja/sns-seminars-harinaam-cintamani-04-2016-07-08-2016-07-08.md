# SNS Seminars Harinaam Cintamani 04 2016 07 08

## Theme

The lecture, drawn from the Harināma Cintāmaṇi tradition, addresses the central obstacle to tasteful chanting: false ego. Sacīnandana Mahārāja establishes that anyone who wishes to chant with taste must reckon with the ego, a constant disturbing companion on the path. A person absorbed in 'I and mine' is 'worthless like a pile of ashes' — just as fire placed in ashes is extinguished, the light of Kṛṣṇa consciousness cannot take root in one governed by false ego. Through vivid images (the groomed poodle whose fine appearance conceals a low nature) and stories (the sculptor who carved statues of himself to deceive Yamarāja, yet was noosed and dragged off through flattery and craving for praise), he shows how death and Māyā catch a person precisely through false ego and bodily identification. Using the Hitopadeśa parable of the tortoise carried by two cranes — the tortoise being ourselves in the drying lake of our lifetime, the birds being guru and śāstra — he warns that reacting to slander and insult from false ego (the tortoise fatally 'opening its mouth') brings our downfall, while reacting in Krishna's service saves us. Haridāsa Ṭhākura, the Nāmācārya, and the scripture Harināma Cintāmaṇi are themselves these 'two birds,' warning that hearing the glories of the holy name while clinging to the materialistic concept of life is a grave offense against the name. The remedy is buddhi-śuddhi — the conscious purification of realizing one is an eternal soul, not the body — which Scripture (Hari-bhakti-vilāsa) calls the precondition without which yajñas, homas, and japa are fruitless. Recounting his last meeting with Śrīla Prabhupāda, Mahārāja distills the essence of kṛṣṇa-upadeśa: 'I am not the body, I am the eternal soul,' a realization that becomes a lifeline through life's hardest trials since the one certainty is that we must die. He then leads a practical, guided method of chanting: settling body and mind, affirming one's eternal identity as Krishna's servant, entering through visualization of the sacred Yamunā, taking an inner dip so material identification floats away like silt, and singing from the recovered spiritual self — without 'aham' and 'mama,' in the posture of the small, joyful servant, or by dropping from the head into the heart-space where the soul and the Lord reside. The lecture closes in the Vaiṣṇava katha tradition with a story meant to move the heart 'to laugh and to cry' — a rock that refuses to become human for fear its heart would harden, teaching that it is the ego, not the heart, that turns stony. Firmness in the world is sometimes needed, but always from the servant's position, never from ego. Finally, proper chanting means working against long-entrenched habits as muscle grows against resistance; simply staying with the Holy Name — boarding its 'boat' — lets real Krishna consciousness arise by the power of the Name itself.

## Practical application

Before sitting for japa, deliberately step off the platform of bodily identification. When someone has offended you, notice the tendency to chant the offender's name in your mind instead of Krishna's, and consciously stop identifying with the body — at least during chanting — remembering, 'I am not this body; I am an eternal part and parcel of Krishna, the servant of the servant of the servants.' Recite the pre-chanting meditation on your true identity: by nature the eternal servant of Kṛṣṇa, fallen since time immemorial into a gross and subtle body and the threefold miseries, now by the mercy of the spiritual master restored to knowledge of yourself as His infinitesimal eternal servant, ordered to serve — and chant from that settled position. Prepare the body and mind: sit tall, let go of the day's activities and the lingering 'echo' of conversations, breathe deeply and exhale tension and restlessness. Then enter chanting through visualization — picture the calm, sacred Yamunā (or any place where you become peaceful), take an inner dip so your material identification floats away like black silt, and rise with only your spiritual identity intact. Sing 'I am the eternal servant of Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa' again and again from that level, without 'I and mine' and without even a spiritual ego, in the posture of the small, happy servant; or leave the head and drop into the heart-space where the soul and the Lord reside and sing directly to Him. Concentrate on the divine sound vibration, letting the Holy Name flow like a river into the heart and using the mind to enter it — beginning concretely by focusing on the first 'Hari' of the mantra. Follow Haridāsa Ṭhākura's instruction: regard Krishna as your only wealth, surrender everything to Him, worship His feet, turn from sense gratification, and chant. Expect that improving your chanting means pushing against strong, long-entrenched habits — like building muscle against resistance — so exercise discipline. When you hear an instruction that works for you, put it into practice immediately rather than deferring it. And when firmness is required in life, act from the position of a servant, never from ego. Trust that simply staying with the Name and 'boarding its boat' allows real Krishna consciousness to arise by the power of the Name itself.

## Glossary

- **false ego (ahaṅkāra)** — The material misidentification of the self with the body and mind, expressed as 'I and mine'; the constant disturbing companion and the great obstacle to tasteful chanting, through which death and Māyā 'catch' a person.
- **aham and mama** — 'I' and 'mine' — the twin roots of material identification (bodily rights and considerations); true chanting is done without them.
- **buddhi-śuddhi** — Purification of intelligence/consciousness — removing the illusion of being the body and mind and coming to the platform of the soul; the precondition without which yajña, homa, and japa are fruitless.
- **japa** — The practice of chanting the holy name, typically on beads; the personal, meditative form of Hari-Nāma addressed throughout the lecture.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational or guided singing of the holy name; here the setting into which the affirmation of soul-identity flows and where Krishna consciousness is tangibly experienced.
- **Hari-Nāma / Holy Name** — The transcendental name of Krishna, non-different from Him; staying with it ('boarding its boat') brings about real Krishna consciousness by its own power.
- **Nāmācārya** — 'The teacher-authority of the holy name' — the title of Haridāsa Ṭhākura, exemplar and guide in the practice and glorification of chanting.
- **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** — The Nāmācārya, whose instructions on chanting (surrender to Krishna as one's only wealth, worship of His feet, turning from sense gratification) frame the lecture's method.
- **Harināma Cintāmaṇi** — The scripture presenting Haridāsa Ṭhākura's teachings on the holy name and its offenses; together with guru it forms one of the 'two birds' of guru and śāstra.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture; together with the guru it provides the rules that carry the practitioner toward the kingdom of God, as the two cranes carry the tortoise.
- **guru** — The spiritual master; by whose mercy one comes to know oneself as Krishna's eternal servant, and one of the 'two birds' bearing the soul across the lake of life.
- **kṛṣṇa-upadeśa** — The instruction of/about Krishna that one is meant to give when preaching; its essence is to understand that life is eternal — 'I am the soul, not the temporary body.'
- **Yamarāja** — The lord of death who administers the results of one's actions; in the sculptor's story he catches the ego-bound soul through its flattery and denial of death.
- **Māyā** — The Lord's illusory material energy that binds the soul through identification with its roles and false ego, ensuring its downfall unless it takes shelter of guru and śāstra.
- **yajña** — Vedic sacrifice; along with homa and japa, declared fruitless when performed without buddhi-śuddhi — like ashes the fire cannot burn.
- **homa** — A fire sacrifice or oblation; grouped with yajña and japa as ineffective without the realization that one is the eternal soul.
- **Hari-bhakti-vilāsa** — The Vaiṣṇava scripture on devotional conduct cited as warning that ritual acts without buddhi-śuddhi bear no fruit.
- **Hitopadeśa** — A classical collection of instructive fables, source of the tortoise-and-cranes parable used to depict the soul carried by guru and śāstra across the lake of life.
- **sādhana** — Regulated spiritual practice; here defined as deconstructing all false coverings and filling the God-shaped emptiness in the heart with Krishna rather than sense (ego) gratification.
- **Yamunā** — The sacred river of Krishna's holy land; the object of pre-chanting visualization and inner bathing by which material identification is washed away like silt.
- **Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa** — The Divine Couple; the recovered spiritual identity is expressed as being 'the eternal servant of Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa.'
- **katha** — Transcendental narration/storytelling; the Vaiṣṇava tradition of transmitting instruction through story and emotion, 'to laugh and to cry,' exemplified in the lecture's closing.
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee of Vishnu/Krishna, or pertaining to that tradition; here characterizing the katha method of teaching through narrative and feeling.
- **threefold miseries** — The three classes of material suffering (from one's own body/mind, from other living beings, and from nature/providence) endured by the soul wandering in the cycle of birth and death.
- **gross and subtle body** — The physical body (gross) and the mind, intelligence, and false ego (subtle) with which the soul misidentifies; realizing one is separate from both is the platform of chanting.
