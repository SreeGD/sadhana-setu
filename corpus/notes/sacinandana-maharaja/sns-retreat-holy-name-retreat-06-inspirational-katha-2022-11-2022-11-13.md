# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 06 Inspirational Katha 2022 11 13

## Theme

Drawing on the Uddhava Gītā, Sacīnandana Mahārāja opens by recalling the ways Kṛṣṇa tells Uddhava that devotees grow mindful of him — studying the śāstras, associating with devotees, beautifully chanting his names and glories, and remembering him while reminding one another of him. He dwells on this last category: remembrance is meant to be shared, and a gathering that chants and remembers together reaps a benefit far beyond private practice. But such collective remembrance demands undivided attention, so he urges the assembly to fast from distractions (phone and WhatsApp addiction, wandering attention) and to help one another stay focused, lest a rare opportunity be squandered. He then demonstrates remembrance as līlā-smaraṇam, guiding a meditation on Kṛṣṇa roaming the valley of Govardhan on an autumn-moon night with a longing heart — mistaking a bee's hum for Rādhā's anklets, a stirring blade of grass for her arrival, and the moon for her face, so that loving absorption turns every sense impression toward the beloved. The pastime's heart is the mutual indwelling of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa: each so fully dwells in the other that Kṛṣṇa, unable to locate himself, offers himself at Rādhā's lotus feet, saying only she can tell him where he belongs. A sakhī's question — whether Kṛṣṇa resides in the kuñja of Rādhā's eyes or of her heart — establishes that though he is the gallant hero of Vṛndāvana, only Rādhā is the adept knower of the mellows of rasa and the supreme reservoir of bhāva. Mahārāja closes by turning meditation into method: devotees should 'make photos in the heart,' consciously absorbing the beautiful darśana so it becomes lasting inner memory; chant prayerfully, bringing before Kṛṣṇa the things in their lives they wish would disappear; and pray during chanting that their spiritual path be made clear and its obstacles removed.

## Practical application

Treat japa and Hari-Nāma not as solitary mechanical recitation but as shared, attentive remembrance. Before chanting, consciously fast from distractions — set down the phone, quiet WhatsApp and a wandering mind — and, in a group, gently help your neighbors stay focused so everyone leaves enriched. While chanting, practice līlā-smaraṇam: let the Holy Name carry the mind into Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, as Mahārāja modeled with the Govardhan autumn-moon meditation, allowing loving absorption to draw every impression toward the Lord. 'Make photos in the heart' — when you receive a beautiful darśana or arrangement of the deities, deliberately absorb it so it remains an inner memory you can return to during chanting, training real smaraṇa rather than passing sense-impression. Chant prayerfully: bring before Kṛṣṇa the things in your life you long to see disappear, and pray for his mercy to clear your spiritual path and remove its obstacles and obscurities — making each round of the Name a heartfelt prayer rather than rote repetition.

## Glossary

- **Uddhava Gītā** — Kṛṣṇa's final teaching to his devotee Uddhava (in the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam), here cited for the ways devotees become mindful of the Lord.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture; sacred texts whose study is one means of becoming mindful of Kṛṣṇa.
- **līlā-smaraṇam** — Guided remembrance and meditation on Kṛṣṇa's divine pastimes (līlā), used here as the method of deep remembrance.
- **smaraṇa** — Remembrance of Kṛṣṇa; the conscious inner recollection trained by 'making photos in the heart' rather than fleeting sense-impression.
- **Rādhā / Rādhārāṇī** — Kṛṣṇa's foremost beloved; the supreme reservoir of devotional emotion and the one adept in the mellows of love.
- **Govardhan** — The sacred hill and surrounding valley near Vṛndāvana where Kṛṣṇa's autumn-moon pastime in the lecture unfolds.
- **sakhī** — A cowherd girlfriend and intimate companion of Rādhā, here the one who poses the question of where Kṛṣṇa truly resides.
- **kuñja** — A grove or bower; used figuratively for the 'grove' of Rādhā's eyes and the 'grove' of her heart as Kṛṣṇa's dwelling.
- **Vṛndāvana** — The forest land of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, of which he is described as the gallant hero.
- **rasa** — The mellows or relishable flavors of loving exchange with Kṛṣṇa, of which Rādhā is the supreme knower.
- **bhāva** — Devotional emotion or loving sentiment; Rādhā is established as its supreme reservoir.
- **darśana** — Sacred sight or audience — beholding the deity or holy arrangement, to be absorbed into lasting inner memory.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name of the Lord; its chanting is the devotional practice the teaching applies to.
