# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 01 2014 09 18 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

This opening lecture of HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja's Holy-Name Retreat at New Govardhana Village frames the entire retreat as a deliberate, concentrated effort to learn how to chant the holy name properly so that the soul is actually carried into Kṛṣṇa's presence. Mahārāja begins by distinguishing physical arrival from real arrival: having brought the body, the participant must now bring the mind and heart 'to the top' and actively gather them, since spiritual practice is not automatic — even congregational chanting only succeeds when each person concentrates, the collective sound itself mirroring the collective state of mind. He sets the retreat apart from a festival: a festival is for darśana, association, and encouragement, whereas a retreat puts the chanting of the holy name first and everything else second, demanding undistracted attention. The undertaking is formally inaugurated with a brief adhivāsa to invite Kṛṣṇa, and its central diagnosis is given through the Upaniṣadic and Bhāgavata image of the mantra as a bow, the soul as the arrow, and Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa as the target: many devotees have chanted for years yet privately feel they have not reached the Lord — not because the process fails, but because, like an untrained archer, they have not learned to apply it. Learning that application is the purpose of the retreat, which belongs to the practitioner, who is rewarded in proportion to the focus and surrender invested, as a digger is rewarded with gold. Mahārāja models the whole endeavor in a mood of service rather than performance, professing that he travelled only to serve and enliven the Vaiṣṇavas and begging their blessings for the qualification to do so. He grounds the teaching in Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's Harināma Cintāmaṇi — a work placed by Lord Caitanya directly into Haridāsa Ṭhākura's heart and framed as Mahāprabhu drawing the secret tips of nāma-tattva out of His empowered devotee — and in the lineage of the Nāmācārya Haridāsa Ṭhākura and of Bhaktivinoda himself, who at life's end set aside every worldly and devotional accomplishment to do nothing but chant in his Bhakti Kuṭīr beside Haridāsa's samādhi. From this lineage he derives the three best guidelines for going deep: humility (coming to the reality of being a fallen, separated soul, yet without discouragement), prayer (recognizing that success depends wholly on Kṛṣṇa's mercy), and a service attitude (since the Name reveals himself only to one who serves through kīrtana and japa, never to one who demands taste). The lecture culminates in the practice of saṅkalpa — an inner resolve that, like a marathon runner's vow or the pilgrim whose mind was 'already in Badrinath,' carries the practitioner through weakness when willpower fails. Vaiṣṇava culture already runs on such resolves in the form of vratas. Mahārāja asks each participant to clarify a single searching question — their innermost desire for their relationship with the holy name — and to voice that resolve aloud before the Deity while offering a light, placing the vow in the Lord's hands so that it is sustained by His strength rather than one's own.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this teaching by treating each japa or kīrtana session the way Mahārāja treats the retreat: not just showing up, but consciously bringing the mind and heart 'to the top' before beginning, so that practice is entered actively rather than assumed. Put chanting first and everything else second for the duration of the session — set the phone aside and give undistracted attention to the holy name as the central work of purifying consciousness. Approach the Name not as an untrained archer but as a learner: honestly assess where the relationship stands now (struggling with rounds, or relating to the Name like a 'distant relative'), and accept that the fruit comes in proportion to the focus and surrender invested. Cultivate the three best supports — humility (sitting as a fallen, separated soul who nonetheless has an ocean of opportunity ahead), prayer (asking Kṛṣṇa to take one deeper into the Name, since progress depends on His mercy), and a service attitude (serving the Name by wholeheartedly entering kīrtana and japa rather than demanding taste). Make a realistic saṅkalpa and renew it daily: reconnect with the vow made at initiation to chant sixteen rounds, and form a simple standing resolve such as 'I will serve the devotees and not be irritated.' Vow only what can be kept, since breaking a serious vow damages something within. Crucially, voice the resolve aloud — pronounce the innermost desire for one's chanting before the Deity while offering a light, placing it in the Lord's hands so it is protected and sustained by His power. The aim throughout is to fall in love with the holy name and chant with increasing faith, devotion, and love — taking the Name deep into the heart and letting it steady and ground a frantic life — rather than merely completing rounds.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma / Holy Name** — The names of God (Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Rāma); chanting them is the principal spiritual process of this age for bringing the soul into the Lord's presence.
- **japa** — Personal, often soft, repetition of the holy name, typically counted on beads; one of the two direct ways of serving the Name.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational singing of the holy name; here presented not as a lesser activity but as the main thing — the direct service of the Holy Name.
- **adhivāsa** — A ceremony that inaugurates a significant spiritual undertaking by formally inviting the Lord and seeking the deities' blessings, performed in brief here to invite Kṛṣṇa into the retreat.
- **saṅkalpa** — A sacred inner resolve or orientation toward a goal that becomes a friend carrying the practitioner through difficulty when human weakness sets in.
- **vrata** — A vow or observance; the established form saṅkalpa already takes in Vaiṣṇava culture (e.g., the Kārtika vrata, the daily sixteen-round vow, the Janmāṣṭamī fast).
- **darśana** — The auspicious sight of and audience with the Lord (or His Deity); given by Mahārāja as a primary purpose of a festival, as distinct from a retreat.
- **mantra** — Sacred sound; in the bow analogy of the Upaniṣads and Bhāgavatam, the mantra is the bow that propels the soul (the arrow) toward the Lord (the target).
- **Upaniṣads** — Vedic philosophical scriptures; source, with the Bhāgavatam, of the bow-arrow-target image of the mantra.
- **Bhāgavatam (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam)** — The principal Vaiṣṇava scripture on devotion to Kṛṣṇa, cited alongside the Upaniṣads for the bow analogy of chanting.
- **Harināma Cintāmaṇi** — Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's work on the science of the holy name, recording the conversation Lord Caitanya placed into Haridāsa Ṭhākura's heart; the text studied during the retreat.
- **nāma-tattva** — The science or truth of the holy name; the realized śāstric tradition into which the Harināma Cintāmaṇi and the Nāmācārya's lineage initiate the practitioner.
- **Nāmācārya** — The 'teacher (ācārya) of the holy name' — the title of Haridāsa Ṭhākura, the model chanter who chanted from morning till night.
- **samādhi** — The tomb-shrine of a great devotee; here Haridāsa Ṭhākura's samādhi on the Jagannath Puri seashore, beside which Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura later chanted.
- **Bhakti Kuṭīr** — The 'cottage of devotion' beside Haridāsa Ṭhākura's samādhi where Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura, at life's end, set aside all accomplishments to do nothing but chant.
- **kṛṣṇa-prema** — Pure love for Kṛṣṇa; the heart of the true religion meant for this age, taught by Mahāprabhu at Kāśī Miśra's house in Jagannātha Purī.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present degraded age, for which the essence of dharma is congregational chanting of the holy name and the cultivation of pure love.
- **dharma** — Religion or sacred duty; its essence for this age is identified as congregational chanting of the holy name.
- **Mahāprabhu (Lord Caitanya / Śrī Caitanya)** — The incarnation who taught love for Kṛṣṇa by empowering His liberated devotees through inquiry; questioner of Haridāsa Ṭhākura in the Harināma Cintāmaṇi.
- **Siddha-bakula** — The 'perfected' tree that grew instantly from a lifeless toothpick planted by Mahāprabhu to shade Haridāsa and bled when struck — an emblem of the holy name's power to spiritualize even dull matter.
- **siddha** — 'Perfected'; describing the Siddha-bakula as a liberated soul rather than dull matter.
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa; the community Mahārāja seeks to serve and whose blessings he begs as the qualification to serve.
- **dandavat-parikrama** — Circumambulation performed by repeated full prostrations; Mahārāja's example of a daily Govardhana parikrama completed even with typhoid through the strength of a strong saṅkalpa.
