# 15 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 05 01 Morning Lecture ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

In this Day-05 morning lecture on the Harinām Cintāmaṇi at ISKCON Radhadesh, Sacīnandana Mahārāja frames chanting as a journey. Extra kirtan is offered so the Holy Name begins to reverberate continuously in the consciousness — an unstoppable internal kirtan that plays even when one is alone. The Holy Name is Kṛṣṇa's most merciful manifestation, descending to save conditioned souls, and it is identical with Kṛṣṇa Himself: like the messenger who turns out to be the prince in disguise, the Name comes in a manageable form yet is the very Lord of Vṛndāvana come to bring the soul home. This journey passes through 'stations' — nāma-aparādha (chanting thick with offenses), nāma-ābhāsa (a dry, disheartening clearing stage where the Name feels like a burden and many give up), and śuddha-nāma (the pure Name fully manifest). The Name is like the sun always present but hidden by the clouds of the ten offenses; our task is to remove them. The heart of the lecture dwells on the gravest offense — vaiṣṇava-aparādha, offense to a devotee. Where other offenses merely damage the bhakti-latā (creeper of devotion), this one, like a mad elephant, uproots it entirely so its leaves dry up; the same uprooting begins the moment one abandons the regulative principles or disobeys the guru (guru-avajñā). It is so weighty that even Haridāsa Ṭhākura trembled to speak of it. Its devastating power comes from Kṛṣṇa's loving partiality: devotees dwell in His heart and are dear to Him as a beloved child to a doting father, so wounding a devotee wounds the Lord, who personally takes up the case — no number of Ganga baths or extra rounds can casually wash it away. Mahārāja draws on the Ambarīṣa–Durvāsā episode, a Garga Saṁhitā curse-story (Kāliya and Bhuśuṇḍi), and Rūpa Gosvāmī to show both the Lord's fierce protectiveness and His corrective mercy toward erring but dear devotees. The remedy is a culture of honoring Vaiṣṇavas: excusing offenses against oneself, remaining in the society of devotees as 'servant of the servant,' and applying practical trainings — Bhaktisiddhānta's method of naming something good about one's adversary, the 1%/99% principle of focusing on the vast good rather than the small fault, and abandoning the expectation that others conform to our image of them. Following Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura, he distinguishes genuine vaiṣṇava-aparādha (dredging up a devotee's past, already-diminishing, or accidental faults) from permissible, benevolent correction by mature devotees. Criticizing a Vaiṣṇava blocks the full manifestation of the Holy Name in the heart; the safe stance is to beg forgiveness, never look for faults, and sing the devotees' glories.

## Practical application

Chant more, and chant so the Name keeps sounding inside you even in private — aim at a kirtan that does not stop. Recognize which 'station' you are at, and if the chanting feels dry, burdensome, and empty (nāma-ābhāsa), persevere rather than abandon it; this stage is where many quit, and passing through it is essential. Treat the ten offenses as the clouds to be removed, watching especially for inattention, the root of all offenses. Above all, guard against vaiṣṇava-aparādha: never look for a devotee's fault, never broadcast it, and never resurrect their past, already-fading, or accidental failings. When you notice the one trait that irritates you, remember the 1%/99% principle — that fault is a fraction of a person who chants the same mantras, holds the same beliefs, eats only prasādam, and is fighting his own weaknesses. Deliberately train the mind to name something good about anyone you are in conflict with, as Bhaktisiddhānta Ṭhākura required of disputing devotees. Drop the expectation that others conform to your image of them, since that expectation is the chief cause of failure in relationships. Stay in the society of Vaiṣṇavas as 'the servant of the servant,' honor others genuinely as 'Prabhu' rather than as lip service, and follow the guru's instructions and regulative principles so the creeper of devotion is not uprooted. Be a diligent, non-lazy student of the Name — follow the guru's cues to specific texts, seek out books, and approach the subject from every angle. To cure a specific offense: study the text on that aparādha, understand it, find the prescribed remedy, and personalize it into a concrete plan for your own life. And if you do offend, beg forgiveness heart and soul and take shelter of the devotee's lotus feet.

## Glossary

- **Harinām Cintāmaṇi** — The Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava text on the Holy Name being studied in the seminar, presenting Caitanya Mahāprabhu's teaching on chanting and its offenses.
- **kirtan** — Congregational or personal singing/chanting of the Holy Names of Kṛṣṇa.
- **Holy Name (nāma)** — The name of Kṛṣṇa, described as His most merciful manifestation descending to save conditioned souls, and non-different from Kṛṣṇa Himself.
- **japa** — Personal, meditative chanting of the Holy Name, typically counted on beads in daily rounds.
- **nāma-aparādha** — The offensive stage of chanting, in which the soul is so materially covered that many offenses are committed against the Name.
- **nāma-ābhāsa** — The 'clearing' or reflective stage — a shadow of the pure Name where one begins to like the Name but cannot yet recognize it as it is; often dry and disheartening.
- **śuddha-nāma** — The pure Holy Name fully manifest, the 'sun' of the Name shining once the clouds of offense are removed.
- **aparādha** — Offense; specifically the ten offenses that cover and obstruct the manifestation of the Holy Name.
- **vaiṣṇava-aparādha** — Offense against a devotee — the gravest offense, which uproots the creeper of devotion entirely because devotees are supremely dear to Kṛṣṇa.
- **bhakti-latā** — The creeper (vine) of devotion cultivated in the heart, which a mad elephant — offense or disobedience — can uproot and dry up.
- **guru-avajñā** — Disregard or disobedience of the spiritual master's instructions, which acts like a mad elephant uprooting the creeper of devotion.
- **Ācārya** — A spiritual teacher who teaches by example; the serious devotee aims to satisfy the previous Ācāryas as their servant's servant.
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa; to be honored, never criticized, and whose glories should be sung.
- **saṅga** — Association or society, especially the society of Vaiṣṇavas in which a devotee should remain.
- **ananya-bhakti** — Exclusive, undivided devotion; even a devotee who errs but is fixed in ananya-bhakti is to be regarded as saintly.
- **prasādam** — Sanctified vegetarian food offered to Kṛṣṇa, which devotees eat exclusively.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present age of quarrel and hypocrisy, in which people habitually fixate on small faults rather than the good in others.
- **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** — The great chanter of the Holy Name who trembled and shrank from speaking of vaiṣṇava-aparādha when Lord Caitanya asked, showing its gravity.
- **Ambarīṣa–Durvāsā episode** — The scriptural account in which Kṛṣṇa sent the offending sage Durvāsā back to the devotee-king Ambarīṣa for shelter, showing the Lord stands on the side of His devotees.
- **Garga Saṁhitā** — A scripture cited for the story of two dear devotees whose mutual curses (to become a snake and a crow) came true yet who the Lord personally purified as Kāliya and Bhuśuṇḍi.
- **Kāliya** — The serpent on whose heads Kṛṣṇa danced; in the Garga Saṁhitā account, a cursed but dear devotee whom the Lord purified personally.
- **Bhuśuṇḍi** — The devotee, in the same account, who eternally recites the Rāmāyaṇa — a cursed but dear soul purified by the Lord.
- **Rūpa Gosvāmī** — A highly elevated associate of Mahāprabhu whom the Lord used to teach everyone the danger of vaiṣṇava-aparādha; his conduct illustrates that none can fully understand a pure Vaiṣṇava.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — The Gauḍīya teacher who set the criteria distinguishing true vaiṣṇava-aparādha (dredging up past, diminishing, or accidental faults) from benevolent, auspicious correction.
- **Bhaktisiddhānta Saraswatī Ṭhākura** — The Ācārya whose method for resolving quarrels required each disputant to name something good about the other, training the mind to seek the good.
- **Prabhupāda** — The Ācārya who taught that addressing fellow devotees as 'Prabhu' (master) should be genuinely realized, never mere lip service.
- **Prabhu** — Literally 'master' — the respectful address devotees use for one another, regarding others as master and never oneself as their master.
- **Ādi-līlā** — The first division of the Caitanya-caritāmṛta; its 8th chapter is referenced as a source to study on the Holy Name.
- **Ganga** — The sacred Ganges river; the lecture notes that even millions of baths in it cannot deliver one who criticizes a devotee.
