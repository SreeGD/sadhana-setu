# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 06 Workshop 02 2023 08 18 Slovenia

## Theme

This workshop centers on a single distinction: chanting that is 'heart-deep' versus chanting that remains 'lip-deep.' Sacīnandana Mahārāja argues that the holy name reaches the heart only when one quality is present — the awareness that Krishna is personally present in His name, so that to chant is to associate directly with Krishna the person, not to perform a meditation technique or a group song for a pleasant collective feeling. From this premise the lecture develops chanting as the renewal of a relationship: the soul's eternal bond with Krishna was broken by us, which is the very reason we are embodied in this world, and chanting is the rebellious soul turning back — shyly knocking at Krishna's door, calling 'Here I am again, please accept me in Your service.' Mahārāja insists on honesty about one's real motive for kirtan (singing merely because others do, or to be recognized), and models the needed humility through his own ten-year estrangement from his parents, which required begging forgiveness from the heart before the relationship could slowly redevelop — just as the soul must ask Krishna's forgiveness for having abandoned Him across thousands of lifetimes. He shows that the mahā-mantra, though composed only of holy names, is rightly glossed by Prabhupāda as 'please engage me in Your service,' and that the ācāryas reveal many further personal moods within the name. He frames spiritual identity as more fundamental than bodily or mental condition — illustrated by Prabhupāda's treatment of a devotee with mental illness ('I know her, she is a devotee'; the mind is part of the body, and a broken mind, like a broken limb, can be treated without disqualifying the soul) — and teaches dependence on Krishna through the fluctuating health of the body. The workshop then becomes practical, guiding a seated meditation: settle and relax the body while staying alert, draw breath and attention into the heart, and internalize the foundational self-truth — I am an atomic spiritual part and eternal servant of Krishna, distinct from the body as a dweller from a house. Mahārāja folds in Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's three questions (Who am I? To whom do I relate? Where does this relationship take place?) and a parallel three-step meditation (reconnect with the real self, turn the mind to Krishna, affirm that chanting is the best thing one could do), then has participants chant 'connected' japa and kīrtana sustained in one continuous awareness, and share their realization with a neighbor in saṅga. In its final movement the lecture corrects a misunderstanding about suffering: while misery has a teaching function — at the end of his rope a soul may turn to God — suffering is not the devotee's true shelter and does not uproot wrong tendencies from the heart. The real refuge (śaraṇa) is positive knowledge of Krishna, accumulated by regularly reading the Bhagavad-gītā and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, which cures the two weaknesses of the heart (turning away from Krishna, and the material attachment that follows). Finally, Mahārāja gives Lord Caitanya's mood for Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas: one cannot catch Krishna — even the gopīs find it hard — one can only run after Him. The qualification is laulyam, the eager crying out of one who admits he has no qualification; serve without discouragement and without expecting anything in return, offering the heart as fully as possible until Krishna chooses to come, for the very opportunity to serve is itself the devotee's satisfaction.

## Practical application

Before chanting, pause and honestly examine your real motive — are you chanting because everyone does, to be recognized, or to actually call out to Krishna? Begin by settling the body: sit tall with an erect spine, breathe deeply with full awareness of each inhale and exhale, and progressively relax every region from the feet to the crown until the body is fully relaxed yet incredibly alert and awake. Draw the breath and attention into the heart, and there internalize the foundational truth: 'I am an atomic spiritual part of Krishna and His eternal servant — a tiny, eternal, blissful soul living in this body as a person lives in a house, looking out through the eyes as through windows.' Reorient your relationships: turn to Krishna as your only true master and friend, who was with you before birth, is with you now, and will not abandon you even at death. Then chant a round of japa held in one deliberate awareness — 'I am Krishna's servant; Krishna is my only true friend, and now I am turning to Him' — addressing Krishna directly as a living person and praying with the name itself: 'Please accept me, please engage me in Your service.' Treat each name not as mechanical recitation but as a personal appeal of surrender and, where the ācāryas reveal it, other heartfelt moods (calling Him as friend, asking Him to steal your heart, to purify you through His devotees, to show you how to serve). Use Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's three questions to enter real 'Krishna kirtan,' not 'me kirtan': Who am I, really? To whom am I calling? Where does this relationship take place? — and stay inside that subtle inner space, sustaining the same consciousness continuously through both japa and the kīrtana that follows. Sing TO Krishna, not merely in His presence. Do not wait for suffering to drive you to Him; instead actively accumulate positive knowledge of Krishna by daily reading the Bhagavad-gītā and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, which gradually reshapes the heart and cures both its turning away from Krishna and its material attachment. If illness — even mental illness — afflicts you, accept treatment as you would for a broken limb while remaining fixed in your spiritual identity; depend on Krishna through every fluctuation of health as you tolerate the seasons. Above all, cultivate laulyam: cry out, pray, and admit you have no qualification, for that genuine eagerness is itself the qualification. Run after Krishna through enthusiastic service under the spiritual master's direction, without discouragement and without expecting anything in return — and reflect on your realizations afterward in saṅga, recounting your experience of connected chanting to a fellow devotee.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of Krishna (Hari); chanting the name, in which Krishna is understood to be personally present.
- **japa** — Personal, often quiet repetition of the holy name, here counted in 'rounds' and meant to be done as a conscious act of service rather than mechanical recitation.
- **kīrtana (kirtan)** — Chanting or singing of the holy name; in this lecture, ideally a relational act of singing directly to Krishna rather than a group song for a collective feeling.
- **mahā-mantra** — The great chant composed only of the holy names (Hare Krishna…), which Prabhupāda glossed as the prayer 'please engage me in Your service.'
- **ācāryas** — Authoritative teachers by example in the tradition, who reveal the various personal moods hidden within the holy name.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture; a source, together with respected devotees, for understanding the world as a place to re-establish one's relationship with Krishna.
- **Bhagavad-gītā** — Scripture in which Krishna directly instructs the living being; recommended daily reading for accumulating positive knowledge of Krishna.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** — Scripture recommended for regular reading, whose positive knowledge gradually purifies and reshapes the devotee's thought.
- **śaraṇa** — Shelter or refuge; the devotee's true refuge is not suffering but positive knowledge of Krishna, which overcomes the weaknesses of the heart.
- **laulyam** — Intense, eager crying out and praying for Krishna while admitting one has no qualification — an eagerness that is itself the qualification to attain Him.
- **gopīs** — Krishna's cowherd-girl devotees of Vṛndāvana; cited as those for whom even it is hard to 'catch' Krishna.
- **Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas** — Followers of Lord Caitanya's line, given the mood of worshiping Krishna by chasing after Him rather than expecting to catch Him.
- **saṅga** — The association of devotees; the setting in which personal realization in the holy name is meant to be reflected on and shared.
