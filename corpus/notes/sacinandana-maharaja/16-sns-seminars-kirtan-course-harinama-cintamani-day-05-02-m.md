# 16 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 05 02 Morning Session ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This morning session at ISKCON Radhadesh is the culminating study of the ten offenses to the Holy Name (nāma-aparādha) from Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's Harinām Cintāmaṇi, framed less as a list of prohibitions than as a map of how māyā traps a chanter. Mahārāja opens with congregational kīrtana to unite the assembly and settle the mind, and stresses that collaborative, peer-led reflection reveals angles one's own mind cannot. He then locates the root of all offenses in becoming bahya-mukha (outward-facing) and identifying with the ego (ahaṅkāra); the whole antidote is to become antar-mukha, finding Kṛṣṇa seated in the heart, since inattentive chanting is itself the seedbed of every other offense. Working through the offenses — equating the Name with material pious activity (śubha-karma), interpreting or exaggerating the Name, criticizing the authorized (Āmnāya) scriptures, criticizing devotees (sādhu-nindā, treated as the gravest), giving the Name to the faithless, worshipping demigods as independent of Kṛṣṇa, deliberately sinning on the strength of the Name, and attachment to 'me and mine' — Mahārāja shows that the Holy Name is wholly spiritual and non-different from Kṛṣṇa, a desire-tree in which all of the Lord's energies are invested, so it must never be reduced to material logic or material means. A recurring teaching is that faith (śraddhā) is the sole qualification for the Name, that faith is generated naturally by hearing and spreading Hari-nāma rather than by demanding qualification first, and that one must strengthen one's own conviction before giving the Name to others. Throughout, he reframes sin not as mere rule-breaking but as living outside one's constitutional position (avidyā), which surrender (śaraṇāgati) and a single genuine taste (ruci) of the Name naturally dissolve. The arc closes on Bhaktivinoda's principle of 'positive action': one does not merely avoid an offense but cultivates its opposite virtue — actively serving devotees, honoring śāstra, spreading the Name — because whatever the mind dwells on, even in avoidance, it feeds. The offenses are likened to clouds covering the pure sun of the Name, and the session ends with an emphasis on deep study, teaching-to-learn, and offense-avoidance as an expression of love for Kṛṣṇa rather than fear of punishment.

The first remedy for this offense is therefore to understand this difference, accept it as knowledge, and have faith that the holy name is the direct, principal spiritual means to attain the supreme goal of prema.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this by making attentive chanting the daily foundation, since inattention is the root offense: chant with utmost care, remembering that Kṛṣṇa Himself IS the Name and is present in the sound, and chant from the living relationship of a servant with the Supreme rather than with any material desire (a desire-tree gives whatever one asks). Because the Name is fully spiritual and not a pious 'transaction,' hold faith that it is the direct means to prema, not an indirect material means that binds by golden chains. Guard against the two most practical offenses — deliberate sin on the strength of the Name (never plan a wrong intending the Name to fix it; distinguish this sharply from accidental lapses, which simply happen on the purifying path and need not breed guilt) and Vaiṣṇava-aparādha, which in community life most often takes the form of dwelling on others' day-to-day faults. The concrete nightly practice is a review of the day: recall everyone you interacted with, find their good qualities even in unpleasant exchanges, cultivate gratitude, and look past a difficult situation to the good that brought that person to Kṛṣṇa. Actively glorify and serve devotees rather than merely refraining from criticism; drop gossip, sarcasm, and condescension toward newcomers, and sincerely say 'I'm sorry' as humbling ego-work. Honor śāstra as service to the Name (offering tulasī to Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, reading and reflecting to 'mine the jewels'). In outreach, first 'rest' one's own faith through kīrtana, then spread Hari-nāma publicly to plant faith in hearers — following Mahāprabhu with Jagāi and Mādhāi — without exaggerating or 'cheating' to attract people. When chanting, always think of Kṛṣṇa alone rather than any demigod. Above all, replace avoidance with positive cultivation: since the mind feeds whatever it dwells on, do not merely resolve 'I won't criticize' but actively practice the opposite virtue, deepen surrender by giving up 'me and mine,' and increase faith by hearing the nectar of the Name's glories in the association of pure devotees, treating sincere repentance as the atonement for any offense.

## Glossary

- **nāma-aparādha** — Offense against the Holy Name; the ten offenses that act like clouds covering the pure sun of the Name and blocking its potency.
- **nāma-bhāsa / nāmābhāsa** — A 'shadow' or dim reflection of the Name — chanting that is neither pure nor fully offensive; a clearing stage where accidental sin is still being purified.
- **śuddha-nāma** — The pure Holy Name, chanted offenselessly and with pure faith.
- **bahya-mukha** — Outward-facing; turning one's face away from Kṛṣṇa toward the external world, the state in which offenses begin.
- **antar-mukha** — Inward-facing; looking within to find the soul and God seated in the heart — the disposition that overcomes offensive and shadow chanting.
- **ahaṅkāra** — False ego; the first covering of the soul, through whose lens the outward-facing person sees everything.
- **śubha-karma** — Material pious activity (sacrifice, charity, vows, yoga, varṇāśrama duty, etc.) — indirect, secondary means to gradually approach Kṛṣṇa, rejected once love for Kṛṣṇa is attained; equating it with the Name is the eighth offense.
- **prāyaścitta** — Ritual atonement or expiation for sin — one of the pious activities listed under śubha-karma.
- **karma-kāṇḍa** — The fruitive-ritual section of the Vedas, in which Kṛṣṇa invests His external (māyā) energy.
- **brahma-jñāna** — Knowledge of the impersonal Absolute, in which Kṛṣṇa invests His impersonal energy — as opposed to the Name, which holds all His energies.
- **māyā** — Kṛṣṇa's external, illusory energy governing the material world.
- **Vaiṣṇava-aparādha** — Offense against a devotee; treated as the first and gravest offense (sādhu-nindā) because the devotee's heart is in direct contact with Kṛṣṇa within.
- **sādhu-nindā** — Criticism or blasphemy of saintly devotees — the most serious Nāma offense, penalized in heavy terms.
- **sādhu** — A genuine saintly devotee, recognized by exclusive surrender to Kṛṣṇa; a true sādhu never claims the title and thinks himself low.
- **Āmnāya** — The authorized revealed scriptures (Vedas, Upaniṣads, Purāṇas) emanating from Kṛṣṇa; criticizing them is an offense equivalent to criticizing the Name and Kṛṣṇa.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture; actively serving and honoring it is the positive remedy for the offense of criticizing scripture.
- **śraddhā** — Faith — firm conviction in the glories and powers of the Holy Name; the sole qualification for receiving the Name.
- **Harinām Saṅkīrtana** — Congregational public chanting of the Holy Name; plants the seed of faith in hearers and is the remedy for the offense of giving the Name to the faithless.
- **prema** — Pure love for Kṛṣṇa — the supreme goal (the direct fruit of the Name).
- **ruci** — Taste for the Holy Name; even a single genuine taste reveals the Name as a non-exchangeable treasure and removes the temptation to sin.
- **vibhinnāṁśa** — Conscious 'separated parts' of Kṛṣṇa; the category to which the demigods (devatās) and all jīvas belong — servants, not the Supreme.
- **devatā / deva** — Demigod; an empowered jīva-servant of Kṛṣṇa. Worshipping one as supreme or independent of Viṣṇu is an offense; worshipping one as Kṛṣṇa's servant is not.
- **Viṣṇu-tattva** — The category of the Supreme Lord's expansions (Nārāyaṇa, etc.), beyond the material guṇas — distinct from the jīva demigods.
- **jīva** — The individual living entity/soul; a conscious separated part of Kṛṣṇa, including the demigods.
- **guṇa** — The three modes of material nature; Viṣṇu is beyond them, whereas the material world and its beings are conditioned by them.
- **rajas** — The mode of passion; the restless, anxious quality behind material thrills such as the discotheque.
- **avidyā** — Ignorance; living outside one's constitutional position as Kṛṣṇa's servant — the true nature of sin.
- **śaraṇāgati** — Surrender: accepting the favorable, rejecting the unfavorable, and giving up 'me and mine' — as it deepens, the inclination to sin naturally decreases.
- **madhyama-adhikārī** — The intermediate devotee, able to discern who is and is not a devotee; kaniṣṭhas should take shelter of such a devotee to avoid indiscriminate association.
- **kaniṣṭha** — The neophyte devotee, not yet able to discriminate association, prone to being naively 'too merciful.'
- **asat-saṅga** — Bad association — illicit mixing and association with non-devotees (Māyāvādīs, pretenders, atheists); the root cause of sādhu-nindā, best understood as one's own lower nature.
- **Māyāvādī** — An impersonalist who denies the personal supremacy of Kṛṣṇa; association with such is counted as asat-saṅga.
- **varṇāśrama** — The Vedic system of social and spiritual orders (varṇa and āśrama); surrender does not depend on it — one can be a surrendered householder or a false renunciate.
- **cintāmaṇi** — The wish-fulfilling touchstone jewel; a metaphor for the Holy Name, whose 'ornament' only a genuine servant is entitled to wear (also the title Harinām Cintāmaṇi).
- **sudhā** — Nectar; the sweetness and glories of the Name that an offender should hear in the association of pure devotees to increase faith.
- **Mādhurya-kādambinī** — Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's treatise cited for the teaching that association with knowledgeable sādhus restores proper understanding of scripture.
- **prasādam** — Sanctified food; the Vaiṣṇava offers bhoga to Kṛṣṇa first, then offers Kṛṣṇa's prasādam to the demigods.
- **bhoga** — Unoffered foodstuffs prepared for offering; offered directly to Kṛṣṇa in the proper Vaiṣṇava method.
- **tulasī** — The sacred plant offered, along with flowers, in honoring Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and the śāstras as a service of the Name.
- **bhakti** — Devotional service to Kṛṣṇa; the path on which deliberate sinning-on-the-Name acts like a robber blocking progress.
