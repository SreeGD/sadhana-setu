# SNS Seminars Harinaam Cintamani 02 2016 07 05

## Theme

This lecture develops a single theme: sambandha, our eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa, and how it transforms the chanting of the Holy Name. Sacīnandana Mahārāja begins by defining relational chanting — following Śrīla Prabhupāda, we should address Kṛṣṇa directly, speaking to Him as to a person present before us, rather than merely producing sound. He offers concrete aids drawn from Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's Harināma Cintāmaṇi: first learn to chant the sound vibration clearly, then meditate on Kṛṣṇa's form, and if that is not possible, sit before the Deity or a picture of guru and Kṛṣṇa and chant to the Lord. Inattentive chanting, he warns, ignores Kṛṣṇa the way constantly interrupting a conversation ignores one's companion — yet Kṛṣṇa never turns away, so the fault of distraction is entirely ours to correct. The Mahārāja then diagnoses why we resist this shift. Material existence guarantees trouble and ends in death — the Bhāgavatam likens every being to a condemned prisoner led to execution. Through the parables of the fish out of water and the boatman who asks only for his stony path to be cushioned with silk, he shows how we labor to improve our position within the problem instead of accepting the wholly different option of freedom through relationship with Kṛṣṇa. Chanting while lodged inside our worries and achievements yields only a shadow of the Name and a concept of Kṛṣṇa, never the real Name or the Lord Himself. The heart of the teaching is that Nāma and Nāmī are non-different: the Holy Name is not a bunch of syllables but Kṛṣṇa Himself — Śyāmasundara, dark like a tamāla tree and the son of Mother Yaśodā — kindly manifesting in a blissful form of sound. The Absolute Truth is 'somebody', a person one can contact, not 'something'. For most of us Kṛṣṇa remains a 'known stranger'; without knowing His form, qualities, and attributes, His name stays abstract and cannot touch the heart, so a relationship (sambandha) must first be cultivated. To this end the Mahārāja offers practical preparation — sitting straight, opening the lungs, deep breathing — and a guided meditation of three addresses: 'I am your part and eternal servant,' 'You are my only Lord and Master,' and 'I am in this material world only to re-establish my lost relationship with you.' The mood is sincere surrender: presenting oneself as Kṛṣṇa's serious servant and begging for service, not liberation. He illustrates full shelter through the paralyzed Vṛndāvana teacher Śrīmatī, who made the Name her whole refuge, and through his own encounter with a raging bull at the Yamunā, when he instinctively cried out to Kṛṣṇa as his only āśraya — adversity, he reflects, is a mercy that awakens remembrance. Citing Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, he establishes that sambandha-jñāna — knowledge of the relationship between servant and served — is prerequisite to prema. Finally, he gives practical discipline: chant before the Deity or a picture, personally addressing the Lord, and never permit interruptions, for chanting is nāma-sevā, direct service to the divine couple — as the rebuke of Lokanātha Gosvāmī's disciple shows, breaking the round for a mundane demand disqualifies one from that association. The session closes with active recall, participants sharing what they learned and experienced of sambandha.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this by treating each round of japa as a conversation with a person, not a recitation of syllables. Before chanting, settle the body — sit straight to stay alert, roll the shoulders back to open the lungs, and breathe deeply with full exhalation. Then enter the relationship through three addresses drawn from Harināma Cintāmaṇi: 'I am your part and eternal servant,' 'You are my only Lord and Master,' and 'I am in this material world only to re-establish my lost relationship with you.' Chant to Kṛṣṇa directly, keeping a picture of guru and Kṛṣṇa before you or sitting in front of the Deity, so the Name is personally addressed rather than mechanically produced. Follow Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's progression — first make the sound vibration clear, then meditate on Kṛṣṇa's form; if that fails, chant to the Deity. Invest feeling and sincerity — 'one more time, but you really mean it' — turning attention out of one's own self-absorbed world of worries and achievements toward the Lord, and offering the Holy Name as a prayer of return and surrender: 'I belong to You; I have broken our relationship; You are my only love,' begging to be engaged in His service rather than seeking liberation. Guard the practice as nāma-sevā: do not permit interruptions — finish the round first and politely ask a caller or visitor to wait, an etiquette respected in the devotee community — training oneself thereby that the Name is a person one would never abandon mid-conversation. Cultivate knowledge of Kṛṣṇa's form, qualities, and attributes so the Name ceases to be a stranger's syllables and can touch the heart, remembering that even adversity can be a mercy that wakes us to take shelter in the Name. Afterward, reflect on what the relational chanting produced and, where possible, articulate it to another devotee, since active recall deepens the realization that chanting without sambandha is the greatest obstacle to advancement.

## Glossary

- **sambandha** — Relationship or connection — here, the devotee's eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa; chanting 'in sambandha' means chanting while consciously addressing Kṛṣṇa as a person, in relationship with Him.
- **sambandha-jñāna** — Knowledge of the relationship between the servant (oneself) and the object of service (Kṛṣṇa / the Holy Name); taught, following Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, as prerequisite to attaining love for Kṛṣṇa.
- **Nāma** — The Holy Name of Kṛṣṇa.
- **Nāmī** — The named one, Kṛṣṇa Himself; Nāma and Nāmī are non-different — the Name is not mere syllables but the person of Kṛṣṇa manifesting as sound.
- **Śyāmasundara** — A name of Kṛṣṇa meaning the beautiful dark one; He who kindly manifests in a transcendentally blissful form of sound as the Holy Name.
- **prema** — Pure love of God; the goal to which chanting with knowledge of the relationship (sambandha-jñāna) leads.
- **āśraya** — Shelter or refuge; Kṛṣṇa is the devotee's sole shelter, the one to whom one instinctively turns in genuine danger.
- **nāma-sevā** — Service of the Name — chanting understood as direct service to the divine couple, Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa, which nothing should be allowed to interrupt.
- **japa** — The soft, repeated chanting of the Holy Name on beads as one's daily meditative practice.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name of Hari (Kṛṣṇa); the practice of chanting the Name.
- **Māyā** — The illusory material energy that binds the conditioned soul, from whose clutches one is freed the moment one sincerely turns back to Kṛṣṇa in surrender.
- **Bhāgavatam** — The Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, the Vaiṣṇava scripture cited here for its comparison of every living being to a condemned prisoner being led to execution.
- **Harināma Cintāmaṇi** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's treatise on the Holy Name, source of the progression for clear chanting and of the guided meditation for entering relationship with Kṛṣṇa before chanting.
- **tamāla** — A dark-barked tree to which Kṛṣṇa's dark-blue complexion is compared, per Caitanya Mahāprabhu's explanation of the Holy Name.
- **Yaśodā** — Mother Yaśodā, Kṛṣṇa's foster mother in Vṛndāvana; identifying Kṛṣṇa as her son is, per Caitanya Mahāprabhu, the conclusion of all revealed scriptures on the Name.
