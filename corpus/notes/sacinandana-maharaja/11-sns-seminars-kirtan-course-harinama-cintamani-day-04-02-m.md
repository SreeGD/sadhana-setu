# 11 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 04 02 Morning Session 01 ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This fourth-day morning session of HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja's Harināma Cintāmaṇi seminar takes up what the speaker calls the most important secret of chanting: chanting with sambandha — a conscious, living knowledge of one's relationship with Krishna. The lecture opens with the offense of pramāda (inattention), which Haridāsa Ṭhākura singles out as the one obstacle that cannot be conquered by personal endeavor, because it stems from a beginningless mistake — the soul's turning away from Krishna to become bahir-mukha, its face fixed on the external energy. Since the fault is beginningless and self-effort fails, the cure must come from Krishna's mercy, and specifically His sound mercy (anāvṛttiḥ śabdāt — liberation by sound alone). The practical key is to establish sambandha before chanting begins, telling the mind plainly that it is now entering the Lord's name. After framing Harināma Cintāmaṇi as Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's Puri-era dialogue between Chaitanya Mahāprabhu and the Nāmācārya Haridāsa Ṭhākura, Mahārāja weaves in study method (active recap, revision within 48 hours, writing and answering one's own questions) and the maturing of śraddhā through a 'forming, storming, norming' progression in which continuing to chant through difficulty itself cements the relationship. He distinguishes material from spiritual humility, commends a service attitude toward devotees and toward the Name, and maps the three stages of chanting — nāma-aparādha, nāma-ābhāsa, and śuddha-nāma — showing that offenses are treated differently at each level according to the chanter's attitude. The four weaknesses of the heart (false devotion, envy, ambition, and material attachment) are shown to dissolve once one is firmly situated in the knowledge that one is the eternal servant of Krishna; envy in particular is cured by widening a small heart, and attachment by realizing 'I am not the body but the soul.' Turning to hearing Krishna's intimate pastimes, Mahārāja insists the sole qualification is transcendental faith (alaukika śraddhā) — recognizing the gopīs as expansions of Krishna's pleasure potency — and that agitation while reading is a diagnostic that faith has not yet matured. The session culminates in the teaching on kīrtan 'with life' versus 'without life': kīrtan is a long walk alone with Krishna in which one reveals one's heart to Him as to an intimate friend, chanting FOR someone rather than into a void, before the 'temple of the heart.' Harināma Cintāmaṇi's three meditations — I am an atomic spark belonging to Krishna, Krishna is my only infinite master, and this world is a prison-house for reforming my truancy — are to be fixed before every kīrtan. Until this sambandha-jñāna is grasped, the clouds of anarthas remain before the sun of the Name and chanting stays at nāmābhāsa; but the moment one says 'Hare Krishna' with the sense 'O Krishna, I belong to you,' sambandha is established, for the Name is the name of one's eternal father — who has not forgotten the soul even though the soul forgot Him. This general sambandha (the Gītā truth of the jīva as Krishna's fragmental part) is a beginning; one's specific eternal rasa is disclosed later, when offenseless chanting lets the Name itself take the soul to Vrindavan and reveal first Krishna's form, qualities and pastimes and then the chanter's own. The relationship is recognized, not created — like an orphan re-recognizing true parents — and the journey's end is to look again into Krishna's face.

Regulated with such devotion, chanting the name of his dear Kṛṣṇa, he develops prema.  His heart melts and he laughs loudly, weeps, wails and sings. He dances like a madman without regard for the public.

This verse describes the transcendental activities of the person beyond saṁsāra who has attained prema.   He who has been regulated in this way (evam-vrataḥ) particularly chants the names of Kṛṣṇa who is dear to him, or he chants the names which are dear to him.  By chanting he has developed prema (jātānurāgaḥ).In great longing to see the Lord, his heart melts like molten gold. “Oh! That thief, the son of Yaśodā, has entered a house to steal the butter. He should be caught and driven away.” When Kṛṣṇa hears the voice of an old woman in the house, he begins to flee. Seeing Kṛṣṇa in this sudden appearance, he laughs. When the vision disappears, he laments and weeps. He wails, “Oh! I had attained a great treasure, and now it has escaped my hands!  O Lord! Where are you?  Give me an answer!”  The Lord says, “O devotee! Hearing your wailing, I have come.” Again the Lord appears and, seeing him, the devotee sings, “Today I have been successful.” In bliss he dances madly.  He does not care whether people laugh at him, praise him, respect or disrespect him (loka-bāhyaḥ).

## Practical application

Before you touch your beads, stop and establish sambandha — deliberately tell the mind that it is now going to enter Krishna's name, and fix the three Harināma Cintāmaṇi meditations: 'I am an atomic spark of consciousness, an eternal servant belonging to Krishna'; 'Krishna is my only infinite master'; and 'this world is a prison-house meant to reform my turning away from Him.' Chant as though on a long walk alone with Krishna: reveal your mind to Him, speak to Him about everything as to an intimate friend, and chant FOR Him — hold a person in mind, standing before Radha-Krishna in the 'temple of the heart' (great leaders even keep a small deity on the harmonium). To defeat inattention, pour full concentration into the very first 'Hare' — step into the mahā-mantra as into an eternal river and let yourself float; once the first syllable is caught, the rest connects. Since the restless mind cannot be conquered by your own strength, pray morning and evening for Krishna's mercy so that knowledge becomes realization. When chanting turns dry or difficult ('storming'), do not be discouraged — keep chanting all the holy names anyway, and that persistence itself shows Krishna you care and cements the bond. Cultivate tolerance and a service attitude toward fellow devotees and toward the Name, which increases your taste. Meet the weaknesses of the heart with knowledge and honesty: when envy arises, reflect 'this devotee is serving Krishna, and Krishna is to be served,' and remember that, like snowflakes, no two souls are alike, so there is nothing to compare; when attachment or overeating pulls, meditate on its dull, tiring outcome (Nārada's tip) and on 'the body is a temporary dress.' For study, review new material within about 48 hours and refresh prior lessons before adding more, reconstruct the teachings aloud in your own words, and write questions during the day (e.g. 'Why do I need Krishna's mercy to overcome inattention?') to test yourself later. Read Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's works — Harināma Cintāmaṇi, Bhajana Rahasya, Śaraṇāgati, and Kalyāṇa-kalpataru — again and again, since they diagnose the heart's weaknesses and their meaning deepens with each repetition. When reading Krishna's intimate pastimes, hear or read only with respectful, attentive faith that they are wholly spiritual; if you become materially agitated, take that as a sign your faith is not yet mature and stop reading. Above all, abandon disconnected 'shadow' chanting and chant with the living inner sense 'O Krishna, I belong to you' — and wherever you find a kīrtan, sit down, for that is the best place in the material world, even when it is long, the singer untalented, or you are in pain.

## Glossary

- **sambandha** — One's relationship with Krishna; here the conscious, living knowledge — 'I belong to Krishna' — established before chanting, which the lecture calls the heart, cure, and 'life' of kīrtan.
- **sambandha-jñāna** — Knowledge of one's relationship with Krishna; until it is understood, the clouds of anarthas remain before the sun of the Name and chanting stays at nāmābhāsa.
- **pramāda** — Inattention while chanting; the one offense Haridāsa Ṭhākura says cannot be conquered by personal endeavor, requiring Krishna's specific mercy, and from which the covering of the holy name arises.
- **bahir-mukha** — 'Face turned outward' — the soul's beginningless condition of having turned its face (mukha) away from Krishna toward the external energy (bahiḥ).
- **anāvṛttiḥ śabdāt** — A Vedānta-sūtra aphorism meaning one becomes freed (from repeated birth) by sound alone — cited for Krishna's 'sound mercy' through the holy name.
- **nāma-aparādha** — Offensive chanting; the first stage, where one commits offenses without concern and simply lives that way.
- **nāma-ābhāsa** — A 'shadow' or clearing of the pure Name; the stage born of anarthas at which one actively strives to avoid offenses but has not yet grasped sambandha, so identity remains covered.
- **śuddha-nāma** — The pure holy name; the third and offenseless stage of chanting, where the Name reveals the soul's eternal identity and takes it to Vrindavan.
- **aparādha** — Offense; one of the three types of anartha, appearing at both the first and second stages of chanting but treated differently according to the chanter's attitude.
- **anartha** — Unwanted things in the heart (of which offense is one type); the 'clouds and mist of ignorance' that remain before the sun of the Name until sambandha is understood.
- **śraddhā** — Faith; the heading of Day One and the sole qualification for entering Krishna's pastimes — a relationship that matures in stages and is strengthened by reading scripture.
- **alaukika śraddhā** — Transcendental (non-mundane) faith; the specific faith — that the gopīs are expansions of Krishna's pleasure potency — required to hear His intimate pastimes and be freed from the heart-disease of lust.
- **mahā-mantra** — The Hare Krishna mantra, likened to an eternal river one steps into at the first 'Hare' and then floats within.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational or personal chanting/glorification of Krishna; described as 'with life' (filled with sambandha) or 'without life,' and as being alone with Krishna as with an intimate friend.
- **yuga-dharma** — The recommended spiritual practice of the age — for this age, the chanting of the holy names, whose secrets Harināma Cintāmaṇi discloses.
- **Harināma Cintāmaṇi** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's work, written after retiring to Jagannātha Purī, cast as a dialogue in which Haridāsa Ṭhākura answers Chaitanya Mahāprabhu's questions on proper chanting; the seminar's core text.
- **Bhajana Rahasya** — A Vaiṣṇava scripture on the holy name by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura, meant to be read and applied repeatedly; the speaker chants a full month each year based on it.
- **Nāmācārya** — The 'teacher of the holy name' — the title of Haridāsa Ṭhākura, who answers Mahāprabhu's questions while disclaiming that he can only repeat what the Lord empowers him to speak.
- **jīva** — The living entity or individual soul; Krishna's eternal fragmental part who forgot Him, fell into the material world, and whose task is simply to place faith that the pastimes are spiritual.
- **jīv jāgo** — 'Soul, awake!' — the call to wake from the dream of material existence and realize one is an eternal soul, part and parcel of Krishna.
- **rasa** — One's specific eternal loving relationship with Krishna (e.g. cowherd boy, servant of Rādhārāṇī), which is not the sambandha understood at this stage but is revealed later through offenseless chanting.
- **Krishna-prema** — Pure love of Krishna; the supreme treasure attained through chanting with correct knowledge of sambandha, after which even liberation by merging into the Lord's effulgence becomes detestable.
- **līlā** — Krishna's transcendental pastimes, which exist in their own spiritual category and are perceived as fully spiritual only when heard with respect and a service attitude.
- **Śaraṇāgati / Kalyāṇa-kalpataru** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's collections of prayers of longing for Krishna, which Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī told disciples to keep with them because they diagnose the heart's weaknesses and prescribe the remedy.
- **Govinda-līlāmṛta** — A text of Krishna's intimate pastimes, given as an example of reading that may agitate a reader whose transcendental faith is not yet mature — the signal to stop reading.
