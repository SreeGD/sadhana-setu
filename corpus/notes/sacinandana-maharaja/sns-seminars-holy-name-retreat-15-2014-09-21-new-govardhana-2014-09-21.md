# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 15 2014 09 21 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

Beginning with Śrīla Prabhupāda's simple formula for chanting — hear yourself, chant a little louder, and chant sincerely — Mahārāja frames japa as the discipline of attentive hearing of the holy name. He then takes up the 'art of prayer' through the verse 'Oṁ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya,' insisting that a prayer be understood in its scriptural setting: who spoke it, to whom, and why. The central thread is misdirected worship. Hiraṇyakaśipu sought to control the universe through severe tapasya, yet offered his prayers to the 'wrong address' — mistaking the powerful but conditioned Brahmā for the Supreme. The same misdirection appears in ordinary life: the innate human capacity for devotion and adoration, whose rightful object is Kṛṣṇa, gets showered on mortal idols — pop stars, demigods, charismatic performers — a fragment of Kṛṣṇa's energy mistaken for its source. Mahārāja illustrates this from his own days as a musician, when crowds worshipped mere stage charisma. From here flows the lesson of delegated power: Brahmā creates only by power given him by the Lord, and even when Nārada wondered whether his father might be the Supreme, Brahmā humbly corrected him. The greatest delegated power is paired with the deepest humility before God; we should remember the Source of whatever ability we hold and not become puffed up by praise. The lecture then turns to Kṛṣṇa's supremacy and sweetness. Even hostility becomes a path to liberation (Aghāsura), and the cowherd boys' informal, etiquette-ignoring intimacy shows that pure affection delights in familiarity rather than reverence — so much so that Brahmā doubts Kṛṣṇa's divinity precisely because He eats saliva-touched remnants. Brahmā's prideful 'wizard-contest' test of stealing the calves and boys is answered when Kṛṣṇa expands into millions of four-handed Viṣṇu forms, leaving the creator senseless and humbled; Kṛṣṇa corrects him gently, as a son. In Vṛndāvana Kṛṣṇa conceals His majesty so intimate love is possible — sweetness requires the hiding of power. The practical climax: since every ability is borrowed from Kṛṣṇa, the source of intelligence, seeds, water and sunlight, a devotee dedicates all of it back in service, offering with words, mind, and hands each evening. Finally, Mahārāja addresses the soul's bondage to desire: we do not control our desires, they control us; only Kṛṣṇa, Hṛṣīkeśa, the Lord of the senses, can. By the law saṅgāt sañjāyate kāmaḥ, desire springs from association — bad association breeds material desire, and devotee association naturally breeds spiritual desire, a 'spiritual infection' caught like a cough. Real sādhu-saṅga is actively talking about Kṛṣṇa, paradoxically the least-discussed subject in the movement. The same mechanism that bound us is the one that frees us. The 'one thing' to carry away: in whatever you do today, consider how to do it for Kṛṣṇa, returning the favor He has given.

## Practical application

For daily japa, follow Prabhupāda's formula directly: chant a little louder — especially in morning japa — so the vocalized name helps the mind focus and makes self-hearing possible, and chant sincerely with attentive hearing of the holy name. Beyond the rounds, treat the whole day as a field of practice. Because every desire that binds the soul entered through association and superior desires likewise come through association, make devotee association a deliberate tool: actively talk with devotees about Kṛṣṇa's pastimes and qualities rather than about everything else, and let Kṛṣṇa consciousness be 'caught' like an infection. When the mind will not surrender, pray as Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura does in the Gopīnāth prayers — admit you cannot control your own desires and take shelter of Hṛṣīkeśa, asking the Lord of the senses to enter the heart and control them. Guard against subtle misdirected worship and pride: remember the Source of whatever power, intelligence, or skill you have, and do not be bewildered when others praise you. Above all, be a practical spiritualist, not a theoretical pretender — in each ordinary activity (cooking, gardening, building, working, teaching) consciously think 'I am doing this for Kṛṣṇa, I am offering this to Kṛṣṇa,' and at day's end offer what you have done with your words, thought with your intelligence, and worked with your hands back to Him. As you give more back, the Lord gives more. The single instruction to carry into daily life: in whatever you are doing today, find how you can do it for Kṛṣṇa, turning ordinary activity into an offering of gratitude.

## Glossary

- **japa** — The practice of softly chanting the holy names on beads; here defined as attentive hearing of the holy name as one chants, done a little louder so the mind can focus.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari/Kṛṣṇa); the chanting of the holy name that is the heart of the practice.
- **Oṁ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya** — A prayer of obeisance to Vāsudeva (Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Lord); the verse used in the seminar to teach the 'art of prayer.'
- **tapasya** — Austerity and self-control; the severe penance by which Hiraṇyakaśipu sought power, illustrating that real power comes through austerity rather than political maneuvering.
- **Hiraṇyakaśipu** — A demon who performed extreme tapasya seeking total control of the universe and offered his prayers to the 'wrong address,' mistaking Brahmā for the Supreme Lord.
- **Brahmā** — The first created being and engineer of the universe's planetary systems and life forms; powerful but a conditioned living entity who creates only by power delegated to him by Kṛṣṇa, and who exemplifies humility before God.
- **Kṛṣṇa** — The Supreme Personality of Godhead, the original source of all — of Brahmā, of intelligence, and of seeds, water and sunlight; the rightful object of all worship and adoration.
- **Vāsudeva / Nārāyaṇa Viṣṇu** — The majestic four-handed form of the Lord that Brahmā worshipped; Kṛṣṇa expanded each cowherd boy and calf into this form to humble Brahmā.
- **Aghāsura** — A demon who swallowed Kṛṣṇa and was slain; his soul attained liberation through direct contact with the Lord, showing that even hostility toward Kṛṣṇa can become a path to liberation.
- **Nārada** — The sage son of Brahmā who once wondered whether his father might be the Supreme; Brahmā corrected him, affirming his own dependent, delegated position.
- **Vṛndāvana** — The setting of Kṛṣṇa's intimate childhood pastimes, where He deliberately conceals His majesty and opulence so that loving, familiar exchange becomes possible.
- **saṅga** — Association; contact with the objects of the senses or with persons, from which desire arises.
- **saṅgāt sañjāyate kāmaḥ** — 'From association, desire is born' — the law that governs both bondage and liberation: material association breeds material desire, devotee association breeds spiritual desire.
- **kāma** — Desire or lust; the longings that, supplied according to one's karma, control the conditioned soul rather than being controlled by it.
- **sādhu-saṅga** — Association with devotees; genuinely, conversation centered on Kṛṣṇa and spiritual subjects and following devotees' example — the practical means of acquiring spiritual desires.
- **Upadeśāmṛta (Nectar of Instruction)** — Rūpa Gosvāmī's work, cited at text 4, where Prabhupāda explains that good association produces good, spiritual desires in the heart.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — A great Vaiṣṇava teacher whose Gopīnāth prayers (third part) ask the Lord of the senses to enter the heart and control the desires the soul cannot.
- **Gopīnāth** — A name of Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of the gopīs; addressed in Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's prayers of surrender.
- **Hṛṣīkeśa** — 'Master of the senses' — a name of Kṛṣṇa invoked as the only one who can control the desires the conditioned soul cannot.
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda** — The founder-ācārya whose simple instructions — hear yourself, chant a little louder, chant sincerely — frame the practice of japa, and who redirects the demon's misdirected prayers to their rightful object, Kṛṣṇa.
- **karma** — The law of action and reaction by which the conditioned soul is supplied desires it then struggles, and fails, to control on its own.
- **conditioned soul** — The living entity bound by material existence, who keeps wishing not to desire yet remains driven by desires stronger than its determination.
