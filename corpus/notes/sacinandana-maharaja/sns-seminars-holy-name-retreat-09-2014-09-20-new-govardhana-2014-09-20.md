# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 09 2014 09 20 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

In this Holy-Name retreat lecture, HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja addresses why chanting so often turns mechanical and proposes a concrete remedy. He opens by correcting a common misconception — borrowed from new-age kīrtana culture — that kīrtana is about 'discovering your voice' or vocal self-expression. Even sincere devotees can slip into mere voice-training if they chant without truly calling out to Krishna in the helpless mood of one drowning in this world. Against this, Mahārāja offers his central image: chant not merely 'from the heart' but from 'three hearts.' The first heart is dedication — consciously fixing that the activity is FOR Krishna, the way fixing in the mind that cooking is an offering transforms ordinary food into prasāda. The second heart is full engagement of the three God-given gifts — body, mind, and words/voice — in total, exhausting immersion, since the Lord gave senses to act, mind to feel, and a voice to express. The third and most important heart is the favorable mood: the active intention to please Krishna. He sharpens this with the demon's counter-example — one who directed voice, mind, and words toward Krishna but with the intention to kill Him — proving that the same faculties without the desire to please are not bhakti at all. Together these three constitute the performance of bhakti-yoga. Mahārāja further distinguishes doing something 'for' Krishna (orientation, offering) from doing it 'to please' Krishna (adding loving attention and care, like presenting a guest's food thoughtfully rather than throwing it down) — attention being what makes the offering personal and forges an emotional connection. He frames the three hearts as the remedy against the 'clouds' or 'fog' that cover japa and kīrtan, since the root of all offense (aparādha) is inattention. He closes practically: before chanting, first bring Krishna into the mind and dedicate the kīrtan to Him ('this is for you'), sit and engage body, mind, and voice together, sing as active praise rather than mere sound — for 'you have a voice and Krishna has an ear,' a reciprocal exchange — and carry the three-hearts image mentally through the chanting rather than leaving it on the diagram.

## Practical application

Before beginning japa or kīrtana, pause and perform the 'three-heart meditation' as a brief preparatory step. First, bring your worshipable form of Krishna (the deity, or your iṣṭa-deva) into the mind and consciously dedicate the chanting to Him — 'this is for you' — as deliberately as choosing and presenting a birthday gift to a loved one. Second, settle the body to sit properly, hold the mind fixed on the mantra without drifting, and use the voice to sing fully and actively — 'like a little bird' — rather than half-heartedly producing sound. Third, and most important, hold throughout the intention to please Krishna, not merely to chant at Him: sing as active praise, offering your voice so that it draws and increases the Lord's attentive hearing ('you have a voice and Krishna has an ear'). Move consciously through the three hearts while chanting — keeping the image alive mentally — so that attention is sustained and the clouds of inattention, which are the root of all offense (aparādha), are dispelled. Distinguish, as you chant, between merely orienting the act toward Krishna and adding loving care and attention to it; the attention is what makes it personal. The same three-heart dedication can also be extended beyond chanting to the service of devotees and to acts like cooking, where fixing the offering-intention beforehand elevates the whole act and its result.

## Glossary

- **kīrtana** — Devotional singing/chanting of Krishna's names and glories; in this lecture, understood not as vocal self-expression but as a devotional connection that realizes one's relationship with Krishna.
- **japa** — Soft, individual repetition of the holy name on beads; the personal counterpart to congregational kīrtana that the three-heart method is meant to deepen.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of Hari (Krishna); the chanting of the holy name.
- **bhakti** — Loving devotional service to Krishna; here, what genuine chanting becomes when dedication, full engagement, and the intention to please are all present.
- **bhakti-yoga** — The path of uniting with Krishna through loving devotion; said to be performed when an act is for Krishna, uses body-mind-words, and is done with the intention to please Him.
- **prasāda** — Sanctified food (or mercy) offered to Krishna and then accepted by devotees; cited as the example where the conscious offering-intention makes food not merely delicious but 'super delicious.'
- **aparādha** — Offense, especially against the holy name; Mahārāja teaches that the root of all such offense is inattention, which the three-hearts practice counteracts.
- **mantra** — The sacred sound formula of the holy name on which the mind is to stay fixed during chanting.
