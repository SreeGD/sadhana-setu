# SNS Holy Name Retreat Retreat 04 Tuning In 03 2018 11 27 GV

## Theme

Titled 'Tuning In,' this opening lecture frames the Holy-Name retreat as a deliberate act of arrival — choosing to be fully present in order to break an old pattern of behaviour and thought and form a new one, since repeating the same actions while expecting a different result is futile. Ordinary life keeps us confined within a small, habit-built world of fixed desires, values, and expectations; going on retreat means stepping out of that world to reclaim the freedom we already have to live in a way that better serves our present stage of spiritual growth. Real growth occurs in the 'liminal space' — the undefined, in-between state where the old life is left behind but the new is not yet realized — which, though it can feel frightening, is exactly where new paths open (as seekers experienced in the secluded cave at Kadamba Khaṇḍī). Because modern, outward-facing people are afraid to turn inward into the 'temple of the heart,' Mahārāja offers a safe method of entry: the threefold process of hearing (śravaṇa), reflecting and digesting, and finally applying, supported by deliberately withdrawing from phones, social media, and news. He then anchors the retreat in its core purpose through Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura: chanting the holy name is the supreme intelligence of the intelligent because it lets us use the temporary, disease-prone body and mind to attain the eternal Krishna in this very life. Krishna gladly accepts the 'worthless' offering of the mortal body — the ear in hearing, the mind in remembering, the hands in service — and returns Himself. The holy name is the only boat across the ocean of saṁsāra and thus the deep foundation of spiritual life. Mahārāja closes with an archery analogy: the mantra is the bow, the self the arrow, Krishna the target — requiring three actions: take aim (clarify why you came and what you seek), make the effort (apply yourself to chanting), and let go (surrender, letting Krishna give what He wishes). He warns that an expectant craving for a miraculous experience seals one off from the very lesson meant to be received, and urges devotees to hold loyally to their two purposes — their aim and their effort — through which one can travel very far in spiritual life.

## Practical application

Approach a period of focused japa as a real retreat, not business as usual: consciously 'arrive' and resolve to change one entrenched pattern in how you chant rather than repeating old habits and expecting new results. Withdraw from the usual zones of distraction — minimize phone calls, WhatsApp, Facebook, and newspapers — and replace them with spiritual inspiration so you can turn inward into the 'temple of the heart.' Use the threefold rhythm around your chanting: hear about Krishna and His name (śravaṇa), reflect on and digest what you hear by discussing and wrestling with it, then apply the resulting insight. Before you begin your rounds, 'take aim' — get clear on why you are chanting and what you wish to reach — then consider the effort appropriate to you and actually pull the bowstring by chanting sincerely and attentively. Then 'let go': surrender the outcome to Krishna instead of demanding a particular experience, and resist the immature craving for a miracle, which only closes you off; enter the receptive mood of one who waits to be given. Concretely, give the body to the Lord by engaging the senses — the ear in hearing the holy name, the mind in remembering Krishna, the hands in His service — treating chanting as the deep foundation on which the rest of spiritual life is built, and stay loyally connected day after day to your aim and your effort even when outer conditions are hard.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma / holy name** — The name of Krishna; chanting it is presented as the foundation of spiritual life and the only boat capable of carrying the soul across the ocean of material existence.
- **japa** — The personal, repeated chanting of the holy name that is the central practice of the retreat.
- **śravaṇa** — Hearing — receiving spiritual subjects; the first of the threefold process of hearing, reflecting, and applying by which one enters the inner space.
- **bhakti** — Devotional service to Krishna; here called the supreme intelligence of the intelligent because it uses the temporary body and mind to attain the eternal.
- **saṁsāra** — The ocean of repeated material existence (birth, old age, disease, death) that the holy name carries the soul across.
- **mantra** — The sacred sound formula of the holy name; in the archery analogy it is the bow, with the self as the arrow and Krishna as the target.
- **temple of the heart** — The sacred inner space within a person where genuine spiritual growth takes place, which outward-facing modern people are often afraid to enter.
- **liminal space** — The in-between, undefined state in which one has left the old life behind but not yet realized the new or become enlightened — the very space where new paths and insights open up.
- **Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura** — The Vaiṣṇava ācārya whose verses Mahārāja cites — on the verse to keep in the heart and on Krishna gladly accepting the worthless mortal body and returning Himself with His ornaments and crown.
- **Kadamba Khaṇḍī** — A place with a secluded cave where seekers sat in the undefined in-between state until they gained new spiritual realization; used to illustrate the liminal space.
