# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 03 2014 09 18 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

This Holy-Name retreat lecture by HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja develops one central truth and its practical consequence: the Holy Name is non-different from Kṛṣṇa himself (abhinnatvāt nāma-nāminoḥ) — the name is not a mere sound-vibration that represents an object the way 'water' merely points to the liquid that quenches thirst, but is the Supreme Person present in transcendental sound. From this premise the Mahārāja shows how a devotee can move from abstract acceptance of this truth to direct realization of it. He first borrows the yogic technique of trāṭaka — fixing all the mind's power on a single small point until it becomes sharp as a laser — and traces how the tradition applied it to the Name through nāma-trāṭaka or 'Nāma Brahma': the practice of writing the letters of the Mahā-mantra and gazing upon them while chanting. He grounds this in a lineage of great devotees who kept such name-stones — Vīracandra Prabhu (son of Nityānanda), Jagannātha dāsa Bābājī, and Gaura-kiśora dāsa Bābājī, who taught that one who chants while looking upon the Name will one day see Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa personally appear from it — and notes that even Prabhupāda's early mantra cards were meant to be chanted before, not merely read. The lecture's heart is the ritual worship of the Nāma Brahma: because the Name is a person, one treats it as a Deity — offering beads, water, sandals, and a peacock feather — so that chanting becomes worship and a personal relationship with the Name is established. Drawing on Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's prayer, the Mahārāja describes the Name's progressive awakening (entering through the ears, dancing on the tongue, producing ecstatic symptoms) and uses the bud-and-flower analogy to show how the Name, at first closed and revealing little, gradually blossoms to disclose Kṛṣṇa's form and qualities as Bhakta-vatsala and Dīna-bandhu, finally 'kidnapping' the devotee into Vraja. He closes by turning the teaching inward: examine honestly your present relationship with the Holy Name, and pray to the Name itself — not to extract anything, but in a surrendered, worshipful mood — to reveal himself by his own mercy.

## Practical application

Approach japa as worship of a person rather than as recitation. Before chanting, pause to examine honestly where your relationship with the Holy Name actually stands today and what you wish to improve, then pray earnestly to the Name itself to help you develop that relationship. Use focus aids in the mood of nāma-trāṭaka: place a written form of the Mahā-mantra (a Nāma Brahma, or even a mantra card) before you and let your gaze and mind rest on it, withdrawing attention from everything else so the mind grows concentrated and steady on the Name. Treat the Name reverently — set it before you and honor it in silence as you would a Deity — and let the chanting itself be the offering. Chant attentively, not to extract an experience or result from the Name, but as prayer and loving service, calling out with the surrendered understanding that the Name is Kṛṣṇa present in sound. This worshipful, attentive chanting trains the mind on the Name, and over time — as a bud opens into a flower — the Name reveals himself by his own mercy. The Nāma Brahma practice is a traditional aid, not a strict requirement; the Name equally reveals himself through sincere singing and meditation offered in the same humble, prayerful spirit.

## Glossary

- **abhinnatvāt nāma-nāminoḥ** — 'Because of the non-difference of the name and the named' — the principle that the Holy Name is identical with Kṛṣṇa, not a mere representation of him; cited as a morning verse.
- **Mahā-mantra** — The 'great chant' — Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma Hare Hare — chanted in japa and kīrtana.
- **japa** — The practice of softly chanting the Holy Name, usually on beads, as personal meditative prayer.
- **trāṭaka** — A yogic concentration technique of fixing the gaze and all mental power on a single small object (a candle flame, the tip of the nose) until the mind no longer wanders and becomes sharply focused.
- **nāma-trāṭaka / Nāma Brahma** — Applying trāṭaka to the Holy Name — writing the letters of the Mahā-mantra and gazing upon them while chanting to focus the mind; also the name-stone or written Name worshipped as the Lord in sound.
- **Nāma Prabhu** — The Holy Name understood and addressed as a person, Lord of the Name — the Name worshipped as Kṛṣṇa himself.
- **kaṇṭhī-mālā** — Neck-beads offered to the Nāma Brahma in its ritual worship, signifying that the Name is understood and adorned as Kṛṣṇa.
- **Bhakta-vatsala** — An epithet of Kṛṣṇa: one who is affectionate to and protective of his devotees.
- **Dīna-bandhu** — An epithet of Kṛṣṇa: 'friend of the fallen' and the destitute.
- **Vraja** — The transcendental land of Kṛṣṇa's intimate pastimes, into which the matured Holy Name carries the devotee to witness Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa's līlā.
- **Vīracandra Prabhu** — Son of Nityānanda Prabhu; a great devotee who kept a Nāma Brahma chiseled on a black stone upon his altar.
- **Jagannātha dāsa Bābājī** — A renowned Vaiṣṇava saint who kept a Nāma Brahma and gazed upon it while chanting.
- **Gaura-kiśora dāsa Bābājī** — The spiritual master of Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī; he kept a Nāma Brahma and taught that one who chants while looking upon the Name will one day see Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa appear from it.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — A Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava ācārya whose prayer describes the Holy Name's awakening — entering the heart through the ears, dancing on the tongue, and producing ecstatic symptoms.
