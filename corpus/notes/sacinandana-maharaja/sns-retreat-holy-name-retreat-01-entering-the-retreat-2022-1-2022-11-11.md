# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 01 Entering the Retreat 2022 11 11

## Theme

This opening lecture frames the Holy-Name retreat as a deliberate immersion in the sanctified atmosphere of Vrindavan, whose very features — the Yamuna, the dust, and Govardhana hill — are active agents that assist a sincere devotee's search for Krishna. Sacīnandana Mahārāja teaches that to enter the dhāma consciously one must withdraw from mediated, phone-screen 'secondary witness' experience and directly hear, see, and remember the holiness of the land, taking shelter especially of Giri-rāja Govardhana, the foremost servant of Hari (Haridāsa-varya), who shelters beginner and advanced devotee alike and showers mercy on anyone who even attempts to serve Krishna by hearing and chanting. He then defines what a retreat actually is: not another class, seminar, or kirtan, but an ancient observance of the Vaiṣṇava Sampradāya (the Japa Anuṣṭhāna in its milder form, the Puraścaraṇa in its intensive form) that pushes a devotee out of the comfort zone into 'liminal space' — the threshold or 'waiting room of God' — where the prison of the mind can be escaped and the heart genuinely transformed. The arc moves from atmosphere to structure: the traditional limbs of an anuṣṭhāna (fixed place / Sthāna, fixed time, fixed number / saṅkhyā, the japa-mālā, and restraint), undertaken in a mood of humble, persistent dependence on the Lord's mercy and under the shelter of Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmī. Practically, the lecture distills to one 'do' — committment ('I am committed to' rather than 'I wish to') — and one 'don't' — distraction, above all the politics, criticism, and fault-finding that destroy a devotee's focus, with the highest request being daily morning prayer to Giri-rāja. It closes by grounding everything in the science of the Holy Name: the name is non-different from Krishna and immensely potent, yet quality of chanting depends on quality of feeling. Only the materially exhausted (akiñcana) — those free of the false pride bred by birth, wealth, education, and beauty — can call out feelingly. The mature chanter therefore unites two attitudes hand in hand: an intense, sincere attempt to chant, and an honest sense of personal incapacity, so that all hope rests on the mercy of the name. The retreat engages all five limbs of devotion at once (pañcāṅga bhakti), making real transformation of the heart possible.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this by first changing the conditions of attention: switch off the phone and social media for the duration of practice, fix clear and protected windows, and sit in one fixed place (Sthāna), ideally facing the same direction in the same posture — 'locking down' rather than wandering. Treat each japa session as an anuṣṭhāna with definite limbs: a set time, a fixed number of rounds (saṅkhyā), the japa-mālā in hand, and some restraint or abstinence. Shift from being an observer to being actively engaged — the single task is to purify the japa and deepen one's relationship with the Holy Name. Sustain this not by willpower alone but by commitment, replacing 'I wish to chant attentively' with 'I am committed to,' which awakens unused energy; remembering that focus plus energy yields new results, while chatter and fault-finding squander the rare opportunity. Cultivate the inner mood that makes chanting effective: approach the name and the great devotees as 'a small devotee who keeps failing,' steadily paying obeisances to Guru and Krishna, and root out the false pride that comes from material assets — for only the akiñcana, who holds the name as his only hope, can chant with feeling. Hold the verse and mood of humility 'around the neck' while chanting, uniting sincere effort with an honest sense of one's own deficiency. Begin each day by praying to Giri-rāja upon waking, add one's prayers to those of Mahāprabhu, the six Gosvāmīs and the saints who prayed in the dhāma, and turn realization into action through devotee association — pairing with a partner to discuss and check on the one concrete thing that will yield the most fruit, encouragingly and never as a policeman.

## Glossary

- **Vrindavan / Vṛndāvana** — Krishna's sacred land of pastimes; its atmosphere — recognized by the Yamuna, the dust, and Govardhana hill — is the sanctified setting for the retreat, where the dhāma itself helps the devotee.
- **Govardhana / Giri-rāja / Giriraj** — The hill of Vrindavan, lifted by Krishna; addressed as a person and worshiped as the foremost servant of Hari who shelters all and showers mercy on those who try to serve Krishna.
- **Govardhana praṇāma mantra** — The prayer of obeisance to Govardhana, whose meaning is that he destroys all misery (kleśa) and bestows the highest bliss (paramānanda).
- **kleśa / klesha** — Misery, affliction, or suffering — that which Govardhana is invoked to destroy.
- **paramānanda** — The highest, supreme bliss — that which Govardhana is invoked to bestow.
- **Haridāsa-varya** — 'The best of the servants of Lord Hari' — a title of Giri-rāja Govardhana for his all-embracing, serving, sheltering nature.
- **dhāma** — A holy place or abode of the Lord; here the holy dhāma of Vrindavan, sanctified by Caitanya Mahāprabhu, the six Gosvāmīs, and saintly persons.
- **Vaiṣṇava Sampradāya** — The disciplic line / tradition of Vaiṣṇavas, within which the holy-name retreat is an ancient and well-established practice.
- **anuṣṭhāna / Japa Anuṣṭhāna** — Undertaking something with firm resolve so that all attention and energy revolve around one task; the milder form of a holy-name retreat, aimed at purifying one's japa and relationship with the Holy Name.
- **puraścaraṇa / Puraścaraṇa** — A more concentrated, intensive form of holy-name observance; illustrated by Jagannātha dāsa Bābājī's purificatory practice of such rigor that a single lapse meant restarting the whole vow.
- **Sthāna** — Fixed location — the first of the five traditional items of an anuṣṭhāna; sitting in the same place, direction, and posture, in focused concentration.
- **saṅkhyā** — A fixed number — e.g. a vow of 64 rounds a day — one of the traditional limbs structuring a retreat vow.
- **japa-mālā** — The string of chanting beads — the 'materials of worship' used in counting japa during the vow.
- **japa** — The practice of chanting the holy names of Krishna, especially on beads; purifying and deepening it is the central task of the retreat.
- **Hari-nāma / holy name** — The holy name of Krishna, non-different from Krishna Himself; a single sincere utterance is more potent than all one's sins, though quality of chanting depends on quality of feeling.
- **akiñcana** — One who is materially exhausted or possesses nothing — free of the false pride bred by birth, wealth, education, and beauty; only such a person can call out to Krishna and chant with sincere feeling.
- **liminal space** — The threshold or 'waiting room of God' — no longer in the old environment and not yet in the new — into which a retreat deliberately pushes a devotee so something new can be discovered.
- **pañcāṅga bhakti** — The five limbs of devotion engaged together — holy place, holy association, the holy names, the holy deities, and the study of śāstra — held to be the most powerful forms of devotional service.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture; its study is one of the five limbs of devotion, and it is full of devotees whose single act transformed their entire life.
- **Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmī** — Leading Gosvāmīs of Vrindavan whom Mahāprabhu ordered to care for devotees coming to Vṛndāvana; the retreat's observances are carried out under their shelter and mercy. Rūpa is honored as the ācārya of this line, whose bhajana-place at Ter Kadamba the retreat visits.
- **kṛpā koro** — 'Give your mercy' — the plea of the faithful beggar in Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa's illustration, expressing the mood of humble, persistent dependence behind fixed-place practice.
