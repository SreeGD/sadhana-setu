# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 04 Evening Krishna Katha 2023 08 17 Slovenia

## Theme

This evening Kṛṣṇa-kathā opens by framing the whole retreat around one principle: kīrtana and smaraṇa belong together — we chant about Krishna in order to remember Him, never as separate acts. Prabhupāda's image of chanting like a child crying for his mother sets the mood, and Mahārāja unpacks that 'crying' from his own boyhood memory of losing his mother: it requires a vivid inner picture of the beloved and a loss of all other interest. Applied to the Holy Name, this means we cannot truly remember Krishna while chanting unless we know who He is — how He looks, thinks, and what He prefers. From this the lecture's central purpose follows: hearing Kṛṣṇa-kathā is what makes the chanted Name into real Krishna consciousness, and such hearing depends on grace — an empowered speaker and an attentive, open-hearted listener. Mahārāja then reveals Krishna's nature through līlā: as Akhila-rasāmṛta-sindhu He sustains a fully personal, intimate relationship with unlimited souls at once, concealing His majesty (aiśvarya) by yogamāyā so that devotees relate to Him as dearest friend rather than in awe. A long sequence of Vṛndāvana pastimes illustrates the mood of intimate service — the gopīs crafting flower-decorations purely for Rādhā and Krishna's pleasure; Krishna reciprocating their unspoken longing as the knower of every heart; the ever-fresh disguises He adopts; the loving quarrel (prema-kalaha) in which Rādhā, supremely soft-hearted, 'sentences' Krishna to dress her hair. That hairdressing becomes the inner meaning of mañjarī-sevā — Rūpa Gosvāmī's symbolism of Rādhā's dark hair as her thoughts always absorbed in dark-complexioned Krishna, and the minute, exacting care of service modeled in Vilāpa-kusumāñjali. The Lord delights in becoming His devotee's servant, manifesting Kusuma-sarovara from His flute to serve Rādhā when no water is at hand. Mahārāja insists the dhāma is transcendental — even its animals are great souls — and that Krishna descends to attract conditioned souls in whatever way each can feel drawn. The remedy for a bored, impure heart is to hear Krishna-līlā therapeutically until eagerness grows; chanting then matures from delight in melody to absorption in Rādhā and Krishna themselves. Prabhupāda's 'scientific process' guarantees that by singing about Krishna one will surely meet Him. The lecture closes with a practical method — a guided mental pilgrimage to Vṛndāvana, beginning at Prabhupāda's samādhi and proceeding through vivid sensory recall of the Deities to chant the Name in the mood of the dhāma — together with counsel on retreat practice: take up more kīrtana, end each day by reviewing it and priming the last thought before sleep, and pace oneself through sustained immersion rather than burning out.

## Practical application

Make every round of japa an act of remembrance, not mechanical recitation: chant in the mood of a child crying for its mother — a helpless, longing call. To make that crying real, two things are needed, so cultivate both: carry a clear inner picture of Krishna (how He looks, thinks, and what He prefers) and let go of competing interests so attention rests on Him alone. Because that picture depends on knowledge, feed your chanting by regularly hearing and reading Kṛṣṇa-kathā — Rādhā-Krishna līlā and works like Vilāpa-kusumāñjali and Śivarāma Mahārāja's books — and approach such hearing 'therapeutically': when boredom or clock-watching arises, keep hearing until eagerness grows and the heart is purified. Before hearing or chanting, pray as Mahārāja models — that Krishna empower the speaker's tongue and that your own heart be open and attentive. When physical pilgrimage to the dhāma is impossible, do the inner pilgrimage: start at Śrīla Prabhupāda's samādhi asking his mercy, build the scene through concrete sensory recall (the marble, steps, tamal tree, the specific Deity forms), ask the Deities' permission to proceed, settle at a chosen spot — the Yamunā's bank or near Govardhana — and chant the Holy Name there in the mood of being present in Vṛndāvana. On retreat, take up extra kīrtana and join the early guided japa, since congregational chanting is the heart of the practice and group practice helps concretize what you are cultivating. End the day deliberately: sit quietly, review what you learned, and prime your last thought before sleep — for that final thought travels through the night and shapes the day that follows. Throughout, pace yourself so the immersion deepens rather than burns out, trusting that this 'scientific process' yields sure results — by singing about Krishna one will certainly meet Him.

## Glossary

- **kīrtana** — Chanting or singing the glories and names of the Lord; meant to be joined with remembrance, and the congregational heart of a Holy-Name retreat.
- **smaraṇa / smaraṇam** — Remembrance of Krishna; the inner act that chanting is meant to carry, so that the Name becomes genuine Krishna consciousness.
- **Hare Krishna mahā-mantra** — The 'great chant for deliverance'; to be chanted in the mood of a child crying for its mother rather than mechanically.
- **Kṛṣṇa-kathā** — Talks and narrations about Krishna; the means by which one comes to know Him, so that chanting His name can become real remembrance.
- **rasa** — Transcendental loving relationship or 'taste'; the relishable mellow exchanged between Krishna and His devotees.
- **Akhila-rasāmṛta-sindhu** — Krishna as the ocean of all nectarean rasa with unlimited waves — able to sustain a fully personal relationship with unlimited souls at once.
- **aiśvarya** — The majesty or opulence of the Lord, which Krishna conceals so devotees can relate to Him intimately as a dear friend rather than in awe.
- **yogamāyā** — The Lord's internal potency that conceals His majesty within His pastimes, preserving the intimacy of loving relationships.
- **līlā** — The transcendental pastimes of Krishna; endlessly fresh and variegated, performed to attract conditioned souls.
- **gopīs** — The cowherd maidens of Vṛndāvana whose loving service — gathering flowers, crafting decorations — is rendered solely for the pleasure of Rādhā and Krishna.
- **Karuṇāmayī** — Epithet of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī as one who is full of compassion for the fallen, conditioned souls.
- **Sanjhi** — A Vṛndāvana festival of ornamental flower-decoration, an example of how everything in the dhāma serves as a medium of loving service.
- **prema-kalaha** — A 'loving quarrel'; an exchange in which both sides marshal their intelligence and temperament against each other, yet which is wholly an expression of love, not enmity.
- **mañjarī-sevā** — The confidential service of Rādhā's maidservants (mañjarīs), who adorn her to please Krishna; service rendered not to oneself but to enhance the union of the Divine Couple.
- **Vilāpa-kusumāñjali** — Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī's work describing in minute detail how the mañjarīs serve and adorn Rādhārāṇī; studied by those who regard themselves as her servants.
- **ni-kunja** — The innermost secret bower where only Rādhā and Krishna meet; the mañjarīs watch from its eight windows and are admitted only afterward to render service.
- **Kusuma-sarovara** — The 'Lake of Flowers' near Govardhana, manifested by Krishna from His flute to serve Rādhā; called the heartbeat of Govardhan and a dwelling place of great sādhus.
- **dhāma** — The holy abode (Vṛndāvana, the Govardhana forest); not ordinary ground but Krishna's transcendental playground, where even the animals are great souls.
- **parikramā** — The sacred circumambulation path of a holy place; in the story of Mr. Hunter the wounded nīlgāī vanishes into its inner side, teaching that the dhāma's residents are sacred.
- **Giri-rāja Govardhana** — Govardhana Hill, the worshipable 'king of mountains' in Vṛndāvana; a place to settle and chant during the inner pilgrimage.
- **samādhi** — A memorial shrine of a great soul; here Śrīla Prabhupāda's samādhi, the starting point of the meditative journey to Vṛndāvana.
- **sādhu** — A holy person or saint; the dhāma remains a dwelling place of great sādhus, and even animals there may be such elevated souls.
- **japa** — Personal, soft chanting of the Holy Name on beads; the daily practice the whole lecture is meant to deepen into loving remembrance.
