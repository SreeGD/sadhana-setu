# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 12 2014 09 20 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

Mahārāja frames the whole of spiritual progress as a movement of purification: shedding the false bodily concept of 'I and mine' and awakening into sambandha — the living knowledge that I am an eternal soul, an eternal part of Krishna who is my only master. He contrasts material life, where one can wear a 'mask,' fake success, and still be honored, with spiritual life, which permits no pretense because it is entirely the inner work of the heart. As long as we remain absent-minded of who we are, we live a 'life in masks.' Drawing on Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's meditation for chanting, he shows how a devotee actively reforms this absent-mindedness by affirming smallness before the Lord and resolving to become concentrated on Krishna. When that heart-work is brought to the holy name, chanting becomes entry into the 'kirtan zone' — the Dahara Ākāśa, the sacred inner space of the heart — where illusions are forgotten and the soul simply meets and is with Krishna; he notes it is sometimes necessary to learn to be genuinely alone with Him, since the satisfaction of true kirtan can momentarily eclipse even our dearest worldly attachments, pointing to the soul's real fulfillment. Purity is the recurring law: Krishna is the supreme pure (pavitram paramam bhavan), and as one must oneself be fire to enter fire, only purity can come near Him. Living beings are sparks of Krishna's fire — luminous within it, blackened like charcoal once separated — so to 'dance with Krishna' in the rāsa one must become purified, echoing the Skanda Purāṇa's principle 'devo bhūtvā devaṁ yajet,' that to serve the divine one must become divine. Yet the path is merciful: even while still impure and carrying our issues, if during kīrtana we chant simply as servants for Krishna's pleasure, the deeper aspects of the name begin to reveal themselves to those who gain 'eyes to see,' and we can expect miracles. He closes by grounding sambandha in lived experience — every practising devotee has already tasted a genuine contact with Krishna that gave them faith — and invites devotees to churn that memory to the surface, recalling and articulating the precise moment they felt close to Him. His own near-drowning off the Canary Islands becomes the illustration: in utter helplessness, prayer past mere protest opened a deep connection in which the Lord heard, was seen, and saved him, revealing His unforgettable mercy.

## Practical application

Begin each round of japa by deliberately reforming absent-mindedness with Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's affirmation — 'I am a small part of the Lord, He is my only master, and my business here is to become concentrated on Krishna' — so the chanting starts from sambandha rather than from the masked, bodily 'I and mine.' Treat the holy name not as an outer performance but as inner heart-work: drop the mask and let japa be the instrument that breaks the false identity. Aim to settle into the Dahara Ākāśa, the chamber of the heart, chanting simply as a servant for Krishna's pleasure rather than for any result or show; do not wait to be pure before chanting — chant in your present impurity with the servant's mood, and the purification and the 'eyes to see' will come through the name itself. Cultivate the capacity to be genuinely alone with Krishna in kirtan, letting the name become the place where the world is forgotten and He is simply met. Finally, churn the heart's deeper consciousness: vividly recall the specific moment you once felt close to Krishna — what happened, how it felt, how it changed you — and let that remembered contact, like the maturing of crisis-prayer into earnest prayer, deepen the faith and earnestness you bring to your daily Hari-Nāma.

## Glossary

- **sambandha** — One's eternal relationship with Krishna; the real understanding that the self is an eternal soul and an eternal part of Krishna, beneath all the masks of material identity.
- **japa** — The personal, repeated chanting of the holy names; here, the process by which the mask of false bodily identity is broken and one is liberated from the material concept.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name(s) of the Lord; the chanting that is the process of liberation and of meeting Krishna directly.
- **kīrtana / kirtan** — The glorification and chanting of the holy name; when done as heart-work it becomes the 'kirtan zone' where illusions fall away and one is with Krishna.
- **Dahara Ākāśa** — The sacred inner space within the heart; the subtle 'sky' or chamber entered in deep chanting where the world is forgotten and the soul meets Krishna.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — The Vaiṣṇava teacher whose meditation for chanting — affirming one's smallness before the Lord and the resolve to concentrate on Krishna — is used to reform absent-mindedness in japa.
- **pavitram paramam bhavan** — 'You are the supreme pure' — a scriptural address to Krishna affirming that He is supremely pure and that only purity can approach Him.
- **rāsa (rāsa dance)** — Krishna's transcendental dance; to 'dance with Krishna' and take pleasure in His company one must first become purified.
- **Skanda Purāṇa** — A Purāṇic scripture cited here for the principle that to serve a divine being one must oneself become divine.
- **devo bhūtvā devaṁ yajet** — 'Having become divine, one should worship the divine' — the Skanda Purāṇa's teaching that approaching and serving the Lord requires becoming purified to His level.
