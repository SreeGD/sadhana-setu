# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 03 Entering the Retreat Workshop 01 2023 08 17 Slovenia

## Theme

Opening the Holy-Name retreat, HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja teaches how to chant the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra so that it stays alive rather than mechanical. He begins with intention: before a single note is sung, one forms a conscious resolve (saṅkalpa) and orients the heart toward Krishna as Īśa — controller, lord and master — with the inner mood of a servant somewhat lost in this world who begs to be accepted. From this he develops the long-debated tension within the Vaiṣṇava tradition between devotional feeling (bhāva) and trained musical skill, framing it through the story of the master musician Govinda Swami and the hoarse-voiced night-watchman Pile, whose devotion the deity Gopāl loved more than polished artistry. Both feeling and skill matter — a true lover offers his song as beautifully as he can — but devotion always takes precedence, for as a Padyāvalī verse cited by Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī shows, Krishna accepted Prahlāda, Sudāmā, Ugrasena and Kubjā despite their lack of learning, wealth, courage and beauty, being won by devotion alone. The cultivation of that devotion is humility, understood not as materialist self-deprecation but as a posture lived only in relationship to Krishna, in body and mind. Mahārāja then distinguishes true Hari-saṅkīrtana — the chanting of genuine devotees who serve the Lord per śāstra — from mere unison shouting and from 'māyā-kīrtana' performed for material ends; even when material results follow, the motive must remain Krishna's pleasure. The heart of the lecture is the practice of the mind: the devotee's greatest enemy is the intruding 'I,' and the restless 'monkey mind' that grabs and drops thoughts is not the self. Drawing on the experiment of failing to hold attention on a thermos, on the jñāna and bhakti paths' shared realization, and on witness consciousness (sākṣī), he teaches the practitioner to anchor identity in the soul as part and parcel of Krishna, to let thoughts pass like clouds on a blue sky, and at every moment of chanting to choose Krishna over the wandering mind. The core instruction for both kīrtana and japa is simple: when the mind walks off from the syllables of the name, do not follow it — notice it (a fortunate moment, not a failure), gently invite it back without chasing it, and re-anchor on the sound. Because the unstudied mind, conditioned by bodily and cultural identification, makes spiritual gains short-lived, this discipline must be strengthened in morning sādhana, which is the foundation on which steadiness throughout the whole day rests. Mahārāja closes with practical tools for living in the world: the 'emotional semaphore' of red-yellow-green to pause before reacting; Sanātana Gosvāmī's discernment of accepting the favorable and avoiding the unfavorable; inner strength rather than external avoidance; redirecting overwhelming compulsions into harmless or devotional channels (vigorous dancing, prayer, nature) rather than fighting them head-on; distracting the mind by giving it Krishna's form, name and service rather than merely suppressing desire; and not being discouraged when lower tendencies recur, since purification, like the first seconds of a shower, is real but not instantaneous. Finally he urges that teachings, like food, must be digested — using retreat free time to complete one's rounds, review notes, and contemplate how to move from forced, mechanical faith to natural faith.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this to daily japa and Hari-Nāma by first fixing the mind and forming a conscious intention (saṅkalpa) before chanting — sitting properly, taking full shelter in Krishna, and orienting toward Him as lord and master with the servant's mood, 'You are my master, I am your servant, please accept me.' Approach the same mahā-mantra freshly each time, as a song never sung before, chanting attentively and as one really means it rather than out of routine. Pronounce each syllable clearly and listen, then exercise the moment-to-moment choice: 'I want to be with Krishna now, I want to chant the holy name.' The central technique for both japa and kīrtana is to disidentify from the mind — remembering 'I am part and parcel of Krishna, eternal and blissful; the monkey mind is not me' — and to let thoughts come and go like passing clouds. When you notice the mind has drifted from the syllables, treat that noticing as a fortunate catch, not a failure; gently invite the mind back without chasing it, and keep chanting regardless, re-anchoring attention on the sound of the name. Keep devotion ahead of polish: offer your chanting as beautifully as you can because you love Krishna, yet know that sincere feeling pleases Him more than vocal skill, and chant for His pleasure alone, never as māyā-kīrtana for material gain, prestige, or results. Cultivate humility in body (menial service) and mind (no pride or role-playing). Make morning sādhana the foundation, since the mind-control practiced there strengthens steadiness through the whole day. In daily life, use the red-yellow-green 'emotional semaphore' to pause before reacting to upsetting thoughts or words; step into the witness role (sākṣī); discern and avoid unnecessary distractions while building inner strength rather than relying on external avoidance; when a strong compulsion or negativity surges, redirect that energy positively — into vigorous dancing in kīrtana, into nature, prayer, or service — instead of fighting or merely suppressing it; and give the restless mind Krishna's form, name and service to dwell on. Expect lower tendencies to keep arising before full purification, refuse discouragement, reframe each wandering as an opportunity to remember Krishna, and prefer chanting within a 'saturated,' devotee-rich atmosphere. Finally, digest the instructions like food: use free time to complete prescribed rounds, review notes, and contemplate how to move from forced, mechanical faith toward natural faith.

## Glossary

- **saṅkalpa** — Conscious intention or resolve formed before a spiritual act; here, fixing the mind and orienting toward Krishna as master before beginning to chant.
- **Īśa** — The controller, lord and master of the universe — a name of Krishna toward whom kīrtana is oriented at its outset.
- **kīrtana / kīrtan** — Glorification of the Lord through song, offered in a servant-attitude; the lecture treats both its devotional feeling and its musical skill.
- **Hare Krishna mahā-mantra** — The great chant of the holy names, to be approached freshly each time and chanted with full shelter taken in Krishna.
- **japa** — Soft, personal repetition of the holy name, usually on beads; governed by the same core instruction as kīrtana — do not follow the wandering mind away from the syllables.
- **Hari-Nāma / Hari-saṅkīrtana** — The holy name of the Lord; true Hari-saṅkīrtana is joining the chanting of genuine devotees who serve Krishna according to the Vedas and allied śāstra.
- **saṅkīrtana** — Congregational chanting; it earns the name only under the right conditions of devotion, not merely from people shouting in unison without eagerness to serve Bhagavān.
- **māyā-kīrtana** — Chanting performed for material ends — warding off disease, prosperity, profit, respect or fame — and thus within the jurisdiction of the illusory potency; contrasted with saṅkīrtana done for Krishna's pleasure.
- **bhāva** — Devotional feeling; in the long-debated question of feeling versus skill in kīrtana, bhāva always takes precedence over musical artistry.
- **Bhagavān** — The Supreme Lord; true saṅkīrtana is performed out of eagerness for His service rather than for any worldly gain.
- **śāstra** — Authoritative scripture (the Vedas and allied texts); genuine devotees serve the Lord according to the principles it establishes.
- **rāga** — A classical Indian melodic mode; Govinda Swami's technically refined kīrtana employed such rāgas and refined instruments.
- **Padyāvalī** — An anthology of devotional verses; a verse from it quoted by Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī shows that Krishna is won by devotion, not by learning, wealth, courage or beauty.
- **jñāna path** — The path of knowledge; recognizing the self as separate from the mind is one of its profoundest realizations, also vital on the path of devotion.
- **bhakti path** — The path of devotion; on it, recognizing that lust and other reactions are the mind, not the self, is essential to staying anchored in Krishna.
- **monkey mind** — An image for the restless, fickle mind that grabs and drops thoughts, generating moods and reactions; its wandering is not the self and must be disciplined, not followed.
- **sākṣī** — The witness or observer; the timeless practice of stepping into the role of one who watches inner events rather than reacting impulsively to them.
- **sādhana** — Regulated daily spiritual practice; morning sādhana is the foundation on which mind-control and steadiness throughout the day rest.
- **Gopāl** — The deity worshipped at the Govardhana-hill temple who, in the story, could not sleep and pined for the night-watchman's devotional singing — showing the Lord relishes love over vocal polish.
- **Govinda Swami** — A master musician who offered a technically stunning kīrtana with refined instruments and rāgas; his story frames the contrast between musical skill and pure devotion.
- **Pile (Paile)** — The temple's hoarse-voiced night watchman — a poor singer but a very good devotee — whose devotional singing the deity loved, illustrating that sincere devotion carries its own value apart from artistry.
