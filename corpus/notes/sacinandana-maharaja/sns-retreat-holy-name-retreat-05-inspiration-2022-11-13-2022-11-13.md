# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 05 Inspiration 2022 11 13

## Theme

The lecture unfolds as a meditation on the unmatched mercy of the holy name and our own failure to fully receive it. It begins with Kṛṣṇa as kāruṇya-sindhu, an ocean of mercy that is never found empty, whose supreme act of compassion is the gift of his innumerable holy names for the deliverance of all living beings. Mahārāja then establishes the name's extraordinary accessibility: Kṛṣṇa has invested his full potency in the name and placed no restriction of time or place upon it — unlike other sacred acts (such as the prohibition against bathing at Rādhā-kuṇḍa at night so as not to disturb the resting Divine Couple), the name may be chanted anywhere, anytime, even at night. This accessibility rests on a deeper truth: the name is non-different from Kṛṣṇa, a transcendental touchstone (cintāmaṇi) that transforms whoever it touches as a touchstone turns iron into gold, so that everyone who chants is touched and changed. Against this towering greatness, the lecture turns inward to lament: even great souls grieve that anurāga, intense loving attachment for the name, has not yet awakened in them, and the gap between the name's magnanimity and our own want of taste should move the heart to sorrow. The arc resolves in a call to active endeavor, anchored in Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's final instruction: try anything and everything in this life to develop attachment for the holy name — even chanting in the heat of the midday sun, demonstrating by one's eagerness that one truly wants the name.

## Practical application

A devotee can carry this lecture directly into daily japa and Hari-Nāma. First, approach the chanting time as contact with Kṛṣṇa himself, not a mere ritual — because the name is the touchstone, every round is an opportunity to be touched and transformed, so chant attentively rather than mechanically. Second, take advantage of the name's freedom from restriction: when other devotional acts are bound by time or place, the name remains available, so fill the gaps of the day and even the night with chanting, treating its accessibility as Kṛṣṇa's personal invitation. Third, let the honest recognition that anurāga has not yet fully awakened become a prayer rather than discouragement — allow the contrast between the name's greatness and one's own lack of taste to deepen humility and longing at the beads. Finally, act on Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's last teaching by trying every available means to cultivate attachment, including offering deliberate effort and eagerness — as in chanting through the heat of the midday sun — so that one's endeavor itself becomes evidence to Kṛṣṇa of a sincere desire for his name.

## Glossary

- **kāruṇya-sindhu** — "Ocean of mercy" — a description of Kṛṣṇa as inexhaustibly and constantly compassionate, never found empty of mercy.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of Kṛṣṇa (Hari); the chanting of the Lord's names as the central devotional practice.
- **cintāmaṇi** — A transcendental touchstone or wish-fulfilling gem; here, an image for the holy name, which transforms whoever it touches as a touchstone turns iron into gold.
- **anurāga** — Intense, spontaneous loving attachment — the awakened taste and longing for the holy name whose absence the devotee laments.
- **japa** — The practice of softly repeating the holy name, usually counted on beads.
- **Rādhā-kuṇḍa** — The sacred pond associated with Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa's pastimes; cited for the restriction against bathing there at night so as not to disturb the resting Divine Couple — a contrast to the holy name's freedom from such limits.
- **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī** — The Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava ācārya whose final teaching urges devotees to try anything and everything to develop attachment for the holy name.
