# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 09 Ter Kadamba Conclusion 2022 11 15

## Theme

This concluding session of the Holy Name Retreat traces how the chanting of the holy name ripens into love for Krishna, anchoring the practice in the legacy of Rūpa Gosvāmī. Sacīnandana Mahārāja recalls how Caitanya Mahāprabhu personally empowered Rūpa Gosvāmī at Prayāg — first condensing the science of devotional service and rasa and instructing him to expand it (yielding Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, which Prabhupāda summarized as The Nectar of Devotion), and through a further empowerment enabling the dramas (Vidagdha-mādhava, Lalita-mādhava) that set the standard of rasa and nāṭya-śāstra for portraying Rādhā and Krishna — pointing to the advanced stage of chanting while meditating on their relationship. Before departing for Benares, Mahāprabhu condensed his whole teaching into a single verse: by constantly thinking of and remembering Krishna, even an ignorant (ajña) person can, by Krishna's mercy, cross to the far shore of the ocean of transcendental love. Mahārāja then unfolds the mechanism Sanātana Gosvāmī gives — chanting in japa and kīrtana leads to smaraṇam, constant remembrance awakens love for Krishna within the heart. The goal is not even liberation but reaching that far shore, attained by following the footsteps of Rūpa Gosvāmī (rūpānuga), as the servant of the servants of his servants. Coming out of the intensive 64-rounds nāma-yajña, he notes the two feelings that naturally arise — accomplishment, and the quiet inner urging that 'next time it can be better' — and turns this toward the practical question of how to become genuinely absorbed and remember Krishna while chanting. His central take-home, framed as an instruction of Śrīla Prabhupāda, is that the name alone is not the complete process: one must also hear and read Bhagavad-gītā and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam in the association of devotees, for the name and the narrations about Krishna are two combined processes that water the seed of bhakti so the creeper of devotion sprouts, grows strong, and finally reaches the lotus feet of Krishna.

## Practical application

Carry the intensity of the retreat into daily life, but not as chanting in isolation. Each round of japa is meant to lead to smaraṇam — so the working question at the beads is always 'how can I become more absorbed and actually remember Krishna while I chant?' rather than merely completing rounds. Pair the holy name with regular hearing and reading of Bhagavad-gītā and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, ideally in the association of devotees, because the narrations about Krishna are what give the chanting something to remember and water the seed of bhakti so it can sprout and grow. Receive both the sense of accomplishment after intense chanting and the quiet 'it can be better' as encouragement, not discouragement — a steady aspiration to deepen absorption. Hold the aim consciously: not liberation but love for Krishna, cultivated by following in the footsteps of Rūpa Gosvāmī (rūpānuga), taking the humble position of servant of the servants. For those who are ready, the advanced direction is to chant while meditating on the dealings of Rādhā and Krishna as Rūpa Gosvāmī revealed them — but the foundational, take-home instruction for everyone is simply to increase hearing of Krishna's pastimes alongside the chanting.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Soft, individual repetition of the holy name on beads; here the practice that leads to remembrance of Krishna.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational or vocal chanting/glorification of the holy name.
- **smaraṇam** — Remembrance of Krishna; the inner result that chanting in japa and kīrtana is meant to produce, which in turn awakens love.
- **rasa** — The transcendental relationship or 'taste' of loving exchange with Krishna; the science Mahāprabhu condensed and had Rūpa Gosvāmī expand.
- **Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu** — Rūpa Gosvāmī's systematic treatise on devotional service and rasa, summarized by Prabhupāda as The Nectar of Devotion.
- **nāṭya-śāstra** — The classical science/standards of dramatic art, which Rūpa Gosvāmī's plays exemplified in portraying Rādhā and Krishna without inconsistency.
- **Vidagdha-mādhava** — One of Rūpa Gosvāmī's empowered dramas depicting the pastimes of Rādhā and Krishna.
- **Lalita-mādhava** — Another of Rūpa Gosvāmī's empowered dramas depicting the dealings of Rādhā and Krishna.
- **ajña** — An ignorant or unlearned person — who, per Mahāprabhu's condensed verse, can still cross to love of Krishna by constant remembrance and Krishna's mercy.
- **rūpānuga** — A follower in the footsteps of Rūpa Gosvāmī; one who attains the goal as the servant of the servants of his servants.
- **nāma-yajña** — A sacrifice of the holy name; here the intensive 64-rounds day of chanting during the retreat.
- **bhakti** — Devotional service to Krishna; described as a seed sown in the heart that, watered by chanting and hearing, grows into the creeper reaching Krishna's lotus feet.
- **Bhagavad-gītā** — Krishna's spoken teaching; one of the scriptures to be heard and read alongside chanting.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** — The scripture of Krishna's narrations and pastimes; hearing it nourishes the creeper of devotion.
