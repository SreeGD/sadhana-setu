# 07 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 03 02 Morning Session ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This morning session, part of the Kirtan Course study of Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's Harināma Cintāmaṇi, examines why chanting so often stalls at the level of nāma-bhāsa — a dim, shadow-reflection of the Holy Name — and how to move beyond it into pure chanting and, ultimately, prema. Mahārāja identifies two inner obstacles that cloud the Name: weakness of the heart (hṛdaya-daurbalya, expressed as anarthas) and offenses (aparādhas). He unfolds the four scriptural weaknesses of the heart — attachment to trifling things, deceit, envy of others' progress, and longing for position and fame — and gives the master remedy: realizing one's constitutional position as an eternal servant of Krishna, which quiets the need to look good, be famous, or outpace anyone. The heart of the lecture is a close treatment of pramada (inattention), named as the root covering offense from which the other anarthas arise: without curing it, prema will not appear even if one chants continuously. Inattention is traced to three causes — indifference, laziness/inertia (tamo-guṇa), and restlessness of the mind (rajo-guṇa) — each paired with a specific remedy: replacing indifference with a rising higher taste and secluded, attentive chanting; replacing inertia with enthusiasm modeled on Vaiṣṇavas like Haridāsa Ṭhākura and immersion in devotee association; and steadying restlessness through focus, sacred discipline (ekādaśī and appearance days), and giving up enjoyment to chant with devotees. Woven throughout is a parallel teaching on how scripture itself should be studied — through the threefold method of śravaṇa, manana, and nididhyāsana (understood here as working out how to actually apply what is learned) — with Mahārāja repeatedly pressing presenters for personal realization and application rather than mere summary. The arc closes on preparing the heart before japa and worshiping the Holy Name as a pūjārī worships the Deity, and on committing as a group to one practice for the week as a path to a first real breakthrough.

## Key teachings

- **[00:02:02.140]** Two inner obstacles keep the chanting at the nāma-bhāsa (shadow/reflection) level, yielding only a partial, dim realization of Krishna in the Holy Name: weakness of the heart (hṛdaya-daurbalya / anarthas) and offenses (aparādhas). These act like clouds obscuring the name and must be removed for pure chanting.
    - cf. On the Way to Krsna
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 01
- **[00:03:49.500]** The scriptures list four weaknesses of the heart: (1) attachment to trifling things unrelated to Krishna (e.g. sports, hobbies); (2) deceit — showy devotion, wanting to appear better than one is; (3) envy of another's spiritual progress; (4) longing for position and fame. These afflict all conditioned souls.
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila
    - cf. Nectar of Devotion, The
- **[00:06:34.880]** The safe remedy for every weakness of the heart is realizing one's constitutional position as an eternal part-and-parcel servant of Krishna: no need to be famous, to look good before others, or to outpace anyone. This understanding brings relief and inner strength — 'I am good enough as Krishna's dear servant.'
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 10
    - cf. Teachings of Lord Caitanya
- **[00:07:57.360]** Deep-seated attachment to trifling matters is removed only through purification of the heart, not by will alone. The strength of such attachment is illustrated by a lecturer who watched a football match on his tablet while delivering an academic talk — unable to detach even mid-lecture.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 06
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
- **[00:11:23.060]** Attachments run deep and are extremely difficult to give up on one's own — even devotees find them hard to surrender. Genuinely relinquishing them becomes possible only when the holy name enters the heart and, like the sun, spreads its light within.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 01
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 06
- **[00:12:27.760]** Cultivate the habit of calling out 'Hari! Hari!' when opening a spiritual book — a practice from Caitanya Mahaprabhu's time. It is done to glorify Krishna and to remind oneself that what follows is about Krishna, never for show or to impress others with one's sadhana.
- **[00:13:45.540]** Pramada — inattention or carelessness in chanting — is a covering offense (an 'apparadha' in the 'cloud' category obscuring the name). Even if one overcomes every other offense and chants the name continuously, prema will not appear as long as pramada persists; it is the root offense from which all other anarthas arise.
    - cf. A Second Chance
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Antya-lila
- **[00:14:35.360]** Inattention has three sources, each to be identified and remedied: indifference (feeling neither one way nor the other, leaving one cold), laziness or inertia (the pull of tamo-guna, dullness and sleep while chanting), and restlessness of the mind (the agitation of rajo-guna). Curing inattentiveness clears the road to steady progress toward prema; while it remains, the holy name withholds its mercy.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 08
    - cf. VALUES  OF  LIFE  BOOK  8
- **[00:21:52.920]** A spiritual text should be studied through the ancient threefold method: śravaṇa (hearing/reading to collect the information), manana (reflecting on it through open group discussion), and nididhyāsana — which here means not abstract meditation but working out how to actually apply what is learned to one's own life.
    - cf. Srimad Bhagavatam
- **[00:23:08.940]** In collaborative study the discussion leader's task is to ensure every member feels encouraged to contribute — drawing people out sensitively ("what do you think about this point?") rather than confronting the silent — because the goal is to involve everyone and hold the whole group's attention.
- **[00:29:22.640]** Inattention is the root of all the clouds and mist that stand between the devotee and the Lord present in His holy name; there are three causes of inattention, and correspondingly three remedies to cure it.
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila
    - cf. Teachings of Lord Kapila, the Son of Devahuti
- **[00:26:36.720]** The remedy for restlessness is to become focused and fixed: restlessness arises when we are scattered and unfixed in life, so at least on sacred days (ekādaśī / the Lord's appearance days) one should give up thinking of eating and enjoyment — even ordinary recreations like biking — and chant in the association of devotees, which concentrates the mind. It is a practical discipline, not merely a theory.
    - cf. Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu
    - cf. VALUES  OF  LIFE  BOOK  8
- **[00:31:50.160]** The restlessness (flickering) of the mind arises from identifiable causes: the desire for fame and success, entanglement in good and bad relationships (especially the man-woman relationship, which strongly affects the mind), interests directed at things other than Krishna, an attitude of cheating or pretending to want what one does not deserve, and attachment to material things — which breeds anxiety and worry (e.g. worrying when a family member is unwell).
    - cf. VALUES  OF  LIFE  BOOK  8
    - cf. Teachings of Lord Kapila, the Son of Devahuti
- **[00:33:16.200]** One should put the greatest effort into curbing the restlessness of the mind, because attaining a steady mind — even through intense concentration for a very short period — attracts Krishna's mercy and releases the nectar of the Holy Name 'in no time at all.'

- **[00:34:11.420]** The higher taste for the Holy Name and the Lord is developed gradually through practice — daily chanting, observing special days such as appearance days and fasting days, praying for mercy, association with devotees, association with the scriptures, and hearing classes — and this rising taste is what dissolves the lower, material taste.

    - cf. Teachings of Lord Caitanya
- **[00:39:03.080]** Spiritual texts must be personally digested and applied, not merely heard: reading or discussing scripture without reflecting on how to apply it goes 'in one ear and out the other,' whereas reflecting with the intention to apply it in one's life yields real benefit — this is why Mahārāja pressed the presenter for a personal application rather than a summary.

Inquire from him submissively and render service unto him. The self-realized souls can impart knowledge unto you because they have seen the truth.
- **[00:40:44.420]** The remedy for laziness/inertia in chanting is to replace it with enthusiasm: follow the footsteps of an inspiring, experienced Vaiṣṇava (e.g. Haridāsa Ṭhākura), develop regularity, build up discipline, and actively search out the association of Vaiṣṇavas.
    - cf. Nectar of Instruction
    - cf. Teachings of Queen Kunti
- **[00:42:52.940]** When you teach or share, speak as a person about what struck you and came alive in you personally rather than merely repeating the text — personal realization has a far greater effect on the audience than restated information.
- **[00:44:19.340]** The practical, tested antidote to sluggish chanting is immersion in the association of Vaiṣṇavas who genuinely relish the holy name — being among people happy in their chanting, one learns quickly and taste awakens (Dominic's own testimony: coming to Rādhādeśa, he chanted more rounds and began to enjoy japa).
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 03
- **[00:45:47.520]** The root of indifference to the holy name is lingering attraction to material things: the mind wanders toward what it is attached to, so material attachment produces the absence of taste for chanting. It works on a scale — the stronger the material attachment, the deeper the indifference — because we get immediate, visible results from material effort but do not see fruit from chanting right away.
    - cf. Bhagavad-gita As It Is
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
- **[00:46:28.400]** Quality of chanting matters more than quantity — even sixty-four rounds chanted with indifference are of no real value; it is not a matter of finishing rounds but of attentive, engaged chanting.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this by treating attention as the central discipline of japa rather than treating rounds as a quota to complete — sixty-four rounds chanted with indifference are of no real value, so the aim is attentive, engaged chanting. Begin each session by preparing the heart: read the first verse of the Śikṣāṣṭaka, call to mind the ten offenses to the Holy Name, read an ācārya's prayer such as Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's, cultivate gratitude to the spiritual master, or hear some kīrtan to enter the right mood. Then chant consciously from the very first 'Hare,' calling out to Krishna as one who has forgotten Him, remembering the meaning and sitting properly — worshiping the Name with the same care a pūjārī gives the Deity or śilā. Match the remedy to the obstacle: for restlessness, become focused and fixed, and at least on ekādaśī and appearance days give up thoughts of eating, recreation, and enjoyment to chant in devotee association; for laziness, replace inertia with enthusiasm by following an inspiring Vaiṣṇava like Haridāsa Ṭhākura, building regularity and discipline, and seeking out devotees who relish the Name; for indifference, chant in a secluded place restraining the senses, near Tulasī and the deities, in holy places or in the company of a Vaiṣṇava with taste. Cultivate the rising higher taste gradually through daily chanting, observing fasting and appearance days, praying for mercy, hearing classes, and association with scripture and devotees — this rising taste is what dissolves the lower, material taste that feeds indifference. Guard the heart's weaknesses by resting in one's identity as Krishna's servant, calling 'Hari! Hari!' when opening a spiritual book, and refusing to make sādhana a show. Study scripture to apply it, not merely to hear it — reflect on how each point lands in your own life. Finally, choose one practice with a group, commit to it through the week, and hold each other accountable, aiming for a first breakthrough experience in japa.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name of the Lord (Krishna); the practice of chanting it.
- **japa** — Soft, personal chanting of the Holy Name, typically counted on beads in rounds.
- **nāma-bhāsa** — A shadow or reflection of the Holy Name — chanting that yields only a partial, dim realization of Krishna, short of pure chanting.
- **hṛdaya-daurbalya** — Weakness of the heart; one of the two inner obstacles that cloud the Name, manifesting as anarthas.
- **anartha** — An unwanted thing or impurity in the heart; a material contamination obstructing devotion.
- **aparādha** — An offense against the Holy Name; here also used for the 'clouds' that cover and obscure the Name.
- **pramada** — Inattention or carelessness in chanting; named as the root covering offense from which other anarthas arise.
- **prema** — Pure, mature love of God — the goal of chanting, which cannot appear while pramada persists.
- **tamo-guṇa** — The mode of ignorance; its pull produces laziness, inertia, dullness, and sleep during chanting.
- **rajo-guṇa** — The mode of passion; its agitation produces the restlessness and flickering of the mind.
- **śravaṇa** — Hearing or reading — the first stage of studying a text, in which one collects the information.
- **manana** — Reflection on what has been heard, worked out here through open group discussion.
- **nididhyāsana** — The third stage of study; understood here not as abstract meditation but as working out how to actually apply the teaching to one's own life.
- **ekādaśī** — The eleventh lunar day, observed as a sacred fasting day; an occasion to give up enjoyment and chant with concentration.
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka** — The eight verses composed by Caitanya Mahāprabhu; its first verse is recommended as preparation before chanting.
- **sādhana** — Regulated spiritual practice undertaken to develop devotion.
- **kīrtan** — Congregational or melodic singing of the Holy Name; used here to enter the right mood before japa.
- **pūjārī** — A priest who worships the Deity in the temple; the model for the care one should give to chanting the Name.
- **śilā** — A sacred worshipable stone form of the Lord (e.g. śālagrāma), tended by the pūjārī.
- **Tulasī** — The sacred basil plant, dear to Krishna; chanting near her is recommended as a remedy for indifference.
- **ācārya** — A spiritual preceptor who teaches by example; the ācāryas' prayers are read to prepare the heart.
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee of Vishnu/Krishna; association with Vaiṣṇavas who relish the Name awakens taste and cures sluggish chanting.
- **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** — The great Vaiṣṇava exemplar of steady, enthusiastic chanting, cited as a model for overcoming laziness.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — The ācārya whose prayers are read to prepare the heart before chanting; author associated with the Harināma Cintāmaṇi.
- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — The founder of the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava tradition; the practice of calling 'Hari! Hari!' when opening a book dates from His time.
