# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 09 The Heart of the Holy Name 02 2023 12 06

## Theme

The lecture opens on the retreat's governing question: the scriptures heap unlimited glory on the holy name, yet the practitioner often fails to taste its sweetness — why the gap between promise and experience? Following Rūpa Gosvāmī's diagnosis, Mahārāja locates the fault not in the name but in our diseased perception: a tongue afflicted by beginningless avidyā tastes the supremely sweet name as bitter. The cure is twofold — anudina, chanting regularly and persistently even when taste is absent (like a jaundiced patient eating rock-candy until the very medicine that tasted bitter restores true taste), and ādara, chanting with affectionate respect, whose opposite, anādara or inattention, is the substance of all offense. From here the talk becomes a praxis manual for raising japa to the level of affectionate respect. Against the backdrop of a caution about standardization — invaluable for mass-producing objects but dangerous when applied to people, since bhakti is intensely personal and devotees need individualized guidance ('boil the milk,' not water it down; quality must never be eclipsed by quantity) — Mahārāja stresses that Mahāprabhu's instruction on japa is deliberately simple (chant with nibandha, determined perseverance, and all perfection follows) precisely to throw responsibility back on each practitioner to discover their own rhythm. He then unfolds the verse's named aids to attentive chanting: manaḥ-saṁharaṇam (a trainable three-step withdrawal of the wandering mind — notice the occupying thought, step back and recall 'I am an eternal soul, part of Krishna,' then fill the cleared space with the mantra); śauca (cleanliness, external but far more importantly internal — 'mind your mind,' refusing to let lamentation, anger, envy or hurt make the mind a dumping-place where Krishna's lotus feet will not stay); mauna (silence during the practice — speaking mid-japa renders it fruitless, illustrated by Lokanāth Gosvāmī and Jagannāth dās Bābājī); and mantrārtha-cintanam (meditating on the meaning of the mantra, where the Ācāryas reveal the four names and their import — Hari who snatches away avidyā, Hara/Rādhā the hlādinī-śakti who steals Krishna's mind, Krishna the lotus-eyed reservoir of ānanda, and Rāma the essence of expertise — so that this understanding settles as a bhāvana, an underlying mood that silently colors the whole chanting). Prāṇāyāma and posture are offered as optional aids, not regulations. The closing movement lifts the goal above technique: Bhaktivinoda's practical instructions, avyagratā (chant unhurriedly, as with a dear friend) and anirveda (non-dejection, since dejection is self-centeredness and advancement rests on Krishna's mercy / rāgānugā, the path of grace), culminate in the contrast between kevalatvena (mere utterance, which may grant liberation) and sneha-saṁyuktatvena (chanting imbued with affection, which alone carries one into Bhagavān's personal association). Echoing Īśvara Purī, the summit of the holy name is not liberation but the relish of Śyāma's names. Mahārāja closes with candid personal testimony — after twenty-five years he saw no change of heart and nearly left, because the essence, attentive and affectionate chanting, had been overlooked — and the urgent charge that the holy name, the one practice Prabhupāda gave, must actually work in our own life, lest, finding no taste there, we go looking for it in material enjoyment.

- **[00:45:05.240]** Ādara (affectionate respect) for the holy name is necessarily a personalized practice, because affection arises from deep within and cannot be divided — one cannot be in two minds about it. Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī noted that from a certain point instructions are meant for serious initiated practitioners: there is no single technique identical for everyone. Each devotee must find their own rhythm in japa — breathing pace, speaking speed, and habits all differ — and the many recommendations exist to help one look and find one's own way.
- **[00:48:25.260]** Concentration / withdrawing the mind onto the holy name is itself an act of respect (illustrated by the phone analogy): when a person turns to their device mid-conversation, the speaker feels not valued. Affectionate respect toward the holy name means giving it the undivided, gathered attention we would want from a person who values us.
- **[00:50:24.440]** Mental withdrawal (manasa-saṁharaṇa) is a trainable three-step method: first become aware of the thought currently occupying you, then step back and observe it without judgment — remembering 'I am an eternal soul, a part of Krishna, existing beyond these temporary material concerns' — and then immediately fill the cleared inner space with concentration on the mantra or remembrance of Krishna.
    - cf. Teachings of Lord Kapila, the Son of Devahuti
    - cf. ENG_GCL_Book_2018_19.docx
- **[00:51:38.720]** Withdrawing the mind from its habitual occupations must precede thinking of Krishna — otherwise there is no space for Krishna in the mind and spiritual life stays a 'pious dream' carried into death. Theoretical understanding alone won't hold the space; unless an active alternative (chanting the holy name) is placed there, the mind refills with its material concerns.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
- **[00:53:40.100]** Prāṇāyāma is offered as an optional aid (not a vidhi/regulation, per Sanātana Gosvāmī in Hari-bhakti-vilāsa) to concentrate the mind before japa when it is disturbed, and after japa when it is tired. The cycle is pūraka (inhalation), kumbhaka (retention), and rechaka (exhalation), in a ratio of roughly 16:32:64 mātras — about 6 seconds in, 12 holding, 3 out.
    - cf. Bhagavad-gita As It Is
- **[00:59:30.300]** All these preparatory techniques have a single aim: to polish one's japa and raise it to the level where one can chant the holy name with affectionate respect.
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Antya-lila
- **[00:59:50.760]** The next recommended limb of attentive chanting, after mental withdrawal, is śauca — cleanliness.

- **[01:00:00.000]** External cleanliness (e.g. bathing before japa) is genuinely helpful — overnight the body brings toxins to the skin's surface, and tamasic disorder like an untidy room dulls the mind — but internal cleanliness matters far more. One who remembers the lotus feet of Bhagavan becomes purified inside and out regardless of outer circumstances; this is why some great devotees chant even without bathing. It is not, however, a license to chant inattentive, 'spaced-out' rounds.

- **[01:04:06.800]** Do not let another person 'walk with dirty boots through your heart.' - **[01:04:53.740]** A practical contemplation to regain distance from an upset: inquire into identity step by step — I am the soul, not the body (confirmed in life, not only 'in the lecture'); I am a divine part of Kṛṣṇa; and Kṛṣṇa is real, His hand arranging one's life. Having gained that distance, fill the emptied mental space with the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra, focusing fully on each word; mindful rounds then ripen into prayerful rounds, and when one feels a gap or inability, the remedy is to turn to the Lord in prayer.

- **[01:09:11.020]** The third principle of quality japa is mauna (silence): do not interrupt the chanting to speak, so the japa is not mixed with other thoughts. - **[01:12:29.240]** Silence during japa is essential: as long as one speaks while chanting, the japa is considered fruitless.     - cf. Srimad Bhagavatam
- **[01:13:52.840]** Maunam means: when you are talking with Krishna in japa, you do not talk with other people — and observing this expresses affectionate respect toward Krishna. The silence applies during the practice itself, not as a general rule of life.

Applied to modern life: resist the reflex that every incoming phone call or notification is too important to let pass — most can wait, and missed calls can be returned.
- **[01:15:35.960]** According to Rūpa Gosvāmī, learning this point (silence/non-interruption) is one of the keys that makes one's japa become sweet.
- **[01:16:05.280]** Everyone wants their japa to change, but few are willing to change themselves and their practices. Without personal change, spiritual advancement remains only a sentimental dream — there is no automatic, standardized process by which merely chanting, taking prasādam, and dancing carries one forward.

Silence (mauna) is easy; the work is collecting the mind onto the name (manaḥ-saṃharaṇa). Because we chant so often, the mind slips into automatic pilot — and without deliberately focusing it, old mental habits flood back in. Only a partly-focused chant leaves the rest of the mind idle, so the whole mind must be invited into the chanting.

- **[01:26:14.620]** Principle four is mantrārtha-cintanam — meditating on the meaning of the mantra. The Ācāryas and Śrīla Prabhupāda have repeatedly given meanings of the holy name because when one chants aware of the meaning, the real benefit (artha — which also means 'benefit') becomes manifest. One should meditate on that benefit: 'O Krishna, O Artha (goal/benefit), engage me in Your service.'
    - cf. Srimad Bhagavatam
- **[01:28:01.980]** The Hare Krishna mantra contains four names, not three: Hare (which can indicate Rādhā or Krishna), Krishna, and Rama. To these apparent names alone one might see no meaning — it is the Ācāryas who reveal the subtle import hidden within the names.
- **[01:28:45.820]** Prabhupāda's beautiful meaning of the mantra: the conditioned soul, in the helpless mood of the fifth verse of Śikṣāṣṭaka, cries that he has fallen into the ocean of material existence and begs to be lifted up — 'lift me up' meaning 'bring me to You.' That is possible only by being with Krishna, joining the mood of the spiritual world where everyone serves Rādhā and Krishna. This mood must be learned in this world and practiced while chanting: 'O Rādhā, O Krishna, please engage me in Your service.'
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Antya-lila
- **[01:30:20.600]** The first meaning of 'Hare' is Hari: Krishna who, by revealing the tattva (truth) about Bhagavān, snatches away (harati) avidyā and all its effects. So when we chant the name, the Lord mercifully removes the ignorance covering our mind and psychology — the very covering that keeps us from tasting the name as sweet.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 01
- **[01:31:45.580]** The second meaning of 'Hare' is Hara: the personification of Krishna's hlādinī-śakti (pleasure-giving potency), who gives Krishna the highest delight and steals (harati) Krishna's mind away — and she is renowned as Rādhā. (Illustrated by the pastime of Krishna bathing downstream, his mind captured by flowers drifting from Rādhā bathing upstream — 'Bhagavān has lost his mind.')
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila
- **[01:32:19.020]** Knowing the meaning of the names makes that meaning pervade the consciousness as a bhāvana — an underlying impression or mood ('mood' in our movement's language) that one is not consciously aware of yet which colors all one's expression. - **[01:32:49.020]** Chanting with meditation on meaning is for advanced practitioners, not a contradiction of Prabhupāda's instruction. Prabhupāda's 'don't think of anything, just hear' is the beginner's class; but the holy name is meant to be a school with successive classes, and at the higher level the bhāvana born of understanding the names stays with and deepens the chanting.
- **[01:36:32.460]** The meanings of 'Krishna' and 'Rāma': Krishna is the lotus-eyed proprietor of all ānanda, the joy-giver and darling (son of Nanda) of Gokula who delights all residents by his playfulness. Notably, 'Rāma' can validly mean Rāmacandra, Balarāma, or Krishna — all three are correct, depending on the chanter's own devotional inclination, which answers the question of who one's iṣṭa-devatā is.

- **[01:41:28.660]** Avyagratā — chant without haste. The Mahārāja confesses his own pull to rush rounds when the day runs late, then teaches the antidote: you are never hasty with a dear friend; to hurry your japa as though you have no time disturbs the loving exchange. In japa you are with the Lord, so chant unhurriedly.
- **[01:42:30.200]** Anirveda — non-dejection. Citing the Padma Purāṇa, a heart overpowered by lamentation or anger has no room for Krishna to manifest. Humility is a two-edged sword: it can deepen honest reflection on one's faults, or curdle into dejection that abandons the practice as 'useless.'

- **[01:44:02.340]** The main active principle of spiritual advancement is not one's own effort but the mercy of Krishna. Room for improvement always exists, yet progress rests on grace, not striving.
- **[01:45:45.140]** Rūpa Gosvāmī aligns this mercy-centered path with rāgānugā bhakti: what others (after Vallabhācārya) call puṣṭi-mārga — where the goal is reached by grace, not effort — he calls rāgānugā, the path that trusts Krishna's wish for one's affection. Therefore dejection or depression destroys spiritual life, because it is a form of self-centeredness: dwelling on oneself rather than on Krishna.
- **[01:46:06.000]** Two ways of chanting the name: kevalatvena — mere utterance of the syllables, alone — and sneha-saṁyuktatvena — chanting imbued with affection. Echoing Rūpa Gosvāmī's Upadeśāmṛta (verse 7: anudinā, daily, and ādarāt, with affectionate regard), the first kind may grant liberation from this world, but only the second carries one into the personal association of Bhagavān.

- **[01:47:14.020]** Closing with a verse of Īśvara Purī (quoted by Bhūrijana Prabhu): let brāhmaṇas pursue aṣṭāṅga-yoga, let others study the Vedas, let renunciants retire to the forest to meditate — by such paths they may possibly attain liberation, the kingdom of fearlessness. The summit of the Holy Name is not liberation but the relish of Śyāma's names.

- **[01:50:46.660]** The holy names are not meant to be rushed through mechanically in the assumption that they will work on us automatically; they are relished — chanted with affection and love in the heart — so that one could happily go on relishing them for millions of lifetimes.

## Practical application

Make the holy name personal and praxis-driven rather than mechanical: the heard instructions carry no benefit unless taken to heart and applied while chanting your own rounds. First, keep chanting daily and persistently (anudina) even when taste is absent — the regular chanting is itself the medicine that gradually cures the diseased tongue. Chant with the whole self: sit upright, engage mouth, words, full attention and heart together, and address the Lord through His name as a person, not a sound. Before japa, if the mind is disturbed, optionally raise energy and steady the breath (bhastrikā, or measured prāṇāyāma — pūraka, kumbhaka, rechaka) and sit briefly in silence; bathe when you can, since external cleanliness helps, but treat internal cleanliness as primary. The core work is manaḥ-saṁharaṇa, gathering the wandering mind: notice the thought occupying you, step back and observe it without judgment while remembering you are an eternal soul and part of Krishna, then immediately fill the emptied space with the mantra — clearing must precede thinking of Krishna, or the mind simply refills with material concerns. Refuse to let anyone 'walk with dirty boots through your heart': when wronged or upset, name the disturbance rather than be ruled by it, regain distance through the identity contemplation (I am the soul, a part of Krishna, whose hand arranges my life), and turn the gap into prayer. Observe silence during japa (mauna) — do not interrupt chanting to speak, treating these as healthy recommendations with reasonable exceptions rather than rigid rules; resist the reflex that every call or notification cannot wait. As you advance, meditate on the meaning of the names (mantrārtha-cintanam) so the bhāvana — 'Krishna is stealing away my avidyā,' 'Rādhā delights Krishna' — quietly colors the chant, and pray 'O Rādhā, O Krishna, please engage me in Your service.' Chant unhurriedly (avyagratā), as you would never rush a dear friend, and never give way to dejection (anirveda), trusting Krishna's mercy rather than your own effort. Be honest in self-examination (part of nibandha) — look and see how the practice has actually changed you, find your own rhythm of breath and pace, and aim not at liberation but at relishing the name with affection. Above all, somehow make the holy name work in your own life: if your practice has gone dead, change yourself and your habits rather than excusing it, because without taste in the name you will inevitably seek taste in material enjoyment.

## Glossary

- **avidyā** — Beginningless ignorance; the diseased covering of perception that makes the supremely sweet holy name taste bitter to the conditioned tongue.
- **anudina** — Daily, regular chanting; the first remedy — persistent practice that cures the diseased tongue even when no taste is yet felt.
- **ādara** — Affectionate respect/regard in chanting; the second remedy and the overarching goal toward which all the techniques aim.
- **anādara** — Inattentive neglect; the opposite of ādara, and the substance of offenses to the holy name.
- **manaḥ-saṁharaṇam** — Gathering or withdrawing the wandering mind and collecting it back upon the holy name; a trainable three-step discipline of attentive chanting.
- **śauca (śaucam)** — Cleanliness — external (e.g. bathing) but far more importantly internal purity of the mind ('mind your mind').
- **mauna (maunam)** — Silence during the practice of japa; not interrupting chanting to speak with others, since to do so renders the japa fruitless.
- **mantrārtha-cintanam** — Meditation on the meaning of the mantra; chanting aware of the import of the names so the real benefit (artha) becomes manifest.
- **nibandha / nirbandha** — Regulation; determined perseverance of the mind in chanting, which also includes honest self-examination of one's progress.
- **bhāvana** — An underlying impression or mood, not consciously attended to, that silently colors all of one's expression — and, in chanting, the mood born of understanding the names.
- **hlādinī-śakti** — Krishna's pleasure-giving potency, personified as Rādhā (the meaning of 'Hara'), who gives Krishna the highest delight.
- **kevalatvena** — Mere utterance of the syllables of the name, alone; this kind of chanting may grant liberation from material existence.
- **sneha-saṁyuktatvena** — Chanting imbued with affection; the kind that alone carries one into the personal association of Bhagavān.
- **rāgānugā** — The path that trusts Krishna's wish for one's affection, aligned with reliance on grace rather than effort (what others call puṣṭi-mārga).
- **puṣṭi-mārga** — The 'path of grace' (term after Vallabhācārya) in which the goal is attained by mercy, not personal effort; Rūpa Gosvāmī instead calls this rāgānugā.
- **avyagratā** — Chanting without haste; the unhurried attentiveness one would naturally give to a dear friend.
- **anirveda** — Non-dejection; refusing the despondency that, as self-centeredness, destroys spiritual life and blocks Krishna from manifesting in the heart.
- **prāṇāyāma** — Breath control offered as an optional aid (not a vidhi) to steady the mind before or after japa; its cycle is pūraka, kumbhaka, rechaka.
- **pūraka / kumbhaka / rechaka** — The three phases of the breath in prāṇāyāma — inhalation, retention, and exhalation — used here in a ratio of roughly 16:32:64 mātras.
- **prāṇa** — The vital energy or life-air brought into the system (e.g. via bhastrikā) to ready the body for concentrated chanting.
- **vidhi** — A scriptural regulation or rule; prāṇāyāma is explicitly classed as an optional aid rather than a vidhi (per Sanātana Gosvāmī).
- **prema** — Love of God; the sole goal toward which the previous ācāryas performed their solitary, hidden japa.
- **sphūrti** — A spontaneous flash or appearance of remembrance of the Lord, which cannot arise in a mind overtaken by lamentation, anger and fear.
- **iṣṭa-devatā** — One's cherished/worshipable form of the Lord; since 'Rāma' may mean Rāmacandra, Balarāma or Krishna, the chanter's own inclination answers who this is.
- **tattva** — Truth or established reality; Hari, by revealing the tattva about Bhagavān, snatches away avidyā.
- **artha** — Meaning, and also benefit/goal; meditating on the meaning of the name makes its real benefit manifest ('O Krishna, O Artha...').
- **prajalpa** — Useless or idle talk; its most dangerous form is blasphemy or speaking ill of others, which Bhaktivinoda warns against.
- **mahā-mantra** — The Hare Krishna mantra, which contains four names — Hare (Rādhā/Krishna), Krishna, and Rāma — to be chanted with determined perseverance.
- **mātra** — A unit of measure for the duration of breath in prāṇāyāma (here approx. 16:32:64 mātras, ~6 in, 12 hold, 3 out).
- **ānanda** — Bliss/joy; Krishna is the lotus-eyed proprietor and giver of all ānanda.
- **Bhagavān** — The Supreme Personality of Godhead; sneha-saṁyukta chanting carries one into His personal association.
- **aṣṭāṅga-yoga** — The eightfold yoga system; in Īśvara Purī's verse, one of the paths that may yield liberation but not the relish of the holy name.
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka** — Mahāprabhu's eight verses; its fifth verse voices the soul's helpless cry to be lifted from the ocean of material existence.
- **Upadeśāmṛta** — Rūpa Gosvāmī's instructional text; its seventh verse pairs anudinā (daily) with ādarāt (with affectionate regard).
