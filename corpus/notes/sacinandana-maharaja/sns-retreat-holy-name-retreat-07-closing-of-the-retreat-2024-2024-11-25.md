# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 07 Closing of the Retreat 2024 11 25

## Theme

Delivered at the closing of a Holy-Name retreat, this lecture gathers the retreat's threads into a single charge: chant the holy name not as performance but as prayer and address — speaking to Kṛṣṇa from the serious, sincere core of oneself, calling out with the longing and yearning that Caitanya Mahāprabhu himself modeled, so the dormant devotional heart awakens. Mahārāja withdraws the scattered mind back to the name, frames the concluding mahā-mantra kīrtana as an act of surrender (lifting the hands, singing from the heart even with a broken voice), and grounds the whole practice in the Goswamis' and Prabhupāda's teaching that the name and the named are non-different — Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are fully identical with their names (illustrated by Rādhā's presence at Radha-Raman through a silver name-plate). Realizing this identity is named the greatest adventure: one walks first by faith and then beyond faith by sustained practice, and the revelation comes only when the divine couple are pleased to grant it. The retreat's real aim is to re-establish the broken relationship with Kṛṣṇa, attempted in solitary time at Govardhana, and to carry it home. Mahārāja then addresses obstacles and encouragements: the true obstacle is not Māyā but the weakness of our own attachment; mechanical chanting must be infused with emotion and persevered in even when results seem absent (Bhaktisiddhānta's counsel to brahmacārīs); Kṛṣṇa reciprocates at the point of total helpless dependence (Draupadī) and regards only the love offered, not its material worth (Vidura's banana peels); a wandering mind is best redirected into active service rather than merely fought. The lecture culminates in a sustained glorification of Govardhana through Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī's Govardhana-vāsa-prārthanā — Giri-rāja as non-different from the divine realm, an expansion of Rādhārāṇī and best of Hari's servants, the great giver of shelter who once lifted himself to protect Vraja and now shelters the world from the torrents of Kali-yuga; its phala-śruti promises that one who hears these ten verses will soon reside near him and attain direct service to the divine couple. The apparent contradiction — if all pray to reside at Govardhana, who will help others reach it? — is resolved by Prabhupāda, who though living in cities worldwide was always internally in Vṛndāvana: one carries Govardhana's shelter within while going out on the spiritual master's order. The retreat's saṁskāras — the 'mental photos' pressed into the heart — are its greatest treasure, to be opened and renewed regularly. The secret of Prabhupāda's equanimity (and of Bharata Mahārāja's unruffled peace) was constant union with Kṛṣṇa, which gives an 'oceanic mind.' Mahārāja closes by prescribing a simple way to take the retreat home — a daily sacred time alone with Kṛṣṇa, a song from the Śaraṇāgati to address the Lord followed by one's rounds — and by noting that holy-name instruction must be at once spiritually inspiring and practically experiential, and that congregational chanting offered loudly and with deep feeling reaches even the higher planets.

## Practical application

Treat every session of japa and kīrtana as prayer and address, not performance: before beginning, deliberately withdraw the mind from its scattered objects and bring it back to the name, then chant from the serious, sincere part of yourself, speaking directly to Kṛṣṇa with longing and yearning in the mood of Caitanya Mahāprabhu — a broken voice is no obstacle, because it is the heart driving the sound that matters. When the mind wanders, do not merely fight the distraction; redirect it into active service of Kṛṣṇa — for example, picture yourself serving Him on a jeweled throne. Persevere through mechanical, seemingly fruitless chanting: infuse it with emotion and devotion, keep humility and earnest desire together, and do not abandon the path even if love does not appear after years, trusting that bhakti is real and unfailing and that Kṛṣṇa reciprocates when one reaches helpless dependence on Him and offers with love rather than material grandeur. Establish a daily 'sacred time' alone with Kṛṣṇa — Mahārāja's own method is to go out in the morning with a Śaraṇāgati songbook, sing one song to address the Lord, and then chant one's rounds — which yields strength, nourishment, joy, and an 'oceanic mind' undisturbed by life's waves. Carry the retreat home: regularly open the 'treasure-box of the heart' to examine and renew the impressions (saṁskāras) of Govardhana and the Deities, keep their shelter embedded in consciousness so the mind does not descend into agitation once you leave, and use the whole experience to advance your service. Even while duty carries you elsewhere in the world, remain internally in Vṛndāvana as Prabhupāda did, and chant congregationally with loud, heartfelt feeling — remembering that the holy name is non-different from Kṛṣṇa Himself, the deepest reason chanting is the heart of the practice.

## Glossary

- **kīrtana** — Congregational or vocal chanting/singing of the holy names of God; here taught as prayer and direct address to Kṛṣṇa rather than musical performance.
- **Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra** — The 'great chant for deliverance' — the central sixteen-word prayer (Hare Kṛṣṇa / Hare Rāma) chanted on japa beads and congregationally.
- **japa / rounds** — Soft, individual recitation of the mahā-mantra on prayer beads; a fixed daily count is counted in 'rounds.'
- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — The 16th-century avatāra of Kṛṣṇa who modeled chanting in a mood of intense longing and separation, the standard for how to call out to the Lord.
- **the name and the named are non-different** — The core teaching of the Goswamis and Prabhupāda that the holy name is fully identical with the Lord — Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are present in their names.
- **Māyā** — Illusion, the deluding material energy; here Mahārāja clarifies it is not the real obstacle in chanting — our own weak attachment is.
- **Ajāmila** — A fallen brahmana of the Bhāgavatam delivered at death by calling his son's name 'Nārāyaṇa'; cited to show the name's saving power even when chanted without intent.
- **Bhaktisiddhānta** — Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, Prabhupāda's spiritual master, who counselled brahmacārīs to persevere in chanting even if no love appears after ten years.
- **brahmacārī** — A celibate student or monk in the renounced/training stage of spiritual life.
- **bhakti** — Loving devotional service to God; described here as real and unfailing.
- **Draupadī** — The queen of the Pāṇḍavas whose honor Kṛṣṇa protected only when she surrendered completely; illustrates reciprocation at the point of helpless dependence.
- **Vidura** — A great devotee whose humble offering of banana peels Kṛṣṇa relished, showing the Lord regards the love behind an offering, not its material worth.
- **saṁskāra** — A deep impression pressed into the heart/consciousness; here the lasting 'mental photos' of the retreat to be cultivated and renewed.
- **Śaraṇāgati** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's songbook of surrender; Mahārāja sings one of its songs daily to address the Lord before chanting his rounds.
- **Govardhana / Giri-rāja** — The sacred hill of Vraja ('king of mountains'); glorified as non-different from the divine realm, an expansion of Rādhārāṇī and the best of Hari's servants, and the great giver of shelter.
- **Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī** — One of the six Goswamis of Vṛndāvana, author of the prayer for residence at Govardhana cited at the close.
- **Stavāvalī / Govardhana-vāsa-prārthanā** — Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī's collection of prayers (Stavāvalī) containing the 'Prayer for Residence at Govardhana,' ten verses glorifying Giri-rāja.
- **kuṇḍa** — A sacred pond or water-tank; Govardhana's kuṇḍas, courtyards, and groves witness Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa's pastimes.
- **phala-śruti** — The traditional concluding statement of the fruit/result gained by hearing or reciting a text — here, soon residing near Govardhana and attaining service to the divine couple.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present degraded age of quarrel and hypocrisy, from whose 'raging torrents' Govardhana offers shelter.
- **Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī / Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa** — The divine couple; Rādhā is the supreme devotee and feminine counterpart of Kṛṣṇa, present beside Him even through her name (as at Radha-Raman).
- **Hari-dāsa-varya** — 'The best of Lord Hari's servants' — a title of Govardhana Hill from the prayers of Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī.
- **king of the suras** — Indra, lord of the demigods, whose fury Kṛṣṇa repelled by lifting Govardhana to shelter all of Vraja.
- **Goswamis** — The six principal disciples of Caitanya Mahāprabhu who systematized Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava theology, including the teaching that name and Lord are non-different.
- **Bharata Mahārāja** — The sage-king who, insulted by King Rahūgaṇa, was only momentarily ruffled then peaceful — likened to a vast ocean undisturbed by a stone.
- **Candra-loka** — The higher planetary realm of the moon; cited as an example of higher-planet residents drawn to join loud, heartfelt congregational chanting.
- **Vṛndāvana** — Kṛṣṇa's eternal abode of sweet pastimes; carried internally by an advanced devotee (as Prabhupāda did) regardless of physical location.
