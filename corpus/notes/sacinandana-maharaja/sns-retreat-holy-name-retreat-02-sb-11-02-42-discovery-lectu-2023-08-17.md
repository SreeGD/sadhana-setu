# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 02 SB 11 02 42 Discovery Lecture 01 2023 08 17 Slovenia

## Theme

The lecture, given by HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja on Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.2.42 at a Holy-Name retreat in Slovenia, argues that philosophical conviction about Krishna consciousness is only the starting point: hearing and being convinced must mature into direct, personal experience of spiritual life. Using the verse's teaching that devotion (bhakti), direct experience of the Lord, and detachment from material things arise and grow together — as pleasure, nourishment, and relief from hunger come with each bite of food — Mahārāja shows that only the experienced 'sweetness' of the process of bhakti (taste in kīrtan and chanting) or of its object (Radha-Krishna) can release the last subtle traces of material attachment that the four regulative principles leave untouched. He frames such experience diagnostically, asking whether one's experience of Krishna is as tangible as tasting a meal and whether genuine detachment from material life follows. Drawing on Ramahari's Oxford research, he distinguishes outer 'arrangements' (Krishna ordering events) from inner 'encounters/ecstasies,' and, following Jīva Gosvāmī, cautions against loosely calling extraordinary inner feelings 'ecstasy,' which strictly requires the conditions of sāttvika-bhāva. The lecture's central thread is faith (śraddhā): its purpose is to strengthen faith, success in every spiritual relationship is proportional to faith, and losing faith halts progress. Faith is of two kinds — forced/imitative and natural (svābhāvika) — distinguished by motivation, reflection, and reliance on one's own realization (the kaniṣṭha leans on others' recorded realizations; the madhyama lives by his own and deals with the four classes through prema, maitrī, kṛpā, and upekṣā). Natural faith, illustrated by Uttarā, the rain-bringing child, Vṛtrāsura, and Kuntī, confers real strength (vīrya) and is unshakable. The route from forced to natural faith is genuine devotional experience, and the supreme means — the only path that truly works in Kali-yuga — is congregational chanting of the holy name. But chanting yields its effect only when joined to Caitanya Mahāprabhu's instruction to chant in deep humility on four levels: body (menial service), mind (no grudge, no superiority), heart (regret, above all for having left Krishna), and speech. The retreat is thus a 'training camp for humility,' where success is measured by humility rather than admiration, where worldly success that breeds pride makes one a spiritual loser, and where faith — the foundation on which both Harināma Cintāmaṇi and Bhajana Rahasya rest — is the mother that protects the devotee, with all experience ultimately granted by Krishna's mercy.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this by treating daily japa and Hari-Nāma not as theory to be confirmed but as practice meant to yield direct experience. During chanting, refuse to fix the attention on the 'screen' of the distracted, conditioned mind; instead withdraw the senses — visualize Krishna, gaze at the altar, or close the eyes — and chant following Caitanya Mahāprabhu's instruction to chant in deep humility on the body (taking up small, menial service), the mind (serving without grudge, giving up the sense of being better than others, and when the critical mind judges fellow devotees, catching it and turning to one's own shortcomings), the heart (genuine regret, above all sorrow for having left Krishna), and the speech (speaking pleasantly). One should examine one's own faith honestly: is it merely forced and imitative — chanting only to 'tick it off the list,' to discharge a vow, or to keep up appearances and belong — or natural, performed consciously to reach Krishna? Forced faith and forced humility lead nowhere; they must ripen into the natural kind rooted in one's own experience. Make real effort not to 'buy' Krishna's favor but to show Him 'I really want it,' while knowing that experience and mercy are ultimately His to grant. Protect devotional consciousness during intensive practice by pacing oneself — eating and sleeping enough that staying awake is not torture — and deliberately avoiding unfavorable conversations that pull the mind from devotional subjects. Above all, prefer congregational chanting with other devotees, the supreme and most easily fruitful process, and let faith in the holy name and in Krishna be the foundation, measuring true success by humility and dependence on Krishna's mercy rather than by admiration or worldly result.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord; here the practice of chanting Krishna's names, held to be the supreme spiritual process for the present age.
- **japa** — Soft, repeated personal chanting of the holy name, usually counted on beads.
- **kīrtan / kirtan** — Congregational singing of the holy name; the lecture holds chanting with others to be the supreme means of gaining devotional experience.
- **bhakti** — Devotion, devotional service to the Lord; its sweetness can be tasted either in the process (chanting) or in its object (Radha-Krishna).
- **śraddhā** — Faith; the foundational disposition of the heart that turns it toward Krishna, indispensable for all advancement.
- **svābhāvika** — Natural or spontaneous; describes the strong, unshakable kind of faith that arises from one's own experience, as opposed to forced/imitative faith.
- **SB 11.2.42 (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam)** — The verse expounded: devotion, direct experience of the Lord, and detachment from other things arise simultaneously and increase together for one who takes shelter of the Lord — as pleasure, nourishment, and relief from hunger come with each bite of food.
- **Bhāgavatam** — The Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, the scripture whose hearing nourishes the devotee; the source of the verse under discussion.
- **prasādam** — Sanctified food offered to the Lord and then honored by devotees as His mercy.
- **sāttvika-bhāva** — The bodily symptoms of genuine spiritual ecstasy whose strict conditions, per Jīva Gosvāmī, must be examined before inner feelings can properly be called ecstasy.
- **ecstasy** — The proper term for advanced spiritual rapture; Jīva Gosvāmī prefers to call ordinary deep feelings 'extraordinary feelings,' reserving 'ecstasy' for what meets the conditions of sāttvika-bhāva — a word used more loosely in common ISKCON usage.
- **kaniṣṭha-adhikārī** — The neophyte devotee, marked by forced faith who accepts dogma without reflection and relies only on realizations recorded by others in scripture.
- **madhyama** — The intermediate devotee with developed natural faith, who reflects on the philosophy, relies on his own realization, and relates to four classes through prema, maitrī, kṛpā, and upekṣā.
- **prema** — Loving devotion; the madhyama devotee's attitude of love toward Īśvara, the Lord.
- **Īśvara** — The Lord, the Supreme Controller; the proper object of loving devotion (prema).
- **maitrī** — Friendship; the madhyama devotee's attitude toward fellow devotees.
- **kṛpā** — Compassion or mercy; the madhyama devotee's attitude toward the innocent and the fallen.
- **upekṣā** — Indifference or avoidance; the madhyama devotee's attitude toward the envious atheist.
- **vīrya** — Spiritual strength or potency; conferred by natural faith, whereas forced faith leaves one weak.
- **dīkṣā-guru** — The initiating spiritual master; if one's faith in him is lost, one should take shelter of a śikṣā-guru rather than stop advancing.
- **śikṣā-guru** — The instructing spiritual master, whose shelter sustains advancement when faith in the dīkṣā-guru falters.
- **brāhmaṇa** — A spiritually qualified person/priest; cited among those (mantra, holy place, deity, doctor, guru) in whom success depends on one's faith.
- **Pañcarātra-pradīpa** — The scriptural source cited for the principle that one's success with a mantra, holy place, brāhmaṇa, deity, astrologer, doctor, and guru is proportional to one's faith in them.
- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — Śrī Caitanya, whose instruction to chant the holy name in deep humility — on body, mind, heart, and speech — is the through-line that makes chanting effective.
- **tilaka** — Sacred clay markings on the body; cited as an outward form that forced faith imitates and that, by itself, is not what matters.
- **śikhā** — The tuft of hair worn by devotees; cited as an external mark that, like tilaka, is less important than the condition of the heart.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present degraded age, in which, of the four paths, only chanting the holy name truly delivers spiritual experience.
- **karma** — The path of fruitive/regulated action; yields a comfortable life but not real spiritual experience in Kali-yuga.
- **jñāna** — The path of knowledge; yields some insight but not real spiritual experience in Kali-yuga.
- **yoga** — The path of mystic discipline; yields a taste of well-being but not real spiritual experience in Kali-yuga.
- **smaraṇa** — Advanced meditative remembrance of the Lord; the kind of practice treated in Bhajana Rahasya, which nonetheless begins by establishing faith.
- **Harināma Cintāmaṇi** — A devotional treatise on chanting the holy name that begins by establishing faith as the foundation of chanting.
- **Bhajana Rahasya** — A book of highly advanced practices (including smaraṇa) that, like Harināma Cintāmaṇi, begins with establishing faith.
- **Jīva Gosvāmī** — The Vaiṣṇava theologian cited for cautious analysis of devotional feelings and for the reasoning that chanting the holy name is the easiest and best process, producing extraordinary feelings that change the heart.
- **Yamarāja** — The lord of death; sleeping with the head to the west is avoided because it is said to prepare one for him.
- **brahmāstra** — A supreme celestial weapon; Aśvatthāmā's brahmāstra targeted Uttarā's unborn child, prompting her faith-driven flight to Krishna.
- **Uttarā** — Abhimanyu's widow whose natural, unshaken faith led her to run spontaneously to Krishna to save her unborn child, even though Krishna had not protected her husband.
- **Vṛtrāsura** — The 'defeated' enemy of Indra who, though struck down, prayed to be taken to Krishna — illustrating that failure leading to humble dependence is the true spiritual victory.
- **Kuntī** — The devotee who prayed that in bad times Krishna is always present, exemplifying faith, gratitude, and heart-humility operating together as the basis of turning to Krishna.
