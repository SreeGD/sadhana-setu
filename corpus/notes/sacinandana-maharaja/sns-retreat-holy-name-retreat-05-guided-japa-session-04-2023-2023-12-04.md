# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 05 Guided Japa Session 04 2023 12 04

## Theme

This guided japa session frames chanting as a progressive, deliberate practice rather than a passive routine. Sacīnandana Mahārāja opens by reminding the devotee that advancing in chanting means consciously forming new habits, because appreciation of the holy name unfolds through unlimited ascending levels — there is always a next level to reach. He then turns to the practical setting of the retreat: chanting in small japa circles, which generates fresh energy and sharpens focus, especially when the body is tired, making the group a deliberate aid to attentiveness. From here the lecture develops its central paradox — that even while sitting together as a group, the practitioner must learn to be alone with Krishna. Japa is essentially individual and inward, focused on Krishna, and in this it differs from kirtan, where one hears and responds to others. Mahārāja grounds this in practice through Rupa Gosvami's standard: japa should be chanted softly, loud enough only for oneself to hear, so as not to disturb neighbors or slip into leading others — which would make it kirtan rather than japa. Those with loud voices and loud minds especially need to draw the chanting quietly inward. The arc closes by naming the deeper purpose: learning to be alone with Krishna amid many people is a transferable skill, so the japa circle trains the very inwardness the devotee will need when chanting at home or in any crowd.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this by treating daily japa as a habit to be consciously shaped rather than left to drift, always reaching toward the next level of appreciation for the holy name. When energy flags or the body is tired, chanting in a small circle of devotees can be used deliberately to renew focus. Within that group, the practitioner's inner task is to be alone with Krishna: keep the attention turned inward on Krishna and the sound of the name, rather than listening and responding to others as one would in kirtan. Following Rupa Gosvami, chant softly — just loud enough for your own ears — so that you neither disturb those nearby nor begin leading them. This is especially important for those with loud voices and restless, 'loud' minds, who should consciously draw the chanting quietly inward. Practiced this way, the japa circle becomes training: the inwardness cultivated there is the same skill the devotee carries into solitary chanting at home or into any crowded setting.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Individual, inward chanting of the holy name, focused privately on Krishna and chanted softly enough for only oneself to hear.
- **kirtan** — Communal, responsive chanting in which one hears and responds to others, or leads them — distinguished here from the inward solitude of japa.
- **holy name** — The name of Krishna, whose appreciation unfolds through unlimited ascending levels of realization.
- **Rupa Gosvami** — Authority cited for the standard that japa should be chanted quietly — loud enough only for oneself to hear.
- **Krishna** — The Supreme Person who is the object of the chanter's inward focus during japa.
