# SNS Holy Name Retreat Retreat 10 Inspiration 2018 11 29 GV

## Theme

This lecture is a meditation on the glories of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī and on why Her name and shelter are the surest, sweetest gateway to Kṛṣṇa. Mahārāja begins from a striking observation — that even strangers far outside Vṛndāvana spontaneously greet one another with 'Rādhe Rādhe' — and argues this is no accident: Kṛṣṇa, seated within every heart, personally inspires people to become aware of and glorify Her. Indeed, Kṛṣṇa Himself is the chief propagator of Her glories: through an aerial voice He arranged that She be publicly crowned and bathed as Vṛndāveśvarī, the queen of Vṛndāvana, and He even takes the humble role of a constable roaming Vṛndāvana to proclaim Her glory — modelling that the supreme service in Her kingdom is simply to broadcast it. Yet Rādhārāṇī, shy by nature, is reluctant to accept Her own supremacy and finally accepts the reign only on behalf of Her beloved Śyāmasundara, holding Her sovereignty for His sake rather than Her own. The mood of this whole realm is shown to be one of 'joyful jokes' — the teasing play of the flute hidden in Lalitā's braid reveals that Vṛndāvana devotion is sweet laughter and intimate delight, not solemnity. From this exalted vision Mahārāja draws the practical heart of the talk: Rādhārāṇī is supremely merciful and is the easier path to Kṛṣṇa. Approaching Kṛṣṇa directly demands high qualifications, but Her heart, soft like butter, melts the moment She sees someone sincerely and honestly take shelter at Her lotus feet. This is why even an ordinary person's calling of 'Rādhe Rādhe' is auspicious — invoking Her name itself draws Her merciful attention. And because She is intimately present within the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra — the address 'Hare' invoking Her eight times — chanting is itself a direct appeal to Her. The lecture culminates in the instruction to consciously offer one's chanting to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī, transforming japa from routine into a heartfelt act of taking shelter at Her feet.

## Practical application

Practically, the devotee is invited to reframe japa as a direct, personal appeal to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī rather than a routine duty. Because 'Hare' in the mahā-mantra invokes Her — eight times in each round — every cycle of chanting can be approached as calling out to Her for shelter, conscious that Her name itself draws Her merciful attention. The recommended attitude is sincerity and honesty over qualification: one need not feel advanced or worthy, since Her heart melts the instant She sees a soul honestly take shelter at Her feet, and even a simple 'Rādhe Rādhe' uttered anywhere — in the marketplace or to a passing officer — is auspicious. Concretely, Mahārāja's instruction is to consciously offer the final mantra or session of chanting to Rādhārāṇī, dedicating it to Her as an offering. The devotee can also carry the lecture's mood into daily Hari-Nāma: chanting in the spirit of 'joyful jokes' and intimate sweetness rather than grim solemnity, and embracing the supreme service of Her kingdom — broadcasting Her glories, beginning with the spontaneous greeting 'Rādhe Rādhe.'

## Glossary

- **Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī** — The supreme goddess of devotion and Kṛṣṇa's foremost beloved; the queen of Vṛndāvana whose mercy is the easier path to Kṛṣṇa.
- **Kṛṣṇa** — The Supreme Lord; here shown as the inner inspirer within every heart and the chief propagator of Rādhārāṇī's glories.
- **Vṛndāvana (Vraja)** — The transcendental land of Kṛṣṇa's intimate pastimes; the realm whose mood is one of sweet, loving play.
- **Vṛndāveśvarī (Vṛndāvaneśvarī)** — 'Queen of Vṛndāvana' — the title under which Rādhārāṇī was publicly crowned and bathed as sovereign of Vṛndāvana.
- **Śyāmasundara** — 'The beautiful dark-hued one,' a name of Kṛṣṇa; the beloved for whose sake Rādhārāṇī accepts the reign of Vṛndāvana.
- **Lalitā** — One of Rādhārāṇī's principal intimate companions (sakhīs); in whose braid the flute is described as hidden during the teasing play of Vraja.
- **Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra** — The great chant for deliverance; its address 'Hare' invokes Rādhārāṇī, so Her name is present eight times within it.
- **Hare** — The vocative address within the mahā-mantra that invokes Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī (Harā), making chanting a direct appeal to Her.
- **japa** — The practice of personal, repeated chanting of the holy names, here to be offered consciously as shelter at Rādhārāṇī's feet.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord and the practice of chanting it.
- **Rādhe Rādhe** — A spontaneous greeting and invocation of Rādhārāṇī's name, auspicious wherever and by whomever it is uttered.
- **sankalpa** — A conscious vow or resolved intention — here, the deliberate offering of one's chanting to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī.
