# 09 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 03 04 Evening Session ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This evening session at the Kirtan Course leads participants through the transcendental drama of Lord Caitanya's own saṅkīrtana as a template for their chanting. Mahārāja opens in early Navadvīpa, where the golden-limbed Lord — radiant as molten gold — inaugurated the movement by leading devotees each evening along the Ganga to the house of Śrīvāsa Ṭhākura to chant together, invoking the intimate associates (Nityānanda, Advaita, Gadādhara, Śrīvāsa, Vidyānidhi, Haridāsa) whose remembrance purifies the heart. From there the talk traces the Holy Name's overwhelming potency: Gaurāṅga roaring 'Hari!' like a thousand lions until the coverings of the universe seemed to shatter, weeping in the mood of separation (vipralambha) for the lotus-eyed treasure of his life, manifesting the eight sāttvika-bhāvas in his body, and passing through the full cycle of separation and reunion (milana). His ecstatic dancing — floating above the devotees, radiant with color — reveals that even ordinary devotees' dancing purifies the earth and the heavens, so his own is beyond estimation. The Name is shown to be indiscriminately merciful: it liberated Ajāmila, turned a criminal into a saint, and breaks the chains of saṁsāra for all who merely hear it. Mahārāja then grounds this in method and mood — Jagadānanda Paṇḍita's eyewitness instruction that the Name must be chanted in the association of devotees, lest it become mere nāmābhāsa or nāmāparādha; the keeping of bhukti, mukti and siddhi at a distance; the serving of the three grades of devotee (kaniṣṭha, madhyama, uttama) as the very purpose of one's work; and the eternal, presently-manifesting pastimes of the Lord in Māyāpur, seen by the pure of heart (as in Jaiva-dharma). The arc closes by turning kīrtana into a sacred arena of prayer: participants are urged to solidify a firm kīrtana-saṅkalpa, to recognize that real qualification comes not from technique but from empowerment (śakti) begged for in prayer and conferred by the devotees' blessings, and finally to plead directly for the Lord's mercy (kṛpā) — since mercy, not one's own endeavor, is the very root of success in devotional service, to be begged for even by the unfortunate who make no effort to attain it.

## Practical application

Let your daily japa and Hari-Nāma be shaped by three movements this lecture models. First, chant in the association of devotees whenever possible: Jagadānanda Paṇḍita warns that the Name spoken among non-devotees may leave the mouth but will not be śuddha-nāma, slipping into nāmābhāsa or offensive nāmāparādha — so attend the evening kīrtanas and hold the devotees' company as the ground of your chanting, keeping the desires for sense enjoyment (bhukti), liberation (mukti), and mystic powers (siddhi) at a distance. Second, deepen the chanting with meditation and mood: as you sing, picture with the mind's eye the golden Lord leading the devotees along the Ganga to Śrīvāsa's house, and let your heart lean toward the mood of separation — crying inwardly for Kṛṣṇa — since that burning longing, not mechanical completion of rounds, is the highest attainment of chanting. Third, make chanting a sacred arena of prayer: form and hold a firm kīrtana-saṅkalpa dedicating your japa to a definite aim rather than leaving it casual, and instead of trusting technique alone, beg directly for the Lord's mercy — 'Show your mercy to me' — approaching him as fully compassionate, because mercy (kṛpā) is the root of all success and even the unfortunate who make no endeavor should still plead for it. Alongside this, serve the devotees without discrimination — the beginner (kaniṣṭha), the constant chanter (madhyama), and the advanced (uttama) — treating such service never as an interruption but as the very purpose of your work, following Mahāprabhu who took the dust of the devotees' feet declaring himself their servant. Take up kīrtan in full seriousness now, for dancing in kīrtan is the entry point to eternal service in the rāsa-līlā of Vṛndāvana.

## Glossary

- **saṅkīrtana** — Congregational chanting of the Holy Name; the movement Lord Caitanya inaugurated in the house of Śrīvāsa Ṭhākura, where the devotees of Navadvīpa gathered each evening to chant together.
- **kīrtana** — Chanting and singing of the Lord's names and glories; here treated not only as devotional song but as a sacred arena for prayer and meditation.
- **Gaurāṅga / Mahāprabhu** — Lord Caitanya, who appeared in Navadvīpa with a golden complexion (gaura = golden) as the fountainhead and leader of the saṅkīrtana movement.
- **Śacīnandana** — 'The son of Śacī' — a name celebrating Lord Caitanya's bond with Mother Sacī, whose love for him was so great that Kṛṣṇa covered her consciousness so she would not see him fall senseless in ecstasy.
- **vipralambha** — The mood of separation from Kṛṣṇa; awakened by the Holy Name, it moved Mahāprabhu to weep for hours for the lotus-eyed Lord, and is described as the highest attainment of chanting.
- **milana** — The mood of meeting or reunion with the Lord that ripens out of separation; after hours of calling, Mahāprabhu would suddenly perceive Kṛṣṇa present and laugh in the joy of reunion.
- **sāttvika-bhāva** — The eight ecstatic bodily transformations of pure love manifested during chanting — such as becoming immovably heavy then feather-light, burning hot then icy cold — symptoms of divine ecstasy, not ordinary bodily states.
- **kṛṣṇa-prema / prema** — Pure love of Godhead; the treasure Mahāprabhu distributed indiscriminately, and the only thing the devotees ask of the Lord when he offers them whatever they wish.
- **saṁsāra** — The cycle of material existence and bondage; its chains are said to fall away simply by hearing the name of Lord Caitanya.
- **śuddha-nāma** — The pure Holy Name, chanted without offense; attained in the association of devotees, in contrast to its two deviations below.
- **nāmābhāsa** — A dim reflection or 'clearing stage' of the Name — sound that leaves the mouth but falls short of the pure Name; a result of chanting among non-devotees, and an obstacle to pure devotion.
- **nāmāparādha** — Offensive chanting of the Holy Name, which, like nāmābhāsa, obstructs the attainment of pure devotion to Kṛṣṇa.
- **bhukti** — The desire for sense enjoyment; to be kept at a distance by one who wishes to chant the Holy Names purely.
- **mukti** — The desire for impersonal liberation; likewise to be kept at a distance from the aspiring chanter.
- **siddhi** — The desire for mystic or yogic powers; the third of the three desires to be renounced in order to chant purely.
- **kaniṣṭha-adhikārī** — The beginner-grade devotee; the first of the three grades of devotee Mahāprabhu described and instructed his followers to serve.
- **madhyama-adhikārī** — The intermediate devotee, characterized by continuous chanting; the level one rises to by chanting the holy names without cessation.
- **uttama-adhikārī** — The advanced, first-class devotee, so filled with Kṛṣṇa that merely seeing him inspires others to chant 'Hare Kṛṣṇa.'
- **rāsa dance** — The intimate transcendental dance of Kṛṣṇa in Vṛndāvana; dancing in kīrtan now is described as the entry point to this eternal service.
- **kīrtana-saṅkalpa** — A firm inner resolve or vow dedicating one's chanting to a definite spiritual aim, to be consciously solidified rather than left as casual practice.
- **śakti** — Spiritual empowerment; the chanter's real qualification, begged for through prayer and conferred by the blessings of the assembled devotees, enabling one to become an instrument voicing the Holy Name.
- **kṛpā** — The Lord's mercy or compassion; identified as the very root of success in devotional service, to be begged for directly ('Show your mercy to me') even by one who makes no endeavor to attain it.
- **tapas** — Austerity or penance; invoked in the glory of the Name — Vālmīki is said to perform tapas for Lord Caitanya.
- **Harinām Cintāmaṇi** — The text of Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura on the Holy Name, woven into the kīrtana as the basis of a guided prayer session, showing that the Name and heartfelt prayer belong together.
- **Jaiva-dharma** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's work; its chapter 21 illustrates how the Lord's eternal Navadvīpa pastimes manifest directly before pure-hearted devotees, who are served by Śacīdevī, Sītā Devī and Mālinī Devī and then left weeping in ecstasy.
