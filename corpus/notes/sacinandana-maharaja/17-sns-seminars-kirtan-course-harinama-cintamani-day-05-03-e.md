# 17 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 05 03 Evening Session ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This evening session (Day 5 of the Harināma Cintāmaṇi kīrtana course at ISKCON Rādhādeśa) weaves the story of Kholāvecā Śrīdhara into a meditation on what actually attracts Kṛṣṇa and how the holy name is to be chanted. Mahārāja opens with the cluster of qualities that shelter in a devotee's heart like bees in a hive — humility (feeling lower than a blade of grass), gentleness, sweet speech, disinterest in sense gratification, and forbearance — and then embodies them in Kholāvecā Śrīdhara: a banana-seller so poor he lived in a leaking hut with one torn cloth, yet as honest as Yudhiṣṭhira, who kept only half his meager income for himself and gave the rest to the worship of Mother Ganga. Kṛṣṇa, relishing intimate reciprocation, came daily disguised as a beautiful brahmin boy to quarrel with Śrīdhara for two hours over the price of bananas; when Śrīdhara recognized His divinity he surrendered utterly — 'I am your dog; take what you want, give what you want, because I love you.' From this, Mahārāja draws the central law of bhakti: the Lord relishes the smallest gift offered in love and ignores the costliest offerings of non-devotees; it is devotion, not the value of the gift, that draws Him. The lecture then turns to the holy name itself — the devotees dearest to Kṛṣṇa are those who chant day and night with love, minds free of material desire, calling with ever-increasing devotion; because the Lord knows He is controlled by that love, He never leaves them, remaining as ever-present as the sky overhead. Chanting is therefore to be a longing call — 'O Hari, O Govinda, O only goal of my life' — not mechanical repetition. This is illustrated through the Śrīvāsa darśana narrative, where Caitanya transforms before the devotee into blackish Kṛṣṇa flanked by Balarāma, surrounded by Brahmā, Śiva, the sages and gopīs beneath Ananta Śeṣa's canopy — revealing the Holy Name's source as the Supreme Lord Himself. The Lord treasures humble, babbling prayer over eloquence (as a parent hears a child's da-da, ga-ga as the speech of a great paṇḍita), empowers the devotee's very ability to praise by commanding Sarasvatī onto his tongue, and is conquered by nothing but bhakti — Bhīṣma, Yaśodā, and Satyabhāmā all defeated Him through love, not power. Śrī Caitanya's unique gift is that He has shattered the concealment with which the Lord ordinarily guards bhakti like a hidden treasure, freely distributing love of God through the chanting of His own names. The arc returns to Śrīdhara, who, offered any boon, rejected wealth, a kingdom and even the aṣṭa-siddhi, asking only to eternally re-enact the loving argument over banana-prices — and received prema-bhakti not given even to Brahmā or Śiva. The Lord's greed for His devotee's love is dramatized when He seizes and drinks the foot-water from Śrīdhara's patched iron pot, and when, on His last night in Navadvīpa, He accepts Śrīdhara's humble bottle-gourd and has Mother Śacī cook it — proving Bhakta-vatsala Kṛṣṇa accepts offerings for the love in them, not the substance. Mahārāja closes with Caitanya's final Māyāpur order — chant, glorify, and let no sound but 'Krishna' leave the lips, waking, eating or sleeping — and Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's observation that the devotee is told to chant twenty-four hours a day and never told to stop. The sweet secret of Śrīdhara, eternally Kṛṣṇa's associate Kusumāsava Gopāl, is this: learn to call out for Kṛṣṇa, for the ever-present Lord can never be separated from the one who ceaselessly calls His name; materially destitute Śrīdhara was supremely rich because he was rich in bhakti — and that devotional wealth is the true wellness to pursue.

## Practical application

For the practitioner, the session reframes japa from a quota to be finished into a heartfelt calling out. Chant the way Śrīdhara loved — 'O Hari, O Govinda, O only goal of my life' — cultivating longing rather than mechanical repetition, remembering that the devotees dearest to Kṛṣṇa are those who chant day and night with a mind free of material desire and with ever-increasing devotion. Take heart from the Lord's tenderness toward inarticulate prayer: you need not be learned or eloquent, for He receives a devotee's stammering words as a parent receives a child's babbling, and He Himself grants the very ability to glorify Him — so chant without self-consciousness. Approach the holy name in the mood of the qualities Mahārāja names at the outset: humility lower than a blade of grass, sweet speech, forbearance, and disinterest in sense gratification. Let Śrīdhara's honesty-in-poverty and his prioritizing of devotional service above personal necessity govern daily life, so that integrity and service to the Lord are never compromised by material lack. Offer whatever small thing you have — a bottle-gourd, foot-water, an innocent word — knowing the Lord relishes the love in the gift, not its value, and never lets a devotee's offering be wasted. Value the sweet exchange (rasa) of loving service above every material boon and even liberation, as Śrīdhara refused wealth, kingdom and mystic powers for the sake of his loving quarrel with the Lord. Seek the association and remnants of devotees — their foot-dust and foot-water are named as substances that awaken prema — and prize a devotional atmosphere over physical comfort. Above all, take Caitanya Mahāprabhu's final instruction as personal: think of nothing but Kṛṣṇa whether eating, sleeping or waking, and let the chanting continue toward twenty-four hours a day — for there is instruction to begin chanting but never any instruction to stop, and the constant call keeps the ever-present Lord always at hand.

## Glossary

- **Harināma / Holy Name** — The names of Kṛṣṇa, whose source is revealed to be the Supreme Lord Himself; chanting them with love is the central practice of the age.
- **japa** — The devotee's personal repetition of the holy names; the lecture urges it be done as a longing call to the Lord rather than mechanical recitation.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational or call-and-response glorification of the Lord through song, such as 'Jaya Jaya Mahāprabhu, Jaya Viśvambhara.'
- **saṅkīrtana** — Congregational chanting of the holy names begun by Śrī Caitanya as the yuga-dharma; the movement through which He freely distributes bhakti.
- **bhakti** — Devotional service to the Lord; the sole means by which He is captured, kept hidden from the Vedas and the general populace yet freely given by Caitanya.
- **prema-bhakti / prema** — Pure love of God, the highest gift, granted to Śrīdhara though withheld even from Brahmā and Śiva.
- **Bhakta-vatsala** — The Lord as affectionate to His devotees, who never lets a devotee's offering be wasted.
- **darśana** — Direct sight of the Lord, which overwhelms the devotee with awe, wonder and ecstatic love, as when Śrīvāsa fell flat before Mahāprabhu.
- **sukṛti** — 'Beautiful/pious activities' — the special potency the Lord transmits through His loving sidelong glance, empowering the devotee to perform devotional service.
- **rasa** — The sweet loving exchange or relished mellow between the Lord and His devotee, valued by Śrīdhara above all opulence and liberation.
- **aṣṭa-siddhi** — The eight mystic perfections, which Śrīdhara rejected when offered any boon.
- **paṇḍita** — A learned scholar; the Lord hears a devotee's simple words as though they were the speech of a great paṇḍita.
- **gopīs** — The cowherd maidens of Vraja, present with folded hands in the darśana of Kṛṣṇa.
- **tamāl** — A dark tree whose blackish hue is compared to Kṛṣṇa's complexion.
- **Ananta Śeṣa** — The thousand-hooded serpent whose canopy spreads overhead in the vision of the Lord.
- **devatās** — The demigods (e.g., Brahmā and Śiva) who surround the Lord during the revealed vision.
- **kuṇḍa** — A sacred pond; the 64 kuṇḍas built by Viśvakarmā for the saṅkīrtana party's water mark the eternal pastimes at Māyāpur.
- **Māyāpur / Madhuvan** — The holy land of Caitanya's birth, said to be non-different from the sacred forests of Vṛndāvana (corresponding to Madhuvan).
- **tomar kukur** — 'Your dog' — Śrīdhara's words of complete surrender to the Lord, abandoning all bargaining and resting on humble love.
- **Kholāvecā / Kusumāsava Gopāl** — Kholāvecā Śrīdhara, the poor banana-leaf seller; eternally Kṛṣṇa's associate Kusumāsava Gopāl of Vṛndāvana, asked to descend for an exclusive loving exchange with the Lord.
- **Sarasvatī** — The goddess of learning, whom the Lord secretly commands to appear on Śrīvāsa's tongue so he can speak the prayers.
