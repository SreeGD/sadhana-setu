# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 06 2014 09 19 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

Built around the life of Mahārāja Kulaśekhara — the devotee enshrined on the temple threshold as the emblem of faith — this lecture frames śraddhā (faith) as the single gateway into bhakti and into the chanting of the Holy Name. Mahārāja opens at that threshold, where the dust of the devotees is honored as mahā-mahā-prasāda and where hearing the history of a faith-filled devotee multiplies one's own faith a hundred thousand times. He establishes that faith is personified as Śraddhā Devī, robed in white, who must be prayed into the heart; that faith is not self-generated but received through association with and hearing about the faithful; and that even an unqualified speaker's words become potent when they glorify Vaiṣṇavas, just as ordinary water is sanctified by bathing the Śālagrāma Śilā. The body of the talk narrates Kulaśekhara's story: the Kaustubha gem, repository of all jīvas, descends as a prince who falls under rajas and tamas, is reclaimed by the mercy of the Lord through His devotees, and beholds the Lord's smile in Vaikuṇṭha — the dormant love in the heart rising to conquer all material desire. As king, Kulaśekhara models the supremacy of devotee-worship over even worship of the Lord: humble concrete service, honoring remnants, trusting only Vaiṣṇavas, and rapt absorption in hearing the Rāmāyaṇa so complete that the līlā became present reality and he repeatedly mobilized his army to rush to Rāma's aid — once nearly drowning, until Rāma personally lifted him from the water and consoled him with the promise of reunion, his ecstasy showing the bodily symptoms of prema. When envious ministers slandered the Vaiṣṇavas, the king staked his life in a cobra-ordeal to vindicate them, illustrating the central teaching: glorifying Hari and His devotees washes away all sins, even the offenses of an offender, while Vaiṣṇava-aparādha — criticizing the devotees — is the gravest, dooming offense. The mature conclusion of his life is renunciation: handing the kingdom to a worthy heir and retiring to Raṅganātha at Śrīraṅgam to sweep the temple in menial service. The lecture closes by turning his Mukunda-mālā-stotra into practical instruction — beg that the royal swan of the mind enter Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet THIS very day, before death chokes the throat with kapha, vāta, and pitta — and by the rāja-haṁsa analogy: as the swan pierces Himalayan storms to reach the Mānasa lake, the chanter must try again and again to settle the mind on the mantra, even learning to ride the mind's restless energy into the Holy Name. Kīrtana is to be a 'crying melody' of longing, and the true wealth of a Holy-Name retreat is chanting together with devotees.

## Practical application

For the devotee at japa and Hari-Nāma, the lecture prescribes faith first: cultivate and increase śraddhā at the very threshold of practice, because faith is the one gate into bhakti, and it is received — by hearing the lives of faith-filled devotees like Kulaśekhara and by associating with and serving Vaiṣṇavas, honoring their remnants and the dust of their feet in a mood of humility. Make hearing of the Lord's pastimes so absorbed that the līlā becomes present and real, since genuine hearing dissolves the distance between hearer and the Lord and feeds the dormant love in the heart. In the act of chanting, adopt Kulaśekhara's prayer as a personal determination: 'I will concentrate now — not tomorrow, not after some indulgence,' begging the swan of the mind to enter Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet today while one is still healthy, because at death the throat is choked and remembrance is impossible. Expect the mind to resist like the swan facing Himalayan storms; the discipline is not to win once but to try and try again — when the mind drifts, immediately re-engage it with fresh resolve, and beyond merely fighting the storm, learn to ride the mind's own restless energy, redirecting its disturbance into concentration on the Name. Let the tune of kīrtana be a 'crying melody,' a heartfelt call of longing and separation rather than performance. Guard above all against Vaiṣṇava-aparādha — never criticize or find fault with devotees, since glorifying them purifies even an offender while offending them is ruinous. And treat collective chanting as the heart of practice: the gifts of the Holy Name are bestowed when one chants together with devotees, so the retreat's real work is shared kīrtana, not solitary effort alone.

## Glossary

- **śraddhā** — Faith; here the abiding faith in the Lord, His names, His abode, and His servants that is the sole gateway into devotional service. Personified as the white-robed goddess Śraddhā Devī.
- **Śraddhā Devī** — The personification of faith as a goddess robed in white signifying purity from all material things, whom the devotee prays to seat in the heart.
- **bhakti** — Devotional service to the Lord; the 'palace' into which śraddhā is the one gate of entry.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name of the Lord (Hari); the practice of chanting it, and the focus of the retreat.
- **mahā-mahā-prasāda** — Literally 'the great-great mercy'; here the honored dust of the devotees and of the temple threshold, smeared on the body as a token of reverence for the devotees' feet.
- **prasāda / prasādam** — Sanctified food remnants offered to the Lord and to Vaiṣṇavas; honoring it — even the spot a Vaiṣṇava has touched — expresses humility before devotees.
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee of the Lord; worship of Vaiṣṇavas is held higher than worship of the Lord Himself, and only the devotee of His devotee is His real devotee.
- **Vaiṣṇava-aparādha** — Offense against a Vaiṣṇava (devotee); presented as the gravest offense, which dooms one even though glorifying the same devotees is supremely purifying.
- **kathā** — Sacred narration or recitation of the Lord's pastimes; rapt hearing of kathā makes the līlā present and dissolves the distance between listener and the pastime.
- **līlā** — The transcendental pastimes of the Lord; deep absorption in hearing makes the līlā an immediate, living reality for the devotee.
- **prema** — Pure ecstatic love of God; manifested in Kulaśekhara as gushing tears and a voice so choked he could only stammer 'Rām, Rām, Rām.'
- **kīrtana** — Congregational singing of the Holy Name; meant to be a 'crying melody' expressing longing and separation, and most potent when performed collectively with devotees.
- **jīva** — The individual living soul; the Kaustubha gem is described as the repository of all jīvas.
- **Kaustubha** — The jewel worn by the Lord, here an eternal divine paraphernalia that descended as the prince Kulaśekhara, son of King Dṛḍha-vrata.
- **rajas** — The mode of passion; under its sway the princely incarnation became absorbed in horses, contests, and weaponry — 'a knight of rajas.'
- **tamas** — The mode of ignorance; together with rajas it overwhelmed the prince raised in material absorption.
- **Vaikuṇṭha** — The eternal spiritual world of the Lord; beholding the Lord's smile there awakened the prince's dormant love and finished his material life.
- **Śālagrāma Śilā** — The sacred stone form of the Lord; the water that bathes it becomes sanctified, an analogy for how even an ordinary soul's words become potent when they glorify Vaiṣṇavas.
- **Rāmāyaṇa** — The epic of Lord Rāma; Kulaśekhara heard it day and night with such absorption that nothing could distract him.
- **Mukunda-mālā-stotra** — Kulaśekhara's devotional poem; its opening prayer begs that the swan of the mind enter Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet this very day.
- **rāja-haṁsa** — The royal swan/goose that pierces fierce Himalayan storms to reach the lotus-filled Mānasa lake; an analogy for the mind struggling to settle on the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra.
- **Mānasa** — The lotus-filled Himalayan lake the royal swan strives to reach; figure for the mind's destination at Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet.
- **kapha, vāta, pitta** — The mucus, air, and bile (the three bodily humors) that choke the throat at death, making remembrance of the Lord then impossible — hence the urgency to cultivate it now.
- **Goloka Vṛndāvana** — The supreme spiritual abode, the place of Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, named as the goal toward which the practitioner strives.
- **Raṅganātha** — The form of the Lord at Śrīraṅgam (Raṅga-kṣetra) whom Kulaśekhara served, sweeping the temple in menial devotion after renouncing his kingdom.
