# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 10 2014 09 20 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

Drawing on Caitanya Mahāprabhu's own life, this lecture argues that the single qualification for seeing Kṛṣṇa and relishing His holy name is bhakti — not learning, social status, beauty, or even moral spotlessness. Mahārāja begins with the principle that by studying what moved the heart of Caitanya Mahāprabhu, a devotee learns how to grow rich in devotion. He shows that Yogamāyā normally veils the Lord, and only when the Lord lifts that curtain — as in the 21-hour manifestation at Śrīvāsa's house — can He be directly seen, and that the Lord secretly protects His devotees even before His advent (the boatman who saved Gaṅgādāsa's family was the Lord Himself). The fallen are never disqualified, for the Lord is Patita-pāvana; what He will not tolerate is the duplicity of the 'straw-stick man' who feigns humility yet blasphemes behind one's back — the fault for which the peerless singer Mukunda was refused darśana. The heart of the lecture is the extended story of Mukunda: his magnetic pure kīrtana that drew the Navadvīpa bhaktas like bees to honey, young Nimāi's staged mockery and challenge to dry debate (which Vaiṣṇavas know to avoid), the Lord's deliberate engineering of Mukunda's rejection to instruct the world, and Mukunda's response of unlimited patience and faith — rejoicing and dancing 'I will see, I will see' even when told it would take ten million births. From this Mahārāja draws the central instruction: the secret of kīrtana is humility (the true devotee feels he has no bhakti at all), and one should never pray for Kṛṣṇa's darśana but only for bhakti, since Duryodhana, Hiraṇyakaśipu and others saw the Lord yet 'saw nothing' for lack of devotion, while hunchbacked Kubjā — a social reject — recognized and served Him because she had bhakti. He clarifies that bhakti is both the way (sādhana — kīrtana, hearing the Bhāgavatam) and the goal (bhāva- and prema-bhakti), and that we practice the way precisely to gain the eyes that perceive Kṛṣṇa. The lecture closes by establishing kīrtana as the best process — revealed by Caitanya Mahāprabhu through Mukunda the kīrtana leader — to be treated as therapeutic and entered whole-heartedly, with Mukunda's prayer on the devotee's lips: 'My Lord, shower my useless heart with Your bhakti.'

## Practical application

Reframe your japa and Hari-Nāma as a plea for devotion rather than a demand for experience. Do not chant in order to get Kṛṣṇa's darśana, a vision, or a quick taste of prema; chant to beg for bhakti itself, since only the eyes given by devotion can recognize the Lord even when He stands before you. Make Mukunda's prayer your own — 'My Lord, shower my useless heart with Your bhakti' — and let humility, the genuine sense that you have no devotion at all, be the mood you bring to the holy name. Treat kīrtana and japa as therapeutic: enter them whole-heartedly with the single aim of leaving with more devotion in the heart than you brought. Cultivate Mukunda's patience and faith: do not lose heart or abandon the process when prema does not appear in a few days — measure success by sincerity of begging, not by speed of results. Guard against the one fault the Lord will not tolerate — duplicity; let your chanting and your conduct outside the saṅgha match, never praising bhakti in kīrtana while dismissing it elsewhere. Take encouragement that no degree of fallenness disqualifies you, since the Lord is Patita-pāvana, and that worldly status, learning, or beauty count for nothing — like Kubjā, the lowest by worldly measure can serve Him if there is devotion. Finally, prize the association of advanced devotees and pure kīrtana, whose spiritual potency is magnetic, and avoid dry argument and debate, which cannot satisfy the heart that seeks the nectar of the name. Remember that the practice (sādhana — chanting, hearing the Bhāgavatam) is the very means by which the goal (bhāva and prema) ripens, so engage in it daily as both the path and the seed of the love it aims at.

## Glossary

- **bhakti** — Devotion; loving devotional service to the Lord. Spoken of in two senses — as the way (sādhana, the service we render) and as the goal (love that is maturing or matured). The sole qualification by which one can recognize and see Kṛṣṇa.
- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — Kṛṣṇa appearing as the golden avatāra in Navadvīpa, who taught love of God through the congregational chanting of the holy name; also called Gaurāṅga and, in His youth, Nimāi Paṇḍit.
- **Yogamāyā** — The Lord's internal spiritual potency, here described as the 'curtain' that veils God from the conditioned living entity until the Lord chooses to remove it.
- **Patita-pāvana** — 'Savior (purifier) of the fallen' — a name of the Lord expressing that no degree of fallenness disqualifies one from His mercy; He never abandons even a fallen devotee.
- **darśana** — Seeing or being in the presence of the Lord (or a holy person); here, the direct vision of the Lord that is granted only through bhakti.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational glorification of the Lord through song; the best process for awakening devotion, revealed by Caitanya Mahāprabhu through Mukunda the kīrtana leader.
- **tṛṇa-daṇḍī** — Literally 'straw-stick man' — the duplicitous person who feigns humility to one's face but blasphemes behind one's back; the pretense the Lord will not tolerate.
- **saṅgha** — The assembly or association of devotees; the spiritual community in which kīrtana and devotional practice are shared.
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa; one who, knowing the limits of dry logic, does not entangle himself in useless argument but seeks the taste of the holy name.
- **prema / prema-bhakti** — Fully matured, perfected love of God; the ultimate goal of devotional practice.
- **bhāva / bhāva-bhakti** — The dawning, maturing stage of love of God that precedes prema; bhakti as the goal in its ripening phase.
- **sādhana / sādhana-bhakti** — Bhakti as the way — the regulated devotional service one practices (kīrtana, hearing the Bhāgavatam, and the other processes) to gain the eyes that perceive Kṛṣṇa.
- **Bhāgavatam** — Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, the foremost devotional scripture; hearing it is one of the central processes of sādhana-bhakti.
- **Nṛsiṁha / Narasiṁha** — The Lord's half-man, half-lion incarnation; emanated from the Lord's body during the manifestation at Śrīvāsa's house, and the form before whom Hiraṇyakaśipu stood yet 'saw nothing' for want of devotion.
- **Śyāmasundara** — 'The beautiful dark-complexioned one' — a name of Kṛṣṇa; one of the forms that emanated during the 21-hour manifestation at Śrīvāsa's house.
- **Kubjā (Trivakrā)** — The hunchbacked woman of Mathurā who, a 'reject' by all worldly standards, recognized and served the Lord with sandalwood — proof that devotion, not status or beauty, qualifies one to see Kṛṣṇa.
- **universal form (viśva-rūpa)** — The Lord's cosmic manifestation; beheld by the devotionless Duryodhana, who 'saw but saw nothing' because only bhakti grants the eyes to truly perceive the Lord.
- **Mukunda** — The peerless kīrtana singer through whom Caitanya Mahāprabhu revealed kīrtana as the best process; his rejection, patience, humility, and prayer for bhakti form the central teaching of the lecture.
