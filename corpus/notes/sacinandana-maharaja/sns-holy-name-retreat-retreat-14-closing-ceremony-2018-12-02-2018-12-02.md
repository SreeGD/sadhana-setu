# SNS Holy Name Retreat Retreat 14 Closing Ceremony 2018 12 02 GV

## Theme

Delivered at the close of a Holy-Name Retreat, this lecture frames chanting as a deliberate turn inward: just as one can flip an internal 'switch' from external engagement to internal absorption (closing the eyes and calling up the form of Giriraj), so meditation begins by consciously choosing to go within. Mahārāja establishes that the holy name is wholly transcendental while our tongue and senses are material, so Krishna cannot be reached by our own effort — yet, out of mercy, He reveals Himself to whoever turns to Him in sincere service. This is illustrated through devotees who attracted the Lord not by fame but by heartfelt service: Kolāveca Śrīdhara, Śuklāmbara Brahmacārī, and Vijaya the scribe. Practically, chanting deepens through three progressively engaged faculties — body (sitting properly, clear pronunciation, attentive listening), mind (repeatedly refocusing on the transcendental syllables), and heart (addressing the Lord feelingly in prayer) — and turning to Him with all three is itself devotional service. The lecture then grounds the practice in sambandha, one's eternal relationship with Krishna: drawing on Tapana Miśra (who learned from Nimāi that the holy name is at once the means and the goal of life), Jagadānanda Paṇḍit (the wandering soul awakened by a saint to its identity as Krishna's eternal servant), and Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's threefold self-meditation (an atomic particle of consciousness, distinct from body and mind, an eternal blissful servant of Krishna). The material world is described as a 'house of correction' whose only real duty is to reform the heart's tendency to remain absent from Krishna, with chanting and kīrtana as the means of calling Him back. Finally, Mahārāja teaches internal pilgrimage (smaraṇa) — mentally re-walking the sacred places of Vraja such as Surabhi-kuṇḍa and Ter Kadamba and chanting there, begging mercy and forgiveness from the authorities (Rūpa Gosvāmī, Prabhupāda, the deities of Vraja, Giriraj Govardhana, and the holy names themselves). The dhām's accelerated gift of Kṛṣṇa consciousness is to be remembered and returned to as a source of encouragement, guidance, and inspiration. The lecture closes by praying directly to the holy name in the words of Rūpa Gosvāmī, hands folded over the heart, begging the Name to appear and dance upon the tongue.

## Practical application

Begin each japa session by consciously flipping the inner 'switch' from outward engagement to inward absorption — close the eyes and call up a sacred form (such as Giriraj) to settle the attention before the first round. Chant through the three faculties in sequence: with the body, sit properly, pronounce each syllable clearly, and listen attentively; with the mind, gently and repeatedly bring wandering attention back onto the transcendental syllables, beginning with 'Hare'; and with the heart, address Krishna feelingly, offering a prayer that acknowledges long forgetfulness and now offers oneself as a sincere, serious servant. Hold the mood of sambandha throughout — 'I belong to You' — using Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's threefold reflection (I am an atomic spark of consciousness, distinct from body and mind, an eternal joyful servant of Krishna) to anchor identity. Treat the chanting as the work of reforming the heart's tendency to stay away from Krishna rather than as a ritual obligation. When away from Vraja, practice internal pilgrimage: vividly re-walk its holy places in the mind, chant there mentally with attention, and beg forgiveness and mercy at places like Surabhi-kuṇḍa, addressing the authorities and the holy names again and again as those who have witnessed everything and can fulfill all desires. Return repeatedly to the remembered gift of the dhām for fresh encouragement and inspiration. Close the session by folding the hands over the heart and praying directly to the holy name — in Rūpa Gosvāmī's words — for it to sweetly appear and dance upon the tongue.

## Glossary

- **Giriraj** — Giriraj Govardhana — the sacred Govardhana Hill, worshiped as non-different from Krishna; here the form called up in the mind's eye to begin meditation.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari/Krishna); the practice of chanting the names of Krishna.
- **japa** — Soft, individual repetition of the holy name, usually counted on beads.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational or vocal glorification of the Lord through chanting His names.
- **sambandha** — One's eternal relationship with Krishna; the foundational knowledge of how the soul is connected to the Lord.
- **māyā** — The Lord's illusory material energy that covers the soul's true identity and keeps it bound in the material world.
- **smaraṇa** — Remembrance; the meditative practice of vividly recalling the Lord, His pastimes, and His holy places — here, internal pilgrimage to Vraja.
- **dhām** — The holy abode of the Lord (e.g., Vraja/Vṛndāvana), non-different from Krishna and able to bestow accelerated Kṛṣṇa consciousness.
- **Vraja** — The sacred land of Krishna's pastimes around Vṛndāvana, the focus of the internal pilgrimage described.
- **Surabhi-kuṇḍa** — A holy pond in Vraja, site of the Govinda-abhiṣeka where Surabhi the cow and Indra bathed Krishna; also a place where offenses are forgiven.
- **Govinda-abhiṣeka** — The ceremonial bathing and coronation in which Krishna was crowned 'Govinda,' protector and master of the cows.
- **Ter Kadamba** — A sacred site in Vraja associated with Rūpa Gosvāmī, recalled during internal pilgrimage.
- **brahmacārī** — A celibate student or monk in the renounced student order; e.g., Śuklāmbara Brahmacārī.
- **Paṇḍit** — A learned scholar; an honorific, as in Nimāi Paṇḍit (Caitanya Mahāprabhu) and Jagadānanda Paṇḍit.
- **vīṇā** — A stringed musical instrument; in the closing prayer the holy name is glorified as the very life of Nārada's vīṇā.
- **Mahāprabhu** — Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, Krishna appearing as the great devotee who spread the chanting of the holy name.
