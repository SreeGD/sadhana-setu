# SNS Holy Name Retreat Retreat 09 Introduction 2018 11 29 GV

## Theme

In this introductory talk for a Holy-Name Retreat, HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja frames chanting as an exercise in cultivating the right inner mood. He opens with the scriptural counsel that devotees who wish to deepen their love for Krishna should go to the līlā-sthānas — the places of the Lord's pastimes — and there absorb themselves exclusively in His Holy Names. He clarifies that the scriptural image of singing 'with long-drawn-out voices' points not to a musical technique but to longing: the inner yearning behind the sound is what counts. He then draws two lessons from nature's birds. From the cātaka (cakora) bird, who refuses all ordinary water and thirsts only for rain from the dark Ghanaśyāma monsoon cloud, the devotee learns exclusive longing — to reject every lesser shelter and cry out for Krishna alone, the cloud-like Lord who quenches all thirst. From the cakravāka bird, cursed to be separated from its mate and wandering in search of it, the devotee learns that the search for Krishna implies a personal bond — a sambandha — rather than abstract devotion. He gathers these into two guiding principles for chanting: first, the thirst to come close to Krishna and taste His mercy (rasa); and second, the desire to make one's first stumbling attempts to enter into a real loving relationship (sambandha) with Him.

## Practical application

Approach japa and Hari-Nāma as the cultivation of longing rather than mere repetition. Before chanting, kindle the mood of yearning — let the inner desire for Krishna, not the outer sound or melody, be what you offer. Practice the cātaka's exclusive thirst: refuse to settle for 'lesser waters' (lower shelters, distractions, or substitutes) and direct your whole appeal to Krishna alone as Ghanaśyāma, the cloud who alone can quench the heart's thirst. Carry the cakravāka's mood of relationship into the rounds: chant as one calling out to a dear person you are personally bound to and seeking, not to an abstraction. Hold the two principles together while chanting — a thirst to come near Krishna and taste His mercy (rasa), and the willingness to make even stumbling, beginning attempts at an actual loving relationship (sambandha) with Him. Where possible, deepen this by chanting in the līlā-sthānas, the holy places of the Lord's pastimes.

## Glossary

- **līlā-sthāna** — A place where the Lord performed His pastimes; a holy site recommended for absorbed chanting of the Holy Names.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name of the Lord (Hari); the practice of chanting Krishna's names.
- **japa** — Soft, often individual chanting of the Holy Names, typically counted on beads.
- **cātaka** — The legendary bird (also called cakora) said to drink only rainwater from the cloud, refusing all other water — an emblem of exclusive longing for Krishna alone.
- **cakora** — Another name used for the cātaka bird, which thirsts only for the rain of the monsoon cloud.
- **Ghanaśyāma** — 'Dark like a rain cloud' — a name of Krishna as the cloud-like Lord whose mercy, like monsoon rain, quenches the devotee's thirst.
- **cakravāka** — The ruddy-goose bird, said to be separated from its mate and to wander crying for its beloved — an emblem of the mood of relationship and the longing of separation.
- **sambandha** — One's personal relationship or loving bond with Krishna; devotion grounded in relationship rather than abstraction.
- **rasa** — The taste or sweet relish of loving exchange with Krishna; the mercy a devotee thirsts to savor through chanting.
