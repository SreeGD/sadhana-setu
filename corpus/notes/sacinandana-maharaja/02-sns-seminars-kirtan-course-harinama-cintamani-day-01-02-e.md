# 02 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 01 02 Evening Session ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This evening session opens HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja's Kirtan Course, founded on Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's Hari-nāma-cintāmaṇi — one of his last, ripe compositions, which records the historic dialogue on the holy name between Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Haridāsa Ṭhākura at Siddha Bakula. Mahārāja deliberately brings the session from the small opening ceremony into the temple room so the whole community can share in it, noting that a community's serious, deep effort in kirtan naturally attracts advanced devotees who add their own realization. The arc moves from method to mood. On method, he transmits Prabhupāda's simple instruction — chant sincerely and listen to your own chanting: trace the holy name as a circuit of the heart, rising from the heart, over the lips, and traveling to Krishna, so that attentive hearing draws the wandering mind into the sound. The book's own summary is equally practical: worship Kṛṣṇa, chant His name, follow His instructions. Mahārāja then names the real crisis of a mature devotee — not doubt in the process, but doubt in oneself, discovering through honest self-examination that after decades the heart remains untransformed and one's relationship with Krishna is still merely theoretical. The remedy is to keep faith in the perfection of the process (demonstrated by Prabhupāda, seen chanting even in sleep, never covered by ignorance), assume the fault is one's own, and study deeply how the process actually works. The title itself teaches this: cintāmaṇi is the touchstone that turns whatever it touches to gold, so the Holy Name, touching the mind, transforms the very heart. He sets the scene in Jagannātha Purī (Nīlācala), home of Lord Jagannātha and of Caitanya Mahāprabhu — the golden 'moving Brahman' and desire tree (kalpa-vṛkṣa) who grants even the rarest treasure, prema. Caitanya revealed the yuga-dharma through different associates, but entrusted the full glory of the Holy Name to Haridāsa Ṭhākura, the Namācārya, who answered only after grasping the Lord's feet and confessing his own unworthiness — modeling that authority to speak flows from humility and the Lord's mercy, not scholarship. The path, then, is to follow exactly in the footsteps of the ācāryas, illustrated by his grandfather's snowstorm lesson: when you can no longer see the North Star, your only safety is to step where one who knows the way has stepped. The session culminates in the mood of separation, vipralambha: not artificial claims to see Krishna, but felt longing — the more separation, the more advancement. The one price for the Lord is intense eagerness (laulyam), which Prabhupāda drew from Rūpa Gosvāmī; as longing grows to a boiling point, Krishna recognizes it and enters the heart as a concrete inner presence, even while the devotee remains outwardly in the material world. Paradoxically, separation is experienced not as loss but as the greatest bliss — the Lord simultaneously absent and present — and the advanced devotee even prefers it, for in meeting there is one Krishna, but in separation Krishna appears from every corner. Its practical remedy and nourishment is hearing and reading about Krishna, which is why the retreats speak about Him from morning till evening.

## Practical application

Reserve dedicated, guided japa time and protect it — a busy schedule of courses and duties can crowd out chanting itself, so deliberately set apart a session to immerse fully. In that time, apply Prabhupāda's whole method: chant sincerely and hear yourself. Keep count, but make each round a circuit of the heart — let the name rise from your heart, pass over your lips, and travel to Krishna, then hear it return; this attentive listening is what holds the wandering mind and draws it into the mantra, since the quality of the name depends on the chanter's inner absorption, not the tongue alone. When practice seems to stall and you doubt your own ability, do an honest self-examination (is the heart transformed? do I think of Krishna through the day?) — then keep faith in the process, assume 'I must be doing something wrong,' and study Hari-nāma-cintāmaṇi to learn Haridāsa Ṭhākura's step-by-step entry into the name. Chant in a mood of humility rather than scholarship, praying to be empowered and following exactly in the footsteps of the ācāryas as one follows a guide through a snowstorm — do not invent your own path. Above all, cultivate eagerness and the mood of separation (vipralambha): do not artificially claim to see Krishna, but let longing grow; intense greed for the Lord (laulyam), not accumulated pious deeds, is the only price, and as it reaches a boiling point Krishna enters the heart. Because Krishna cannot be grasped by material senses, engage the tongue in His service — chanting and honoring prasādam — and He reveals Himself spontaneously. When separation feels like painful absence, treat it not as loss but as the very means of attainment, and nourish it by hearing and reading about Krishna daily, absorbing yourself in the Krishna book and favorable literature.

## Glossary

- **Hari-nāma-cintāmaṇi** — 'The touchstone of the Holy Name'; Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's late work recording Caitanya Mahāprabhu's dialogue with Haridāsa Ṭhākura on the holy name, and the foundation of the Kirtan Course.
- **cintāmaṇi** — The touchstone or philosopher's stone; a jewel that transforms whatever it touches into gold — likened to the Holy Name, which turns the mind pure and Kṛṣṇa conscious.
- **japa** — The practice of chanting the holy name on beads while keeping count, done attentively with sincerity and feeling rather than mechanical repetition.
- **kirtan** — Congregational or personal glorification of Krishna through chanting the holy name.
- **vipralambha** — The mood of separation from Krishna; from vipra ('that which is distant'), signifying that the distant Lord is attained precisely through the feeling of longing — the method of advancement, not an obstacle.
- **laulyam** — Intense eagerness or greed for the Lord; per Rūpa Gosvāmī, the only 'price' that purchases devotion — not accumulated pious deeds — and the source Prabhupāda drew 'Krishna consciousness' from.
- **prema** — Pure love of Godhead; the highest and rarest treasure granted by Caitanya Mahāprabhu, the desire tree.
- **yuga-dharma** — The prescribed spiritual process for the age; in Kali-yuga, the chanting of the holy name, which Śrī Caitanya descended to reveal.
- **kalpa-vṛkṣa** — The desire tree; an image for Gaurāṅga Mahāprabhu, who grants everyone whatever they seek, up to love of Godhead.
- **avatāra** — A descent of the Lord; here Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, the golden avatāra who came to deliver conditioned souls.
- **Nīlācala** — Jagannātha Purī, the setting of the book, where both Lord Jagannātha and Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu reside.
- **saṁsāra** — The cycle of material bondage and repeated birth and death, from which Caitanya Mahāprabhu descended to deliver living entities.
- **Namācārya** — The teacher of the holy name; the title of Haridāsa Ṭhākura, entrusted by Caitanya Mahāprabhu with revealing the full glory of the Name.
- **ācārya** — An authorized spiritual teacher who teaches by example; one follows in the ācāryas' footsteps as a guide through unknown terrain.
- **prasādam** — Sanctified food offered to Krishna; honoring it engages the tongue in His service, through which He reveals Himself.
- **Siddha Bakula** — The place in Jagannātha Purī where Caitanya Mahāprabhu asked Haridāsa Ṭhākura for the easiest means to deliver suffering souls — the narrative frame of the book.
- **moving Brahman** — A description of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu as the Supreme Absolute personally moving among living beings, contrasted with the fixed worshipable form of Lord Jagannātha.
- **mode of ignorance** — Tamo-guṇa, the material quality of darkness and inertia; the pure devotee (as Prabhupāda, chanting even in sleep) is never covered by it.
