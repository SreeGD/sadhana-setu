# 14 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 04 05 Closing Kirtan ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This closing session of the Kirtan Course study of the Harināma-cintāmaṇi draws together two complementary threads on how a devotee relates to the chanting of the Holy Name. The first concerns the offenses to the Name (nāma-aparādha): Mahārāja stresses that studying an offense is only half-finished when one can merely name or identify it. Genuine understanding is threefold — grasping what the offense is, knowing its remedy, and knowing how to actually apply that remedy in one's own life so as to overcome and guard against it. The second thread turns from diagnosis to disposition: the path of the Holy Name is not something to be driven relentlessly. There is real value in slowing down, resting, and taking restful pauses, so that japa and bhajana can be absorbed and internalized rather than merely rushed through. Together these teachings frame a mature approach to the Name — one that is both diligent about offenses and gentle about pace.

## Practical application

When a devotee encounters one of the offenses to the Holy Name in the Harināma-cintāmaṇi, the practice is not to stop at recognizing it. For each offense, complete the full triad: name the offense clearly, learn its prescribed remedy, and work out concretely how that remedy applies to your own chanting and conduct — so that the offense can be both overcome and guarded against going forward. Alongside this vigilance, resist the urge to push through rounds mechanically. Allow japa and bhajana room to breathe — chant at a pace that lets the Name be absorbed, and take restful pauses rather than driving the practice relentlessly. In this way daily chanting becomes both careful and unhurried: attentive to offenses, yet paced for absorption.

## Glossary

- **Holy Name (Hari-Nāma)** — The name of the Lord (Kṛṣṇa/Hari), whose chanting is the central practice being discussed; treated as non-different from the Lord himself.
- **nāma-aparādha** — An offense against the Holy Name — an attitude or act that obstructs the full effect of chanting; understanding one requires knowing the offense, its remedy, and how to apply that remedy.
- **japa** — The soft, often personal repetition of the Holy Name, typically counted on beads, which the teaching says should be paced for absorption rather than rushed.
- **bhajana** — Devotional worship and practice centered on remembering and chanting the Name, which likewise benefits from rest and unhurried absorption.
- **Harināma-cintāmaṇi** — The classic text by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura on the glories of the Holy Name and its offenses, which forms the basis of this Kirtan Course seminar.
