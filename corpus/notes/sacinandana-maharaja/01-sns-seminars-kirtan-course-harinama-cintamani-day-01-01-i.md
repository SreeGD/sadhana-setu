# 01 SNS Seminars Kirtan Course Harinama Cintamani Day 01 01 Introduction ISKCON Radhadesh

## Theme

This introductory lecture frames HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja's Kirtan Course, built on Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's Harinām Cintāmaṇi, as a shift from mass, surface-level kirtan toward training in the art of chanting itself — 'samyak' kirtan, the complete, total immersion Rūpa Gosvāmī describes. Mahārāja establishes that chanting the Holy Name is the yuga-dharma of Kali, citing Śukadeva Gosvāmī's condensation of the entire Bhāgavatam into a single verse: though Kali is an ocean of faults, kirtan of Krishna alone grants mukta-saṅga (freedom from sin and karma) and paraṁ vrajet (the highest destination, love of God). Because this fruit comes only when chanting is done properly, the course teaches correct method — noting that ISKCON, though wholly about the Holy Name, spent its first fifty years without systematic education in the chanting itself. The lecture grounds itself in the exemplars of the tradition: Bhaktivinoda, who abandoned everything in 1901 to live at Jagannātha Purī for exclusive absorption in the Name, and above all Haridāsa Ṭhākura, whom Harinām Cintāmaṇi presents as the foremost taker of exclusive shelter — a Muslim-born saint whose previous-life saṃskāras drew him to chant day and night, whose pure chanting converted a prostitute and imprinted the Name on the Siddha-bakula tree, demonstrating the Name's independent, all-transforming potency. Mahārāja maps the book's structure (Vaiṣṇava theology, the grades of the Name, the ten offenses, and the culmination of chanting with bhāva) and introduces the doctrine of grades of chanting — impure chanting, chanting with offenses, and nāma-ābhāsa, the sincere shadow of the pure Name, which is Krishna himself like the sun obscured by our clouds of mistake. The course's spiritual disposition is set by Śikṣāṣṭaka verse 3: chant in humility, cultivated through tolerance, forgiveness, and a servant's mood (sevonmukhe), since the Name yields only to the attitude of service and is won by mercy, not endeavor alone. The lecture closes on the power of sankalpa — a firm inner resolve that acts as an 'inner motor' carrying the practitioner through hardship (illustrated by Bhīma Nirjala Ekādaśī and Mahārāja's own typhoid-stricken Govardhana daṇḍavat-parikramā) — urging each participant to write a formal vow, framed as accountable service under the gaze of guru and Prabhupāda, and to seal it with prayer for divine blessing.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this by treating japa not as a quota to complete but as an art to enter deeply — coming to each session with an open heart, expectations set aside, ready to imbibe and then carry back and share what is received. Begin by forming a written sankalpa: a firm, resolute intention for how you will use your time, framed as service ('I am here to serve') and offered under the watchful gaze of guru and Prabhupāda; renew that inner resolve each morning so it becomes the 'inner motor' that carries you through days when concentration is hard. Chant in the humble state of mind prescribed by Śikṣāṣṭaka verse 3, cultivating humility concretely through tolerance of inconveniences and of people not to your liking, and through forgiveness. Because the Name reveals itself only to the servant (sevonmukhe), pair your japa with tangible service — serving fellow devotees, the community, and the Name itself by chanting attentively 'with charity and compassion rather than selfishness.' Aim to chant free of offenses, understanding that even nāma-ābhāsa — the sincere but still-imperfect attempt — already lets one begin to feel the sun-light of Krishna within the Name. Since advancement comes by mercy and not effort alone, bookend each day with prayer before the Deities, asking to be blessed to make the very best of your practice. Finally, remember the opportunity does not act on you automatically: like carrying your own cup to the water, you must personally apply yourself — and let personal cultivation of the Name precede and empower any attempt to share kirtan with others, beginning with a small group at home.

## Glossary

- **kirtan** — Congregational or personal chanting/glorification of Krishna's names; here treated as an art to be trained in, not merely performed.
- **samyak** — Rūpa Gosvāmī's term for complete, total immersion in chanting the Holy Name — the depth of kirtan the course aims for.
- **yuga-dharma** — The prescribed process of self- and God-realization for a given age; for Kali-yuga it is the chanting of the Holy Name.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present age of quarrel and hypocrisy — 'an ocean of faults' whose one redeeming quality is that kirtan of Krishna grants liberation.
- **mukta-saṅga** — Freedom from all sins, karma, and material attachment/bondage, obtained through kirtan of Krishna.
- **paraṁ vrajet** — 'One reaches the highest destination' — attaining love of God and re-establishing the lost relationship with the Lord.
- **bhajan** — One's own personal, inward devotional practice and worship of the Lord.
- **Harinām Cintāmaṇi** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's treatise — which he called the most special book he wrote — recording the conversations on the Holy Name between Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Haridāsa Ṭhākura in Purī; the course's foundational text.
- **sādhana** — Regulated spiritual practice; the systematic method of the Holy Name laid out in Harinām Cintāmaṇi.
- **nāma-ābhāsa** — A 'shadow' or facsimile of the pure Name — the sincere but still-imperfect attempt to chant correctly, on which one already begins to perceive Krishna's light.
- **saṃskāras** — Impressions or purifying influences carried from previous lives; in Haridāsa Ṭhākura they surfaced early and drew him to the Holy Name.
- **jīva** — The individual living soul, whose nature and relationship with Krishna and His energies form the theology of Harinām Cintāmaṇi's first chapter.
- **bhāva** — Spiritual emotion or love; chanting with bhāva is the culminating 'end game' of Krishna consciousness.
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka** — The eight verses of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu; verse 3 prescribes chanting in a humble state of mind.
- **sevonmukhe** — 'With an attitude of service'; the disposition — unattainable by mind and senses alone — required to enter into chanting the Holy Name.
- **japa** — Personal, meditative repetition of the Holy Name, typically on beads; to be done 'nicely,' with attention and compassion.
- **sankalpa** — A firm inner resolve or vow set in the heart that acts as an 'inner motor,' carrying the practitioner through tasks otherwise beyond them; an ancient Gauḍīya ceremony paralleling the vows within initiation.
- **Nirjala Ekādaśī** — Bhīma's waterless Ekādaśī fast, cited as an illustration of how a sankalpa vowed the night before makes even a strict abstention feel easy.
- **parikramā** — Circumambulation of a holy place (e.g., Govardhana), here performed by daṇḍavats and sustained by an initial sankalpa despite illness.
- **daṇḍavats** — Full prostrated obeisances; a strenuous mode of parikramā in which one advances by repeatedly lying flat before Krishna.
- **sankirtana** — The congregational chanting movement of the Holy Name, spread by carrying back and sharing what one receives in practice.
- **Bhajan Kuṭīr** — The cottage (next to Haridāsa Ṭhākura's samādhi) where Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura lived in Purī for exclusive absorption in remembering the Holy Name.
- **samādhi** — The memorial tomb-shrine of a pure devotee; Haridāsa Ṭhākura's stood beside Bhaktivinoda's cottage, marking him as the exemplar.
- **Siddha-bakula** — The tree under which Haridāsa Ṭhākura chanted, said to have become a devotee with the Name imprinted on its body — evidence of pure chanting's independent potency.
