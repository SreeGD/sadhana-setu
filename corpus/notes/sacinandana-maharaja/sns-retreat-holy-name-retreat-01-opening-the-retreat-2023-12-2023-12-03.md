# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 01 Opening the Retreat 2023 12 03

## Theme

This opening session of HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja's Holy Name Retreat at Govardhan is a spiritual orientation that frames the whole retreat before any sustained practice begins. Mahārāja consecrates the hall as a Hari-Kīrtan-sthalī, repeatedly invites everyone to chant fully and wholeheartedly, and explains that one must first 'tune in' — setting down the burdens, hopes, and arguments of outside life so the mind can refocus on why one has come. He establishes that the transcendental sound of the holy name works opposite to the agitations that damage us, going to the very root of the nerves and restoring them to life. Through the Svapna-vilāsāmṛta-aṣṭakam he reveals the identity of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu — the golden combined form of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa who floods the universe with pure love and makes every being chant — and shows, via Rādhā's bewilderment in the dream, the mystery of the lover and beloved merged into one. He glorifies Harināma as the greatest, still-undiscovered treasure: worshipped even by the Upaniṣads, Nārada, and Śukadeva, destroying all karma at once where impersonal meditation cannot, and manifesting in two forms — the Person named and the more-merciful sound vibration that can rescue even one who has offended the personal form. The retreat's defining concept is the Japa Anuṣṭhāna: a concentrated personal effort to effect a lasting transformation, not merely to inform. Mahārāja distinguishes information from transformation (Dhṛtarāṣṭra is the warning example of one fully informed yet unchanged), offers a set of uncomfortable diagnostic questions to test whether one is merely informed about chanting or actually changed by it, and describes chanting as an alchemical process (Hari-bhakti-vilāsa) that turns an ungolden person into a twice-born brāhmaṇa. He calls for a firm commitment rather than a wish, protecting the inner environment by minimizing devices and stopping the mind from judging, and presents Govardhana Hill itself — Giri-rāja, the ~24-km manifestation — as the retreat's real deity, to be approached with daily obeisances and humility. He assures that the dhāma and the chanting act on us whether we feel it or not, that a 'present' is already prepared for each retreatant to claim, and that real change requires divine help, for the finite cannot reach the infinite by its own steps — yet one small step toward Kṛṣṇa draws unlimited steps in return. Drawing on Caitanya Bhāgavata and Caitanya-caritāmṛta, he relays Mahāprabhu's singular parting instruction rooted in affection (sneha): chant, worship, and glorify Kṛṣṇa and think of nothing else; constant remembrance is the whole of the practice. The retreat's true aim is a change of desire — redirecting longing toward Kṛṣṇa. He locates the source of the Śikṣāṣṭaka in Mahāprabhu's own lived separation-ecstasy at Jagannath Puri, names kīrtana as the most feasible means of liberation in Kali-yuga, and diagnoses tastelessness for the name as avidyā — using Rūpa Gosvāmī's jaundice analogy and his two prescriptions, anudina (daily) and the secret of ādarāt (with reverent, heartfelt attention). Finally he insists taste is awakened experientially, like swimming, leading the assembly directly into chanting; reveals (in later sessions) how Mahāprabhu unveiled Govardhan's identity by worshipping a śilā in Rādhā's mood and giving one to Raghunātha dāsa; and urges that hearing the glories of the Name (śravaṇam) kindles the will to chant, so the single-pointed purpose remains: to chant extra japa and absorb oneself fully in the Holy Name.

## Practical application

Begin by 'tuning in': consciously set down the day's burdens, hopes, and arguments and refocus the mind on chanting, listening to the holy name with focused effort so the words carry real meaning and restore rather than agitate you. Treat japa not as one practice among many but as your most direct relationship with Kṛṣṇa — turning to Him and pleading to be accepted, since that pleading is itself the relationship. Honestly diagnose where your chanting stands by asking the uncomfortable questions: What goes on in you when you chant? Do you feel Kṛṣṇa's presence? Do you look forward to chanting or only to finishing your rounds? Is your japa forced or born of genuine enthusiasm (utsāha)? Does it leave you energized and seeing clearly or exhausted? Do you chant in one concentrated sitting or scatter it? Use the honest answer as the starting point, then fix a clear intention for what you want your japa to become — making a firm commitment, not a mere wish, and a willingness to change yourself, not just to want change. Protect your inner environment: minimize devices and internet, give the time entirely to Kṛṣṇa, and when the 'ink' of criticism darkens the mind, deliberately redirect it to seeing the good and the opportunity. Apply Rūpa Gosvāmī's two-part cure — chant daily (anudina) and, the secret for awakening taste, chant with reverent, heartfelt attention (ādarāt). Chant loudly, fully, and with deep feeling and yearning rather than mechanical volume, learning the name experientially by actually doing it, like swimming. Feed your chanting by hearing the glories, potency, and mercy of the Name (śravaṇam kindles kīrtanam), rise early to chant extra japa, and treat the chanting itself as the supremely important part — never a filler. Where you chant near the dhāma, begin the day by offering obeisances to Giri-rāja Govardhan and praying for his help to cross your inner thresholds, approaching with humility and gratitude. Above all, redirect desire toward Kṛṣṇa and aim at constant remembrance — thinking of Him while sleeping, eating, and awake — trusting that your one small, sincere step draws unlimited reciprocation, because the deep transformation is worked not by your effort alone but by nāma-kṛpā, the mercy that flows from the holy name itself.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Kīrtan-sthalī** — A place consecrated for the kīrtana (glorification/singing) of Lord Hari; here, the retreat hall set apart as sacred ground for chanting the holy names.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational singing and glorification of Kṛṣṇa's names; named in the lecture as the most feasible means of liberation in the Age of Kali.
- **Harināma / Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord; described as the greatest treasure, manifesting in two forms — the Supreme Person named and the even more merciful sound vibration of the name.
- **japa** — Soft, personal, repeated chanting of the holy name on beads; the central practice the retreat seeks to transform.
- **Japa Anuṣṭhāna** — An established Vaiṣṇava observance of concentrated personal effort undertaken to effect a lasting transformation in one's life — the retreat's defining model, aiming at change, not mere information.
- **Svapna-vilāsāmṛta-aṣṭakam** — An eight-verse prayer narrating Rādhārāṇī's dream, used to reveal that Kṛṣṇa will appear with a golden complexion (as Caitanya Mahāprabhu) to flood the world with pure love; declared true, not illusion.
- **Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — The combined form of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa appearing as a golden brāhmaṇa, who teaches and inundates the universe with pure love of God through the holy name.
- **Rādhā / Rādhārāṇī** — Kṛṣṇa's foremost beloved and the embodiment of His pleasure potency; her mood of love and separation is the inner mood Mahāprabhu displays.
- **Kṛṣṇa** — The Supreme Person, object of all the chanting, worship, and remembrance the retreat cultivates.
- **brāhmaṇa** — A spiritually qualified, 'twice-born' person; used in the alchemy analogy where chanting transforms an ordinary person into one purified and elevated.
- **saṁskāra** — A purificatory rite or constituent of the transforming process; Nāma (chanting the name) is named as one such transformative constituent.
- **Hari-bhakti-vilāsa** — A Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava devotional manual cited for the alchemical analogy that bell metal can be turned to gold, as an ordinary person is transformed by chanting.
- **darśana** — Philosophy or seeing clearly in the right direction; necessary but, by itself, insufficient to transform — knowledge alone does not change the heart.
- **utsāha** — Genuine enthusiasm or eagerness; a sign of healthy japa, as opposed to forced, mechanical chanting.
- **sambandha** — Right relationship/clear connectedness; used here for the clarity and energized 'seeing clearly' that authentic chanting produces.
- **avidyā** — Not mere ignorance but a diseased state of consciousness in which one has forgotten one's identity as a spiritual being; the cause of tastelessness for the holy name.
- **anudina** — 'Daily' — Rūpa Gosvāmī's first prescription, chanting the holy name every day, regularly.
- **ādarāt** — 'With reverence / heartfelt attention' — Rūpa Gosvāmī's second prescription and the secret key that awakens taste in chanting.
- **śravaṇam** — Hearing — here, hearing the glories, potency, and mercy of the holy name, which kindles the will to chant (kīrtanam/japa).
- **nāma-kṛpā** — The mercy that flows from the holy name itself; the supreme transforming agent that, beyond one's own effort, actually accomplishes the change.
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka** — Mahāprabhu's eight instructional verses on chanting, born of his own lived ecstasy of separation at Jagannath Puri; chapter that gives the path to the innermost ecstasies — the path of the holy name.
- **sneha** — Affection/love; the basis of Mahāprabhu's parting instruction — those who love him should speak of nothing but Kṛṣṇa.
- **adhikāra** — Qualification or eligibility; per Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī, devotees who truly love Caitanya have no qualification to speak of anything but Kṛṣṇa.
- **Govardhana / Giri-rāja / Giridhārī** — The sacred hill near the retreat, regarded as the retreat's true deity — a non-different manifestation of Kṛṣṇa and the most intimate place of Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa's pastimes; approached with obeisances and humility.
- **śilā** — A sacred stone of Govardhan worshipped as the very body of Kṛṣṇa; Mahāprabhu embraced one in Rādhā's mood and gave one to Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī as his worshipable deity.
- **dhāma** — The holy abode (e.g., Vṛndāvana/Govardhan); it cleanses and acts on the devotee whether or not its effect is felt, intensifying the chanting.
- **Kali / Age of Kali** — The present degraded age; despite its faults it retains one great quality — that by kīrtana of Kṛṣṇa one is liberated.
- **praṇāma mantras** — Verses of obeisance offered to the guru-paramparā at the opening of spiritual activity, modeling that practice begins by taking shelter of the disciplic succession.
- **guru-paramparā** — The disciplic succession of spiritual teachers (here including Śrīla Prabhupāda and Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura) under whose shelter the session is begun.
- **Brahman** — The impersonal Absolute; meditation on it cannot free one from past karma, whereas the mere appearance of the holy name destroys all karma at once.
