# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 04 Guided Japa Session 03 2023 12 04

## Theme

This guided japa session by HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja centers on a single decisive claim: conscious intention — not technique — determines whether spiritual practice bears fruit. He opens with the observation that those who act with a clear goal in view reach it, while those without an inner intention (sankalpa) or a conscious awareness of the end (prayojana-bodha) will fail even with a million techniques; for both the japa chanter and the devotee, intention is what matters most. He then traces the long-term consequence: devotees who keep the goal in view stay enthusiastic in Krishna consciousness for life, whereas those who lose sight of it 'idealize normalcy,' settling into a conventional, distracted spirituality and losing their utsāha. From this diagnosis the talk turns practical. He recovers the ancient Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava practice of setting intention before chanting — praying to the mālā to protect one's consciousness from distraction and consciously offering the chanting to Krishna in his tribhaṅga form. This intention-setting, he insists, must be honest: drawing on Srila Prabhupada's blunt Paris morning-walk teaching that a devotee's worst enemy is himself (not external faults like overeating or fault-finding), and that Prabhupada taught honestly rather than flattering us. Honest intention-setting is itself a prayer — turning to Krishna, admitting one's forgetfulness and distraction, and asking to be accepted while sincerely trying one's best. Finally he grounds attentiveness in the body: sitting up properly, with shoulders rolled back, chest open, and spine straight, signals the body that a shift is coming and counteracts the slide from 'automatic pilot' into dullness and sleep. The arc moves from why intention matters, to what is lost without it, to how to set it honestly, to how the body supports it.

## Practical application

Before picking up the mālā, pause and consciously set your intention rather than chanting on 'automatic pilot.' Make the goal explicit to yourself so you are chanting toward Krishna and not merely completing rounds — this guards against 'idealizing normalcy,' the slow settling into a distracted, low-enthusiasm routine. Follow the Gauḍīya practice of praying to the mālā ('O mālā, protect my consciousness from distraction') and offering the chanting to Krishna in his threefold-bending (tribhaṅga) form. Let the intention-setting be an honest prayer: admit to Krishna that you have forgotten him and are distracted, and ask to be accepted while you sincerely try your best — humility and truthfulness, not self-flattery, serve the chanter. Watch the real enemy, which is your own inattentive self, rather than fixating on external faults. Support attentiveness through posture: sit up with shoulders rolled back, chest open, and head and spine in a straight line, which creates room to breathe and signals the body that a shift into focused practice is beginning, helping you resist the drift into dullness and sleep during sustained japa.

## Glossary

- **sankalpa** — A resolved inner intention or vow; the conscious resolve set before practice that, in this teaching, is the decisive factor in whether japa bears fruit.
- **prayojana-bodha** — Conscious awareness of the ultimate goal or end of practice; keeping the final purpose in view, without which even countless techniques fail.
- **utsāha** — Enthusiasm or inner drive in spiritual life; the motivation that is sustained by keeping the goal in view and lost when one 'idealizes normalcy.'
- **japa** — The meditative chanting of the Lord's names, typically on a mālā; the practice this session is guiding.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name(s) of the Lord (Hari/Krishna); the names chanted in japa.
- **mālā** — The string of beads used to count rounds of japa; in the Gauḍīya practice described, one prays to the mālā to protect one's consciousness from distraction before chanting.
- **tribhaṅga** — The 'threefold-bending' posture of Krishna — gracefully curved at neck, waist, and knee — the form of the Lord to whom the chanting is consciously offered.
- **Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava** — The tradition of Krishna devotion stemming from Caitanya Mahāprabhu in Bengal (Gauḍa), the lineage whose ancient intention-setting practice is recovered here.
