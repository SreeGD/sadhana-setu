# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 10 Kirtan with Inspiration 2023 12 07

## Theme

This lecture frames the closing kīrtan-and-japa session of a long Holy-Name retreat as a movement from purification to prayer. Sacīnandana Mahārāja opens with a contrast he draws from Sanātana Gosvāmī's Hari-bhakti-vilāsa: good japa depends on a pure mind, because when the mind is impure the three guṇas (tamas, rajas, sattva) intrude and disturb the chanting — so a short burst of kīrtan beforehand makes the japa that follows easier. Kīrtan, unlike japa, demands no pure mind as a precondition; it can be done from any state, and the act of kīrtan itself cleanses the mirror of the mind of its accumulated dust. But this cleansing requires active participation: one must enter the 'river of sound' and fully immerse oneself, not merely practice manaḥ-saṁharaṇa (gathering or withdrawing the mind). Mahārāja then maps the inner journey onto Mahāprabhu's Śikṣāṣṭaka. Recalling a class by Bhūrijana Prabhu, he notes that once a devotee genuinely recognizes he is fallen, he naturally takes up the third verse — not merely feeling humility but practicing it — and the fruit of that humility ripens in the fourth verse, where Mahāprabhu prays not for wealth, followers, or enjoyment but only for unmotivated devotion. Having spent a long retreat (64+ rounds) confronting their own limitations, the devotees are now ripe to chant in a prayerful, supplicating mood. The arc culminates in practice: humble the body — kneel, even prostrate, fold the hands before the chest — so that outward humility shapes the inner attitude of prayer, and then carry that prayerful mood from the kīrtan into the beads, chanting japa from whatever devotional state is present in the heart at this moment, which is what makes japa enlivened and energized with bhakti.

## Practical application

Begin a japa session with a short kīrtan rather than going straight to the beads: kīrtan needs no pre-purified mind and itself wipes the dust from the mirror of the mind, settling the guṇas so the japa that follows comes more easily and purely. While chanting kīrtan, do not just try to hold the mind in place — actively dive into the 'river of sound' and let yourself be absorbed. Let the body carry the mood: kneel or even prostrate, fold the hands in prayer before the chest, and raise the voice in a supplicating, prayerful tone, allowing this outward humility to shape an inner attitude of genuine prayer. Internally, follow the Śikṣāṣṭaka movement — honestly acknowledge your fallen, limited condition (the fruit of a long, demanding practice such as many rounds over a retreat), let that ripen into real humility, and pray, like Mahāprabhu's fourth verse, not for wealth, followers, or enjoyment but only for unmotivated devotional service. Then carry that prayerful attitude directly onto the beads: chant japa from whatever devotional state your heart actually holds in this moment. Chanting from that authentic level — not a forced or pretended one — is what makes the japa enlivened and energized with bhakti.

## Glossary

- **kīrtan** — Congregational or vocal chanting of the Lord's names; here presented as needing no pure mind beforehand and as itself cleansing the mind, making subsequent japa easier.
- **japa** — Personal, often soft or silent, repetition of the mahā-mantra on beads; said to depend on a pure mind for its quality.
- **mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' of the Hare Kṛṣṇa names sung or chanted; here sung from a humble, prayerful posture.
- **Hari-bhakti-vilāsa** — A devotional treatise by Sanātana Gosvāmī, cited as the source for the teaching that good japa depends on a pure mind.
- **Sanātana Gosvāmī** — One of the six Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana and author of the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa, credited with the point on purity of mind and japa.
- **guṇas** — The three modes of material nature — tamas (ignorance/inertia), rajas (passion), and sattva (goodness) — which intrude on chanting when the mind is impure.
- **tamas** — The mode of ignorance or inertia, one of the three guṇas that can disturb the chanting.
- **rajas** — The mode of passion, one of the three guṇas that can disturb the chanting.
- **sattva** — The mode of goodness, one of the three guṇas that can disturb the chanting.
- **manaḥ-saṁharaṇa** — Gathering or withdrawing the mind; named as insufficient by itself — kīrtan is meant to be a fuller immersion in sound rather than only this drawing-in of the mind.
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka** — Caitanya Mahāprabhu's eight instructional verses; its third verse (humility) and fourth verse (prayer for unmotivated devotion, not wealth, followers, or enjoyment) structure the lecture's progression.
- **Mahāprabhu** — Caitanya Mahāprabhu, author of the Śikṣāṣṭaka, whose prayer for pure, unmotivated devotion is held up as the model mood.
- **bhakti** — Devotion; the loving, prayerful state that, when carried into japa, makes the chanting enlivened and energized.
- **Bhūrijana Prabhu** — A senior devotee teacher whose class on recognizing one's fallen condition and taking up the third verse of the Śikṣāṣṭaka Mahārāja recalls.
