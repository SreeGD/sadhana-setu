# SNS Retreat Holy Name Retreat 13 Questions and Answers 2023 12 08

## Theme

This question-and-answer session from HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja's Holy Name Retreat addresses the central problem of the restless mind in japa and how to enter what he calls 'the world of the holy name.' He begins from the premise that the material world is inherently disturbing — things simply happen — and that the remedy is not to be shaken but to return to the reality of being an eternal soul, part and parcel of Kṛṣṇa, who is directly addressed through His most accessible form, the holy name. The purpose of life is to make sambandha, a living relationship with Kṛṣṇa, entered through sincere, conscious prayer ('My dear Lord, please accept me') rather than mechanical sound. Mahārāja diagnoses why the mind resists: whatever it focuses on expands and seems all-important, though the same urgent thought proves trivial hours later. The deeper obstacle is not pain or pleasure but avidyā — ignorance that forgets the self and identifies with the body, functioning through tamas (covering) and moha (delusion) — and its practical face is disinterest, what ISKCON calls 'lack of taste.' Since we are drawn only to what we have tasted, the solution is to raise chanting to the level where one relishes the holy name; then one turns to Kṛṣṇa regardless of the 'waves of life.' Throughout, he insists on two things held together: we absolutely need Kṛṣṇa's mercy, which is freely given and cannot be forced or tricked by 'testing' prayer, and we absolutely need our own sincere attempt. The change that attracts His mercy is the inner shift from self-interest to 'I really want to please Kṛṣṇa.' He frames every stage of advancement — even ecstatic love (Śikṣāṣṭaka 6) — as begging, born of humility that honestly admits one's insufficiency while keeping eyes on where one wants to go. A recurring practical thread is that the holy name needs nourishment: just as Kṛṣṇa never comes without His potency (Rādhā), the name comes with the Bhāgavata, and without kṛṣṇa-kathā the chanting goes dry. So one should hear about Kṛṣṇa before and after japa — not during — which settles the agitated mind the way autumn rains let muddy water clear. He addresses householder life (perfection is attainable in gṛhastha; renunciation is internal, not cloth), suffering and illness (advancement removes not pain but being overwhelmed by it; address problems on the level where they arise, including medical and therapeutic help), depression and tamo-guṇa, emotional well-being, and guarding the mind's digital inputs. He closes on the retreat's true purpose: not to create 'retreat junkies' but to teach devotees to 'fly their own plane' — to carry home one applied jewel, since application, not accumulation, is the heart of learning, and Kṛṣṇa values the sincerity of the attempt (the spider's grains of sand) over its magnitude. In the modern world there is nowhere external left to flee; the only place to go is deep within, into Kṛṣṇa consciousness and the chanting of the holy names. He offers all of this humbly, as a fellow student of japa drawing on scripture and his own practice.

## Practical application

For daily japa and Hari-Nāma, the lecture yields a concrete discipline. Before chanting, gather the wandering mind — travel plans, lingering upsets — and bring it back to the soul, the one thing eternally present; then chant with awareness of the name's meaning in a mood of prayer, making the chanting a conscious appeal of the soul rather than mechanical sound. At the actual time of chanting, simply chant: entertain neither negative thoughts nor even positive, service-planning thoughts — japa is your private time with Kṛṣṇa, so make Him alone the center first, and relationships and service will naturally prosper afterward. When the mind seizes a seemingly urgent thought, do not write it down to handle later (you will never get to chanting); instead slow the mind by intently hearing even a single Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra until its grip loosens. When attention wavers, never give up — try again and again and pray in your fallen condition ('my mind is attracted to so many things but not to You; unless You are merciful I have no hope'). Chant according to your capacity rather than chasing a bead count, since mechanically pushing beads while the mind wanders breeds a bad habit; chant even a small amount attentively and increase from there. Feed the name with kṛṣṇa-kathā: hear, read, and steadily study the Bhāgavatam before and after rounds (not during) to clear the muddy mind and enliven dry chanting. Simple aids like counting on the fingers are fine, but keep prayers to the guru-paramparā and Vaiṣṇavas before chanting or in a deliberate pause so they don't fragment the name into a mental checklist. Address the inner shift — move from 'what can I get Kṛṣṇa to do?' to 'what must I do to please Him?' — and water the weak desire through good association, hearing recordings, and repeatedly asking 'what do I really want in life?' Support the practice with a healthy lifestyle (sleep at night, increase sattva, abandon tamasic habits), guard what you read and watch, seek qualified medical or therapeutic help when needed, and cultivate good sangha, an inspiring goal, and an end to negative self-talk. Above all, carry home one realization that struck you and actually apply it daily — what you do now is what you will become — because hearing only becomes the holy name's working when it becomes daily practice.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari/Kṛṣṇa); the practice of chanting His names.
- **japa** — Soft, often individual chanting of the holy name, here on beads; one's 'private time with Kṛṣṇa.'
- **Mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' — Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare.
- **sambandha** — A living relationship with Kṛṣṇa; establishing this relationship is described as the purpose of life.
- **śravaṇam, kīrtanam, smaraṇam** — Hearing, chanting, and remembering Kṛṣṇa — the inner service contrasted with outer service in the world.
- **kṛṣṇa-kathā** — Narrations and topics of Kṛṣṇa; the 'nourishment' the holy name needs to be relished, which cleanses the mind and attracts it to Kṛṣṇa.
- **Bhāgavata / Bhāgavatam** — The Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam; the activities of Kṛṣṇa and His devotees, identified as the potency accompanying the holy name.
- **avidyā** — Ignorance that forgets the self and identifies with the material body — the real obstacle to chanting.
- **tamas** — One function of avidyā: the covering of true knowledge (also tamo-guṇa, the mode of darkness/inertia underlying much depression).
- **moha** — One function of avidyā: delusion, mistaking the false for the real.
- **sattva** — The mode of goodness; increasing it (regular hours, lighter habits) helps counter tamo-guṇa.
- **māyā** — The Lord's illusory energy; together with the mind and old habits it makes turning back to Kṛṣṇa difficult.
- **buddhi-yoga** — The intelligence Kṛṣṇa reciprocally gives to one who makes sincere effort to come to Him.
- **līlā** — Kṛṣṇa's pastimes; to 'enter the world of the holy name' is to enter and meditate on His līlā.
- **Kṛṣṇa-loka** — Kṛṣṇa's spiritual abode, where the residents of Vṛndāvana chant His names continuously.
- **gṛhastha** — The householder āśrama; a bona fide ashram for spiritual progress in which perfection and love for Kṛṣṇa are fully attainable.
- **sannyāsa** — The renounced order; Mahārāja stresses that renunciation is internal, not a matter of changing into sannyāsa cloth.
- **āśrama** — A spiritual order or stage of life; inner detachment, not merely external āśrama, is the criterion of progress.
- **ācāryas** — Teachers who teach by example; with the Gosvāmīs and Caitanya Mahāprabhu they prepare practitioners for the spiritual reality.
- **Gosvāmīs (of Vṛndāvana)** — The six Gosvāmīs, role models of absorption in Kṛṣṇa whose former caves in Vṛndāvana the speaker references.
- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — Kṛṣṇa in the mood of His devotee; a supreme role model, served by intimate householder associates.
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka** — Caitanya Mahāprabhu's eight verses on chanting; its sixth verse ('nayanaṁ') describes even ecstatic love as a form of begging.
- **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī** — Vaiṣṇava ācārya who characterized every stage of advancement, even ecstatic love, as begging for Kṛṣṇa's mercy.
- **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** — Great chanter of the holy name; his blessing of imprisoned brāhmaṇas illustrates eagerness/necessity as the asset to cultivate.
- **Giriraj / Govardhana** — Govardhana Hill, worshipped as Kṛṣṇa; invoked for mercy on a distressed soul and supremely accessible through sincere prayer (Param-maṅgala-hitakārī).
- **Rādhā-kuṇḍa** — The sacred pond dear to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa; with Giri-Govardhana, accessible through prayer without any travel plan.
- **Brijavāsī** — A resident of Vraja/Vṛndāvana; their sympathy and blessings to visiting devotees are described as very auspicious.
- **dhāma (Vṛndāvana, Śrī Dhāma Māyāpur)** — The holy places Prabhupāda wanted devotees to visit yearly, fully absorbed in hearing and chanting.
- **guru-paramparā** — The disciplic succession of teachers; one may pray for their blessings, best before or in a pause from chanting.
- **Vaiṣṇavas** — Devotees of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa; serving, praising, and seeing the good in them belongs after japa and kīrtana.
- **sangha / association** — Devotee fellowship; it does not do remembrance for you but equips you with knowledge to practice on your own.
- **sankalpa** — A vow or resolve; here, the determination to turn from self-interest toward pleasing Kṛṣṇa.
