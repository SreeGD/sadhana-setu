# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 11 2014 09 20 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

The lecture is the closing session of a Holy-Name retreat, and Mahārāja moves from a concrete diagnosis of the chanting to its deepest philosophical root. He begins by observing that, by the very sound of the kīrtana, only about half the chanters are truly present in the holy name while the rest drift in 'the dreamland of the mind'; attentive chanting — consciously linking the mind to the syllables of the name — is audibly different from mechanical chanting and demands deliberate effort. As the retreat ends he warns of relapse: the new experience of attentive chanting is young while old habits of mindless indulgence are strong, so the work is to build new habits and carry something transformative home rather than mere nostalgia. He then anchors the whole teaching in Svayambhuva Manu's instruction to Dhruva (and Bhāgavata narrative): by regaining one's constitutional position and serving the Lord — the reservoir of pleasure dwelling in all as the Supersoul — one quickly forgets the illusory 'I and mine' and wakes from the dream. Even the liberated Dhruva, who saw the Lord at age five, slipped back into bodily identification and grievous offense (the slaughter of the Yakṣas), showing that 'I and mine' (ahaṁ-mama) is the root of all material bondage and is removed not by struggle but by grace and devotional service. Drawing on the Harināma-cintāmaṇi and Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's purport, he shows that even an initiated chanter who clings to bodily identity falls from the path, because the pure soul is wrapped in coverings beginning with ahaṅkāra. Through vivid images — a costume ball of Māyā with Time/death as the final player, 'Facebook' as intensified illusion, and the holy name as the 'magical wand' and the medicine brought by Lord Caitanya — he insists realization must be practical, not theoretical. Chanting from within the dream is 'shadow chanting' and the tenth offense (perpetuating bodily vanity); it bears no fruit because one cannot travel east and west at once. The remedy is sambandha-jñāna received from a pure soul: three meditations establishing that I am Kṛṣṇa's eternal servant, Kṛṣṇa is my only master, and the material world is a reformatory. Technically this is bhūta-śuddhi — without it Hari-bhakti-vilāsa warns no japa or rite yields result. He closes by reconciling this with worldly duties: one does not abandon roles but performs the same actions as a servant of Kṛṣṇa, pausing before each role to resolve to act in service (per Śrīdhara Svāmī), echoing the surrender-prayers of the pūjārīs and the Brahma-sambandha mood — the single act all Vaiṣṇavas perform: disconnect from māyā and connect with spiritual reality.

## Practical application

Before chanting a single round, sit down not only with the body but with the soul — consciously occupy your spiritual seat with the positive meditation: 'I am a soul, the eternal servant of Kṛṣṇa; Kṛṣṇa is my only master; this world is a place meant to reform my God-forgetfulness' (the threefold sambandha-jñāna). This bhūta-śuddhi pushes the body, roles, and the whole 'I and mine' into the distance and places you in reality before japa begins. Make CC Madhya 22.33 your inner prayer — sincerely tell Kṛṣṇa that though you forgot Him for years, today you surrender as His servant and beg to be engaged in His service; the chanting that follows then becomes genuine śravaṇam and kīrtanam. During japa, actively link the mind to the letters and sound of the name rather than letting it run in the background of a wandering mind; when attention drifts, give 'a little more effort' and begin again, treating inattention as something to overcome, not tolerate. Do not keep illusion near the heart while chanting, and watch for subtle bodily vanity (even a senior devotee craving respect), which is the tenth offense and turns practice into fruitless 'shadow chanting.' Recognize that years and age alone do not equal advancement — one can chant forty years with the anchor of false ego still lodged. Outside of japa, do not abandon your duties as parent, spouse, or worker; instead, before stepping into each role, pause and resolve, 'I really want to do this as a service for Kṛṣṇa,' engaging the same body, mind, and conditioned traits in His service. Done daily, this conscious meditation keeps the chanter on the spiritual platform so the holy name can do its work of dissolving illusion.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari/Kṛṣṇa); the practice of chanting it, central to the retreat.
- **japa** — Soft, individual repetition of the holy name, usually counted on beads.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational or vocal chanting/singing of the holy names of the Lord.
- **māyā** — Illusion; the Lord's deluding energy that makes the soul identify with the temporary body and material designations.
- **ahaṁ-mama** — The conception of 'I and mine' — bodily identification and possessiveness — described as the very root of attraction to and bondage in the material world.
- **ahaṅkāra** — False ego; the first and most subtle covering of the soul, on which rest intelligence, mind, bodily self-conception, and the gross elements.
- **Supersoul (Paramātmā)** — The localized aspect of the Supreme Lord who dwells within every being as the indwelling witness and reservoir of all pleasure.
- **sambandha-jñāna** — Knowledge of one's eternal relationships — with Kṛṣṇa, with the material world — received from a pure soul, placing everything in its right relation.
- **bhūta-śuddhi** — Purification from false identification with the material elements; the meditative re-establishing of one's spiritual identity before performing devotional acts.
- **śravaṇam** — Hearing about the Lord; a foundational practice (limb) of devotional service; here, alert, attentive hearing rather than listening on 'automatic pilot.'
- **kīrtanam** — Glorifying/chanting about the Lord; a foundational limb of bhakti, paired with śravaṇam.
- **bhakti** — Devotional service to the Lord; loving service performed from the platform of the soul.
- **darshan** — A direct, sacred vision or audience of the Lord (or the Deity).
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee of Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa; one who performs the essential act of disconnecting from māyā and connecting with spiritual reality.
- **sampradāya** — A bona fide disciplic succession or spiritual lineage transmitting authentic teaching.
- **Brahma-sambandha (mantra)** — An initiatory surrender-prayer of the Vallabha sampradāya, said to be given by Kṛṣṇa to Vallabhācārya, in which one dedicates body, family, wealth, and identity to the Lord and begs His shelter.
- **threefold miseries** — The three classes of material suffering (from one's own body/mind, from other living beings, and from nature/providence) endured in repeated birth and death.
- **neophyte** — A beginner in spiritual life; here, one who knows the soul only theoretically and so never advances from material toward spiritual consciousness.
- **Yakṣas** — A class of celestial beings whom Dhruva, acting from bodily identification and vengeance, slaughtered — an act that drove him into offense and entanglement.
- **Harināma-cintāmaṇi** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's treatise on the holy name and its offenses, cited on the offender absorbed in 'I and mine' and on fixing the mind in one's identity with Kṛṣṇa.
- **Hari-bhakti-vilāsa** — A scriptural manual of Vaiṣṇava practice, cited as warning that without bhūta-śuddhi no japa, homa, or rite — even if done by the rules — yields any result.
- **Nectar of Devotion** — Prabhupāda's summary study of Rūpa Gosvāmī's Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, cited as affirming the Vallabha line as bona fide.
