# SNS Seminars Harinaam Cintamani 01 2016 07 04

## Theme

Drawn from Harinām Cintāmaṇi — the teachings on the holy name spoken by Nāmācārya Haridāsa Ṭhākura in devotional ecstasy and compiled by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura as a dialogue in which Caitanya Mahāprabhu questions and Haridāsa answers — this lecture maps the whole arc of becoming a genuine chanter. Haridāsa's own posture sets the tone: he disclaimed authorship, calling Caitanya the player and himself merely the instrument, modeling the chanter's humility and instrumentality. The work unfolds in three movements: Section 1 establishes the glories of the holy name to awaken deep faith (śraddhā), the sole qualification and the 'entry into the palace of the holy name' — without which chanting stays background music while the restless mind holds the foreground. Section 2 (chapters 4–14) treats pure chanting, which requires not only knowing the ten offenses to the name (nāma-aparādha) and offenses in service (sevā-aparādha) but learning to purify and overcome them; the name is Krishna Himself, like the sun, and offenses are the clouds and fog that hide it, so one may chant endlessly while inner darkness remains. Section 3 (chapter 15) names the goal: not finishing rounds but chanting for Krishna's pleasure — the sign of true bhakti, engaging mind, words and activity to please Krishna rather than for one's own enjoyment — whereupon Krishna reciprocates, entering one's life, giving signs and giving rasa, reawakening the soul's eternal loving relationship with Him. Mahārāja frames the practical entry point as honest self-examination (is japa a living reciprocal relationship or a chore one feels condemned to by an initiation vow?) coupled with genuine desire, since technique is useless without the hunger to improve. He then unfolds Haridāsa's central glorification: the holy name is the single greatest treasure in Krishna's treasure house — greater even than prema-bhakti or Krishna's Kaustubha and cintāmaṇi gems — not by abstract ranking but because of our present condition: covered by imperfect material senses we cannot see Krishna, yet the Name is fully accessible to those very fallen senses, willingly descending first onto the tongue and then into the heart of any willing conditioned soul. In this dull material world only two spiritual entities exist — the individual soul and the holy name — and when they meet the greatest auspiciousness comes, like a dying spark restored to life in fire. Because chanting lives on faith alone (unlike jñāna, yoga, and karma with their yajñas, baths, and considerations of time, place and purity), where there is no faith the name does not act — so much so that glorifying the name to the faithless is itself a named offense, and a guru must renounce a disciple who has lost faith. Faith is therefore constantly under attack, especially by Kali through opposing texts, competing ideas, and devotees speaking ill of one another; it is nourished only through the association of faithful devotees. Citing Vyāsadeva's ladder (learned via Bhakti Vijñāna Goswami), Mahārāja traces the journey of faith: śraddhā gives birth to vīrya (energetic endeavor), which gives smṛti (remembrance of Krishna's teachings under pressure), which gives samādhi (undivided concentration), which gives prajñā (realization discriminating ātmā and prakṛti from puruṣa). The real mountain faith must move is internal — ahaṅkāra, false ego — and the slippery path requires the 'walking stick' of the saints' mercy. Applied to japa: firm faith that the Name is the greatest treasure makes the vīrya to chant sixteen rounds effortless; smṛti protects one's vow (as the godbrother who dreamt of Prabhupāda at his door then completed his rounds); the worst enemy is inattention (laziness, distraction, restlessness), overcome only by the blessings of Krishna and the Name. Spiritual strength is received, not self-generated — it flows from loving service (sevā) to the spiritual master and paramparā. Mahārāja distinguishes ordinary faith, distributed among the three modes (tāmasic, rājasic, sāttvic), from the transcendental alaukika-śraddhā that Krishna consciousness requires, which Viśvanātha Cakravartī locates in firm, exclusive, confident determination to follow the bhakti scriptures and processes of devotion. The Bhāgavatam is the Lord's representative form (pratinidhi-rūpa), left behind when Krishna departed so fallen souls could take shelter; reading it daily keeps Krishna present, as Mahārāja found when absorbed study dissolved his physical pain. Crucially, one need not wait for faith: beginning the practice itself generates faith, redirecting the soul's misplaced thirst toward Krishna. For anyone whose japa has declined he counsels not despair but 'chanting reform' — start with a number one can truly do attentively (e.g. four rounds), rebuild the supportive setting, and the full sixteen return. When the tongue is covered by avidyā the name tastes bitter, but Rūpa Gosvāmī's jaundice analogy holds: as sugar both cures the jaundiced palate and is the sweet goal, daily attentive chanting is at once the cure and the goal — provided it is done attentively, since mechanical mumbling is 'a different mantra,' not the mahā-mantra. The lecture closes on the prescribed method of serving the guru — offering seat, water, bath, cloth and ornaments — and only then, with his permission, entering the worship of the Divine Couple.

## Practical application

Begin not with technique but with an honest audit: is my japa a living, reciprocal relationship with Krishna, or a chore I feel condemned to because I vowed sixteen offenseless rounds at initiation? Name where you actually stand — that is the necessary starting point. Then cultivate desire, because instruction only reaches those who genuinely long to experience a lift in the Holy Name. Since chanting lives entirely on faith, work on faith directly: fix your śraddhā firmly in the bhakti scriptures and the process of chanting itself, and read Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam daily as a way of keeping Krishna present. Nourish faith through the association of faithful devotees (go where the food is distributed), and guard it against Kali's attacks — opposing texts, competing ideas, and devotees criticizing one another online — by staying anchored in transcendental consciousness rather than slipping into mundane concepts, especially on contentious contemporary issues. Feed the faith→strength→remembrance chain by serving the spiritual master and paramparā with love, since spiritual strength is received through service, not self-generated. In the japa itself, shift the goal from finishing rounds to chanting for Krishna's pleasure, engaging mind, words and attention as your side of a two-way relationship in which Krishna reciprocates. Treat inattention — laziness, distraction, restlessness — as the chief enemy, and chant attentively while learning what the ten offenses (nāma-aparādha) and service offenses (sevā-aparādha) are and how to overcome them, since offensive chanting leaves the inner darkness intact. Do not wait to feel faith or taste before starting: the practice generates both. If the tongue finds the name bitter, recognize avidyā as a disease, not a stop-sign, and apply Rūpa Gosvāmī's sugar-cure — keep chanting daily and taste gradually returns. If your chanting has declined, don't despair or quit; undertake a 'chanting reform': drop back to a number you can genuinely do attentively (e.g. four rounds), avoid inattention, rebuild the supportive setting (a clean altar, a fixed sitting place), and the full sixteen will return. Above all, chant properly and attentively — mechanical, mumbled repetition is effectively a different mantra — and let firm faith that the Name is the single greatest treasure, valued above eating, resting, or spacing out, make the endeavor effortless and even make you want to chant more. Where worship of the Divine Couple is undertaken, first serve the guru (offering seat, water for the feet, water to drink, bath, cloth and ornaments) and proceed only with his permission.

## Glossary

- **Harinām Cintāmaṇi** — The scripture from which the seminar is drawn — teachings on the holy name spoken by Haridāsa Ṭhākura in devotional ecstasy, noted by his associates and compiled by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura as a dialogue between Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Haridāsa.
- **Nāmācārya** — The 'teacher (ācārya) of the holy name'; the title of Haridāsa Ṭhākura, the authority on chanting.
- **śraddhā** — Faith; the single indispensable qualification for chanting, defined here as the exclusive, confident determination to attentively follow the process of devotion as described in the bhakti scriptures.
- **alaukika-śraddhā** — Transcendental faith — the distinct, otherworldly faith Krishna consciousness requires, contrasted with the mode-bound faith of worldly people.
- **japa** — The soft, repeated personal chanting of the holy name (typically on beads), the daily practice at the center of the lecture.
- **Hari-Nāma / holy name** — The name of Krishna, understood as non-different from Krishna Himself — like the sun, and the one spiritual entity accessible to the conditioned soul's fallen senses.
- **mahā-mantra** — The Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra; when chanted mechanically and inattentively it is said to become 'a different mantra,' not the mahā-mantra.
- **bhakti** — Devotion; engaging mind, words and activity with the intention to please Krishna rather than for one's own enjoyment.
- **prema-bhakti** — Loving devotion to Krishna; ecstatic love of God, the ultimate fruit of chanting.
- **rasa** — Spiritual taste or relish that arises within a relationship with Krishna, given by Him when one pleases Him.
- **nāma-aparādha** — Offenses against the holy name; the ten offenses one must learn to recognize, purify and overcome for pure chanting.
- **sevā-aparādha** — Offenses in devotional service, which likewise obscure the manifestation of the holy name.
- **sādhana** — Regulated devotional practice, which grows from the foundation of faith placed in the bhakti scriptures.
- **sevā** — Service; loving service to the spiritual master and paramparā, through which spiritual strength is received into the heart.
- **guru-paramparā** — The disciplic succession of spiritual masters; serving it with love brings strength in the heart.
- **vīrya** — Vigor or energetic endeavor to pursue the practices of bhakti; born from deep faith.
- **smṛti** — Remembrance — the strength to recall Krishna's essential teachings precisely when needed, under pressure; born from vīrya and protective of one's vow.
- **samādhi** — Undivided concentration; born from remembrance in the journey-of-faith chain.
- **prajñā** — Realization that discriminates the individual soul and material energy from the Supreme Soul; the fruit of samādhi.
- **ātmā** — The individual soul, to be discriminated in realization from material energy and the Supreme Soul.
- **prakṛti** — Material energy or nature, to be discriminated from the soul and the Supreme Soul.
- **puruṣa** — The Supreme Soul; distinguished in prajñā from ātmā and prakṛti.
- **ahaṅkāra** — False ego; the internal 'mountain' standing between the soul and Krishna, which faith and spiritual strength must cross.
- **avidyā** — Ignorance; when it covers the tongue one has no taste for the holy name and it seems bitter — a spiritual disease, not a reason to stop.
- **guṇas (three modes)** — The three modes of material nature governing ordinary faith — tāmasic (indulgence, intoxication, inertia), rājasic (wealth, gratification, fame), and sāttvic (equal vision, contentment, goodness).
- **jñāna, yoga, karma** — The paths of knowledge, mystic yoga, and ritual action — which demand many conditions (yajñas, ritual baths, considerations of time, place and purity), unlike chanting which requires faith alone.
- **yajña** — Vedic sacrifice or ritual; one of the many conditions demanded by the paths of karma, jñāna and yoga but not by chanting.
- **Kaustubha** — The transcendental jewel worn by Krishna; invoked to illustrate that even Krishna's form crowned and bearing the Kaustubha is attained through faithful chanting.
- **cintāmaṇi** — The desire-fulfilling touchstone gems associated with Krishna's abode; cited among the great gifts against which the holy name is still called the greatest treasure.
- **māyā** — The illusory material energy; everything in the material world is dull, inert māyā except the two spiritual entities — the soul and the holy name.
- **pratinidhi-rūpa** — The 'representative form' of the Lord — the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, left by Krishna when Kali entered so fallen souls could take shelter and keep Him present.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** — The scripture that is the Lord's representative form; reading it daily keeps Krishna present in one's life.
