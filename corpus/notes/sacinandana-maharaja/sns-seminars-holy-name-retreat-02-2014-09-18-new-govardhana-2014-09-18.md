# SNS Seminars Holy Name Retreat 02 2014 09 18 New Govardhana Village

## Theme

This Holy-Name retreat lecture by HH Sacīnandana Mahārāja unfolds the truth that the holy name of Kṛṣṇa is identical with Kṛṣṇa himself — fully spiritual, transcendentally blissful, never touched by māyā, with the name and the named being one. Because Kṛṣṇa's name, form, qualities, and pastimes are all the Absolute Truth, they cannot be grasped by the material senses and mind of a soul conditioned by the three modes; spiritual senses are required. Yet the name can choose to reveal itself even to the conditioned, provided we 'get it right.' Kṛṣṇa consciousness is thus an 'open secret' — a reality ordinarily shared only between a pure devotee and Kṛṣṇa, now made openly available. The circumstance that unlocks it is śraddhā, faith understood by folk etymology as 'giving the heart' (śrat = heart, dā = to give). Faith — not any other qualification — is the sole right to chant and enter the secret; without it the name remains a 'close stranger,' near from childhood yet undisclosed even after thirty years of chanting. Mahārāja develops this through the parable of the unreachable forest girl carried across seven rivers and three oceans by an emissary to an all-powerful, kind prince: the holy name (and the agent of grace) bridges the otherwise uncrossable distance between the soul and Kṛṣṇa, and reveals itself as Kṛṣṇa only gradually — first as a mere symbol or sound, even an 'exquisite torture,' until one is at last ushered into the 'palace of the holy name' and recognizes that the name IS Kṛṣṇa. He traces how faith matures: from weak komala-śraddhā, born when doubt is removed and a favorable inclination arises, into clear and absolute faith that protects the practitioner like a mother. Maturing faith confers successive blessings — vīrya/bala (inner strength to overcome lust and greed), smṛti (timely remembrance of scripture and sādhus), samādhi (absorption that places one where Kṛṣṇa is found), and prajñā (clear discrimination of self from body and realization of Kṛṣṇa). This strength is not self-generated but received as mercy, echoing Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja's prayer for the 'walking stick' of the saints' mercy. Faith must be invested in two resting places: śāstra (which reports the reality beyond our reach) and the process for this age, the chanting of nāma. Hearing of Kṛṣṇa's qualities — Patita-pāvana, Bhakta-vatsala — grows faith while faith in the world's lies falls away. He grounds the need for faith in our constitutional position: the living being is essentially prakṛti, meant to be enjoyed, while Kṛṣṇa alone is the puruṣa, the enjoyer. Drawing on the Harināma Cintāmaṇi, Jīva Gosvāmī's gold-prospector analogy, Śrīla Prabhupāda's correction that the books were written to be read, and the Bhāgavatam's locating of faith's birthplace in sat-saṅga (whence śraddhā ripens into rati and prema-bhakti), Mahārāja insists that real advancement means being pulled out of one's comfort zone, since illusion is to keep doing what one always did while expecting a new result. Crucially, we do not find Kṛṣṇa consciousness — we are found by it, by mercy; the holy name does the seeking, as in his own story of running from the Harinām party only to be caught by the name. Finally he addresses the senses (the five wild horses): the name is cintāmaṇi, a wish-fulfilling jewel that gives whatever one meditates on while chanting — material desire yields material results (Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's 'most disturbing point'), while the desireless receive pure prema. Chanting without sambandha-jñāna misdirects the chanter and feeds the material desires that drive high attrition. The lecture therefore closes where it began — on śraddhā as giving the heart with the clear, honest desire 'I want You, Kṛṣṇa.'

## Practical application

For daily japa and Hari-Nāma, the central instruction is to invest faith — to give your heart. Begin by accepting that you cannot yet taste or perceive the name with material senses, and chant anyway on initial faith, trusting that 'the journey itself is God' and will carry you, just as the forest girl left home and followed a stranger. Treat the name not as a stranger but cultivate a genuine relationship with him, so he chooses to reveal himself rather than remaining a 'close stranger' even after decades. Anchor your practice in the two resting places: regularly hear and read śāstra (Prabhupāda's books — 'I wrote them for you') about Kṛṣṇa's qualities, world, and names, and follow a definite, learned process of how to chant (as taught through the Harināma Cintāmaṇi); hearing plus an immediate process are both required. Seek sat-saṅga, the association of devotees, where hearing of Kṛṣṇa becomes relishable and śraddhā awakens. Chant honestly: examine what your mind dwells on while chanting, because the name is a cintāmaṇi / kalpa-vṛkṣa that fructifies whatever you meditate on — so consciously bring the desire 'I want You, Kṛṣṇa,' and admit and reject the material desires that have led nowhere over lifetimes rather than secretly feeding them like a 'cheater.' Pursue the practice through every hardship — cold, discomfort, the 'mosquitoes' of distraction — and step out of habit and comfort zone, since doing what you always did yields only what you always got. Receive the strength to do this as mercy, begging for the 'walking stick' of the saints' grace rather than relying on your own effort, and hold the mood of humble gratitude — you are being found, not finding — watching for the disturbances and 'interruptions' in your small world as possibly Kṛṣṇa's arrangement, and investing your faith there. As faith matures it will gift you strength against lust and greed, timely remembrance (smṛti), absorption (samādhi), and ultimately realization (prajñā).

## Glossary

- **Holy Name / Nāma** — The name of Kṛṣṇa, fully spiritual and non-different from (identical with) Kṛṣṇa himself; the means by which Kṛṣṇa is attained in this age.
- **Kṛṣṇa** — The Supreme Lord, the Absolute Truth, whose body, name, form, qualities, pastimes, and entourage are all as good as himself.
- **Māyā** — Material illusion / illusory energy; the holy name, being spiritual, is never touched by it.
- **Three modes of nature (guṇas)** — The three qualities of material nature that condition the living entity, so that material senses and mind cannot grasp Kṛṣṇa's name or form.
- **Śraddhā** — Faith; by folk etymology śrat ('heart') + dā/dhā ('to give') — the act of giving one's heart; the sole qualification and entry point into the open secret of Kṛṣṇa consciousness.
- **Komala-śraddhā** — Tender or weak beginning faith, which matures into clear, absolute faith that protects the practitioner.
- **Rasa** — Spiritual taste or relish, experienced when one chants and perceives Kṛṣṇa's form and qualities.
- **Rati** — Awakened attachment; the revelation of one's divine relationship with Kṛṣṇa, arising after śraddhā in sat-saṅga.
- **Prema / Prema-bhakti** — Pure love of Kṛṣṇa; the ultimate fruit given by the holy name to one free of material desire.
- **Vīrya / Bala** — Spiritual strength, vigor, and energy in the heart — a blessing of growing faith that enables one to conquer obstacles like lust and greed.
- **Smṛti** — Remembrance; a blessing of matured faith whereby the instructions of scripture and sādhus return precisely when needed, the faithful being gifted an undisturbed mind.
- **Samādhi** — Absorption in the holy name beyond being disturbed or undisturbed, arriving on the very platform where Kṛṣṇa is found.
- **Prajñā** — Clear discrimination of the self from the body and realization of Kṛṣṇa; first-class realization.
- **Śāstra** — Revealed scripture; one of the two resting places of faith, which tells of the spiritual reality beyond our reach.
- **Sat-saṅga** — The company/association of devotees; the Bhāgavatam's identified birthplace of faith, where hearing of Kṛṣṇa becomes relishable.
- **Sambandha-jñāna** — Knowledge of Kṛṣṇa and of one's relationship with him; without it the chanter is misdirected.
- **Cintāmaṇi** — A wish-fulfilling 'thought jewel' or touchstone; the holy name, non-different from Kṛṣṇa, gives whatever one meditates on while chanting and transforms whatever it contacts, as a touchstone turns iron to gold.
- **Kalpa-vṛkṣa** — Desire tree; like it, chanting fructifies whatever the mind dwells on — including one's fears.
- **Prakṛti** — The feminine, enjoyed principle; the constitutional position of the living being, meant to be enjoyed by the Lord.
- **Puruṣa** — The masculine enjoyer; Kṛṣṇa alone, who enjoys prakṛti.
- **Patita-pāvana** — An epithet of Kṛṣṇa: savior or purifier of the fallen.
- **Bhakta-vatsala** — An epithet of Kṛṣṇa: one who is affectionate to and the loving friend of his devotees.
- **Harināma Cintāmaṇi** — Scripture by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura on the holy name, cited for the teaching that the Lord placed all His powers in the name and that faith is the sole qualification to chant; used at the retreat to teach the process of chanting.
- **Bhāgavatam** — The Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, cited for locating faith's birthplace in sat-saṅga and tracing its ripening into rati and prema-bhakti.
- **Jīva Gosvāmī** — Vaiṣṇava ācārya who compares the chanter to a gold prospector — one who hears a report of gold, places faith in it, leaves everything, and doggedly practices the process to obtain it.
- **Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja** — Author of the Caitanya-caritāmṛta, recalled for his prayer that, being feeble on a slippery path, he begs the saints for the 'walking stick' of their mercy.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — Vaiṣṇava ācārya whose 'most disturbing point' is that the cintāmaṇi of the name gives the materially desirous material results, but gives pure prema to the desireless.
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda** — Founder-ācārya of ISKCON, recalled (from Germany) for correcting that his books were written to be read, not only distributed — 'I wrote them for you.'
- **Haribol** — The call to chant the holy name; used here to denote the practice of chanting that will die out without faith invested in śāstra and process.
