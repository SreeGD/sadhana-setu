# BJP Seminars Japa Seminar Day 03 2020 01 16 ISKCON NVCC

## Theme

This concluding session of a three-day japa seminar by Bhūrijana Prabhu draws the whole course to a single point: learning to make the effort to hear the Holy Name attentively and in the proper attitude. Bhūrijana begins by radically simplifying the task of japa — we can only hear the one mantra we are chanting right now, having no control over rounds already past or yet to come, so the realistic scope of effort is to be with Krishna in this present mantra, even aiming for just one Mahāmantra heard clearly. The method is hearing each syllable; the fruit is a felt lightness, ease, and satisfaction. He frames the Holy Name as a door that, unlike the deity doors, is always open — the only obstacle is our own consciousness, which we close by following the wandering mind. From here the lecture widens into why this practice matters: the soul wanders the banyan tree of material existence driven by desire, so purifying desire is the very purpose of the temple (which Prabhupada conceived as a learning institution transmitting Krishna's teachings) and of holy association. Citing Ṛṣabhadeva, the speaker locates human life's purpose in unending spiritual bliss rather than sense gratification, and through varṇāśrama as a chain of dependence shows a society governed by Krishna's word. The central diagnosis follows: what holds us back from surrender is fear — fear of losing independent enjoyment and of not being protected — and beneath even that, the root disease is the absence of a relationship with Krishna, from which flow loneliness and endless searching. Impersonalism/Mayavada is named as the worst form of this disease. Against it stands pure dependence (Madhavendra Puri) and the realization, ripening at bhāva-bhakti, that the name is non-different from Krishna, so to chant is to meet Him personally — as Ajamila's deliverance proves Krishna responds to mere proximity to His name. The practical heart of the talk is preparation and persistence: settle the mind before chanting, work with the wandering mind rather than fighting it, pray for mercy, and keep returning again and again to the simple goal of hearing. The final movement addresses the mind's two failures — distraction (passion) and sleepiness (ignorance) per Viśvanātha Cakravartī — remedied by cultivating goodness (sattva) through practical adjustments, then offering that clear consciousness to Krishna. At the neophyte stage the decisive thing is not control of the mind but getting desire right ('I want to be a good servant'), never stopping chanting, and never stopping depending on Krishna, since pride, not weakness, opens the door to maya. The seminar's sustained thrust: make a living connection with Krishna, turn toward Him rather than away, and surrender — for knowing is not surrendering, and only attentive, service-mood hearing delivers.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this to daily japa by drastically shrinking the goal: do not plan many rounds or brood over rounds going badly — aim only to hear, clearly, the one Hare Krishna mahāmantra you are chanting right now, beginning with 'Hare.' Prepare before you begin: settle the mind, know your destination and intent, and approach with reverence, treating this as fortunate time set aside for nothing but being with the person of Krishna present in His names — so you don't 'ride off and forget where you were going.' Concentrate by hearing each syllable, offering your tongue and ear in a serving mood, and chant as personal address to a person who is actually listening ('Hare' calling out to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī). Expect the mind to wander — this is natural and not cause for anxiety; the work is simply to bring it back gently, again and again, since japa is a repeated returning, not a single achievement. Rather than fighting the mind, work with it: redirect it to seeing Krishna and to a prayerful sense of your own helplessness, and beg for help — praying to Krishna, guru, the Vaishnavas, Srila Prabhupada, and Sri Sri Gaura-Nitai. When the mind fails through distraction (passion) or sleepiness (ignorance), apply practical remedies to cultivate goodness: stand up, chant a little louder, rest when needed, sleep properly, eat less. Use honest self-inquiry away from the beads — ask what fear stops you from surrendering, and resolve to accept Krishna's decisions rather than clinging to your own plan. Let feeling fallen drive you deeper into Krishna's shelter rather than into self-absorbed brooding, which is just the mind still fixed on oneself. Do not force remembrance of pastimes; hear and chant to Krishna as a person, and let remembrance arise naturally. Extend the same attentiveness to Bhāgavatam class, to congregational kīrtana, and to all service, so every act becomes conscious devotional service. Above all, at the neophyte stage, do not fret over controlling the mind — keep your desire right ('I want to be a good servant of Krishna, Guru, Vaishnavas, and the deities'), take good guidance, follow the process, never stop chanting, and never stop depending on Krishna.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Soft, individual chanting of the Holy Name on beads; here, the practice of attentively hearing one Hare Krishna mantra at a time.
- **Hare Kṛṣṇa mahāmantra** — The 'great mantra' for deliverance; this specific configuration of the Lord's names has special potency and was the mantra Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Śrīla Prabhupāda specifically asked devotees to chant.
- **Holy Name / Hari-Nāma** — The name of the Lord, understood to be non-different from Krishna Himself, so that hearing one's chanting is an actual personal meeting with Him.
- **bhāva-bhakti** — The stage of devotional ecstasy at which the realization dawns naturally that the Holy Name is non-different from Krishna; it cannot be artificially manufactured.
- **māyā** — The Lord's illusory energy, which awards false ego and a sense of independent selfhood when the soul turns away from Krishna; its expansions (shopping, internet) only temporarily fill the void of a missing relationship with Krishna.
- **ahaṅkāra** — False ego; the sense of independent selfhood awarded by Maya, by which one sees everything as meant for one's own pleasure and separate from Krishna.
- **Mayavada / Impersonalism** — The doctrine that would rather not exist than give itself to Krishna, objecting even to individual existence with free will; described as amounting to atheism, and labelled by Ramanujacarya as Shankara's 'covered Buddhism.'
- **varṇāśrama** — The system of social and spiritual orders, presented here as a chain of dependence in which no one is independent — ultimately governed by Krishna's word transmitted through śāstra and the brahmanas.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture, which the brahmanas study and assimilate fully so they answer from Krishna's instruction rather than personal opinion.
- **brāhmaṇa** — One who studies and teaches scripture; Prabhupada urged supporting brahmanas to teach Bhagavad-gītā so as to transmit Krishna's teachings, not merely to perpetuate Sanskrit lineage.
- **Bhagavad-gītā** — Krishna's teaching to Arjuna; cited for the banyan tree of material existence (ch. 15), offering work in goodness to Krishna (ch. 17), and its conclusion that one should carry out Krishna's word.
- **Ṛṣabhadeva** — An incarnation of the Lord whose teachings establish that human life's purpose is unending spiritual bliss, not the sense gratification available even to lower species.
- **prema** — Pure love of God; named in the layered ideal of how to chant (with surrender, prema, ruchi, and humility).
- **ruchi** — Taste or relish for the Holy Name; one element in the layered ideal of chanting, though the irreducible minimum remains simply to hear.
- **sattva (mode of goodness)** — The mode in which consciousness is clear with neither distraction nor sleepiness, bringing peace and happiness; cultivated by practical adjustments and meant to be filled with Krishna and offered to Him as devotional service.
- **modes of passion and ignorance** — The two ways the mind fails in chanting per Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura — distraction (passion) and sleepiness (ignorance) — remedied by cultivating goodness.
- **seva** — Devotional service; what any activity becomes when consciously connected to Krishna and offered to Him.
- **Ajamila** — The fallen soul who, calling his son 'Narayana' at death, was delivered — proof that Krishna's mercy responds to mere proximity to the name, taking it as His devotee calling and sending the Vishnudutas.
- **Vishnudutas** — The messengers of Vishnu sent to protect Ajamila when Krishna heard the syllables 'Narayana' and took it as His devotee calling out to Him.
- **Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī** — The Lord's internal energy, addressed by the word 'Hare' in the mahāmantra; chanting begins as personal address to Her.
- **Madhavendra Puri** — The exemplar of pure dependence on Krishna, who would not even beg for food, embodying the devotee's resolve to do nothing but chant and depend wholly on Krishna.
- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — The incarnation who specifically requested the chanting of the Hare Krishna mahāmantra and whose instruction is simply: never stop chanting; He taught beginners by giving the names (Hari Haraya Namaḥ) to sing.
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda** — The founder-ācārya whose conception of the temple as a learning institution and whose process of hearing-and-chanting (without artificially forcing pastimes) the seminar follows.
- **Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati** — An ācārya cited as a self-controlled soul who would chastise and 'beat' the mind — one temperament for handling the mind during chanting.
- **Raghunatha dasa Goswami** — An ācārya cited as one who pleads with and flatters the mind, begging it to let him hear just one Hare Krishna mantra — the contrasting temperament for handling the mind.
- **pūrva-saṁskāras** — Impressions and tendencies carried from previous lives; one of the factors (along with how covered one is by the modes and how strictly one follows) determining how long purification takes.
- **jñānī** — A learned or discerning person; cited to show that accumulated knowledge alone does not deliver, as even jñānīs are swept away by the current of māyā — knowing is not surrendering.
- **gurukula** — The traditional school of spiritual education; named alongside everyday spiritual life as a setting whose ultimate goal is obedience to Krishna.
- **Bhāgavatam** — Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam; hearing it is a purifying chance to set desire straight, and its class deserves the same attentiveness endeavored for in chanting.
- **Sri Sri Gaura-Nitai** — Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Nityānanda; among those one prays to for extra help when the mind wanders during japa.
