# BJP Seminars Japa Seminar Day 01 2020 01 14 ISKCON NVCC

## Theme

This lecture opens by establishing Vrindavan as the spiritual world itself — a living dham meant for hearing and chanting — and grounds the practice of japa in the living entity's constitutional position: we are eternal fragmental parts of Krishna whose very nature is to glorify Him. Our suffering in the material world stems from forgetting this and craving glorification for ourselves instead. Bhūrijana Prabhu surveys how Kali-yuga, through materialistic education and the internet, corrupts consciousness and distracts people from ever asking life's central question — whom am I meant to serve? Drawing on the Seventh Canto of Srimad Bhagavatam, he holds up Prahlada Maharaja as the true hero and follows Narada's exposition of varnasrama, in which a whole culture silently instills values. From this he draws the principles of discipline: obedience requires controlling the mind, so mind-control is the essence of education; true education trains the student to accept real values over Maya's illusory 'projections on smoke' — the same discernment exercised in japa when one watches the mind wander and refuses the illusion. He stresses that vani (the teacher's instruction) surpasses vapu (physical presence), that the disciple's task is to make the master's heart his own, and that Srila Prabhupada's secret was the sweetness of Vraja he carried everywhere through the parampara. The heart of the lecture is japa as a personal meeting with Krishna through His holy names: prepare the mind with a deliberate resolve (sankalpa) that 'this is my time to be with Krishna,' chant attentively because inattentive chanting insults the Person one has invited (aparādha), and reduce the whole practice to one simple act — hearing. A wandering mind is natural and no cause for discouragement; our only job is to keep bringing it back and try our best, while Krishna, supremely liberal, delights in the mere sincere effort. He frames the mahāmantra as Prabhupada translated it — a prayer to be accepted as Krishna's servant — and the chanter's mood as surrender of the false enjoyer's position. The lecture closes with practical diagnostics and remedies: gauge good chanting by attentive hearing and the resulting hunger to chant more; use the ācāryas' 'stick method' and gentle pleading to discipline the mind; cultivate a desperate need to take shelter; read sastra to know Krishna as a person; rest in good consciousness; and recognize that continuous chanting of the holy names is the only truly safe shelter for consciousness.

## Practical application

Before chanting, the devotee prepares the mind with conscious intention: decide deliberately what you are about to do, offer prayers to guru, the Vaiṣṇavas, Srila Prabhupada, and the deities, and make a firm resolve (sankalpa) — 'for this fixed period I have nothing else to do except be with Krishna; other duties can wait.' Approach japa as a real personal meeting with Krishna, not a mechanical exercise: chant as bhakti, an offering of ear, tongue, and mind to the Supreme Person. Keep the goal modest and attainable — simply to hear even one Hare Krishna mahāmantra clearly, beginning with 'Hare,' touching the sound vibration with your consciousness through hearing; do not strain for quantity or volume. Treat inattentive chanting as a genuine insult (aparādha) to the Person you have invited, and treat a wandering mind not as failure but as natural — your only job is to keep gently bringing it back, again and again, without anxiety. Hold the standard of steady improvement rather than perfection: chant a little better than last time. When distraction strikes, recognize it works as a chain — refuse the first stray thought before it leads to a second and third — and remind yourself, 'This is my sadhana, my time to be with Krishna.' Use honest self-inquiry ('Do I actually want to be with Krishna?'); the humility this exposes turns naturally into prayer to Radharani, Krishna, Mahaprabhu, Srila Prabhupada, and Nityananda for strength and for freedom from maya's grip. Chant in the mood of the mahāmantra as Prabhupada translated it — a plea to be accepted as a servant, surrendering the false enjoyer's position. Diagnose your own chanting: attentive hearing that leaves you hungry to chant more is good; a wandering or dozing mind that leaves you feeling cheated is bad, and the remedy is to become conscious of the lapse and chant more distinctly, hearing each word. Discipline the 'rascal mind' with either Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's forceful 'stick method' or Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī's gentle pleading. Cultivate a felt, desperate need to take shelter, and counter casualness by remembering the seriousness of the endeavour and your fallen position. Support japa by regularly reading Bhagavad-gita, Srimad Bhagavatam, and Caitanya-caritamrta to come to know Krishna's qualities, and by taking proper rest in a prayerful consciousness so morning japa comes more easily. Finally, do your part and leave the rest to Krishna, who is supremely liberal and delights in sincere effort — and remember that continuous chanting is the only truly safe shelter for the consciousness.

## Glossary

- **Japa** — The personal, often soft, repetition of the holy names of Krishna — here understood as a personal meeting with Krishna anytime through His names, the practice that frames the whole lecture.
- **Hare Krishna mahāmantra** — The great chant for deliverance ('Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna Hare Hare, Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama Hare Hare'); even hearing one clearly, beginning with 'Hare,' is held to make one very fortunate, since one name of Krishna can destroy all sin.
- **Sankalpa** — A deliberate resolve or determination made before chanting — 'for this fixed period I have nothing to do except be with Krishna' — the essential mental preparation that frames the session.
- **Aparādha** — Offense; here, the insult of turning one's attention away from Krishna while invoking His presence in the holy name — inattentive chanting treated as insulting an invited guest, which makes Krishna seem far away though He is near.
- **Bhakti** — Devotional service; chanting performed as bhakti is an offering of the ear, tongue, and mind to Krishna as the Supreme Person, not an impersonal exercise.
- **Sādhana** — Regulated spiritual practice; japa is one's sadhana, and even resting well in prayerful consciousness is treated as part of it.
- **Kīrtana** — Glorification of Krishna through chanting of His names; continuous, unbroken kirtana is described as the only truly safe shelter for consciousness.
- **Vrindavan (dham)** — Krishna's eternal spiritual abode — not an ordinary town but the living spiritual world itself, meant for hearing and chanting; dham refers to such a holy abode.
- **Giri Govardhan / Giraj** — Govardhan Hill in Vrindavan, worshipable as non-different from Krishna; Giraj (the king of mountains) bestows abundant mercy on those who take shelter there and simply hear and chant.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present age of quarrel and hypocrisy, whose specific working is to corrupt innocent youth — through materialistic education and the internet — filling consciousness with what is hard to remove.
- **Varṇāśrama** — The Vedic social-spiritual culture of occupational divisions (varṇa) and stages of life (āśrama); Narada explains it to Yudhisthira as the medium that silently instills values into consciousness, beginning with student (brahmacari) life.
- **Brahmacari** — A celibate student under spiritual training; the foundational āśrama where one learns obedience and mind-control as the basis of education.
- **Maya** — Illusion; the Lord's deluding energy that presents illusory values as important — 'projections on smoke' with no substance — which true education and attentive japa train one to see through.
- **Vani** — The instruction or teaching of the spiritual master, held to be more important than vapu (physical presence); the disciple's task is to assimilate the master's values and heart as his own.
- **Vapu** — The physical presence or bodily form of the spiritual master, contrasted with and subordinate to vani (his instruction).
- **Parampara** — The disciplic succession through which teaching and the sweetness of Vraja are transmitted; following Prabhupada means becoming servants down the parampara culminating in Krishna of Vrindavan.
- **Vraja** — The land and loving mood of Vrindavan; Srila Prabhupada's 'secret' power was the sweetness of Vraja — his loving relationships with Radha-Krishna, his guru, and Mahaprabhu — that he carried everywhere.
- **Prahlada Maharaja** — The great child-devotee of the Seventh Canto of Srimad Bhagavatam, held up as the true hero because he is the first ordinary person to set a perfectly followable example.
- **Nrsimhadeva** — The half-man, half-lion incarnation of Krishna who appears in the Seventh Canto; though His pastime is central, Prahlada (not the Lord) is named the canto's hero for exemplarity.
- **Ajamila** — The fallen brahmana who was delivered for chanting the name of Narayana at death; his episode illustrates Krishna's liberality — because the devotee chanted His name, the Lord takes protection as His own duty.
- **Mahaprabhu** — Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu, who gave the gift of associating with Krishna anytime through the holy name; prayed to for strength during chanting.
- **Radharani** — Srimati Radharani, the supreme energy of the Lord and object of prayer during chanting; the mahāmantra addresses Her as 'energy of the Lord.'
- **Nityananda Prabhu** — Lord Nityananda, an associate of Mahaprabhu prayed to — along with Radharani, Krishna, Mahaprabhu, and Srila Prabhupada — for strength and freedom from maya's grip.
- **Vaiṣṇavas** — Devotees of Vishnu/Krishna, to whom prayers are offered at the start of chanting.
- **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura** — The acarya whose forceful 'stick method' of disciplining the mind — rebuking it, 'Rascal mind, I don't want to listen to you' — is offered as one remedy for distraction.
- **Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī** — The acarya whose gentle pleading approach — 'My dear mind, please just hear one mantra' — complements the 'stick method' for disciplining the mind.
- **Svarupa Damodara** — A confidential associate of Mahaprabhu, on whose authority total ecstatic absorption in chanting (dancing, crying, laughing) is held up as the very essence of Srimad Bhagavatam and true effect of chanting.
- **Goswamis / Godas** — The Goswamis are the exalted masters of Vrindavan; 'Godas' (servant) is the humble self-designation Prabhupada used, saying Vrindavan is for the Goswamis while he is merely a servant.
- **Bhagavad-gita, Srimad Bhagavatam, Caitanya-caritamrta** — The principal scriptures to be read and studied regularly so as to know Krishna as a person and appreciate His qualities — that He is soft, kind, and accepts the good in us.
