# BJP Seminar Japa Retreat Morning lecture Day 02

## Theme

Built on the Ajāmila narrative of the Bhāgavatam, this lecture traces how the holy name works as the supreme atonement and, beyond that, as the means of capturing the Lord's attention and love. It opens with the fragility of conditioned life: even a faultless brāhmaṇa boy, Ajāmila, was ruined by a moment of bad association, and the reactions of his sinful life had to be paid — yet the material shelters he leaned on (compared to crossing the ocean by gripping a swimming dog's tail) prove powerless at death, when consciousness clings to whatever one most cultivated. By attachment Ajāmila had named his son Nārāyaṇa, so his terrified, even foolish, dying cry of 'Nārāyaṇa' invoked the name's own potency and the Viṣṇudūtas arrived to halt death itself. Their debate with the Yamadūtas turns on the verse's deeper meaning: nāma-saṅkīrtana is the supreme atonement because, unlike a formal vrata that removes only the reaction, it destroys the very root of sin — and because, as Prabhupāda draws out from Śrīdhara Svāmī and Viśvanātha Cakravartī and places inside the verse itself ('yatas tad-viṣayā matiḥ'), the name draws the Lord's mind toward the chanter so that He reciprocally feels obliged to protect one who belongs to Him. That divine attention is not mere notice but claiming, protective love, which is why it brings relief rather than dread. Because Kṛṣṇa is omnipresent (as shown by Nanda seized by Varuṇa's agents and by the Nṛsiṁha–Prahlāda pastime) and yet, by His acintya-śakti, present in a fully personal form, He hears and turns to the chanter instantly. By kaimutya-nyāya, if even Ajāmila's offenseless nāmābhāsa won full deliverance, how much more does the Lord attend to one who chants in service, in prema, and most of all in the mood Śrī Caitanya teaches. The lecture then pivots inward: when you chant, Kṛṣṇa's attention is likewise on you and He sees your inattention and boredom, so the sincere attempt to be present — free of pramāda — is itself a large part of the offering. Citing Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's instruction, 'do not try to see Kṛṣṇa, but act so that Kṛṣṇa will see you,' the teaching reframes devotion as melting the Lord's heart through humble service rather than demanding vision, since Kṛṣṇa reveals Himself only to one with prema and 'Vṛndāvana consciousness.' The mechanics of that reciprocation are illustrated by the dāmodara-līlā: Yaśodā's vātsalya-prema conquers Kṛṣṇa's omniscience so that He genuinely feels fear and yet, by His own potencies (sva-saṅkalpa, īśa-śakti), the rope stays always two fingers short — until His heart melts at her loving endeavor and His kṛpā-śakti, the king of His potencies, binds Him. Viśvanātha reads the two-finger gap as pariśrama (the devotee's endeavor) plus kṛpā (His mercy): neither alone suffices. This bhakta-niṣṭhā meeting Kṛṣṇa's sva-niṣṭhā becomes the model for all service and for japa itself, culminating in the lecture's central reversal of consciousness — to stop standing at the center as the seer and enjoyer of the world and instead know oneself as the seen, the object of Kṛṣṇa's loving gaze.

## Practical application

Approach each round not as a quota to 'get over and done with' but as a meeting in which Kṛṣṇa's attention is actually upon you — He sees the very inattention and boredom you imagine no one notices. Give the mind one simple task: to hear one name, one mantra. 'Simple' is the mode of goodness; entertaining grand thoughts, plans, and concepts engages the intelligence (which, per Kapila Muni, is born of passion), branching one stray thought into a network of complexity from which the mind can no longer be recalled — so keep gently returning the mind to goodness, its natural home, and to hearing. You need not yet chant with love or full shelter; what is asked is the sincere endeavor to chant attentively and offenselessly, especially free of pramāda (inattention), because that small sincerity — the attempt to be present with Kṛṣṇa — is itself a large part of the offering. Hold the right question: not 'what am I seeing or enjoying?' but 'what is Kṛṣṇa seeing — someone sincerely trying to be with Him, someone surrendered to the wandering mind, or someone merely pushing beads to tally rounds?' Adopt the mood 'do not try to see Kṛṣṇa; act so that Kṛṣṇa will see you,' shifting from being the subject who sees and enjoys to being the object who is seen. This same principle extends beyond japa: on Ekādaśī, through the 64 rounds, even when tired or distracted, work simply to please Him; in any service — even sweeping the temple floor — ask 'how can I act so that Kṛṣṇa turns toward me and His mercy is evoked?', remembering that endeavor (pariśrama) and His mercy (kṛpā) are both indispensable. And go for darśana not only to see the deities but to be seen by them — to present yourself, bringing a gift before presenting your list of wants. When the thought arises 'the Lord's attention is on me,' let the response be not pride but the sobering 'what is He seeing?' — again placing yourself as object, not subject.

## Glossary

- **nāma-saṅkīrtana** — Congregational or vocal chanting of the holy name of Viṣṇu; declared in the lecture to be the supreme atonement, superior to ordinary penances.
- **nāmābhāsa** — A 'shadow' or clearing stage of the holy name — chanting that is offenseless but not yet pure devotion. Ajāmila's innocent calling of his son's name, with no motive or duplicity, is the example.
- **nāmāparādha** — Offense against the holy name. The specific offense contrasted here is sinning on the strength of the name — deliberately sinning while planning to chant afterward to be freed.
- **prāyaścitta** — Prescribed ritual atonement or penance for sins (e.g., a twelve-year vrata); it removes the reaction but, unlike the holy name, leaves the root of sin intact.
- **vrata** — A vow or austerity undertaken as atonement; cited as a formal penance that addresses only the effect of sin, not its root.
- **yatas tad-viṣayā matiḥ** — The verse phrase meaning that chanting draws the mati — taken here as the attention or consciousness — of the Lord toward the chanter.
- **mati** — Mind, attention, or consciousness; in the verse, the Lord's attention that the holy name attracts to the one who chants.
- **Yamadūtas** — The agents of Yamarāja who, by legalistic argument, sought to drag the unatoned Ajāmila to be judged and punished.
- **Viṣṇudūtas** — The Lord's own messengers who halted death and rebuked the Yamadūtas, declaring Ajāmila sinless because his sins had already been counteracted by the name.
- **Yamarāja / Dharmarāja** — The lord of death and justice, appointed to uphold dharma; here shown failing to discern who is truly sinful and who is sinless.
- **dharma** — Religion, righteous law, or duty; the principle the Yamadūtas claimed to enforce but, per the Viṣṇudūtas, did not truly understand.
- **prema** — Pure love of God; the highest mood of chanting and the qualification by which Kṛṣṇa reveals Himself to the devotee.
- **kaimutya-nyāya** — The logic of 'what to speak of' (a fortiori reasoning): if even Ajāmila's bare calling won deliverance, how much more is one who chants in service, in prema, and in Caitanya Mahāprabhu's mood attended to by the Lord.
- **acintya-śakti** — Inconceivable potency — Kṛṣṇa's power to be simultaneously all-pervading and present in His fully personal, individual form (as Nṛsiṁha emerging from the pillar).
- **pramāda** — Inattention or negligence in chanting; the offense one should especially endeavor to avoid by striving to chant attentively.
- **vātsalya-prema** — Parental love of God; Mother Yaśodā's motherly affection for Kṛṣṇa, exemplified in the boiling-milk (dadhi-mathana) pastime.
- **dadhi-mathana** — The churning-of-yogurt / boiling-milk pastime in which Yaśodā set Kṛṣṇa down to save the overflowing milk pot, prompting His childlike anger.
- **Vraja-bhakti** — The devotion of the residents of Vraja (Vṛndāvana); its loving consciousness 'conquers' Kṛṣṇa's omniscience so that, desired simply as a boy, He forgets His Godhood.
- **sva-saṅkalpa-śakti / īśa-śakti** — Kṛṣṇa's potency that fulfills His own desire; it acts so that no matter how much rope is added, the cord remains exactly two fingers too short.
- **vibhūti** — Kṛṣṇa's potency of opulence and might; one of the lesser potencies that, by itself, Yaśodā's endeavor could never overcome.
- **kṛpā-śakti** — Kṛṣṇa's mercy-potency, called the 'king of all His potencies'; evoked when His heart melts at the devotee's loving endeavor, it is what finally bound Him.
- **pariśrama** — The devotee's sincere endeavor or labor; together with kṛpā it forms the two-fingers-short measure — endeavor alone cannot bind Kṛṣṇa, yet without it His mercy is never evoked.
- **bhakta-niṣṭhā** — The devotee's steadiness in persistence and in the sincere attempt to please Kṛṣṇa, which draws His attention.
- **sva-niṣṭhā** — Kṛṣṇa's own steadiness in His merciful nature, by which He reliably reciprocates the devotee's sincere endeavor.
- **vidhi** — Regulated devotional practice following rules and injunctions; the level on which a servant applies the principle of Yaśodā's spontaneous love.
- **Ekādaśī** — The eleventh lunar day, observed by fasting and increased chanting (here, 64 rounds), offered as an occasion to remember that Kṛṣṇa's attention is upon us.
- **darśana** — Sacred seeing of the deity; reframed here as going not only to see the Lord but to be seen by Him — to present oneself before Him.
- **japa** — Soft, individual repetition of the holy name on beads; the central practice to which the whole teaching of being seen by Kṛṣṇa is applied.
