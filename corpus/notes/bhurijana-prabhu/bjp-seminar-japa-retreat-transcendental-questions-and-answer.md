# BJP Seminar Japa Retreat Transcendental questions and answers Day 04

## Theme

Framed as a question-and-answer session on the holy name, this lecture by Bhūrijana Prabhu builds from the dignity of inquiry to the practice of attentive japa and finally to the inner faith that sustains it. He opens by affirming that a transcendental question — like Parīkṣit Mahārāja's about the duty of one about to die — is glorious in itself, and uses honest contemplation of death to press the sharper question of whether there is real life after birth, since a life not pointed toward a higher purpose 'adds up to a zero.' Turning to japa itself, he treats Prabhupāda's reference of roughly seven and a half minutes per round not as a rule but as a diagnostic of attention, and explains the mechanics of the wandering mind (born of goodness and false ego) being carried off by the passionate intelligence — countered by acknowledging the mind, then redirecting it to hear one name and one mantra at a time, since attentive chanting need not take any longer than inattentive chanting. He then situates chanting among the limbs of bhakti: the holy name is supremely powerful and independent of formal initiation, yet guru, śāstra, deity worship and the association of devotees are not rivals to the name but exist to teach pure, offenseless, loving chanting — and he warns, with Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's analysis, that ignoring this breeds sahajiyāism and the misuse of 'all I need is the holy name.' The decisive factor, he stresses, is the chanter's own receptivity, not the name's capacity (the Bill Gates analogy): qualification means becoming a servant of the name rather than its enjoyer, following Haridāsa Ṭhākura's fixity and 'crying like a baby for its mother.' Because the wandering mind cannot be perfected by one's own power, humility is the necessary intermediate step that attracts Kṛṣṇa's mercy, and sincerity itself is caught through association with sincere devotees. He gives practical supports — periodic communal japa, four simple principles (hear one mantra, be determined, try again, stay detached from results) — and illustrates attentive nāmābhāsa through Ajāmila, the law of final thoughts through Bharata Mahārāja, and fearless detachment through Jaḍa Bharata. He closes on relationships and faith: see every devotee as Kṛṣṇa's instrument and recognize each is on a different path, ground everything in strong sādhana, and understand that weak determination stems from weak faith — both faith in the positive (loving service in Goloka is what truly satisfies) and faith in the negative (no material situation can ever satisfy the soul). Above all, good association is named the greatest practical gift of Kṛṣṇa for increasing that faith and determination, and the hlādinī potency, Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī as Kṛṣṇa's compassion, is shown to be the intimate channel of His mercy.

## Key teachings

- **[00:01:08.700]** A transcendental question is glorious in and of itself, independent of its answer. Bhūrijana cites Śukadeva Gosvāmī praising Parīkṣit Mahārāja's inquiry — what is the duty of all persons in all circumstances, especially one about to die — as itself glorious.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 06
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 01
- **[00:01:51.880]** Honestly contemplating one's own death is an accurate, humbling thought: of the billions alive now, almost none will remain in 100 years. This realism should prompt one to orient life so that when death comes one is as ready as possible, given one's actual capacity.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 02
- **[00:03:31.940]** Beyond 'is there life after death,' the sharper question is whether there is real life after birth: most lives are merely filling up space — enjoying, manipulating, pushing away and pulling toward — and at death the whole sum 'adds up to a zero.' A life must be pointed toward a higher purpose to mean anything.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 04
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 04
- **[00:08:45.660]** On how long one round should take: Bhūrijana recalls Śrīla Prabhupāda once saying 16 rounds in two hours at most — about seven and a half minutes per round. He uses it not as a rigid rule but as a reference point.
- **[00:09:11.200]** The speed of a round is diagnostic of attention, not a goal in itself. Tiredness and inattention tend to make chanting drag; when one is able to hear and it flows smoothly it can go faster. The mind takes time to 'get in gear,' wandering to seemingly essential things, so the real work is bringing attention back to hearing the holy name.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 07
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 01
- **[00:10:04.480]** Train the mind by acknowledging it, then redirecting it to hear one name and one mantra at a time; as soon as the mind begins actually hearing the holy name, the taste for hearing it increases.
    - cf. VALUES  OF  LIFE  BOOK  4
    - cf. Nectar of Devotion, The
- **[00:10:40.560]** The mind is born of the mode of goodness combined with false ego, while the intelligence is born of the mode of passion combined with false ego; the chanter tends to be carried off by this passionate intelligence, and once passion gains momentum it is very hard to pull back.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 03
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 03
- **[00:11:14.660]** Do not give passion full scope to drag the mind away; the moment you notice the mind wandering, become detached, pull the mind back, and be determined simply to hear — and when you genuinely hear, everything changes.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 07
    - BG 6.35: *śrī-bhagavān uvāca
asaṁśayaṁ mahā-bāho mano durnigrahaṁ calam
abhyāsena tu kaunteya vairāgyeṇa ca gṛhyate* — Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa said: O mighty-armed son of Kuntī, it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment.
- **[00:12:03.700]** Attentive chanting need not take longer than inattentive chanting — theoretically there is no reason it should, since the same Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra is being said either way; the difference is attention, not duration.
- **[00:14:44.540]** Align and harmonize the whole of one's life with spiritual advancement; this is hard because material nature resists, and it requires full surrender and sincerity — but with determination that life is meant for one purpose, one will do whatever that purpose requires (e.g. rising early).
    - BG 18.66: *sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ* — Abandon all varieties of religion and just surrender unto Me. I shall deliver you from all sinful reactions. Do not fear.
    - cf. Nectar of Devotion, The
- **[00:17:26.620]** Chanting the holy name is the essential, most important limb of practical devotion; the other aṅgas of bhakti — initiation, guru, deity worship, study of śāstra, residing in the holy dhāma — are not rivals to the name but exist precisely to support and protect the chanting so that pure chanting can arise.
    - cf. Nectar of Devotion, The
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 06
- **[00:18:24.940]** Though the holy name is so powerful that it does not depend on formal initiation (as Mahāprabhu taught Sanātana Gosvāmī, with Prabhupāda's purport), a spiritual master and the other items of devotional service are still needed — because one must learn to chant properly and offenselessly, and those items teach and support that pure, offenseless, loving chanting.
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Antya-lila
    - cf. The Nectar of Devotion
- **[00:20:11.740]** Though the holy name is supremely powerful and can in principle give everything by itself, simply chanting in isolation is not the recommended path. Glorifying the name's potency is true, but it is not a complete explanation of the process — to actually take up proper chanting one needs guidance: śāstra, association of devotees, and dīkṣā/śikṣā in a bona fide line.
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 01
- **[00:20:51.320]** Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura explained that many of the problems among followers of Caitanya Mahāprabhu arose from misunderstanding this point — chanting Kṛṣṇa's names without grasping the proper process and tattva. People then concoct wrong conclusions, fall into sahajiyāism, and use 'all I need is the holy name' to justify abandoning regulative principles (intoxication, illicit association).
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
- **[00:23:38.380]** The decisive factor is receptivity, not the name's capacity. The name is independent — a transcendental autocrat ready, willing, and able to give everything — so the real question is one's own qualification for accepting the mercy. (Illustrated by the Bill Gates analogy: knowing the wealthy donor exists does not mean one knows the process to receive even a dollar from him.)
    - cf. Teachings of Lord Caitanya
- **[00:24:45.100]** Qualification means learning very deeply how to serve the holy name — to become a servant and stop regarding the name as an object of one's own enjoyment. False ego and personal desires actually prohibit us from receiving what the name is ready to give; the attitude must be molded into pure loving service, with the same fully surrendered, Kṛṣṇa-centered mood that prevails in Goloka Vṛndāvana.
    - cf. Gita Values
    - BG 18.66: *sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ* — Abandon all varieties of religion and just surrender unto Me. I shall deliver you from all sinful reactions. Do not fear.
- **[00:26:40.540]** Accepting the shelter of a bona fide spiritual master (ādau gurv-āśrayam) is the primary among the limbs of devotion whose whole purpose is to shape this pure receptive attitude — not an optional extra one can bypass by going to the name alone.
    - cf. Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 02
- **[00:28:46.680]** Following Haridāsa Ṭhākura means chanting continuously and with that mood; Prabhupāda's simple formula is to chant like a baby crying for its mother — with that intensity and single-minded clarity of wanting only Kṛṣṇa, accepting nothing else. When we claim 'Kṛṣṇa, I only want You,' He tests whether the desire is real; Haridāsa's fixity (unmoved when a prostitute was sent at night, with no self-centered enjoying spirit) showed what he truly wanted.
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Antya-lila
- **[00:30:27.460]** A pure devotee has no separate sense of self or existence apart from being Krishna's servant, meant for Krishna's pleasure. Haridāsa Ṭhākura was so fixed in this — 'I am Krishna's and Krishna is mine' — that Māyā Devī herself came to test him and departed with reverence for his unwavering, humble surrender.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 01
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
- **[00:35:01.540]** Sincere practice of attentive chanting is necessary but cannot by itself perfect the wandering mind. The decisive intermediate step before receiving mercy is humility — honestly realizing 'I cannot do this by my own power.' Pride ('I can do it') makes Krishna conclude one needs no help, whereas the humble devotee who prays sincerely attracts Krishna to solve the problem.
    - cf. Gita Values
    - BG 18.66: *sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ* — Abandon all varieties of religion and just surrender unto Me. I shall deliver you from all sinful reactions. Do not fear.
- **[00:36:13.220]** Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura's counsel: stay where you are, chant regularly, read Caitanya Bhāgavata and Caitanya-caritāmṛta, and grow more enthusiastic and purified. Mahāprabhu is especially merciful to those who are humble in mind and consider themselves incapable, because such people take shelter of him — for it is impossible to cure the mind's distraction by one's own strength, yet one must still do one's part of the hard work to attract that mercy.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 03
- **[00:38:07.380]** Sincerity is acquired through association, just as one becomes a drunkard by associating with drunkards. Prabhupāda's answer (Melbourne, 1975): to become sincere, associate with those who are sincere — the devotees. Spiritual qualities are caught from company, not manufactured alone.
    - cf. Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 03
- **[00:39:02.440]** A practical method to keep the retreat's resolve alive: every week or two, with friends, family, in a nāma-haṭṭa group, or at the temple, devote about an hour to japa and attentive chanting — read a little from a japa book or Caitanya-caritāmṛta and renew the simple intention to listen, to actually hear even one mantra.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 06
- **[00:40:35.000]** Periodic communal japa is a powerful support: devotees gathering — even once every two weeks or a month — simply to sit and chant together attentively is uplifting and gives the strength to carry one's practice forward, counteracting the tendency to forget good resolutions once 'the rest of life takes over.'
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila
    - cf. Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu
- **[00:41:40.900]** Keep the chanting on a simple, practical foundation of four principles: try to hear one mantra at a time; be determined (without determination nothing happens, but with determination Krishna reciprocates quickly); try and try again, since perfect success is given by Krishna alone; and remain detached from the result.

    - cf. Book-9-10_converted-eng_00001
- **[00:43:37.320]** Ajāmila's deliverance shows the power of focused chanting: though he called the name of his son, his mind was fully concentrated, free of aparādha and pramāda — this attentive nāmābhāsa, not inattentive chanting, is what brought the Viṣṇudūtas and his wonderful result.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 06
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 08
- **[00:44:43.000]** Bharata Mahārāja's fall is not divine neglect but the law that one attains the object of one's final thoughts: absorbed in his pet deer at death, he took a deer's body — yet Krishna mercifully granted him full memory of his past life rather than 'pulling out the rule book,' so the deer gained the firm determination to never again make that mistake.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 10
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 05
- **[00:48:33.820]** The ācāryas explain Jaḍa Bharata's seemingly difficult birth was no real misfortune: because he stayed fully with Krishna, even the moment of near-sacrifice to Kālī could not disturb him. Genuine detachment from matter is impossible by force — it arises only when one holds something so wonderful (Krishna) so tightly that everything negative becomes negligible.
    - cf. Nectar of Devotion, The
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 05
- **[00:50:09.740]** The great danger of devotee association is aparādha born of familiarity: seeing the same person daily, one takes them for granted and begins to see only their faults. The skill is to gain the benefit of association without offending.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
- **[00:51:54.640]** See everyone equally as Krishna's instrument — Krishna has something to teach you through each person, even through dealings that feel like dishonor or seem unhelpful. Treat all dealings as Krishna working through them for your benefit.
    - cf. Ramayana
    - cf. Gita Values
- **[00:54:43.240]** Recognize that each devotee is on a different path to Krishna — coming from different material backgrounds, karma, and situations — so the work of removing the 'vines of materialism' differs for everyone. Therefore do not rank devotees as better or worse than one another, or yourself above them.
    - cf. Book-5-6-Design-2_converted-1
- **[00:53:53.940]** Prioritize strong sādhana — attending the morning program, chanting japa attentively, and serving devotees and Krishna. A strong sādhana gives the spiritual strength and presence of mind to handle relationships and difficulties in proper perspective; everything else then follows.
    - cf. Teachings of Lord Kapila, the Son of Devahuti
- **[00:57:02.120]** While chanting, concentrate on the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra; do not strenuously resist other sounds, simply return the focus to the mantra. A peaceful or sweet sound is acceptable if it helps you chant better, but neglect it the moment it becomes a distraction.
    - cf. A Second Chance
- **[00:58:12.460]** Peace is not the ultimate goal — love for Krishna is; and full peace can never be ours in this world. Yet because peace is born of the mode of goodness, arrange a simple life in goodness (avoiding passion and ignorance as far as practical) so the consciousness becomes peaceful enough to actually focus on Krishna's service.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 07
    - BG 2.66: *nāsti buddhir ayuktasya na cāyuktasya bhāvanā

- **[01:01:34.560]** Krishna's potencies are tiered, not homogeneous. He has unlimited śaktis performing specific functions, and some are naturally more essential and intimate to him than others — even his Māyā-śakti and the powers governing the material world have their place, but they are secondary. The supreme, most intimate potency is the hlādinī (pleasure-giving) śakti, ultimately Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī. Bhūrijana likens it to a government where many departments exist but some are dearer to the head of state.
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila
- **[01:03:16.360]** Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī is Krishna's compassion. Prabhupāda taught that one should not offer to Krishna directly but place the offering in Rādhārāṇī's hand, asking her to give it to Krishna; in her humble mood she pleads that this devotee is better than herself and begs Krishna to bestow mercy on him. Thus the supreme hlādinī potency and the Lord's mercy are intimately connected.
    - cf. Nectar of Instruction
- **[01:06:01.400]** To develop the desire for determination in chanting, act in ways that cultivate it — the key is association with devotees, taken as 'everything.' Chant Krishna's names seriously and properly, follow the process, and study śāstra (Bhagavad-gītā, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, Caitanya-caritāmṛta) in the company of devotees; from this, faith increases.
    - cf. Teachings of Lord Kapila, the Son of Devahuti

- **[01:06:51.300]** Weak determination usually stems from weak faith, which has two sides. Faith in the positive: being with Krishna in Goloka Vṛndāvana is exactly what I want and will bring full satisfaction — and that this is true. Faith in the negative: trusting that no situation in the material world, whatever it is, can ever satisfy the soul.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 09
- **[01:08:17.720]** The 'if only' syndrome — believing 'if only I had this, then I'd be happy' — keeps shifting its object and is precisely the false hope that drags the soul through the 8,400,000 species of life. Bhūrijana illustrates with his own changing wishes (hair, a hang-glider's wish to soar like a hawk, wealth, beauty, the perfect family) to show every material attainment fails to satisfy; Krishna will grant the desired body, but it brings no real fulfilment.

- **[01:10:01.660]** Determination on the spiritual path depends on faith that no material situation — whatever it may be — will ever satisfy the soul. Without that conviction we will not become determined.

- **[01:10:29.060]** The one thing that brings satisfaction to the soul is to be situated in one's actual position: loving service to Krishna in Goloka Vrindavan.

- **[01:10:42.440]** Sometimes we must 'touch the fire' — let our conditioned efforts driven by the modes burn us — to learn the simple lesson that no material situation satisfies, while Krishna is all-powerful and all-merciful and His shelter and love are what we truly long for.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this by making attentive hearing — not speed or quantity — the measure of japa: hear one name and one mantra at a time, and the moment the mind wanders, acknowledge it, become detached, and pull it back to hearing, refusing to give passion full scope to drag it away. Use Prabhupāda's roughly seven-and-a-half-minutes-per-round as a self-diagnostic of attention rather than a target, remembering that attentive chanting need take no longer than inattentive chanting. Keep the practice on four foundations — try to hear one mantra at a time, be determined, try and try again (perfection is given by Kṛṣṇa alone), and stay detached from the result. Recognizing that the wandering mind cannot be cured by one's own strength, cultivate genuine humility and pray sincerely, doing one's own hard part to attract Kṛṣṇa's mercy. Support the chanting through the full process — accepting a bona fide guru, studying Bhagavad-gītā, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and Caitanya-caritāmṛta, and worshiping the Deity — understanding these as protectors of pure chanting rather than alternatives to it, and avoid the sahajiyā error of using the name to justify dropping regulative principles. Make association central: gather with friends, family, a nāma-haṭṭa group, or at the temple every week or two to chant attentively together for about an hour and renew the simple intention to hear, since sincerity and faith are caught from sincere company. Arrange a simple life in the mode of goodness so the consciousness is peaceful enough to focus on service, prioritize a strong morning program and sādhana, and treat every devotee as Kṛṣṇa's instrument — seeing each as on a different path and never ranking oneself above them. While chanting, concentrate on the mahā-mantra without straining against other sounds, simply returning to the mantra. Finally, strengthen determination by strengthening faith — both that loving service to Kṛṣṇa in Goloka is what truly satisfies and that no material situation ever will — and follow Prabhupāda's counsel to chant like a baby crying for its mother, offering one's service through Rādhārāṇī's hand.

## Glossary

- **japa** — The personal, often counted, soft chanting of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra on beads; the central practice discussed throughout the lecture.
- **Hari-Nāma / holy name** — The names of Kṛṣṇa, identical with Kṛṣṇa Himself; described as a transcendental autocrat, supremely powerful and independent, yet giving its full mercy according to the chanter's receptivity.
- **Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra** — The 'great chant for deliverance' — Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma Hare Hare — the mantra one concentrates on in japa.
- **aṅga (aṅgas of bhakti)** — The 'limbs' or components of devotional service — e.g., taking shelter of guru, initiation, Deity worship, study of śāstra, residing in the dhāma — which exist to support and protect pure chanting.
- **dīkṣā** — Formal spiritual initiation from a bona fide guru; not strictly required for the name to act, yet needed to learn proper, offenseless chanting.
- **śikṣā** — Spiritual instruction; together with dīkṣā, the guidance in a bona fide line that trains one to chant properly.
- **ādau gurv-āśrayam** — 'First, take shelter of a spiritual master' — the primary limb of devotion whose purpose is to shape the pure, receptive, servant attitude toward the name.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture (e.g., Bhagavad-gītā, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, Caitanya-caritāmṛta); study of it, in devotee association, builds the faith and understanding that support chanting.
- **tattva** — Established spiritual truth or philosophy; misunderstanding the proper process and tattva leads to concocted conclusions and deviation.
- **sahajiyāism** — A deviation that cheaply imitates advanced devotion, here the error of claiming 'all I need is the holy name' to abandon regulative principles such as avoiding intoxication and illicit association.
- **aparādha** — Offense — against the holy name or against devotees; offenses (e.g., from over-familiarity) obstruct the result of chanting, and offenseless chanting is the aim.
- **pramāda** — Inattention or negligence in chanting; Ajāmila's deliverance shows chanting free of aparādha and pramāda.
- **nāmābhāsa** — A 'reflection' or clearing stage of the holy name — chanting that is attentive though not yet fully pure; Ajāmila's concentrated calling of 'Nārāyaṇa' (his son's name) exemplifies its power.
- **Viṣṇudūtas** — The messengers/servants of Lord Viṣṇu who came to rescue Ajāmila at the moment of his attentive chanting.
- **Māyā / Māyā-śakti / Māyā Devī** — Kṛṣṇa's external, illusory potency governing the material world — a real but secondary śakti; personified, she came to test Haridāsa Ṭhākura and withdrew in reverence.
- **śakti** — Divine potency or energy; Kṛṣṇa possesses unlimited tiered śaktis performing specific functions, some more intimate to Him than others.
- **hlādinī śakti** — Kṛṣṇa's supreme, most intimate pleasure-giving potency, ultimately personified as Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī, intimately connected with the Lord's mercy.
- **Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī** — The embodiment of the hlādinī potency and of Kṛṣṇa's compassion; one offers service through her hand, as she humbly begs Kṛṣṇa to bestow mercy on the devotee.
- **Goloka Vṛndāvana** — Kṛṣṇa's supreme abode, where the fully surrendered, Kṛṣṇa-centered mood prevails; loving service there is the soul's actual position and only real satisfaction.
- **sādhana** — Regulated devotional practice (morning program, attentive japa, serving devotees and Kṛṣṇa); strong sādhana gives the strength and perspective to handle relationships and difficulties.
- **nāma-haṭṭa** — A 'marketplace of the holy name' — a congregational group of devotees; a setting recommended for periodic communal japa and renewing one's resolve.
- **dhāma** — A holy place or sacred abode of the Lord; residing in the dhāma is one of the supporting limbs of devotional service.
- **ācārya** — A spiritual authority who teaches by example; the ācāryas explain the deeper meaning of episodes such as Jaḍa Bharata's birth.
