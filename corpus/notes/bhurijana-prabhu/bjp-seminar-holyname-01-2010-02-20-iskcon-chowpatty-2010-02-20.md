# BJP Seminar Holyname 01 2010 02 20 ISKCON Chowpatty

## Theme

This Holy-Name lecture builds a single arc that moves from a diagnosis of the conditioned ego to the practice of taking shelter of the holy name. Bhūrijana Prabhu opens by contrasting mainstream 'you are God' spirituality — which collapses under its own logic (if I am God, how did I forget?) — with the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava method of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, whose aim is the exact opposite: to dismantle the deep habit of regarding oneself as the center of all existence. Our chronic frustration is traced to this false centrality: we feel supreme yet lack supreme power, so by Kṛṣṇa's mercy the universe consistently refuses to cooperate (the Mumbai rush-hour train made tangible). Through Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's songs 'Āmāra Jīvana' and the prayers of Śaraṇāgati — introduced via Prabhupāda's recorded commentary — he maps the anatomy of the materialistic mind ruled by false ego (ahaṅkāra), and shows how old age forcibly strips away every false identification (strength, beauty, ability), revealing 'I am not who I thought I was.' Crucially, honest low self-regard is not yet humility: the converting factor is taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa ('this is all I am, but I am yours'), which makes one powerful, as Arjuna's confidence rested not in himself but in his master's feet. Humility therefore differs from low self-esteem by the focus of consciousness — turning quickly from one's faults to Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet. Applied to japa, even a few seconds of one mantra exposes our inability to control the mind, driving us to shelter. The lecture's second half establishes that Kṛṣṇa must be understood as BOTH all-powerful (vibhu) AND all-merciful, since only that combination makes surrender possible — hence the purpose of studying Bhagavad-gītā and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. This grounds nāmāśraya: chanting in a mood of being fallen and helpless, placing oneself at the lotus feet of the name, which — being non-different from Kṛṣṇa — is taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa Himself. He distinguishes an earlier stage (removing the offense of inattention, pramāda) from the higher stage of shelter, and frames prema as the ultimate goal that offensive chanting can never reach. Drawing on Prabhupāda's '250 rupees' teaching, the cātaka-bird and crying-baby images, and the verses of Rūpa Gosvāmī (Nāmāṣṭaka), Sanātana Gosvāmī (Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta, equating humility with prema), Narottama dāsa, Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī, and Bhaktivinoda's Harināma-cintāmaṇi, he concludes that shelter cannot be willed on but arises from purity gained through attentive, offenseless chanting and steady service (sevā). The practical resolution: chant on two levels — persistently try to hear one mantra by determined effort, and when Kṛṣṇa pulls the heart, cry out in prayerful dependence — never straining into a headache, but begging for the mercy that alone removes inattention.

## Practical application

A devotee begins japa on the attentive stage: be determined to hear even a single mantra of the mahā-mantra clearly, without letting the mind wander to how great or how terrible one is. This honest effort immediately exposes our inability to control the mind and our oscillation between pride and self-deprecation — and that very weakness is meant to drive us to shelter rather than to discouragement. Chant on two levels at once: persist in trying to hear the name, and when Kṛṣṇa gives a pulling in the heart, turn and cry out — 'just look at the state of my mind, please help me, I only want to hear Your holy name' — calling like a needy child crying for his mother, meaning it with full attention so the call carries real intensity. This is nāmāśraya: chanting with the consciousness of being fallen, unfortunate, and helpless, bringing one's actual situation and unfulfilled desires to the holy name, trusting that Kṛṣṇa is both all-merciful and all-powerful and therefore able and willing to give shelter. Do not strain into a headache; relax, keep trying, stay detached from whatever story the mind is telling (and from second-order thoughts about one's thinking), but never detached from the effort to hear — simply drop the commentary and return to the name. Use free will rightly through determination, yet rest one's real hope not in one's own effort but in Kṛṣṇa's mercy, which descends in response to sincere endeavor (as kṛpā-śakti answered Mother Yaśodā's pariśrama in the Dāmodara pastime). Beyond the chanting mat, take Bhaktisiddhānta's prescription — stay where you are, chant regularly, and read Caitanya-bhāgavata and Caitanya-caritāmṛta — and follow Bhaktivinoda's counsel to take time alone in a quiet place to utter and hear the name distinctly, humbly begging for grace. Support the chanting with steady, regular service (sevā) within Prabhupāda's mission: its repeated confrontations with the self-centered material mind, and its many failures, deepen humility and the felt need for shelter. The honest self-examination throughout is the '250 rupees' question — what am I still holding back from Kṛṣṇa? — knowing that He gives Himself fully only to one who makes Him the complete shelter, and that even a little improvement is wonderful.

## Glossary

- **Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava** — The Vaiṣṇava tradition in the line of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, whose method of self-realization aims to break the habit of regarding oneself as the center of existence and to bring the Supreme Lord under control through pure love.
- **Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — The founder-teacher of the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava line, whose central teaching is that humility — feeling lower than a blade of grass — is the basis for taking shelter of the holy name.
- **ahaṅkāra (false ego)** — The distorted sense of existing separately from Kṛṣṇa with oneself as the center of existence; distinct from legitimate ego (the simple sense of being a person), it generates anxiety and dictates materialistic tendencies.
- **nāmāśraya** — Taking shelter of the holy name — chanting with the consciousness of being fallen, helpless, and dependent, placing oneself with full concentration at the lotus feet of the name; because the name and Kṛṣṇa are non-different, it is shelter of Kṛṣṇa Himself.
- **prema-bhakti** — Intimate, pure love for Kṛṣṇa; the soul's deepest thirst and the ultimate goal of chanting, which cannot descend into the heart so long as one commits offenses.
- **pramāda** — The offense of inattention or negligence in chanting — letting the mind wander, completing rounds without hearing even a single name; removed by determined, attentive hearing.
- **niṣṭhā** — Fixed conviction or steadiness — here, the settled understanding 'I am Kṛṣṇa's servant'; the essence of the stage of nāmāśraya, which leads upward to ruci.
- **ruci** — Taste for the holy name and devotional service; the stage to which fixed conviction (niṣṭhā) naturally leads, per Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura.
- **sevā** — Service ('savor,' not a mere job); the field of advancement that forces confrontation with the self-centered material mind and offers repeated opportunities to take shelter.
- **vibhu** — The great or all-pervading one; a description of God as supremely great, under whose order the entire creation moves and who controls time and the creative energy.
- **śakti** — Energy or potency of Kṛṣṇa; even all-powerful time is merely one of His śaktis.
- **kṛpā-śakti** — The Lord's mercy-potency, which descends in response to a devotee's sincere endeavor — evoked, for example, when Kṛṣṇa saw Mother Yaśodā's exertion in the Dāmodara pastime.
- **pariśrama** — Sincere endeavor or hard exertion; Mother Yaśodā's pariśrama in binding Dāmodara drew down Kṛṣṇa's mercy, illustrating that earnest effort attracts grace.
- **mahā-mantra (Hare Kṛṣṇa)** — The great chant of the names of Kṛṣṇa; attentively hearing even one mantra is the basic practice that removes the offense of inattention.
- **nāmābhāsa** — The dim 'dawn' or shadow of the holy name — chanting not yet pure; even this gives the spiritually blind a glimpse of pure devotion.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present age of quarrel and hypocrisy, in which people feel unqualified to be loved and therefore bluff by advertising their qualifications, wealth, and beauty.
- **Vraja / Braja gopīs** — Vraja (Braja) is Kṛṣṇa's intimate realm; the gopīs are its cowherd maidens who serve Kṛṣṇa with selfless love. Prabhupāda's sweetness lay in always carrying this Vraja mood.
- **cātaka bird** — The bird that drinks only rainwater and begs the cloud alone — whether it sends rain or a thunderbolt — emblem of Rūpa Gosvāmī's mood of exclusive, surrendered dependence on the Lord.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — Vaiṣṇava ācārya and author of the songs 'Āmāra Jīvana,' Śaraṇāgati, and Harināma-cintāmaṇi, used here to model honest humility and prayerful chanting in a quiet place.
- **Śaraṇāgati** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's collection of surrender-songs; its prayer of humility shows the meek mood of a Vaiṣṇava who counts himself among fallen people and takes the lowest position.
- **Harināma-cintāmaṇi** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's work teaching that one should concentrate on distinctly uttering and hearing the name, and — since the jīva cannot conquer distraction by his own power — prayerfully beg for the Lord's grace, the sole remedy for inattentiveness.
- **Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta** — Sanātana Gosvāmī's work in which true humility is equated with prema — not a material sentiment but the very act of taking deep, focused shelter of Kṛṣṇa.
- **Nāmāṣṭaka** — Rūpa Gosvāmī's eight verses glorifying the holy name — worshipped by the Upaniṣads, adored by liberated souls, removing great sufferings even when spoken once or disrespectfully, and making karma vanish.
- **Prema-bhakti-candrikā** — Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura's work in which the author laments his fallen condition; this deep humility of an exalted devotee is itself a symptom of advancement.
- **jīva** — The individual living soul, which cannot overcome the illusion of distraction by its own power and depends on the Lord's mercy.
- **Prahlāda** — The child-devotee who made Kṛṣṇa everything with simplicity of heart — laughing when the Lord was present, crying when absent — a model of childlike, exclusive dependence.
- **Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura** — Vaiṣṇava ācārya whose letter prescribes staying where one is, chanting regularly, and reading Caitanya-bhāgavata and Caitanya-caritāmṛta, noting Mahāprabhu's special mercy to the humble.
- **Gopīnāth** — The Supreme Person who is named and described by the holy name — one of the name's two forms, the other being the sound vibration itself, which is held to be even more merciful.
