# Holy Name Seminar — Part 01 (ISKCON Chowpatty)

## Theme

This lecture by Bhūrijana Prabhu reframes the daily chanting of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra around a single, attainable task: be determined to hear just one mantra at a time, in the present moment. The arc moves from method to mood. Bhūrijana first dismantles the mind's strategies — analyzing why it wanders, manufacturing excuses to stop (tiredness, sleepiness, rising early), and endlessly complicating a simple act in the name of an 'easy life,' which he diagnoses as the disease of modern so-called civilization. Against this he sets a deliberately narrow practice: before sitting, make your saṅkalpa (firm resolve), then do nothing but hear the sound vibration — one name, one mantra, now — bathing the consciousness in Kṛṣṇa's names. He insists the chanter's sole responsibility is attentive hearing; one should not artificially try to see the Lord's form, because when chanting is offenseless the Lord reveals Himself on His own terms. Throughout, the talk holds together a division of labor: clear, focused determination is the devotee's part — the one thing he must personally bring — while the actual result (distinct hearing, the Lord's appearance, the soul's awakening) is Kṛṣṇa's mercy, given personally to each soul. Even one determined step toward Kṛṣṇa, who is already present in His name and in the heart, draws many steps from Him in return. A live experiment during the seminar demonstrated that attentive rounds genuinely improve when one resolves 'I'm going to do it now and nothing else' — proving the freedom to chant better is real, not fading enthusiasm. The lecture then deepens into mood: invoking Rūpa Gosvāmī's Padyāvalī and other prayers, Bhūrijana presents the holy name as non-different from Kṛṣṇa Himself — the heart of the Vedas, the devotee's very life, simultaneously the goal of life and the means to attain it — surpassing the opulence of millions of universes. The proper approach is humble surrender: the fallen soul, drowning in repeated birth and death, appeals to the Lord as Dīna-Bandhu, friend of the shelterless, and offers himself without demands. Anxiety is recast as shelterlessness, and chanting as the act of taking shelter. The talk closes on urgency and presence — 'no post-dated blank checks' — devotional life happens now; tiredness itself is an anartha (named laya, dullness) that attentive hearing dissolves; and the whole practice reduces, again and again, to one determined, detached act of hearing Kṛṣṇa's name in the present.

## Practical application

Before each japa session, pause and make your saṅkalpa: consciously decide why you are sitting down, and resolve not to obey the mind but to bathe your consciousness in Kṛṣṇa's names for this time. Then narrow your whole task to one thing — hear one mahā-mantra clearly, at your own comfortable speed, without straining for quantity or burdening yourself with 'all my rounds.' Pronounce each word of the mantra distinctly (use a mantra card if it helps), and form the clear intention to hear before each utterance: 'I am going to hear Hare, then Krishna,' then chant. Keep the practice radically simple: don't combine chanting with email, screens, or other tasks, which lets the mind speed up beyond control. Stay in the present — do not count or frame it as 'one mantra at a time,' because the words 'at a time' make the mind jump to the future, demand to know how long it's held captive, and slip into the mode of passion; your only freedom is the mantra you are chanting right now. Be determined and detached: the mind will inevitably wander, tire, and offer plausible excuses (you got up early, you're tired, this happiness won't last, you don't deserve to enjoy this). Recognize these as the mind's tricks, not Kṛṣṇa's voice. Don't argue or enter philosophical dialogue with it; defer it — 'send me an email later, I'll attend to it' — and simply return to hearing, without lamenting or self-pity. Avoid forming bad habits early, like re-repeating a syllable for fear you didn't hear it, or worrying about mistakes in the next mantra; such anxiety makes you neglect the mantra at hand. If hearing a whole mahā-mantra feels too hard, simplify further to a single word — fix on hearing 'Hare Krishna' and the rest tends to follow naturally and pleasantly. When tiredness or sleepiness (laya) arises, the cure is not to stop but to hear more attentively; the pleasure of connecting with Kṛṣṇa often wakes you up. Do your part — determined, attentive effort 'according to your best capacity' — and depend on Kṛṣṇa for the result, trusting that He reciprocates every sincere attempt and personally gives each soul the mercy of hearing His name. Over time, conviction grows by tasting how attentive rounds improve the whole day's consciousness, and teaching japa to others strengthens your own determination further.

## Glossary

- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari/Kṛṣṇa); the practice of chanting it. Rūpa Gosvāmī's Padyāvalī is cited praising chanting Hari/Nārāyaṇa's name day and night as the heart of the Vedas.
- **japa** — The devotee's daily, often soft, personal chanting of the holy name, traditionally counted in rounds; the central practice the seminar trains.
- **mantra** — The sacred sound formula chanted; here, one repetition of the holy name that the practitioner resolves to hear attentively.
- **mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' — the full Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma Rāma Rāma Hare Hare — the unit of chanting to be heard at a time.
- **saṅkalpa** — A firm resolve made before chanting: knowing why one sits to chant and resolving not to follow the mind but to absorb the consciousness in Kṛṣṇa's names. Bhūrijana says without it 'nothing happens.'
- **anartha** — An unwanted thing or impurity in the heart born of ignorance that obstructs devotion; tiredness during chanting is named as one such anartha.
- **laya** — Dullness or sleep — the specific anartha of drowsiness/inertia that overtakes a chanter; dissolved by simply hearing the mahā-mantra attentively.
- **Kṛṣṇa consciousness** — Awareness of and loving connection with Kṛṣṇa; the devotional life and goal that so much depends on Kṛṣṇa's mercy combined with the devotee's free will.
- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — The teacher of the congregational chanting of the holy name; cited both for ecstatic absorption beyond concern for appearances and for teaching the proper way to pronounce the mantra.
- **Prabhupāda** — The founder-ācārya of ISKCON, quoted on the spiritual master's most important instruction — to chant sixteen (non-offensive) rounds — and on a devotee living in the mercy of Kṛṣṇa.
- **ācārya** — A spiritual preceptor who teaches by example; previous ācāryas recommended aids such as visualizing the mantra's syllables to focus the mind.
- **Gopīnātha** — A name/Deity form of Kṛṣṇa, 'Lord of the gopīs,' worshiped in the temple; recognized as non-different from Kṛṣṇa, manifest before the chanter, and the name one vows to chant for hundreds of thousands of births.
- **Deity** — The worshipable temple form (mūrti) of the Lord; the Deity Gopīnātha is understood to be non-different from Kṛṣṇa, kindly manifesting before the devotee.
- **Rūpa Gosvāmī** — A principal disciple of Caitanya Mahāprabhu and author of the Padyāvalī, whose verses on the supremacy of chanting the holy name are invoked.
- **Padyāvalī** — An anthology of devotional verses compiled by Rūpa Gosvāmī; cited for praising day-and-night chanting of Hari's name above yoga, Vedic study, and forest meditation.
- **Hari / Nārāyaṇa** — Names of the Supreme Lord; chanting them is described as the very heart of the Vedas and the best means to please the Lord.
- **Dīna-Bandhu** — 'Friend of the fallen/shelterless' — a name by which the humble soul, drowning in birth and death, appeals to the Lord as the effulgent moon of mercy to be saved.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture; study of śāstra produces the conviction that chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa is the means to attain what one truly wants in life.
- **mode of passion (rajas)** — The agitated material mode into which the mind falls when it leaps to the future ('how many mantras remain?'); avoided by keeping attention on the present mantra.
- **offenseless chanting** — Chanting free of offenses (aparādha); when chanting is offenseless the Lord automatically reveals Himself, in contrast to 'offensive rounds' that merely fulfill a quota.
