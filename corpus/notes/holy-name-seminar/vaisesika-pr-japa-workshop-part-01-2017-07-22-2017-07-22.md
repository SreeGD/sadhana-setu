# Vaisesika Pr Japa Workshop Part 01 2017 07 22

## Theme

This opening session of a japa workshop by Vaiśeṣika Dāsa builds from a single thesis — that the most valuable achievement in life is the 'individual victory' of purposefully sitting down to chant, since even one mantra fully invested with heart, life, and mind can change one's whole life — into a complete method and theology of attentive chanting. The first movement lays out the practice itself: chant in the supportive energy of a japa circle (extending deliberate hospitality to newcomers), begin with the Pañca-tattva mantra as the Caitanya-caritāmṛta mandates for Kali-yuga, center yourself and set a prayerful intention (saṅkalpa) before starting, chant audibly for your own hearing rather than for speed, keep numerical strength as a discipline, and aim above all at full awareness that you are chanting — because the name is non-different from Krishna Himself, a perfect formula prescribed by Caitanya Mahāprabhu like a doctor's medicine. Supporting disciplines follow: deliberate practice in the association of focused chanters, the Gītā's instruction to bring the wandering mind back again and again, prayer as the doorway to attention (praying to be prayerful when sincerity is hidden), a fixed minimum quota since the one fully-heard mantra often comes only late in the rounds, patience with invisible progress (the stone that cracks only on the hundred-and-first strike), more chanting — never stopping — as the remedy for offensive chanting since even nāmābhāsa purifies, and regular hearing from śāstra and sādhus to keep the restless mind convinced that chanting is the most important activity in the three worlds. The lecture's long central movement is a phrase-by-phrase exposition of Padyāvalī 19, whose seven glorifications of the name form a logical chain: the repository of all auspiciousness (kalyāṇānāṁ nidhānam), transcending both karma and jñāna since bhakti by Rūpa Gosvāmī's definition is free of all other desires; the destroyer of Kali-yuga's filth (kali-mala-mathanam), rendering all defects of mantra, tantra, time, place, and pronunciation faultless because bhāva-grāhī janārdanaḥ — the Lord accepts the devotion, not the phonetics; the purifier of purifiers (pāvanaṁ pāvanānām); the traveler's complete provision (pātheyam) that acts instantly (sapadi) and carries one not only out of Kali's contamination (tāraka) but into a transcendental position of service (pāraka); the one resting place of the greatest poets' words; the very life of saintly persons, whose mature symptom is 'addiction' (āsakti) to the name; and the seed of the tree of dharma, since the supreme dharma is unmotivated, uninterrupted devotional service and its chief means in this age is the holy name. A question period extends the theme — the non-sectarian power of God's names, the gradation from direct Vṛndāvana names down to generic names, the necessity of sambandha-jñāna, empathic preaching tailored to the audience, progressive reading of Prabhupāda's books, and association with Vaiṣṇavas as ISKCON's purpose. The final movement turns the theology into a daily regimen of purposeful, introspective japa: the diagnostic questions 'Am I aware that I am chanting?' and 'On a scale of 1 to 10, how focused and prayerful is my chanting right now?'; inventive retrieval of the mind, whose wandering is its nature (the unbridled horse) rather than a personal failure; quality control after chanting; the purified intelligence overseeing the childlike mind as an inner śikṣā-guru tuned by daily Gītā reading; and three things to remember while chanting — by the mahā-mantra one is in direct touch with Krishna, offenseless chanting alone bestows kṛṣṇa-prema, and 'I am helpless and need Krishna's protection.' Japa is presented as the one arena for complete vulnerability before Krishna: chanting as the begging of an 'eternal beggar,' as seva performed even mechanically until the heart cooperates, and as a prayer of surrender resting on Lord Rāmacandra's vow of protection and Bhagavad-gītā 18.66 — so that one who leans into the name aggressively, hand, tongue, and heart, quickly tastes the mahā-mantra and rises stage by stage to perfection.

## Key teachings

- **[00:02:11.760]** Japa must begin with the Pañca-tattva mantra: as taught in the Ādi-līlā of Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta, there is no possibility of chanting Hare Krishna in the age of Kali without first chanting it. The Pañca-tattva is one Krishna divided into His aspects — Caitanya Mahāprabhu with Nityānanda (His first expansion as Balarāma), Advaita (plenary expansion as Mahā-Viṣṇu), Gadādhara (His internal potency, Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī), and Śrīvāsa (the pure devotee, the living-entity potency) — and in this form Krishna is the most magnanimous of all avatāras, freely giving the pure chanting of the holy names.

- **[00:04:05.920]** Center yourself before chanting: take at least a few seconds to remember where you are and why you are chanting, and set an intention or prayer (e.g., 'inspire me to chant with full sincerity and surrender' or 'by my chanting, please engage me in Your service and give me the best of association'). This fits the nature of the mahā-mantra itself, which is a prayer — 'O my Lord, O energy of the Lord, please engage me in Your service.' A participant's reflection confirmed the effect: setting this intention enabled hearing complete mantras with focus.
- **[00:05:30.800]** The proper mood and method for japa: repeat the holy name audibly so you can hear it yourself (it is not kirtan, so no need to broadcast it); do not chant for speed or 'mileage on your beads,' since the process is never finished — the beads are a circle — though numerical strength (completing 16 rounds) does matter as a discipline. The essence is to be prayerful and fully aware that you are chanting.
- **[00:10:15.000]** The core objective of japa is full awareness — actually noticing that you are chanting and hearing the mantra — because the mantra is Krishna Himself; there is no difference between Krishna and His name. Chanting without this awareness is distracted chanting that is not fully effective.

- **[00:10:46.400]** The maha-mantra is a perfect spiritual formula prescribed by the right authority — Caitanya Mahāprabhu gave it like a doctor prescribing medicine — so one can invest full consciousness in simply hearing it without speculating about its meaning; the mantra will reveal itself.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 03
- **[00:11:44.040]** Deliberate practice in the association of focused chanters accelerates progress: doing something deliberately makes one fully aware of it, and chanting alongside fixed devotees pulls the wandering mind back, whereas alone the mind more easily offers distractions. Practical tip: periodically sit in a japa circle with devotees who chant intensely (illustrated by the speaker's month chanting with a focused godbrother at Govardhana).
- **[00:13:28.340]** Following the Gītā's meditation instruction, wherever the mind wanders, bring it back — again and again, as an exercise.     - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 10
- **[00:17:13.720]** Prayer is the doorway to attentive japa: begin with a saṅkalpa (e.g., praying for the best association and the strength to take advantage of it), and fix the mind on a strong reason for chanting. If you cannot actually feel prayerful, then pray to be prayerful — keep searching for your sincerity, which is hidden within and surfaces from time to time.
- **[00:19:22.260]** Numerical strength matters: keep a fixed minimum quota (16 rounds if vowed, otherwise some floor you never go below), because the quality of experience varies daily and often only late in the quota — after 12, 14, or 15¾ rounds — does one truly hear even one mantra with full awareness, and that single aware mantra gives more meaning than all the rounds before it.

- **[00:20:02.320]** Persistence in japa works invisibly before it works visibly: like striking a stone a hundred times with no apparent effect, each round of chanting creates unseen 'micro-fissures' until the hundred-and-first strike cracks it open — apparent non-progress is still accumulating purification.

Depend on the mantra itself to win the day; hearing even a fraction of the mantra attentively by the end of one's japa is a great victory.

## Practical application

Carve out a daily japa period that exists exclusively for the Holy Name, before the day's desires and obligations rush in. Before chanting: chant the Pañca-tattva mantra; take at least a few seconds to remember where you are and why you are chanting; and set a saṅkalpa — an intention-prayer such as 'inspire me to chant with full sincerity and surrender' or 'please engage me in Your service and give me the best of association.' If you cannot feel prayerful, pray to be prayerful. While chanting: chant audibly enough to hear yourself (japa is not kirtan), never for speed or 'mileage on the beads,' but hold a fixed minimum quota — 16 rounds if vowed, otherwise a floor you never go below — because the one fully-heard mantra often arrives only late in the quota and outweighs everything before it. Run the two self-checks repeatedly: 'Am I aware that I am chanting?' and 'On a scale of 1 to 10, how focused and prayerful is my chanting right now?' Every time the mind wanders — which is its function, not your failure — simply bring it back to the mantra; be as inventive as the mind is: sit periodically in a japa circle with devotees who chant intensely, chant with a chadar over your head or in a closed room, and curate your sound environment all day, displacing mundane noise with recordings such as Prabhupāda chanting, since whatever enters the ear enters the heart. Hold three remembrances while chanting: (1) by the mahā-mantra you are in direct touch with Krishna, like Nṛsiṁhadeva's hand on Prahlāda's head; (2) chanting without offense is by itself sufficient to attain kṛṣṇa-prema; (3) 'I am helpless and need Krishna's protection' — let the mantra mean 'I need help,' dropping the false ego's 'I'm fine,' and meditate on Bhagavad-gītā 18.66 and Lord Rāmacandra's vow while investing the heart in 'I surrender.' When chanting feels dry, distracted, or offensive, the remedy is more chanting, not stopping — nāmābhāsa itself purifies, and progress accumulates invisibly like micro-fissures in a stone; the effect may surface only after the session. When taste is absent, frame japa as seva prescribed by śāstra and push mechanically, like push-starting a stalled car, until spontaneous absorption descends. Afterward, practice quality control ('what gets measured gets improved'): time your rounds (neither twenty minutes nor two), occasionally record your chanting and listen back to expose slurred syllables and mindlessness, note where the mind went, and pick one specific defect to work on for the next few days — a practice that also breeds humility and prayerfulness. Sustain the whole endeavor by daily hearing from śāstra and sādhus — reading Bhagavad-gītā daily (which tunes the intelligence to act as an inner śikṣā-guru overseeing the childlike mind) and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam progressively in order — so the mind stays convinced that chanting is the most important of all activities and gives the name a real try.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Individual, soft recitation of the holy name on beads, distinct from congregational kirtan; chanted audibly enough to hear oneself.
- **mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' — Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma Rāma Rāma Hare Hare — itself a prayer: 'O my Lord, O energy of the Lord, please engage me in Your service.'
- **Pañca-tattva** — The five features of one Krishna: Caitanya Mahāprabhu with Nityānanda (Balarāma, His first expansion), Advaita (Mahā-Viṣṇu), Gadādhara (His internal potency, Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī), and Śrīvāsa (the pure devotee, living-entity potency); their mantra must precede chanting Hare Krishna in Kali-yuga.
- **saṅkalpa** — A deliberate intention or vow-prayer set before beginning a practice, fixing the mind on a strong reason for chanting.
- **kīrtana / harināma-saṅkīrtana** — Congregational, public chanting of the holy names, which benedicts even passersby who hear it.
- **nāmābhāsa** — The 'shadow' or dim reflection of the holy name — chanting still mixed with inattention or offense — which is nevertheless powerful enough to purify.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture; with guru and sādhu, one of the three authorities in which all spiritual claims must be grounded.
- **sādhu** — A saintly devotee; hearing from sādhus and chanting in their association sustains and focuses one's practice.
- **guru / paramparā** — The spiritual master and the disciplic succession through which authorized teaching and the chanting process descend.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present degraded age, whose people are short-lived, quarrelsome, lazy, misguided, and disturbed; the holy name is prescribed as its supreme remedy.
- **mala** — Contamination, filth, toxins — the defining pollution of Kali-yuga that the holy name destroys.
- **Padyāvalī** — An anthology of devotional verses compiled by Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī glorifying chanting as the primary aspect of Krishna worship; verse 19 enumerates seven glories of the holy name.
- **kalyāṇānāṁ nidhānam** — 'The repository of all auspiciousness' — the name as the original cause and reservoir of everything auspicious, independent of every other means.
- **kali-mala-mathanam** — 'Destroyer of the filth of Kali' — the verbal root used in the sense of actually wiping out, not merely churning, the contamination of the age.
- **pāvanaṁ pāvanānām** — 'The purifier of purifiers' — the supreme purifier among all purifying things.
- **pātheyam** — Provisions lovingly packed for a journey; the holy name as the complete provision for the traveler on the path of liberation, needing nothing else.
- **tāraka** — The delivering aspect of the holy name, which frees one from the contamination of Kali.
- **pāraka** — The carrying-across aspect of the holy name, which brings one into the transcendental realm in a position of service.
- **sapadi / sadyaḥ** — 'Instantly, immediately' — the name acts the moment it is chanted, even if the result is felt only later.
- **viśrāma-sthānam ekam** — 'The one resting place' — the holy name as the sole repose of the words of the greatest poets (Vyāsa, Vālmīki, Śukadeva, Rūpa Gosvāmī).
- **jīvanaṁ saj-janānām** — 'The life of saintly persons' — the pure devotee lives only by the name, feeling its absence as others would the loss of food or breath.
- **bījaṁ dharma-drumasya** — 'The seed of the tree of dharma' — the holy name as the origin from which all dharma grows.
- **dharma** — A term with no single English equivalent — rendered by Prabhupāda as religion, regulatory principles, characteristic function, eternal occupation, morality, devotional service, prescribed duty, and more; its supreme form is unmotivated, uninterrupted devotional service.
- **karma** — Fruitive activity, even pious, which cements one's relationship with the material world and obliges rebirth into bodies subject to birth, death, old age, and disease.
- **jñāna** — Cultivation of knowledge aimed at escaping the entanglement created by one's own karma (mukti/mokṣa); still within the material realm.
- **mukti / mokṣa** — Liberation from material existence — the goal of jñāna, which the holy name surpasses.
- **bhakti** — Devotional service; by Rūpa Gosvāmī's definition, devoid of all other desires, uncovered by jñāna and karma, and cultivated favourably solely for Krishna's pleasure.
- **anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam** — 'Devoid of all other desires' — the opening qualifier of Rūpa Gosvāmī's definition of pure devotional service.
- **kleśaghnī** — One of bhakti's six excellences: it destroys all suffering, manifesting immediately upon adopting sādhana-bhakti.
- **śubhadā** — One of bhakti's six excellences: it bestows all auspiciousness, also manifesting immediately in sādhana-bhakti.
- **Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu** — Rūpa Gosvāmī's treatise on devotional service, which describes the six excellences of bhakti.
- **puṇya / puṇya-śravaṇa-kīrtana** — Pious merit; hearing and chanting about the Lord is itself 'spiritual puṇya,' awarding the results of unlimited pious activities without separate rituals.
- **tantra** — Ritual rules, regulations, and enjoined observances (such as the pre-sunrise bath); all their discrepancies become faultless when the holy name is chanted.
- **bhāva-grāhī janārdanaḥ** — 'The Lord accepts the feeling' — Krishna receives the devotion with which His name is chanted, not its grammatical or phonetic perfection.
- **saṁskāra** — Deep impression formed by past practice or purificatory culture, as when Gajendra's previous life's practice enabled his cry to the Lord.
- **āsakti** — Attachment; the mature stage where one is 'addicted' to chanting the holy name — the sign of spiritual health.
- **vaidhī-bhakti** — Devotional practice performed by scriptural rule and the guru's prescription, trusted like a doctor's medicine before its effect is felt.
- **rāgānugā-bhakti** — Spontaneous devotional service; the main means of progressing toward it from vaidhī-bhakti is simply chanting Hare Krishna without offense.
- **sambandha-jñāna** — Knowledge of one's relationship with Krishna; the depth of understanding with which one chants determines how far the name takes one toward prema, and its absence invites offense.
- **kṛṣṇa-prema** — Pure love of God — attainable by offenseless chanting of the mahā-mantra alone.
- **adhikāra** — Qualification or eligibility; in Kali-yuga we have no adhikāra for any process other than depending on the holy name.
- **śikṣā-guru** — Instructing spiritual master; per the Eleventh Canto, properly tuned intelligence acts as a śikṣā-guru — Krishna's representative within — cultivated by daily Bhagavad-gītā reading.
- **mano-gatān** — 'The mind goes' — wandering is the mind's very function, so retrieval, not self-blame, is the practice.
- **Nāma Prabhu** — The holy name as a person: non-different from Krishna yet more merciful, descending to reach out and 'come and get' everyone (Rūpa Gosvāmī's Nāmāṣṭaka).
- **Goloka** — Krishna's supreme transcendental abode, to which the holy name personally leads the chanter.
- **niśācaras** — 'Night rovers' — sources of inauspicious sound whose noise should be displaced by auspicious sound such as recordings of Prabhupāda chanting.
- **kali-kalmaṣa** — The contamination of Kali, which the Śruti states is destroyed by the sixteen names of the mahā-mantra.
- **śruti** — The revealed Vedic sound; the source recommending the mahā-mantra as the most potent names of God.
- **harer nāma verse** — The scriptural declaration whose triple repetition emphatically asserts the holy name and thrice excludes any alternative means in Kali-yuga.
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka** — Caitanya Mahāprabhu's eight verses; the first glorifies saṅkīrtana in seven ways, paralleling Padyāvalī 19's seven glorifications.
- **Yamunāṣṭakam** — Rūpa Gosvāmī's prayers to the river Yamunā, recommended for singing while bathing so as to meditate on bathing in the Yamunā.
- **Puṇḍarīkākṣa** — 'The lotus-eyed Lord'; one who remembers Him is immediately purified in any condition, clean or unclean.
- **dharma-mūla** — 'The root of dharma' — Nārada Muni's designation of the Supreme Lord, paralleling the name as the seed of the dharma tree.
- **kramaśaḥ / krame krame** — 'In order, step by step' — reading Bhagavad-gītā and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam systematically, and rising by degrees to the highest position through hearing and chanting.
- **avatāra** — A descent or incarnation of God; the Pañca-tattva form of Krishna is the most magnanimous of all avatāras, freely giving pure chanting.
- **Vaiṣṇava** — A devotee of Krishna/Viṣṇu; śāstra teaches the senses find their perfection in seeing, touching, and hearing from Vaiṣṇavas.
- **seva** — Service; framing japa as high service to Krishna performed with the tongue sustains chanting even before taste arises.
- **prasādam** — Sanctified food offered to the Lord, one of the gifts that makes contacted people more receptive to the holy name.
