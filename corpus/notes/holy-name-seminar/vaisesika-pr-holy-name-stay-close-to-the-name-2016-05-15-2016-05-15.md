# Vaisesika Pr Holy Name Stay Close to the Name 2016 05 15

## Theme

The lecture 'Stay Close to the Name' moves in a single arc from relief to practice. It opens with the immediate solace of coming into devotee association to hear and chant — an experience backed by śāstra — and with the discipline of physically looking verses up so they become foundational, fortifying points in one's life. The doctrinal ground is laid early: Krishna and His name are non-different, so anyone, at any moment and in any condition, can take help directly from the Lord simply by saying His names — the Vālmīki story showing that even the most fallen need only 'try a little,' because the name itself does the lifting. The long middle of the lecture is a close reading of the Gītā's teaching on the soul: the self is eternal, inexhaustible, and completely different from the perishable body, yet by misusing its slight independence the marginal soul is 'obliged' — bound, held accountable, not victimized by God — to accept subtle and gross bodies from material nature. Transmigration is presented as a precise mechanism: as the wind carries aromas, the soul carries the desires and tastes acquired through association; consciousness 'colored' by the modes, adjudicated beyond our control (daiva-netrena), orders the next custom-built body like a customer speccing a computer, and every body arrives with a fixed allotment of happiness and distress that cannot be renegotiated. The speaker is careful to note that this philosophy does not make devotees emotionally numb — even Bhīṣmadeva lamented — but gives them a definitive framework for processing death, and that soul-vision produces equal respect for all beings (paṇḍitāḥ sama-darśinaḥ). Since taste is acquired through association, and since even 'good' births are ultimately bad because the soul does not belong in matter at all, the conclusion pivots to the remedy: chanting and devotional service as the prime means to rise above the guṇas, like an airplane lifting above the clouds. The final movement is intensely practical and devotional: japa as prayerful meditation in a mood of helplessness; the vocative mahā-mantra as a direct, personal address in which God is fully present; Rūpa Gosvāmī's paradox that the name (vācaka) is even more merciful and accommodating than Krishna Himself (vācya); Chaitanya Mahāprabhu's prerequisite for constant chanting — humbler than a blade of grass, offering all respect while expecting none; and kirtan as the yuga-dharma 'shortcut' by which, per Karabhajana Muni, all the purposes of life are fulfilled in Kali-yuga. The seminar closes on its title instruction — stay close to the name through a written japa vow, a bead bag kept near, exclusive attentive hearing, and asking the Lord directly for everything — and overflows, as hearing and chanting naturally do, into congregational dancing and kirtan.

## Key teachings

- **[00:00:56.840]** Coming into the association of devotees to hear and chant gives immediate relief, and this experience is backed by śāstra: whoever recites the Lord's mysterious appearances/pastimes with devotion in the morning and evening is freed from all the miseries of life — and beyond relief, one rises to the soul's highest position with no ending point, because spiritual life is dynamic (no plateau) and Krishna is endlessly, inexhaustibly attractive.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
- **[00:01:39.400]** Physically look verses up rather than only recalling them — Prabhupāda would say 'find out that verse' even when he knew it perfectly, because the act of getting the book, opening it, and seeing the verse again and again makes verses foundational points in one's life that fortify, give direction, and make one genuinely strong in spiritual life.
- **[00:03:20.540]** Krishna and His name are non-different — the sound vibration itself is Krishna — so anyone can take help directly from the Lord at any moment simply by saying His names; no one can stop us from chanting Hare Krishna right now.
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila
- **[00:04:04.200]** Even the most downtrodden can be delivered by chanting, as the Vālmīki story shows: the marginal (taṭastha) jīva easily lets things get out of hand through bad association, and then feels too sinful and morose even to chant — but the instruction is 'just try a little.' Nārada Muni met Vālmīki where he was, having him chant 'mara' (death) to suit his mood, which became 'Rāma,' and by that chanting he came out of his deep funk. Start chanting from whatever condition you are in; the name itself does the lifting.
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Antya-lila
- **[00:06:15.620]** Our practice is 'following the path of the saints' (tat-tat-karma-pravartanāt): among the many prescribed activities that keep one connected to the saints' path, the chief activity is hearing — especially Krishna's own words in the Bhagavad-gītā, which can be heard over and over and always give solace and spiritual perspective.
    - cf. Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 10
- **[00:08:12.100]** The soul has no death — it is eternal, inexhaustible, free from material contamination, able to go anywhere in the material or spiritual worlds, fully aware, and completely different from the body; but by misusing its slight independence it is obliged to accept subtle and gross bodies from the material energy and undergo so-called happiness and distress — therefore no one should lament the soul's passing from the body. Even 'slight' independence misused becomes a slippery slope, which is why this one verse captures the essence of the Gītā's second-chapter teaching on soul and body.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 02
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 01
- **[00:10:07.160]** We are not the body: the soul is eternal and inexhaustible, while the body is a perishable shell. Death makes this vivid — the body with all its features remains, yet the person is gone, showing we never actually see the person, only the external covering. The practical conclusion is to feed the eternal self rather than the temporary body.
    - cf. Veda, Secrets from the East
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 01
- **[00:12:14.240]** Knowing the philosophy of the soul does not make a devotee emotionally numb — even Bhīṣmadeva lamented, and the speaker describes real heavy-heartedness and disorientation when his own parents passed away despite being able to intellectualize that they had not died. The difference is that devotees, unlike those without Kṛṣṇa consciousness, have a definitive framework for processing death; 'do not lament' means philosophical clarity about our actual situation, not the absence of feeling.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 01
- **[00:14:50.800]** Soul-vision transforms how we treat others: because every soul beyond its layers of contamination is pure and constitutionally a servant of Kṛṣṇa, the person with spiritual vision relates to all living entities equally and with respect, seeing them as parts of Kṛṣṇa rather than as their bodies (paṇḍitāḥ sama-darśinaḥ).
    - cf. Gita Values
- **[00:16:04.140]** The word 'obliged' carries accountability: our suffering in happiness and distress is not God's cruelty, as some theories charge, but the result of our own misuse of the soul's slight independence. Taking full responsibility for our condition, rather than blaming God, opens the vision of the Lord's compassion hidden within it (tat te 'nukampām).
    - cf. Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu
- **[00:17:15.480]** We knowingly drink poison: even while knowing the reality that we are eternal, we lose ourselves in material happiness and distress — the material energy is far more than merely 'distracting' for the soul (echoing Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's jāniyā śuniyā biṣa khāinu).
    - cf. Nectar of Devotion, The
- **[00:18:35.160]** Oblige comes from Latin obligare, 'to bind to' — and we are bound either way: one is forcibly subjected to śāstra like serving a term until one sobers up, or one voluntarily follows it as the 'regulative principles of freedom.' Through good association and step-by-step regulation in devotional service, one gains dominion over the mind and senses and recovers the right use of independence — the elevated, steady stage of niṣṭhā, where one is no longer pulled off the path by the senses' demands.

- **[00:20:59.900]** Transmigration is a precise mechanism, not chance: the soul who desires to enjoy matter independently is placed under material nature, and the body is a machine awarded according to karma, with the Supreme Lord in the heart directing the soul's wanderings — and this cycle continues only until the soul surrenders to Him.

    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
- **[00:24:32.100]** One who takes sense gratification as the aim of life becomes mad after materialistic living and sinful activity, not knowing the temporary body is itself the fruit of past misdeeds; as long as one does not inquire into spiritual values, one is defeated, and a mind that is karmātmaka — 'colored with the fruit of activity' — obliges one to accept material body after material body.

- **[00:27:03.240]** Taste is acquired through association: good association gives a taste for spiritual activities, while association with those of lower taste transmits that lower taste. The main test of advancement in Krishna consciousness is how much taste one has acquired for hearing about Krishna — expressed in the formula KKQ = KC: one's daily intake of kṛṣṇa-kathā, driven by the desire to hear and chant, directly equals one's level of Krishna consciousness.
- **[00:29:40.140]** Per Nārada Muni in the First Canto, every material body comes with a fixed, built-in allotment of happiness and distress that cannot be renegotiated — it arrives automatically — so the intelligent course is to spend one's energy on spiritual elevation rather than on adjusting material happiness.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 04
- **[00:30:15.700]** Consciousness becomes 'colored' by one's association with the modes of material nature — just as air carries aromas — and that coloring, not any choice made at the moment of death, determines the soul's next destination; the transfer is adjudicated beyond our control (daiva-netrena). The practical conclusion the speaker draws: this should motivate us to prepare better now, while we still can.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11

- **[00:31:19.100]** The next body is not random but a custom-made fit: material nature delivers exactly the set of senses — ear, eye, tongue, nose, touch, grouped around the mind — that one's desires 'ordered,' the way a customer specs out a custom-built computer. We are effectively placing orders with material nature through our desires.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 04

- **[00:41:56.040]** The maha-mantra given by the sampradaya is composed in the vocative — a direct address: 'O my Lord, please engage me in Your service.' It is succinct and perfectly symmetrical (two syllables throughout), and the mantra itself is directly the Supreme Personality of Godhead — so when people ask 'Can you show me God?', He is fully present right there in the syllables.

- **[00:42:44.860]** Rupa Goswami distinguishes the vacya (the one named, Krishna) from the vacaka (the name) and teaches a staggering paradox: although name and named are absolutely non-different — provable from shastra — the name is actually superior, being more accommodating and merciful than Krishna Himself, who is already the ocean of mercy (karuna-sindhu). This means chanting gives direct access to God in a way even better than meeting Him in person — the greatest opportunity in the universe.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 06

- **[00:45:53.360]** The name's mercy is practical: even someone downtrodden and entangled in the modes who simply starts repeating the name is plunged back into the ocean of bliss and saved. Perfect knowledge, detachment, all the energies needed for spiritual advancement, and even the necessary paraphernalia come naturally, automatically, and simultaneously just by the service of repeating God's name.
    - cf. Srimad Bhagavatam
- **[00:47:05.320]** Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's main instruction is to chant constantly, and constant chanting requires a specific state of consciousness: humbler than a blade of grass, more tolerant than a tree, offering all respect to others while expecting none for oneself. In one word this is about attitude — something we can adjust — and how we conduct ourselves throughout the day directly affects our chanting: a participant's realization confirmed that restraining oneself during a confrontation produced noticeably better focus in the next day's japa.

- **[00:50:02.560]** The desire for respect is so subtle that even devotional service can be co-opted into a means of gaining prestige; the remedy is to take shelter of the holy name in a mood of helplessness, expecting no respect for oneself while sincerely offering respect to others — the mood of tṛṇād api sunīcena, which Kavirāja Gosvāmī instructs one to wear around the neck like a garland so it is remembered constantly while chanting.
    - cf. Gita Values

- **[00:51:02.420]** Repetition of the divine names is the foundational practice because it puts one in direct contact with the Supreme Personality of Godhead — all of His internal energies are present in the name — and through it one receives knowledge, detachment, and all the paraphernalia needed for advancing in spiritual life.
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila
    - cf. Srimad Bhagavatam
- **[00:51:29.360]** One should maintain a regulated daily practice of repeating God's names; to make this easy, Caitanya Mahāprabhu recommended the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra, which may be chanted quietly as japa or sung aloud while listening carefully to the vibration. Even the tune matters: melodies received in the tradition (such as those Śrīla Prabhupāda sang) can evoke emotion and open the heart.

- **[00:59:41.880]** A life of chanting outweighs material accomplishment — illustrated by a devotee father's principle that he would rather his son be a pot washer who chanted than a doctor who never came to the temple — and steady parental encouragement in kirtan cultivates a lifelong taste for the holy name.
- **[01:00:33.380]** Kirtan, as the yuga-dharma, carries a specific potency of immediate access — a 'shortcut' (like an easy-pass) by which anyone, even children, can plug into Krishna consciousness quickly and find happiness and deep meditation; taste for japa, śāstra, and sādhu-saṅga may develop later, but kirtan grounds a devotee at every stage of life and is easy to pick up and become attached to — a perfect formula for sadhana.

    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
- **[01:03:56.240]** The alternative makes the shortcut precious: pursuing material acquisition and happiness without Krishna is frustrating — even to contemplate — and always yields the opposite of the result sought, a truth practically nobody in the world believes; whereas in saṅkīrtana all one's desires are fulfilled just by singing the names of God.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
- **[01:05:27.200]** Stay close to the name: keep your bead bag near (illustrated by a 20-month-old carrying a bead bag all day, and a devotee chanting concentrated japa alone in a field) and take daily time for japa, letting the world 'turn off' for a while — in an age when demands and information press on us constantly, this deliberate withdrawal is a healthy practice in itself, and japa is one of the closest ways to stay with the Holy Name.
- **[01:08:13.120]** Whatever desires one has — material, spiritual, or for liberation — one should ask the Lord directly rather than anywhere else, because He is their source; by chanting Krishna's names and praying to Him one remains always connected, and Krishna arranges all the paraphernalia needed for spiritual advancement, including good association.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 08
- **[01:08:46.080]** Take a regulated vow for japa: write it down and strictly commit to chanting a fixed number of names every day; a vow is essential for advancement in anything, and by this daily regularity the mind centers on the one activity of chanting, the intelligence becomes spiritualized, and everything needed for spiritual life comes forward. The mood of the chanting is the prayer: 'O my Lord, O energy of the Lord, please engage me in Your service.'
    - cf. Nectar of Instruction
- **[01:11:27.580]** Practical japa instruction: count ten mantras on the segments of the fingers (starting from the ring finger with the thumb), and sit up straight while chanting — upright posture signals to the mind and intelligence that something serious and important is happening. Begin by praying from the heart, asking the Lord to please engage you in His service.

- **[01:13:51.860]** Japa is the meditation of hearing the sound vibration — and hearing it exclusively — because the subtle mind constantly moves from one place to the next. Remind the wandering mind with Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's words that there is nothing to be had in the fourteen worlds except the holy name: everything we are looking for is within the name, so there is no need to go elsewhere — go within and fully concentrate on the chanting.
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Antya-lila
- **[01:14:36.480]** Dancing is the next part of the process after chanting, taught by Caitanya Mahāprabhu and carried on today — it is a natural expression of the holy name, as shown when the public at an outdoor festival spontaneously joined the chanting and danced with great devotion.
    - cf. Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila
- **[01:15:43.180]** Nothing equals the holy name: quoting Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's Harināma Cintāmaṇi, there is no vow like chanting the holy name, no knowledge superior to it, no meditation that comes near it, and no penance equal to it — it gives the highest result, is the greatest act of piety, and is the supreme refuge. This is why the holy name is called the shortcut.

This makes the Hare Krishna mantra uniquely intimate and personal.
- **[01:20:14.860]** Krishna is present within our hearts — He knows everything and is arranging everything. If we place everything at His feet as an offering rather than claiming it for ourselves, He makes all arrangements for us in the most natural and perfect ways. Recognition (like being seen or appreciated) need not be sought directly; it comes indirectly through humility and Krishna's arrangement.

## Practical application

Take a regulated, written vow to chant a fixed number of names every day and keep it strictly — daily regularity centers the mind on the one activity of chanting and spiritualizes the intelligence. Keep your bead bag near you throughout the day, and set aside daily japa time in which the world is deliberately allowed to 'turn off.' Mechanically: sit up straight (signaling to the mind that something serious is happening), count ten mantras on the finger segments starting from the ring finger with the thumb, and begin by praying from the heart, 'O my Lord, O energy of the Lord, please engage me in Your service' — the mahā-mantra's own vocative meaning. Chant in a mood of helplessness, and hear the sound vibration exclusively; when the mind wanders, remind it with Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's words that there is nothing to be had in the fourteen worlds except the holy name, so there is nowhere else to go. Since taste is acquired by association, cultivate good association and measure advancement by your daily intake of kṛṣṇa-kathā (KKQ = KC): hear regularly — especially Bhagavad-gītā — and physically look verses up rather than only recalling them. Guard your conduct through the day, because attitude directly affects japa: practice tṛṇād api sunīcena, offering all respect to others while expecting none, and watch that even devotional service is not co-opted for prestige. Start from whatever condition you are in — too sinful or morose is no disqualification; just try a little. Ask Krishna directly for whatever you need, placing everything at His feet rather than claiming it, and let kirtan — easy to pick up at any stage, even for children — carry the practice into singing and dancing, using melodies received in the tradition and letting the happiness of hearing and chanting overflow into celebration. No matter how spiritually out of shape you are, you can get right back in the game by repeating the names of God while giving attention to the sound.

## Glossary

- **śāstra** — Revealed Vedic scripture; the scriptural authority cited to confirm the speaker's points and realizations.
- **taṭastha** — 'Marginal' — the constitutional position of the jīva soul as Krishna's marginal energy, easily letting things get out of hand through bad association.
- **jīva** — The individual living entity or soul — eternal, inexhaustible, and completely different from the body.
- **tat-tat-karma-pravartanāt** — 'Following the path of the saints' — engaging in the many prescribed activities, chief among them hearing, that keep one connected to the saints' path.
- **paṇḍitāḥ sama-darśinaḥ** — 'The learned see with equal vision' — one with spiritual vision relates to all living entities equally and with respect, seeing them as souls and parts of Kṛṣṇa rather than as bodies.
- **tat te 'nukampām** — Reference to seeing the Lord's compassion hidden within one's own suffering, opened by taking full responsibility for one's condition rather than blaming God.
- **jāniyā śuniyā biṣa khāinu** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's confession, 'knowingly I drank poison' — losing oneself in material happiness and distress even while knowing one is eternal.
- **niṣṭhā** — The elevated, steady stage of practice at which one is no longer pulled off the path by the demands of the senses.
- **saṁsāra** — The cycle of repeated birth and death, driven by the mentality formed of acquired tastes born of the three modes.
- **karma** — Action and its reactions; the body is a machine awarded according to karma, with the Supreme Lord in the heart directing the soul's wanderings.
- **karmātmaka** — 'Colored with the fruit of activity' — a mind so absorbed in fruitive work that it obliges one to accept material body after material body.
- **guṇas** — The three modes of material nature — the 'ropes' that bind the soul to the material world; association with them (guṇa-saṅga) causes birth in good and evil wombs.
- **daiva-netrena** — 'By superior arrangement' — the transfer of the soul to its next body is adjudicated beyond our control, according to how consciousness has been colored.
- **utkramantam** — 'Stepping out' — the soul's literal departure from the body at death, perceivable only by those trained in spiritual science.
- **kṛṣṇa-kathā** — Topics and talks about Krishna; one's daily intake of kṛṣṇa-kathā is the main test of advancement (the speaker's formula KKQ = KC: Krishna-kathā quotient equals Krishna consciousness).
- **japa** — Quiet individual repetition of the holy name; etymologically 'ja' indicates freedom from birth and 'pa' freedom from sin — a prayerful meditation whose essential mood is helplessness.
- **mahā-mantra** — The Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra given by the sampradāya — succinct, perfectly symmetrical, composed in the vocative as a direct address: 'O my Lord, please engage me in Your service' — and itself directly the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
- **vācya** — In Rūpa Gosvāmī's distinction, the one who is named — Krishna Himself.
- **vācaka** — The name that names Him — absolutely non-different from Krishna, yet actually superior, being more accommodating and merciful than the Lord Himself.
- **karuṇā-sindhu** — 'Ocean of mercy' — an epithet of Krishna, whom the holy name nevertheless exceeds in mercy.
- **tṛṇād api sunīcena** — Chaitanya Mahāprabhu's standard for constant chanting: humbler than a blade of grass, more tolerant than a tree, offering all respect to others while expecting none — to be worn 'around the neck like a garland' while chanting.
- **kīrtana** — Congregational singing of the holy names; more than personal meditation, it bonds relationships on a spiritual dimension and grounds a devotee at every stage of life.
- **yuga-dharma** — The prescribed spiritual method for the age; in Kali-yuga it is chanting, carrying a specific potency of immediate access — the 'shortcut.'
- **Kali-yuga** — The present age, praised by those who know the essence of life because in it all the purposes of life are fulfilled simply by nāma-saṅkīrtana.
- **sarva-svārtha** — 'All the purposes of life' — fulfilled, per Karabhajana Muni in the Eleventh Canto, simply by performing nāma-saṅkīrtana.
- **nāma-saṅkīrtana** — Congregational chanting of the holy name — the practice by which all desires are fulfilled just by singing the names of God.
- **sādhu-saṅga** — Association with saintly persons — the means by which taste, including the higher taste for the holy name, is acquired.
- **sādhana** — Regulated daily spiritual practice; kirtan plus japa, śāstra, and sādhu-saṅga form a 'perfect formula' for it.
- **vocative** — The grammatical case of direct address, in which the mahā-mantra is composed — making it uniquely intimate and personal compared to dative mantras of obeisance such as oṁ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya.
- **Harināma Cintāmaṇi** — Work of Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura quoted in closing: there is no vow like chanting the holy name, no knowledge superior to it, no meditation that comes near it, and no penance equal to it.
