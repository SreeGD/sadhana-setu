# Vaisesika Pr Holy Name Kevalastakam 2017 07 09

## Theme

The lecture is a guided meditation on the Kevalāṣṭakam, an eight-verse Sanskrit hymn by an anonymous sage, which the speaker presents first in Dravida Prabhu's poeticized English rendering and then chants in Sanskrit. Its arc moves from declaration to urgency to practice: the holy name of Śrī Hari alone (harer nāmaiva kevalam) is supreme in every category — the sweetest of sweet things, the paradigm of good fortune, the most powerful purifier, 'all that be.' Against this stands the entire cosmos, from Brahmā's realm down to a blade of grass, pervaded by Māyā's illusion; the hymn insists three times over that the holy name is 'the truth, the truth, the only truth.' The name — and anyone who teaches one to always chant and hear it — is one's real guru, father, most faithful friend, and mother, redefining guardianship as connecting a person to the name. Because the final breath may come at any moment, whether in old age or youthful prime, the breath cannot be trusted, so young and old alike must chant incessantly, beginning from childhood. Śrī Hari personally and perpetually dwells wherever devotees with purified, fixed hearts sing His name in ecstasy, while the greatest calamity is to forget the name — mistaking the priceless gem for broken glass. The session culminates in embodied practice: filling the ears with the name, chanting with all sincerity, and engaging the body in the arati and kirtan that follow, where the speaker teaches participants to sway or dance (the 'Swami' step) without inhibition, noting that chanting and dancing in kirtan is an authorized devotional process, not an artificial display.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this by making the holy name the constant, central engagement of the senses rather than an occasional duty. In daily japa, fill the ears with the name — chant so that hearing is attentive and complete — and chant with all sincerity, treating the name not as one item among many but as the only truth amid Māyā's pervasive illusion, the priceless gem never to be mistaken for broken glass. Do not postpone or ration the practice by age or health: since the final breath may come at any moment, chant incessantly now, whether young or old, and begin (and teach) chanting from childhood. Relate to the name personally — as guru, father, most faithful friend, and mother — and honor whoever connects you to it as your true guardian; conversely, guard vigilantly against forgetfulness of the name, which the hymn calls the greatest sorrow and worst calamity. Work toward a purified, fixed heart, knowing Śrī Hari personally dwells wherever devotees sing His name in ecstasy. Finally, engage the body as well as the ears and tongue: in kirtan and arati, sway or dance (even a simple 'Swami' step), letting go of inhibitions, confident that dancing while chanting is an authorized devotional process, not a display.

## Glossary

- **Kevalāṣṭakam** — An eight-verse (aṣṭakam) Sanskrit hymn by an anonymous sage glorifying the holy name of Śrī Hari as the only (kevala) truth; the text at the center of this lecture.
- **harer nāmaiva kevalam** — 'The name of Hari alone' — the hymn's refrain declaring that the holy name of Śrī Hari alone is supreme in every category and is 'all that be.'
- **Śrī Hari** — A name of the Supreme Lord whose holy name is glorified and chanted throughout the hymn; He is said to dwell personally wherever devotees sing His name in ecstasy.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of Hari; the divine name itself, presented as identical with the supreme truth and the object of constant chanting and hearing.
- **Māyā** — The power of illusion said to pervade the entire cosmos, from Brahmā's realm at the top down to the lowly blade of grass; everything under its sway is contrasted with the holy name as the only truth.
- **Brahmā** — The creator-god whose realm is the topmost region of the cosmos, invoked in the hymn to mark the upper limit of Māyā's pervasion.
- **guru** — Spiritual teacher and guide; the hymn identifies the holy name — and whoever teaches one to always chant and hear it — as one's true guru.
- **japa** — The devotee's individual, meditative chanting of the holy name, the daily practice to which the lecture's teachings on attentive hearing and sincere chanting are applied.
- **kirtan** — Congregational singing of the holy name; the lecture extends into a kirtan in which chanting combined with dancing is affirmed as an authorized devotional process, not an artificial display.
- **arati** — A ceremony of worship accompanied by kirtan; the setting into which the speaker carries the hymn's teaching of engaging the whole body in the holy name.
- **'Swami' step** — A simple swaying dance step the speaker teaches participants so they can engage the body in kirtan, letting go of all inhibitions while chanting the holy name.
