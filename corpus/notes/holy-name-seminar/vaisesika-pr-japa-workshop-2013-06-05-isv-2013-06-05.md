# Vaisesika Pr Japa Workshop 2013 06 05 ISV

## Theme

In this 2013 japa workshop at ISV, Vaiśeṣika Prabhu builds a complete method for transforming daily japa from mechanical repetition into attentive, transformative contact with Krishna. He opens by establishing japa as our main sadhana: because Krishna and His name are non-different, chanting the Hare Krishna maha-mantra — the prescribed process for Kali-yuga — delivers one from birth and death and leads to prema. From this foundation he lays out the inner prerequisites: a clear intention that directs attention, active hearing of one's own chanting as the discipline of mind-control, humble and dependent moods (begging for help, taking the name as medicine in our diseased condition, fearing offenses, crying out like a child, knowing oneself as akiñcana-gocara), and firm faith in the name's potency sustained by hearing sambandha-jñāna every day as Haridasa Thakura teaches in Harinama Cintamani. The long central movement of the workshop teaches present-moment awareness as the first principle of improved japa: distinguishing one's eternal life (consciousness, the jīva as Krishna's fragmental part) from the ever-changing 'life situation,' settling and purifying oneself before chanting on the model of bhūta-śuddhi, refusing the 'absence presence' of multitasking, and — in a guided chanting exercise before Śrī Śrī Rādhā Madana-Mohana with debrief — practicing the core technique of noticing the wandering mind without struggle and gently returning to hearing the name. He then normalizes the restless mind through Arjuna's admission in the Bhagavad-gītā and Krishna's answer that practice tames it, offering trainable techniques: three mantras at a time, one mantra at a time, conscious breathing, and experimenting with methods of concentration per Bhaktivinoda Thakura. A theological section deepens the mood: the name is conscious, alive (caitanya) and full of taste (rasa), a touchstone that purifies the senses beginning with the tongue and reveals Krishna (sphūrti) to the humble, attentive chanter — Krishna is fully present in His name with all His qualities. The workshop then turns to lifestyle: centering the day around chanting like an athlete's training, prizing brahma-muhūrta, preparing the night before, regulating sleep, chanting all sixteen rounds in one sitting where possible, setting 'flex goals' beyond sixteen, adjusting for busy householders who should beg for 'one mustard seed of taste,' and filling all the day's gaps with transcendental sound. Progress is measured by steadiness rather than outward ecstasy, and the mind is handled like an expert horseman handles reins — sometimes loose, sometimes firm — while persisting like a barefoot Govardhana parikramā through smooth and thorny stretches alike. The final section covers the mechanics and mood of the mantra itself: precise pronunciation of all sixteen words and thirty-two syllables (chanting 'Rāma,' not 'Rām'), steady pace, honest audits through peer feedback and self-recording, supportive posture, and above all prayerfulness — chanting as a helpless cry in the identity of 'servant of the servant of the servant,' wearing Śikṣāṣṭakam verse 3's four jewels of humility, tolerance, and respect. He closes with saṅkalpa (writing a vow before chanting), fixing a minimum daily number never to be broken, and planning occasional big japa days of 64 or 108 rounds, since there is nothing to be had in the fourteen worlds except the Holy Name.

## Practical application

Begin each japa session deliberately rather than grabbing the bead bag and pacing: sit down with good posture, take a few conscious breaths, settle into the present moment, and remember who you are — not your titles or life situation, but a conscious spirit soul, servant of the servant of Krishna. Write a short saṅkalpa (prayer or vow) stating why you are chanting and keep it nearby; make one heartfelt prayer, then let that feeling linger while you simply hear the mantra. During chanting, your single duty is to listen to each syllable of the maha-mantra: when the mind wanders — and it will, for everyone — just notice without struggle and return to the sound. If concentration is hard, shrink the assignment to three mantras at a time, or chant one mantra as if it were your whole japa, and go slowly without anxiety about pace, since the japa-mālā is a circle with nowhere to hurry to. Remove all interference: switch off every device, chant near Tulasī or in a japa circle with absorbed devotees, and never multitask during japa. Structurally, chant in the early morning (brahma-muhūrta), ideally all sixteen rounds in one unbroken sitting; make this possible by eating lightly at night, going to bed early, laying out beads and books the night before, and rising a few minutes earlier each day until the full quota fits before daily obligations. Hold 'flex goals' beyond sixteen rounds to stay hungry, fix a minimum number you never go below, and occasionally plan a big japa day of 64 or 108 rounds. Sustain faith by hearing sambandha-jñāna daily, and audit your chanting honestly: have a trusted peer listen to ten of your mantras, record yourself for a full round and listen back, pronounce every word and syllable distinctly (including the final 'a' of Rāma), keep a steady pace, and make sure every bead gets a complete mantra. If you are a busy householder, chant whatever rounds circumstances allow with full intention, begging for one mustard seed of taste to carry you through the day, and fill the day's gaps with transcendental sound. Measure your progress by steadiness and dedication, not by dramatic experiences; on hard days handle the mind softly like an expert horseman, and keep going either way — like circumambulating Govardhana barefoot, you cannot turn back.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Soft, individual chanting of a mantra — here the Hare Krishna maha-mantra — counted on beads as one's daily meditation.
- **sadhana** — Regulated daily spiritual practice performed to advance in devotional life; the lecture presents japa as the devotee's main sadhana.
- **maha-mantra** — The 'great mantra' — Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma Rāma Rāma Hare Hare — exactly 16 words and 32 syllables.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present degraded cosmic age, for which chanting the Holy Name is the prescribed method of deliverance.
- **prema** — Pure love for Krishna, the ultimate goal to which chanting the Holy Name brings the practitioner.
- **aparadha** — Offense, especially against the Holy Name or Vaisnavas; exemplary devotees take no chances even with seemingly small offenses.
- **akiñcana-gocara** — Krishna as approachable only by one who possesses nothing else; the mood of having no shelter other than the Holy Name.
- **sankirtana** — Congregational glorification of the Lord through chanting His names; Chaitanya Mahaprabhu declares total victory through it.
- **sambandha-jñāna** — Knowledge of one's relationship with Krishna — His potencies, our dependence on Him, and our conditioning by material nature — to be heard daily alongside chanting.
- **Harinama Cintamani** — Bhaktivinoda Thakura's treatise on the Holy Name, presented as the teachings of Haridasa Thakura, cited for daily sambandha-jñāna and for experimenting with methods of concentration.
- **brahma-muhūrta** — The auspicious pre-dawn period, prized as the best time for chanting one's rounds.
- **Uttamasloka** — A name of the Lord meaning 'He who is praised by choice verses'; the rising sun takes away everyone's lifespan except one engaged in glorifying Uttamasloka.
- **jīva** — The individual living entity — a unit of consciousness, eternally a fragmental part and parcel of Krishna, as taught in the Bhagavad-gītā.
- **bhūta-śuddhi** — The purification of the bodily elements performed before Deity worship, remembering one's identity as servant of the servant; the model for settling oneself before japa.
- **arcana** — Formal Deity worship (pūjā), whose preparatory purification rites the speaker applies to japa.
- **prāṇāyāma** — Regulated breathing exercises, prescribed in some Vaiṣṇava śāstras for centering the mind before worship.
- **japa-mālā** — The string of 108 chanting beads; being a circle, it reminds the chanter there is no need to hurry to 'get somewhere.'
- **sphūrti** — The Lord's self-revelation to the chanter, granted when one approaches the Holy Name in a humble mood with full attention.
- **yajña** — Vedic sacrifice; whatever is offered in it becomes Brahman, illustrating how engagement in the Supreme's service spiritualizes the instrument engaged.
- **caitanya** — Living consciousness; the Holy Name is described as alive and conscious — the very source of all consciousness — not inert sound.
- **rasa** — Spiritual taste or mellow; the Holy Name is full of rasa, whose nectar Rūpa Gosvāmī longs to absorb with millions of tongues and ears.
- **cintāmaṇi** — A touchstone or wish-fulfilling gem; the Padma Purāṇa describes the Holy Name as cintāmaṇi, transforming whoever contacts it.
- **pūrṇa śuddha, nitya-mukta** — 'Completely pure, eternally liberated' — the Padma Purāṇa's description of the Holy Name as absolutely non-different from Krishna.
- **prasāda / prasādam** — The Lord's mercy; also sanctified food offered to the Lord, which is honored as transcendental mercy rather than ordinary food.
- **ānanda** — Spiritual bliss; Radhanath Swami's 'ānanda factor' — a manager's first duty is to have ānanda, gained through absorbed chanting.
- **Śikṣāṣṭakam** — Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's eight instructive verses; verse 3 prescribes humility lower than grass, tolerance like a tree, and offering respect while expecting none — the mood for chanting.
- **durdaivam** — 'Misfortune' — the admission in Śikṣāṣṭakam verse 2 of having no taste for the Holy Name despite its carrying all of Krishna's potencies.
- **a-kāra** — The letter 'a,' which Krishna identifies with Himself ('of letters I am the a-kāra'); dropping the final 'a' of 'Rāma' leaves Krishna off the end of the mantra.
- **saṅkalpa** — A firm vow or resolve; writing a short prayer of intention before japa fixes the mind and sets determination.
- **Mahāviṣṇu** — The form of the Lord from whose breathing the conditioned souls emerge and resume where they left off — the analogy for the mind waking exactly where it stopped the night before.
- **Tulasī** — The sacred plant dear to Krishna; chanting in her presence is recommended as a support for attentive japa.
- **gopīs** — Krishna's cowherd maidservants of Vrindavana; the chanter's true identity is 'servant of the servant of the servant of the maintainer of the gopīs.'
