# Vaisesika Pr Holy Name Keep Hearing and Chanting 2016 08 28

## Theme

Speaking on Śrīla Prabhupāda's Vyāsa-pūjā day, Vaiśeṣika Prabhu builds a single sustained argument: steady, systematic, lifelong hearing and chanting is what actually transforms the heart — 'heart' meaning consciousness itself. Opening from the Bhāgavatam's two-phase map of purification (first lust, anger, and greed diminish; then one directly perceives oneself becoming better), he compares the process to the body healing a wound: the devotee only creates the facilitating environment through the sadhana the ācāryas prescribe, and the healing happens gradually, beyond our control, by Kṛṣṇa's grace. The anchor text is SB 2.8.5 and its purport — the sound incarnation of Kṛṣṇa enters through the ears and cleanses the dust of material association like autumn rains clearing muddy pools — from which he draws the lecture's core contrasts: mercy descends from above through the empowered pure devotee, who like a rain-cloud on a forest fire can deliver the whole world, whereas empiric knowledge and mystic yoga at best chemically settle the mud of one small tank, leaving it ready to be stirred up again. Only devotional service cleanses for good, because the Lord Himself within the heart is the cleansing agent. He then traces Caitanya-caritāmṛta's bhakti-creeper allegory — the seed received from pure-devotee association, watered by hearing and chanting, penetrating the coverings of the universe to reach Goloka Vṛndāvana and bear the fruit of prema — with its two standing warnings: the watering never stops at any stage of maturity, and the mad elephant of vaiṣṇava-aparādha, rooted in guru-avajñā and whimsical independence from devotee association, can destroy the creeper in one stroke (Durvāsā versus Ambarīṣa). Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's progressive stages — śravaṇa-daśā through varaṇa, smaraṇa, and sampatti-daśā — show that every stage begins and continues by hearing alone, which naturally awakens one's constitutional relationship with Kṛṣṇa without artificially engineering it. The final movement grounds all of this in the just-completed festival: devotional service energizes rather than depletes, goals beyond one's capacity build faith in Kṛṣṇa's help, devotees are a greater wealth than gold, expertly synchronized selfless service is itself preaching, and staying 'in the pot' of the devotee community for decades — growing through one's service, not merely going through it — is what produces the mature, absolute faith the scriptures describe. Continuous śravaṇa-kīrtana is what made the festival spiritual, and consistency in that hearing, more than intelligence, is what generates momentum in devotional life.

## Practical application

Keep a fixed, non-negotiable daily minimum of hearing and chanting — 'nityam' means at least regularly every day — and maintain it especially after busy festival seasons, because breaking continuity lets the trail go cold, taste diminish, and agitation return in the gaps; consistency matters more than intelligence. During japa, when the unsteady mind pulls away, remember that the very ability and interest to chant is already Kṛṣṇa's mercy, and that taste grows by the practice itself: capacity to hear increases by hearing. Trust the process the way one trusts a wound to heal — create the environment, tolerate the gradual pace, and let the Lord within the heart do the cleansing rather than measuring results daily. Feed the ears kṛṣṇa-kathā instead of grāmya-kathā (news, elections, movies, sports); read Śrīla Prabhupāda's books aloud so reading becomes simultaneous chanting and hearing, and hear even without full understanding, since the sound vibration is potent in itself. Read the Bhāgavatam whole and in order rather than specializing in select pastimes to engineer a particular rasa — one's relationship with Kṛṣṇa awakens naturally through sustained hearing. Never conclude one is mature enough to reduce śravaṇa-kīrtana; the creeper must be watered at every stage, and giving up the process is itself symptomatic of offense. Guard the creeper by avoiding vaiṣṇava-aparādha: stay in the association of devotees, follow the regulative principles and the spiritual master's instructions rather than one's whims, and cultivate the mood of servant of the servant of the ācāryas. Take direction from scripture rather than speculation, accept service slightly beyond proven capacity to build faith in Kṛṣṇa's help, dovetail duty with love, and remain steadily in the devotee community over years — hearing and chanting in that association is the water that carries the practice from neophyte hearing all the way to prema.

## Glossary

- **śravaṇam** — Hearing about Kṛṣṇa — His name, form, qualities, and pastimes; the first limb of devotional service.
- **kīrtanam** — Chanting and glorifying Kṛṣṇa, especially His holy name; paired with śravaṇam as the beginning of bhakti.
- **śravaṇa-kīrtana** — The combined process of hearing and chanting about Kṛṣṇa, the water that nourishes the creeper of devotion at every stage.
- **japa** — Soft, individual chanting of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra, typically counted on beads as daily practice.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari), non-different from Kṛṣṇa Himself and heard before He is seen.
- **sādhana** — Regulated daily spiritual practice performed under the direction of the ācāryas.
- **vaidhī-bhakti** — Devotional service performed with firm faith in scriptural injunctions, following them every day until natural taste develops.
- **kṛṣṇa-kathā** — Topics and narrations about Kṛṣṇa, the recommended constant hearing for the neophyte.
- **grāmya-kathā** — 'Village talk' — mundane topics such as elections, movies, and sports, to be replaced by kṛṣṇa-kathā.
- **nityam** — 'Constantly/always'; explained by Viśvanātha Cakravartī as meaning at least regularly every day.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** — The sound incarnation of Kṛṣṇa; hearing it regularly is presented as the best method of purification and liberation from illusion.
- **puṇya-śravaṇa-kīrtanaḥ** — Kṛṣṇa as He whose hearing and chanting is itself virtuous — potent and purifying even when heard without full understanding.
- **bhakti-latā** — The creeper of devotion, grown from a seed received in pure-devotee association and watered by hearing and chanting until it reaches Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet.
- **prema** — Pure love of God — the fruit of the devotional creeper, defined as the pure desire to serve and please the senses of the Supreme Lord.
- **rasa** — A transcendental mellow or relationship with Kṛṣṇa; the primary rasas named include servitude, friendship, parental affection, and conjugal love.
- **vaiṣṇava-aparādha** — An offense at the feet of a devotee, likened to a mad elephant that uproots and dries the delicate creeper of devotion.
- **guru-avajñā** — Disobeying the instructions of the spiritual master — the most dangerous form of offense, from which the creeper's uprooting begins.
- **ācārya** — An exemplary spiritual teacher in disciplic succession who teaches by precept and example.
- **ācārya-upāsanā** — Worship of the previous ācāryas and adherence to their instructions, compared to boarding a boat that crosses the material ocean.
- **Gurv-aṣṭaka** — The eight daily-sung prayers glorifying the spiritual master, including the verse comparing him to a cloud extinguishing the forest fire of material existence.
- **brahma-muhūrta** — The auspicious pre-dawn period during which devotees sing daily prayers.
- **śravaṇa-daśā** — The neophyte stage of constantly hearing Kṛṣṇa's name, form, qualities, and pastimes (per Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's progression).
- **varaṇa-daśā** — The stage of acceptance, in which one becomes attached to hearing kṛṣṇa-kathā.
- **smaraṇa-avasthā** — The stage of remembering Kṛṣṇa, deepening through recollection, absorption, meditation, constant remembrance (anusmṛti), and samādhi.
- **anusmṛti** — Constant, unbroken remembrance of Kṛṣṇa, a late phase of the remembering stage.
- **samādhi** — Complete trance-like absorption, the culmination of remembrance.
- **sampatti-daśā** — The perfection of life, in which the soul clearly understands its eternal constitutional relationship with Kṛṣṇa.
- **Goloka Vṛndāvana** — Kṛṣṇa's supreme abode, which the creeper of devotion reaches after penetrating the coverings of the universe.
- **māyā** — The Lord's illusory material energy, from which an empowered devotee can deliver himself and others.
- **jīva** — The individual soul — tiny and highly influenceable, absorbing the mood of whatever association it keeps.
- **Vyāsa-pūjā** — The annual worship of the spiritual master on his appearance day; this lecture reflects on Śrīla Prabhupāda's Vyāsa-pūjā offerings just heard.
- **saṅkīrtana** — Congregational chanting and glorification of the Lord, as in the community's Monthly Sankirtan Festival.
- **darśana** — The audience or sight of the Lord's deity form, which festival guests eagerly come to receive.
- **brāhmaṇa** — A member of the priestly, learned class; even one with all twelve qualifications cannot deliver others without surrender to the Lord.
- **Sudarśana cakra** — The Lord's disc weapon, which pursued Durvāsā Muni across the universe after he offended the devotee Ambarīṣa Mahārāja.
