# Vaisesika Pr Japa Workshop Part 03 2014 01 26 ISV

## Theme

Part 3 of Vaiśeṣika Dāsa's japa workshop (ISV, 26 January 2014) moves from the philosophy of meditation to a complete practical method for chanting the holy name, ending in guided practice. It opens by establishing why meditation matters at all: left to itself the mind drags us restlessly around the world, and balance means not stillness but knowing one's center — which must be a worthy goal, since passion's happiness is nectar in the beginning and poison in the end — and continually returning to it. Japa is presented as the ideal meditation because the mantra, unlike the breath, is inherently spiritual: the holy name is non-different from Krishna (nāma cintāmaṇi), complete and blissful, so a relationship with Krishna can be developed within the name itself, and full attention given to it is itself an offering of love. The meditator's two friends are abhyāsa (daily practice) and vairāgya (detachment as simple indifference), with Krishna's assurance to Arjuna that the wind-like mind can be controlled by these. The heart of the lecture is the 'three P's': pronunciation (chant each word distinctly and hear it, in the humble mood of tṛṇād api sunīcena, since chanting begins at the offensive stage), posture ('sit properly,' experiment with perfect stillness, tolerate urges as the sthita-prajña simply sits — 'don't just do something, sit there'), and prayer (chanting as the child's genuine cry for Mother Harā to reach Father Hari, submitting one's real distress in the tossing ocean of illusion). Around this core he builds the practical machinery: sankalpa before chanting, counted breathing to settle and to notice one is 'being breathed,' turning off every device to counter 'absent presence,' brahma-muhūrta rising and sattva-guṇa supports, the meaning and method of the japa-mālā, remedies for speeding and sagging concentration (chant with Prabhupāda's recording, record yourself, and chant more, not less — even a 64-round day, since incessant chanting destroys its own offenses), graded round-commitments with weekly shelter of a mentor, and gentleness toward the wandering mind. After a guided chanting session, the closing instruction completes the arc: don't rush on — sit still and let the holy name's effect register on the heart, then keep improving through self-set challenges reviewed regularly.

## Practical application

Before japa: fix your sankalpa — consciously ask what your intention for this session is, ideally Pṛthu Mahārāja's mood of 'You decide and give me what is best'; turn off (not merely silence) every device, secure an undisturbed time and place, and settle body and mind with a few rounds of slow counted breathing (inhale four counts, exhale four counts), noticing that you are not so much breathing as being breathed. During japa: sit up straight with hips above the knees, pronounce every word distinctly with lips and tongue and actually hear it rather than lapsing into mechanical hissing, and hold the humility of tṛṇād api sunīcena, remembering that your attention itself is the love you are offering Krishna. When urges, itches, disturbances, or wandering thoughts arise, tolerate them — they announce themselves as permanent but dissipate if not obeyed — and gently bring the mind back like a runaway child, without chastising it; if you walk while chanting, walk mindfully and do nothing else. Use the mālā properly: turn around at the Krishna bead and roll the beads with thumb and middle finger, which itself aids concentration. For quality problems: chant along with Śrīla Prabhupāda's japa recording to stop speeding and dropping syllables, record and listen back to your own chanting, and when concentration sags chant more, not less — occasionally plan a 25- or 64-round day, which recalibrates 16 rounds and reveals the tongue's own taste for the Name; if offenses are present, the remedy is continuous, increased chanting. Structurally: write down a fixed daily chanting time and keep it, rise for brahma-muhūrta when possible, cultivate sattva-guṇa (no heavy food at night, an early cool bath, a clean and simplified chanting space prepared the night before), commit to a graded level of daily rounds held steady for a month with weekly check-ins with a devotee mentor, and review a concrete self-set challenge weekly or monthly. After finishing: don't move straight to the next activity — sit still with eyes closed for a minute or two and consciously feel how the sound of the holy name has affected you.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Private, individual mantra meditation — here, chanting the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra on beads as one's daily practice.
- **mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' — Hare Krishna — explained by Prabhupāda as the child's cry for the mother, in which the conditioned soul appeals to the Lord through His internal energy.
- **sac-cid-ānanda** — Eternity, knowledge, and bliss — the soul's natural constitution; bliss is our normal state, lost only through distraction.
- **abhyāsa** — Repeated daily practice, including inculcating a sacred truth by repeating the same word or passage — one of the meditator's two friends.
- **vairāgya** — Detachment, best understood as simple indifference to outside stimuli, which costs no emotional investment — the meditator's other friend.
- **Supersoul** — The Lord's presence seated within the body beside the individual soul, whom habituated hearing of the mantra positions one to discover.
- **nāma cintāmaṇi** — Opening words of the verse describing the holy name as a transcendentally blissful giver of all benedictions — complete, pure, eternal, liberated, and non-different from Krishna Himself.
- **nāmābhāsa** — The shadow or reflective stage of chanting — a dimmed, not-yet-pure utterance of the holy name through which every chanter initially passes.
- **nāmāparādha** — Offensive chanting — chanting while committing offenses against the holy name, during which pure love of Krishna is not attainable; remedied by chanting continuously and even more.
- **tṛṇād api sunīcena** — The verse enjoining the chanter's mood: humbler than a straw in the street, more tolerant than a tree, offering respect to others while expecting none; Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja advises keeping it 'strung about the neck.'
- **sthita-prajña** — One of fixed, steady consciousness, about whom Arjuna asks 'how does he sit?' — the capacity to simply sit in one place against the world's momentum is itself a mark of such fixity.
- **Harā** — The Lord's internal energy, addressed as 'Hare' in the mahā-mantra — the Mother who helps the conditioned soul achieve the grace of the Supreme Father.
- **Hari** — The Supreme Lord as 'He who takes away' — who reveals Himself to the sincere crier and expertly steals away attachments that are bad for us.
- **Śaraṇāgati** — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's collection of songs on surrender; its closing poem describes the personified holy name taking the chanter by the hand back to the spiritual world and his eternal service.
- **sankalpa** — Solemnly fixing one's intention and determination before chanting, so the session begins with deliberate purpose; per the Bṛhad-Bhāgavatāmṛta, what one desires while chanting one's mantra is fully achieved and more.
- **Bṛhad-Bhāgavatāmṛta** — Scripture cited for the promise that whatever one desires while chanting one's mantra will be fully achieved and more — hence the importance of fixing desire on pure devotional service.
- **brahma-muhūrta** — The auspicious period about one hour and forty-five minutes before sunrise — a special 'channel of opportunity' when free nectar floats in the air, best for japa.
- **sattva-guṇa** — The mode of goodness, cultivated to support morning chanting: light eating at night, an early cool bath, and a clean, simplified chanting space.
- **japa-mālā** — The string of 108 chanting beads, representing the 108 Upaniṣads or the 108 gopīs; the head (Krishna) bead is never chanted on or crossed, and rolling the beads with thumb and middle finger engages a nerve that aids concentration.
- **gopīs** — Krishna's most intimate devotees, 108 of whom the beads of the japa-mālā represent.
- **Śrīla Prabhupāda** — Founder-ācārya whose purports frame the workshop's method ('sit properly,' the stages of chanting, the mantra as a cry for the mother) and whose japa recording is recommended as a pacing aid.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — Vaiṣṇava teacher and author of Śaraṇāgati, whose closing poem describes the fruit of surrendered chanting.
- **Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja** — Vaiṣṇava author who advises keeping the tṛṇād api sunīcena humility verse 'strung about the neck' — carried everywhere, especially while chanting.
- **Pṛthu Mahārāja** — Saintly king whose prayer — 'I don't know what to ask for; You decide and give me what is best, even if it is medicine' — is presented as the highest sankalpa.
