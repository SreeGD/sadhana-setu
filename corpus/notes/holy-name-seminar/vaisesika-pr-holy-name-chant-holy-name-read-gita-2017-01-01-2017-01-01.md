# Vaisesika Pr Holy Name Chant Holy Name Read Gita 2017 01 01 ISV

## Theme

Delivered as a New Year's Day address, this lecture builds a complete case for two daily practices — chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra and reading Bhagavad-gītā — and ends by asking the audience to vow to both for at least thirty days. Vaiśeṣika Prabhu opens by honoring the devotee assembly itself: the Vaishnavas are tadīya, Krishna's own, and hearing scripture in their association is the Bhāgavatam's recommended primary means of spiritual advancement. He lays the philosophical foundation from Gītā chapter 13 — discerning the body (kṣetra), the soul (kṣetra-jña), and the Supersoul — and explains that this vision dawns as hearing lifts one into sattva-guṇa, granting śāstra-cakṣus, the ability to see Krishna's presence in the world through His vibhūtis. Hearing automatically produces virakti, curing asat-tṛṣṇā, the irrational thirst for flimsy temporary things. He then diagnoses bondage: the soul contemplates matter with a proprietary eye, extends its identity into 'my' objects, and thereby suffers their fates; the Īśopaniṣad's īśāvāsya conception — everything belongs to the Lord, accept only your quota as a temporary caretaker — is the cure. Stephen Covey's circles of concern and influence make this practical: media inflates the circle of concern into anxiety, while the wise identify the one point of greatest influence, which the whole graded Vedic canon (Vedas → Upaniṣads → Vedānta-sūtra → itihāsas/Purāṇas → Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, the fruit of the tree) converges upon — the tongue, and specifically the mahā-mantra, which frees one from Kali-yuga's faults and pulls the chanter back to the spiritual world. He gives concrete japa technique (sit straight, close the eyes, listen to the syllables, keep bringing the mind back, add one's drop of sincerity), then argues for daily Gītā reading because the mind, a never-fully-tamed wild animal, derails within every 24-hour cycle and must be retrained with lifelong vigilance against Viṣṇu-māyā's grinding wheel. Reading is to be done as if hearing Krishna directly, through Prabhupāda's Bhagavad-gītā As It Is; per BG 7.1 and SB 1.2.17–21, such hearing purifies the heart, dissolves karma and attachment automatically (Kapiladeva's digestion analogy; the holy sound as 'universal solvent'), and gives the restless mind its one satisfying resting place — Krishna. Dhruva Mahārāja's swift perfection through one mantra and a focused mind proves the method, and the closing counsel is not to take access to the Gītā for granted: treat it as the rarest of opportunities and read at least a page every day.

## Practical application

Take a concrete vow — New Year's Day or any fresh start serves as a 'redo button' — to chant a fixed number of mahā-mantras and read a fixed number of Gītā verses with purports every day for a minimum of 30 days, since 'you can achieve a lot in 30 days but you can tolerate almost anything for 30 days.' For japa: sit up straight like a yogi, close the eyes to shut out other images, and place all attention on hearing the syllables of the mantra, gently bringing the wandering mind back to the sound each time it strays. Do not strain to conjure Krishna directly — the sound is non-different from Him, and He reveals Himself of His own accord (svayam eva sphuraty adaḥ) to one who does the seva of attentive hearing. Before or during chanting, undertake 'the great search for your sincerity' — find even one drop of genuine feeling and add it, because quality of chanting counts, as a mother distinguishes her child's anguished cry from its ordinary sounds. When the mind resists, accept the austerity of gently cornering it into hearing; every such session accumulates like a deposit in a bank account. For reading: keep the Bhagavad-gītā in a prominent place at home, physically take it out and read at least a page daily in the mood of listening directly to Krishna, remembering that this access is rare and one's lifespan finite. Frame the whole practice with the circle-of-influence discipline: withdraw attention from the vast circle of concern (especially constant media consumption, which breeds worry and helplessness), remember the īśāvāsya mood — caretaker, not owner — and keep returning attention to the one point of real leverage, the tongue engaged in the holy name, since where attention goes, energy flows. Maintain this daily retraining of the mind with vigilance to the end of life, never trusting the mind as permanently tamed, and expect detachment, purification, and the joyfulness of brahma-bhūta to arise as automatic by-products of hearing rather than as separate austerities.

## Glossary

- **tadīya** — 'Belonging to Him' — the Vaishnavas as Krishna's own people, most dear to Him; worshiping them pleases Krishna more than anything else.
- **kṣetra** — The 'field' — the material body as the arena of the soul's activity (Bhagavad-gītā ch. 13).
- **kṣetra-jña** — The 'knower of the field' — the individual soul who observes and works within the body; the Supersoul is the super-knower present in every body.
- **sattva-guṇa** — The mode of goodness; when it predominates one gains the clarity to see things as they are and act beneficially.
- **vibhūti** — Krishna's opulent manifestations within the world — the light of the sun, the taste in water, the syllable Om — through which one learns to see Him in all things.
- **śāstra-cakṣus** — Seeing the world through the lens (eyes) of scripture rather than through one's own mind.
- **asat-tṛṣṇā** — Haridas Thakur's term for the intense thirst for temporary, flimsy things that are not good for us and can become addictive.
- **āśā-tṛṣṇā** — Irrational, intensely strong attachment-thirst that one cannot give up alone; transformed into detachment by devotee association and scriptural vision.
- **virakti** — Non-attachment to the temporary things of this world; arises as a natural by-product of hearing about Krishna, not as a separate austerity.
- **saṅga** — Attachment formed by contemplating material objects with a proprietary eye — the mechanism by which the subtle soul acquires a binding connection to matter.
- **īśāvāsya** — The Īśopaniṣad's opening conception: everything animate or inanimate is owned and controlled by the Lord, so accept only your set-aside quota as a temporary caretaker, not an owner.
- **itihāsa** — Vedic historical epics — notably the Mahābhārata, which contains the Bhagavad-gītā — through which Vyāsadeva explains the Upaniṣads accessibly.
- **Gītopaniṣad** — A name for the Bhagavad-gītā indicating it carries the essence of the Upaniṣads.
- **Vedānta-sūtra** — Vyāsadeva's aphoristic condensation of the Upaniṣads, on which the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam is his own natural commentary.
- **Purāṇa** — Vedic histories; the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam is called the king of the Purāṇas and the amala (spotless) Purāṇa.
- **amala** — Spotless — epithet of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam as the spotless Purāṇa.
- **pibata** — 'Drink' — the Bhāgavatam's invitation, taught by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, to drink its nectar through the ears from any position of life.
- **dharma, artha, kāma, mokṣa** — The four conventional Vedic goals — religiosity, wealth, sense enjoyment, and liberation — all of which the Bhāgavatam surpasses.
- **premā pum-artho mahān** — Pure love for God as the supreme goal of human life, beyond dharma, artha, kāma, and mokṣa.
- **mahā-mantra** — The great chant — Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma Rāma Rāma Hare Hare — on which all the Vedas converge; chanting it frees one from the faults of Kali-yuga.
- **Hare** — Address to the Supreme Lord's energy, Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī.
- **Kṛṣṇa** — The Supreme Personality of Godhead, addressed directly in the mahā-mantra.
- **Rāma** — In the mantra, the highest spiritual pleasure.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present degraded age; the Kali-Santaraṇa Upaniṣad states that chanting the mahā-mantra frees one from all its faults and obstacles.
- **jihvādau** — 'Beginning with the tongue' (Padma Purāṇa) — yoga and devotional service start by engaging the tongue in service, the one point of influence from which everything changes.
- **japa** — Individual meditative chanting of the mantra: sitting straight, eyes closed, listening to the sound and repeatedly bringing the mind back to it.
- **śravaṇam** — Hearing — the first and most important of the nine forms of prescribed devotional service; it must be received from Krishna directly or from His pure devotee.
- **brahma-bhūta** — The state of natural joyfulness for no external reason, free of lamentation and hankering, with felt unity and compassion toward all living beings.
- **Brahman / Paramātmā** — The impersonal spiritual effulgence and the localized Supersoul — partial realizations that one who takes directly to Kṛṣṇa consciousness automatically knows in full.
- **svayam eva sphuraty adaḥ** — 'It reveals itself of its own accord' — Krishna manifests Himself to one who serves by hearing His name attentively, rather than being grasped by one's own effort.
- **bhakti-yoga** — The yoga of devotion — hearing Krishna's name, pastimes, and form — which gives the mind its actual resting place and dissolves karma and attachment automatically, like food digested in the stomach.
- **seva** — Devotional service; attentive listening to the holy name is itself the service that invites Krishna's self-revelation.
- **līlā** — The Lord's pastimes, hearing of which is part of fixing the mind on Krishna.
- **Vāsudeva** — Krishna as the supreme goal upon whom one places full attention; after many births of study a rare soul recognizes Him and surrenders.
- **oṁ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya** — The mantra by which Dhruva Mahārāja, following Nārada Muni's instruction to fix his mind, attained complete spiritual perfection within months.
- **Viṣṇu-māyā** — The Lord's material energy, which Prahlāda Mahārāja feared as a great grinding wheel — the reason daily vigilance over the mind is required.
- **arcā-mūrti** — The deity form, not a replica but Krishna personally present with all His incarnations, to whom one can give full attention in bhakti-yoga.
