# Vaisesika Pr Happy Japa Keep Your Vow

## Theme

The lecture presents rigid vow-keeping as a great secret of spiritual advancement: chanting the Hare Krishna maha-mantra every single day without fail. It grounds this in the example of the six Goswamis of Vrindavan, whose saṅkhyā-pūrvaka practice meant rigorously counting their daily rounds and other devotional activities — showing that a vow is something quantified and tracked, not a vague intention. The arc then rises to the ultimate objective taught by Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and echoed in Srila Prabhupada's purport near the end of Bhagavad-gita chapter nine: continuous chanting without stoppage, which brings complete perfection. Until that constant chanting is achieved, the standard is at least sixteen carefully and rigidly chanted rounds daily, which removes all obstacles on the path — and attentive chanting itself becomes the remedy whenever anxiety or obstacles arise.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this by treating the daily japa quota as an unbreakable, counted vow in the spirit of saṅkhyā-pūrvaka: chant every single day without exception, keeping careful track of the rounds rather than approximating. Until continuous chanting becomes natural, complete at least sixteen rounds daily, chanted very carefully and with full attention. When anxiety or obstacles appear in life or practice, respond by chanting sixteen good, fully attentive rounds — giving oneself permission to prioritize japa above other demands. Practiced this way, the vow removes obstacles and advancement comes rapidly, while the long-term aim remains growing toward chanting without stoppage.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Personal meditative chanting of the holy name, typically counted on beads as daily rounds.
- **Hare Krishna maha-mantra** — The 'great mantra' of the holy names — Hare Krishna, Hare Rama — chanted daily as the core practice of Hari-Nāma.
- **saṅkhyā-pūrvaka** — Chanting 'preceded by counting' — keeping a fixed, quantified number of rounds and practices, as exemplified by the six Goswamis.
- **six Goswamis of Vrindavan** — The principal disciples of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu in Vrindavan, exemplars of rigorously counted, vow-based devotional practice.
- **rounds** — Units of japa; one round is one full pass of chanting the maha-mantra on a strand of japa beads, with sixteen daily rounds as the prescribed minimum standard.
- **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** — The avatar who inaugurated the congregational chanting of the holy name and taught continuous chanting as the ultimate objective.
- **purport** — Srila Prabhupada's explanatory commentary on a verse; here, the purport near the end of Bhagavad-gita chapter nine on chanting without stoppage.
