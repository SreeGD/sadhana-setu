# Vaisesika Pr Holy Name Sonicate Your Life 2017 08 13

## Theme

Speaking on Janmāṣṭamī, Vaiśeṣika Dāsa frames the festival day — a full day of nothing but hearing, chanting, and serving Krishna — as a direct taste of 'real life,' which śāstra defines as unbroken spiritual joy (ānandamaya), and draws the conclusion of the wise: if serving Krishna one day brings such bliss, serve Him every day. He grounds this in the definitions of faith (the firm conviction that by serving Krishna all misery is vanquished and all other duties are automatically fulfilled) and of pure devotional service (the favorable cultivation of activities to please Krishna, free of other desires), and in who Krishna is: the original source of all divine expansions, as Brahmā discovered when he stole the calves and cowherd boys, and the 'Self of the self' (ātmānam akhilātmanām) — the original soul who maintains all souls, and therefore the supremely lovable object of everyone's innate self-love. The great secret from Brahmā's prayers is to give up trying to grasp God by intellect and instead adopt a service attitude: stay in whatever situation you are in, but add hearing about Krishna, and life transforms. From there the lecture turns to time economics: hard work merely for material gain is śrama (useless toil condemned by the Bhāgavatam); one's allotted happiness and distress arrive automatically per Nārada Muni, so the only endeavor worth making is spiritual advancement, which no material condition can restrict. Since no lifespan is guaranteed and sleep, work, and commuting consume most of our hours, there is real urgency to identify the 'vital few' core practices that yield the greatest result, and to spiritualize even work and wealth by offering both to Krishna — for the mind follows the money, and Krishna promises (yoga-kṣemaṁ vahāmy aham) to maintain whoever gives Him undivided attention. The seminar's central analogy then arrives: sonication, the laboratory use of sound energy to agitate and transform matter, proves empirically that sound has real power over matter — and the transcendental sound of the holy name, given by Lord Chaitanya, likewise breaks the soul out of its 'frozen' material condition and reawakens its true nature. But right chanting requires more than mechanics: the mantra must be sounded with the right tone and motive, because tone reveals the heart, and ulterior motives (material gain, liberation, or the Māyāvāda idea of 'becoming Krishna') obscure or pervert the name's potency, just as milk touched by a snake's tongue turns poisonous. Hence the supreme importance of the source of hearing: sound from a pure devotee carries the 'saffron dust' of the Lord's lotus feet and awakens the sleeping soul — which is why Pṛthu Mahārāja rejected liberation and begged instead for a million ears to hear from pure devotees, and why one must avoid opening the heart to contaminated, Māyāvāda-tinged sources (testing every teacher against Krishna's own words in the Gītā). The lecture closes with the laboratory protocol for the experiment of chanting: the ten offenses as prescribed conditions, cooling down the 'heated' state of material desire (especially avoiding intoxication), daily sambandha-jñāna to realign one's identity as Krishna's servant, prasādam as part of the same purifying process, and a concrete home program — vowed, counted japa on 108 tulasi beads beginning with the Pañca-tattva mantra, family kirtan that 'sonifies' the whole house, and daily aloud reading of scripture. Even when the name's effect is imperceptible, like ultraviolet rays through clouds, it is working (nāmābhāsa) — Krishna, the source of all energies, arranged the material energy itself precisely so that living entities could vibrate spiritual sound and come fully alive.

## Practical application

Establish a legitimate daily home japa practice: keep a string of 108 sacred beads (ideally tulasi wood, representing the 108 gopīs with the head bead as Krishna) in a bead bag and treat them as sacred, since objects used in Krishna's service become spiritualized like an iron rod in fire. Sit each morning, begin with the Pañca-tattva mantra, and chant one full mahā-mantra per bead, rolling the beads so the chanting becomes a visceral, whole-body expression of the heart. Commit to a fixed minimum of rounds daily (initiated devotees vow at least sixteen) and complete them despite the mind's diversions — this vowed, counted chanting builds verifiable internal strength and integrity. Chant with full attention, whether aloud or mentally, because full attention earns full credit; and consciously tune the inner tone of the chanting, remembering the mantra's meaning — 'O my Lord, O energy of the Lord, please engage me in Your service' — since a sincere call for service, free of desires for gain, liberation, or 'becoming God,' is what attracts Krishna's complete mercy. Create 'laboratory conditions' for the name to act: follow the ten offenses as protocol, avoid intoxication and activities that overheat the mind, screen out material sounds while admitting spiritual sound, and strictly avoid hearing the name or Bhāgavatam from Māyāvādīs or motivated speakers, seeking instead the association of pure devotees. Pair chanting every day with sambandha-jñāna — study of one's relationship with Krishna as His servant — because sleep and the modes of nature misalign us daily and chanting alone, without this realignment, is not enough. Extend the practice through the day: read Bhagavad-gītā and Bhāgavatam aloud daily (svādhyāya) instead of consuming mundane news, gather family and friends for call-and-response kirtan that purifies the whole home, honor prasādam as part of the same purifying process, do your work as an offering by remembering Krishna as proprietor, enjoyer, and goal, and give a portion of your earnings to Krishna — the mind follows the money to His lotus feet, and the experience of still being maintained teaches realized dependence on Him. Finally, do not be discouraged when no effect is felt: educate yourself that even imperfect chanting (nāmābhāsa) is silently transforming you, like ultraviolet rays penetrating clouds.

## Glossary

- **ānandamaya** — Made of bliss; the śāstric definition of real life as unbroken spiritual joy.
- **śraddhā / viśvāsa** — Faith; defined by Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura as the firm conviction that by serving Krishna alone all misery is vanquished and all other duties are automatically accomplished.
- **bhaktir uttamā** — Pure devotional service; the favorable cultivation of activities to please Krishna, free from other desires and uncovered by speculative knowledge or fruitive work.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture; the authority consulted to define real life and identify the core practices of spiritual advancement.
- **ācārya** — An exemplary spiritual teacher who teaches by example; the ācāryas codified the definitions of devotion and the protocols for chanting.
- **Janmāṣṭamī** — The festival of Krishna's appearance day; presented in the lecture as a full-day taste of 'real life' — nonstop hearing, chanting, and serving Krishna.
- **Vāsudeva** — A name of Krishna; after many births, great souls realize Vāsudeva is the object of all service.
- **sampradāya** — An authorized disciplic succession; Brahmā, its founder, tested Krishna and realized Him to be the source of all expansions.
- **ātmānam akhilātmanām** — 'The Self of all selves'; Krishna as the original soul who expands and maintains all souls, making love for Him love for the soul of one's soul.
- **kṛṣṇa-kathā** — Topics or narrations about Krishna; adding their hearing to any situation of life transforms every cell of the body.
- **Brahman** — Spiritual existence and bliss, Krishna's own nature; one who serves Krishna unbrokenly rises above the three modes and becomes situated in it.
- **śrama** — Useless toil; hard work merely for material gain and sense gratification, emphatically condemned by the Bhāgavatam.
- **āśrama** — A life organized for spiritual advancement; explained by the speaker as 'a-śrama' — life with the useless labor trimmed away.
- **mahā-mantra** — The great chant for deliverance — Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma Rāma Rāma Hare Hare — meaning 'O my Lord, O energy of the Lord, please engage me in Your service.'
- **japa** — Individual chanting of the mahā-mantra counted on beads; a primary way to hear the holy name regularly.
- **kirtan** — Loud congregational call-and-response singing of the mahā-mantra; 'sonifies' the home, shaking out bad elements and rearranging its very ether.
- **Pañca-tattva mantra** — The mantra invoking Lord Caitanya and His four principal associates, chanted at the start of each japa session.
- **tulasi** — The sacred plant most dear to Krishna; the preferred wood for the 108 japa beads.
- **gopīs** — Krishna's cowherd-maiden devotees in Vraja; the 108 japa beads represent the 108 gopīs surrounding Krishna, with the head bead as Krishna.
- **hundi** — A temple donation box; used in the thought-experiment showing that the mind follows the money, so wealth given to Krishna draws the mind to His lotus feet.
- **yoga-kṣemaṁ vahāmy aham** — Krishna's promise in the Bhagavad-gītā to personally carry the maintenance and provision of anyone who worships Him with full, undivided attention.
- **sonication** — A scientific technique of applying sound energy to agitate particles in a sample; the seminar's central analogy for how the holy name's transcendental vibration breaks the soul out of its 'frozen' material state.
- **nāmābhāsa** — The dim or imperfect stage of chanting the holy name, which — per Haridāsa Ṭhākura in Harināma Cintāmaṇi — still purifies the chanter even when its effects are imperceptible, like ultraviolet rays through clouds.
- **Māyāvāda** — The impersonalist misconception of merging into or 'becoming' God; the one consciousness that blocks even the holy name's subtle rays, and a disqualifying contamination in a teacher.
- **māyā** — The Lord's illusory energy, under which the conditioned soul sleeps, serving the material world and forgetting his eternal service to the Lord.
- **ku-yogi / su-yogī** — A 'bad yogi' — every conditioned soul asleep under māyā — versus the perfect mystic; human life's duty is to rise from the former to the latter, chiefly by hearing from pure devotees.
- **sambandha-jñāna** — Knowledge of one's relationship with Krishna — that we are His servants, how the modes affect us, and that we have no legitimate relationship with matter; it must be taken in daily alongside chanting for the chanting to have full effect.
- **svādhyāya** — Daily study of scripture, read aloud; reciting Bhagavad-gītā and Bhāgavatam every day reprograms the intelligence from material fear to trust in Krishna's protection.
- **prasādam** — Food offered to Krishna; the 'added ingredient' in the purification process that transcendentalizes consciousness, complementing the disruption effected by hearing.
- **jīva jāgo** — 'Wake up, sleeping souls' — Lord Caitanya's call in Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's song, the awakening voice of the pure devotee.
- **harinama** — Public congregational chanting and distribution of the holy name; devotees who broadcast the name are protected by Krishna, and even those who hear or repeat it derisively receive benefit.
- **Gāyatrī** — A mantra chanted silently within the mind; the lecture affirms its 'sonification' effect operates even without vocal vibration, since subtle mental vibration is still vibration.
- **Bhāgavatam (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam)** — The foremost Purāṇa glorifying Krishna; the source of the lecture's teachings on śrama, Nārada's ledger of karma, Pṛthu Mahārāja's prayers, and the Personified Vedas' question of how spiritual sound acts within matter.
- **Ratha-yātrā** — The festival of pulling the Lord's chariot; the speaker's story of being sunburned on a cloudy Ratha-yātrā day illustrates how the name acts even when imperceptible.
