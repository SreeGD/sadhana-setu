# Vaisesika Pr Holy Name Krishnas Name is Eternal 2016 08 03

## Theme

Vaiśeṣika Prabhu builds the seminar as a single arc from epistemology to practice. He opens with Jīva Gosvāmī's Tattva-sandarbha: since direct sense perception is limited, knowledge of the spiritual world descends through sound — śruti-pramāṇa, hearing from reliable sources. The 'given' of spiritual life is an omniscient, omnipotent source who cannot be reached by intellect or scholarship but reveals Himself to humble receivers through the paramparā of submissive hearers. From there he defines perfection as constant attentiveness to one's eternal duty: citing BG 5.11 and Rūpa Gosvāmī's Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, one who engages body, mind, intelligence, and words in Krishna's service is jīvan-mukta — liberated while living — because intention, not the activity itself, decides bondage or liberation. He then addresses the persistence of material desire: like rivers entering the steady ocean (BG 2.70), desires continue from prārabdha-karma and lingering mental impressions, but the devotee's 'inclination' changes; only the niṣkāma Krishna-bhakta is peaceful, while seekers of bhukti, mukti, and siddhi remain restless on the bhoga-tyāga pendulum. A reading of SB 10.87.36 (Śruti-gīta) grounds this metaphysically: the world is real but temporary, and seeing anything as separate from Krishna is māyā, remedied by sambandha-jñāna. The seminar culminates in the holy name as the practical synthesis of everything preceding: since Krishna's name is non-different from Krishna, eternal, and invested with all His energies — a cintāmaṇi more valuable than a touchstone — constant chanting (harer nāma eva kevalam, the one way for Kali-yuga) is the means to remain the detached observer, sustain remembrance in all circumstances, and directly experience the Lord, closing with the 'Three P's' for attentive japa and the assurance that chanting guarantees the return to Krishna Loka.

## Practical application

Begin each japa session with the Three P's: Pronounce the mantra clearly with the lips so repetition never drifts into something other than the mantra; sit with upright Posture so the body signals the mind and intelligence that the practice is serious; and Pray — open with a sankalpa to Krishna and the Holy Name (for example, asking for the best association and the intelligence to use it), then lean into that prayer from the heart while chanting. Keep the mantra going through every phase of the day and of life — aloud or silently in the mind, even while carrying out prescribed work, since no one but yourself can stop you from chanting. When material desires and old mental 'tapes' surface during chanting, do not be discouraged or fight them as proof of failure: recognize them as prārabdha-karma and lingering mental impressions, observe them with detachment as the watcher ('they have nothing to do with me'), and refuse to chase them, letting the fullness of service leave no inclination for them — as a full man refuses more food. Support the chanting by regularly hearing sambandha-jñāna, as Haridāsa Ṭhākura recommends, so the mind's incline gradually turns toward Krishna. Give your intention, not just your activity, to Krishna: engage whatever assets you have — body, mind, intelligence, words, life, and wealth — in His service. Chant in the association of devotees and in public saṅkīrtana where possible, cultivate the mood of the chanter, and persevere through happy, distressful, and painful situations alike, confident that practice never goes in vain and that impressions accumulate across a lifetime — and even lifetimes — until the taste for the holy name awakens.

## Glossary

- **śruti-pramāṇa** — Evidence received through hearing from reliable sources; the means by which śāstra delivers infallible knowledge of the spiritual world beyond limited sense perception.
- **śāstra** — Revealed scripture, established in Jīva Gosvāmī's Tattva-sandarbha as the infallible source of knowledge of the spiritual world.
- **paramparā** — The disciplic succession of 'submissive hearers' who perfected themselves by hearing from their preceptors and passed the knowledge down under correct conditions on both sides.
- **bhakti** — Devotional service; the method of positioning oneself humbly as a receiver of God's self-revelation, and the process that actually quenches the soul's thirst.
- **bhakta** — A devotee of Krishna; alone among spiritualists he is peaceful, because his desires are directed at fulfilling Krishna's desires.
- **jīvan-mukta** — One liberated even while living in a material body — serving Krishna with body, mind, and words, no longer connected to the body as others are.
- **sad-ācāra** — Purified conduct; easy to perform when every action is done for Krishna's satisfaction and thus freed of material contamination.
- **sthita-prajña** — One of steady intelligence (BG 2.70) who, like the ocean receiving millions of rivers, remains undisturbed by the incessant flow of desires.
- **niṣkāma** — Desireless; the Krishna-bhakta alone is niṣkāma because his desires aim at fulfilling Krishna's desires, and he is therefore śānta (peaceful).
- **śānta** — Peaceful; the state of the niṣkāma devotee, in contrast to the restlessness of those desiring enjoyment, liberation, or mystic perfection.
- **bhukti** — Material enjoyment; traps one in the bhoga-tyāga pendulum in which enjoyment rebounds as suffering and craving returns after renunciation.
- **mukti** — Liberation; when sought for one's own relief it is a subtle self-interested desire that does not hold eternally — one falls back down, like a striking worker who must return to work.
- **siddhi** — Mystic perfection or yogic power; described as mere agitation that gives no peace to those who chase it.
- **bhoga-tyāga** — The pendulum of grasping enjoyment and then discarding it, visible even in children with toys — the fickle cycle of material desire the steady devotee transcends.
- **jñānī** — A salvationist philosopher; in the mirage analogy he recognizes the water is false and stops chasing, but still gets no water.
- **karmī** — A materialistic worker; in the mirage analogy he chases the mirage and drops dead still thirsty.
- **nārāyaṇa-para** — One dedicated to the Supreme Lord, able to serve anywhere — equal in heaven or hell — by fixing the mind on pleasing Krishna and the spiritual master and adapting to any environment.
- **acala** — Steady, unmoving; the quality of the devotee of BG 2.70 who transcends the restless nature of material desire.
- **dhyāyataḥ** — 'Contemplating' (BG 2.62): looking at an object with the sense 'there is something in it for me,' the step that invests ego in an object and converts it into entanglement.
- **saṅga** — Attachment; produced when contemplation makes a neutral object 'mine,' at which point it becomes a problem.
- **sambandha-jñāna** — Knowledge of one's relationship with Krishna, the world's relation to Him, and His ownership and control of everything; hearing it regularly while chanting changes the mind's incline.
- **sādhu-saṅga** — Association with devotees; part of the purification by which one's inclination changes so that objects and mental tapes remain but exert no pull.
- **prārabdha-karma** — The already-activated results of past material contact, which keep running as residual desires in the subtle body's mind even after one's inclination has changed.
- **pūrva-saṁskāra** — Impressions from prior practice that persist even across lifetimes, flooding back unbidden and inclining one to resume devotional practice where one left off.
- **ajāta-ruci rāgānugā sādhana-bhakti** — Devotional practice performed before spontaneous taste has awakened — the current stage of practitioners, which is why regular practice is recommended until impressions preponderate toward Krishna.
- **māyā** — Illusion: perceiving anything as separate from Krishna — not that things are unreal, but that missing their connection to Him makes one miss their relevance; the living being's misconception that changing material forms are permanent.
- **avidyā** — Ignorance; the unreal element in conjunction with which the universe arises from the eternal reality, as a rope plus ignorance produces the snake illusion.
- **sataḥ** — Sanskrit ablative 'from the eternal'; its grammar implies a definite separation of cause and effect, so what is created from sat must differ from it and be temporary (tarka-hatam — the opposing argument defeats itself).
- **Karma-mīmāṁsā** — The school of Jaimini claiming the world and the karmic cycle are eternal with no liberation — refuted by the Vaiṣṇava Upanishadic view that the created world is real but temporary.
- **gauṇa / lakṣaṇā** — Indirect and metaphorical semantic modes of the Vedas, by which statements promising eternal fruits of karma are praise of regulated sacrifice rather than literal fact.
- **cāturmāsya** — A four-month vow whose promised 'inexhaustible' merit is cited as an example of indirect, metaphorical Vedic praise rather than literally eternal reward.
- **cintāmaṇi** — A philosopher's touchstone that transforms whatever it touches; śāstra's description of the holy name, which is pure, complete in itself, eternally liberated, and possesses the sweetness one is actually seeking.
- **harer nāma eva kevalam** — The verse received by Caitanya Mahāprabhu from his spiritual master declaring that in Kali-yuga there is no other way than chanting the holy name — 'no other way' repeated three times.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present age; seen as beneficial by those who know life's essence, because all spiritual desires are fulfilled by the single activity of saṅkīrtana.
- **maha-mantra** — The Hare Krishna mantra of sixteen names and thirty-two syllables given in the Kali-Santarana Upanisad, meaning 'O my Lord, O energy of the Lord, please engage me in Your service.'
- **saṅkīrtana** — Chanting the Lord's names in the association of others, carrying special power — more still in public, where even passersby who hear the name receive its effect.
- **hari-nāma** — The holy name of the Lord, non-different from Krishna and invested with all His personal energies; per Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura it descends from the spiritual world (golokera prema-dhana) and is not material sound.
- **japa** — Individual mantra chanting; performed attentively through the Three P's — Pronounce, Posture, and Pray.
- **sankalpa** — A resolve or opening prayer made before chanting — for example, asking Krishna and the Holy Name for the best association and the intelligence to take advantage of it — into which one leans from the heart while chanting.
- **iha** — 'In this very life and body': liberation that follows from fixed service to guru and Krishna is perceivable now, verifiable by one's own experience.
- **dhāma** — A holy place of the Lord; a source of spiritual impressions that are never lost, though they may be temporarily eclipsed by daily life.
- **yātrā** — A devotional festival or pilgrimage gathering; organizing yatras is given as a way to engage the intelligence in Krishna's service.
- **Tulasī** — The sacred plant whose worship is given as a practical way to engage the body in Krishna's service.
