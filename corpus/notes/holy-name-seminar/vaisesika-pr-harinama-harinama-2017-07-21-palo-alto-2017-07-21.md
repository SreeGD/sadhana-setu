# Vaisesika Pr Harinama Harinama 2017 07 21 Palo Alto

## Theme

The lecture builds an arc from the philosophy of spiritual sound to its lived, public practice. It opens with the Vedic aphorism 'anāvṛttiḥ śabdāt' — transcendental sound vibration uncovers the self, so by contact with spiritual sound one easily discovers one's innate spiritual nature. The speaker then distinguishes two categories of sound: ordinary sounds belong to the closed system of the material universe described in the Bhagavad-gītā, whereas the mantra is a spiritual formula descending from a realm beyond that closed system. The Hare Krishna mahā-mantra is identified as the mantra specifically recommended for the present age, through which one can directly taste transcendence. This experience is self-verifying: as the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam's analogy of eating when hungry shows, the practitioner directly perceives nourishment and satisfaction rather than relying on faith alone. From this foundation the lecture turns outward — congregational kirtan is framed as an offering for the peace of the whole world, chanting Krishna's names invokes auspiciousness throughout the universe, purifies the heart, and makes peace. Saṅkīrtana is modeled as sustained, public, and freely shared: devotees chant through the entire night, repeatedly inviting everyone to come and bring friends, since the holy name is distributed to all without restriction. At the same time, the practice is not confined to temples or festivals — anyone can learn it and chant at home, with the promised fruits of peace of mind and the awakening of love for the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Nor is kirtan left to improvisation: there is a systematic way of learning it and teaching it to others. The lecture culminates in the image of the kirtan venue as a 'Kirtan Oasis' — a place where anyone may drink spiritual water descending from Goloka Vrindavan, the spiritual world itself made freely available through the holy name, with chanting kept going continuously across many locations.

## Practical application

A devotee applies this by treating daily japa and Hari-Nāma as contact with descending spiritual sound rather than ordinary noise — approaching the mahā-mantra as a formula from beyond the material universe that uncovers one's own spiritual nature. Practically: chant the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra regularly, since it is the specific recommendation for this age; expect and attend to direct experience, gauging progress by the felt nourishment and satisfaction the practice itself gives (as with eating when hungry), rather than by faith alone. Chanting need not wait for the temple or a festival — establish the practice at home, looking toward its promised fruits of peace of mind and awakening love for God. Learn kirtan systematically rather than improvising, and be willing to teach it to others. Finally, take the practice public in the saṅkīrtana mood: join congregational chanting and harinama as an offering for the peace of the whole world, keep the mood open and freely shared by inviting everyone without restriction, and where possible help sustain continuous chanting so the venue functions as an oasis where anyone can come and taste the holy name.

## Glossary

- **anāvṛttiḥ śabdāt** — Vedic aphorism cited to teach that liberation comes by transcendental sound — spiritual sound vibration uncovers the self and reveals one's innate spiritual nature.
- **mantra** — A spiritual formula in sound; unlike sounds produced within the closed material universe, it descends from a higher realm beyond that system.
- **mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' — the Hare Krishna mantra, specifically recommended for the present age, by chanting which one can directly taste transcendence.
- **Bhagavad-gītā** — Foundational Vedic scripture cited for the teaching that the material universe is a closed system, whose sounds relate only to its own affairs.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** — Vedic scripture cited for the analogy that spiritual practice is like eating when hungry — its results are directly perceived by the practitioner.
- **bhakti** — Devotional spiritual practice; presented as self-verifying, since one experiences one's own progress firsthand rather than taking it merely on faith.
- **kirtan** — Congregational chanting of the holy names, offered publicly for the peace of the whole world; learned and taught systematically, not left to improvisation.
- **saṅkīrtana** — Sustained, public, congregational chanting freely shared with all — exemplified by all-night chanting with open invitations, distributing the holy name without restriction.
- **harinama (Hari-Nāma)** — The holy name of the Lord and its public chanting; offered as a prayer for everyone that invokes universal auspiciousness, purifies the heart, and brings peace.
- **japa** — Individual, regular chanting of the mahā-mantra as daily practice, which can be done at home by anyone.
- **Goloka Vrindavan** — The supreme spiritual realm; the holy name is described as spiritual water descending from it, making the spiritual world freely available.
- **Supreme Personality of Godhead** — Krishna, our divine source; chanting his names awakens love for him within the heart.
- **Kirtan Oasis** — The speaker's image for a kirtan venue — an open place where anyone can come and drink the spiritual water of the holy name, with a standing invitation to all.
