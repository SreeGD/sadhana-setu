# Vaisesika Pr Holy Name Holy Name 2017 06 02

## Theme

The lecture builds a complete case for mantra meditation, moving from metaphysics to method to ultimate purpose. Vaiśeṣika Prabhu opens with the soul's identity: like a molecule of sunlight to the sun, the self is qualitatively one with Krishna but quantitatively minute, and yoga (from yuj, 'to connect') is the re-linking of that tiny conscious particle with its source. He surveys the yoga ladder — the mechanical discipline of aṣṭāṅga, Patañjali's culmination in Paramātmā-awareness — and distinguishes bhakti-yoga by its cultivation of gratitude and love, expressed above all in repeating the divine names. This brings him to the center of the talk: the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra taught by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu from the Kali-Santāraṇa Upaniṣad, whose three names form a prayerful cry to the all-attractive Lord, the reservoir of highest pleasure. The middle of the lecture confronts the universal obstacle — the restless, 'drunken monkey' mind Arjuna complains of in the Bhagavad-gītā — and presents the mantra as the uniquely effective remedy, because the name is non-different from Krishna himself: his qualities live within its syllables, so the sound entrances and transforms the mind. Anticipating the beginner's experience, he deploys Rūpa Gosvāmī's jaundice analogy (the sweet medicine tastes bitter until it cures the disease) and prescribes the practical apparatus of the tradition: determination, 'numerical strength' (a fixed daily count of mantras, traditionally on 108 japa beads), audible chanting with tongue and lips, short periods of exclusive attention, and abhyāsa — daily repetition that weaves filaments into an unbreakable rope. He grounds the practice in 'sonic theology': Vedic statements (anāvṛttiḥ śabdāt; Nārada Muni's teaching that the mantra is the accessible mūrti, seen 'without difficulty' — amūrtikam) that sound vibration reveals the full form of the Supreme, giving well-reasoned faith rather than blind belief, and invites empirical verification by contrasting a mantra-focused morning with a news-cycle morning. The promised by-products are jñāna (discriminating the higher spiritual energy from the lower material energy) and vairāgya (a non-reactive detachment that improves rather than abandons family life and relationships). The final movement widens the frame: Kali-yuga, though the worst of ages, carries a special dispensation in which chanting alone delivers; japa is the individual practice by which one learns to 'fly one's own plane'; and the deepest purpose of daily chanting is preparation for death — since death is only a change of awareness and the subtle body transmigrates according to consciousness, a lifetime of attentive japa equips one to leave the body successfully, to accompany and benefit the dying and departed, and ultimately, in perfected bhakti, to be reinstated in one's eternal spiritual body beyond birth and death.

## Practical application

Take up a small, fixed, daily japa vow and keep it without fail: choose a number of mantras you can actually sustain — even three to six daily — and chant them every day, ideally first thing in the morning, at least partly sitting down, counting on a string of 108 beads rolled between thumb and middle finger. Chant with tongue and lips, just loud enough to hear yourself, and make listening to the sound the whole task; do not substitute 'mental chanting' for the vowed practice. Carve out even three to five minutes of truly exclusive attention: switch off every device as on an airplane, suspend disbelief for that period, and give the mantra one-pointed focus. Expect the mind to wander within seconds — that is its nature — and measure success by the return of attention, not the absence of distraction; each time the drunken-monkey mind runs off, patiently bring it back to the mantra, training it like a horse-whisperer befriending a wild horse. Expect also that the name may taste flat or even bitter at first: like sugarcane juice to a jaundiced tongue, the medicine works before it tastes sweet, so continue with determination until a taste comes. Fortify the practice with prior research and study (so your faith is well-reasoned), good association with determined practitioners, and hearing about the mantra's importance. Prefer small quantity done deliberately, precisely, and consistently over large quantity done inattentively — like a mṛdaṅga student practicing rudiments slowly. Verify the results empirically: compare the consciousness of a day begun with attentive chanting against one begun with the news cycle, and watch for the unsought arrival of knowledge and detachment — clearer decisions, non-reactivity to daily anxieties, better relationships. Sustained over weeks and months, this daily discipline becomes second nature (abhyāsa), the mantra begins to carry you by its own power, and the practice matures into your personal, portable preparation for the final test of leaving the body.

## Glossary

- **yoga** — From the root yuj, 'to connect' — the process of re-linking the individual soul with its divine source; defined in the lecture as regulating the mind by fixing it exclusively on the higher, spiritual energy for a period every day.
- **aṣṭāṅga-yoga** — The 'eightfold' yoga discipline (sense control, moral living, āsana, meditation, etc.) — a largely mechanical system whose purpose is to turn awareness inward toward the self.
- **sthita-prajña** — Krishna's term in the Bhagavad-gītā for one fixed in consciousness: a person who gives up the unlimited desires flowing from the mind and finds satisfaction in the self alone.
- **Paramātmā** — The 'super spirit' (Prabhupāda's rendering) — the higher conscious being who maintains the individual soul; the culmination of Patañjali's yoga is awareness of Him.
- **bhakti-yoga** — The yoga of devotion, distinguished from other systems by its cultivation of gratitude and love as the forces of connection with Krishna, practiced above all through repeating the divine names.
- **mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' — the 16-word, 32-syllable Hare Krishna mantra recommended in the Kali-Santāraṇa Upaniṣad and taught by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, within which all of God's personal energies are said to be invested.
- **Hare** — The vocative, prayerful name in the mahā-mantra — an appeal meaning 'O my Lord.'
- **Krishna** — The name denoting the unlimitedly attractive nature of the Supreme; the original divine source of whom every soul is a minute part.
- **Rāma** — The name in the mahā-mantra denoting the highest pleasure.
- **mantra** — Literally 'deliverance of the mind' (man = mind, tra = to deliver): a sound whose repetition satisfies the mind and delivers it from the cascade of thoughts and desires.
- **japa** — Personal mantra meditation — repeating the mantra just loud enough to hear oneself, ideally in the morning; the speaker also gives the etymology ja = birth, pa = sin, the process for overcoming birth and accumulated karma.
- **dhyāna** — The meditational yoga system that Arjuna objects is impractical because the mind is strong, obstinate, and restless.
- **kirtan / sankirtana** — Congregational chanting of the holy names — the complementary public practice to japa; Śrī Caitanya's dispensation for the age is attained through chanting and sankirtana.
- **abhyāsa** — Repeated practice until it becomes second nature — the fortifying element of bhakti, likened to daily filaments accumulating into an unbreakable rope.
- **jñāna** — Knowledge — specifically the ability to distinguish the higher (spiritual) energy from the lower (material) energy; one of the two 'free gifts' of attentive chanting.
- **vairāgya** — Detachment — a soothing non-reactivity to everyday anxieties that arrives unsought through chanting practice; perspective rather than abandonment of duty.
- **mūrti** — Form (also 'difficulty') — the form of the Supreme, normally very difficult to fix the mind on, which can be worshipped through its sound representation, the mantra.
- **amūrtikam** — 'Without difficulty' ('a' negating mūrti in its sense of difficulty) — the scriptural promise that one who repeats the mantra will see the full form of the Supreme without difficulty.
- **anāvṛttiḥ śabdāt** — Vedic aphorism cited as 'sonic theology': through sound vibration one becomes liberated, uncovers the higher self, and meets one's divine source.
- **mano-gatān** — 'The mind goes' — the scriptural observation that the mind by nature runs off within seconds, so its wandering during meditation should not surprise the practitioner.
- **sukhī naraḥ** — 'A happy human' — the promised result of yoga practice, the regulation of the mind.
- **numerical strength** — The discipline of fixing a set number of mantras to chant attentively every day without fail, made possible because the mantra has a countable beginning and end.
- **japa beads** — A string of 108 beads used to count mantras, each rolled between thumb and middle finger; each bead represents one of Krishna's 108 principal associates in Goloka Vrindavana, with the head bead (never chanted on) representing Krishna himself.
- **Kali-yuga** — The current, worst age for spiritual life — its people short-lived, quarrelsome, and always disturbed — yet possessing the one great quality that simply chanting Krishna's names carries one across it.
- **Kali-Santāraṇa Upaniṣad** — The Upaniṣad that recommends the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra as the means of deliverance in Kali-yuga.
- **Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — The most recent incarnation of Krishna, who appears in Kali-yuga to give the chanting of the holy name as the age's special dispensation; the mahā-mantra descends through his line of teachers.
- **Rūpa Gosvāmī** — Direct disciple of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu who prescribes determination and regular practice until one gets a taste, and gives the jaundice analogy for the beginner's experience of the holy name.
- **Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura** — Bhakti teacher whose statement — that there is nothing to be had anywhere in the fourteen worlds except this mantra — convinces the mind that everything it seeks is already in the holy name.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** — The Vedic scripture (Bhāgavata Purāṇa) framed by Parīkṣit Mahārāja's question of what to do with seven days left to live; source of Nārada Muni's teaching on the mantra and the glorification of Kali-yuga's chanting dispensation.
- **Śrī Īśopaniṣad** — Upaniṣad cited for its conclusion that placing attention on the supreme cause of all causes yields one result, and attention on the non-supreme yields a categorically different one.
- **fourteen worlds** — The fourteen divisions of the universe in Vedic cosmography.
- **subtle body** — The mind together with the senses it groups (hearing, sight, smell, touch), which travels with the soul from one gross body to the next in transmigration.
- **prasadam** — Sanctified food offered to the Lord; offering it (with chanting) on behalf of the departed benefits their soul and gives closure to the bereaved.
- **mṛdaṅga** — A two-headed drum used in kirtan; the speaker's example of practicing rudiments slowly and precisely as the model for deliberate, careful chanting.
- **Goloka Vrindavana** — Krishna's supreme spiritual abode, where he is surrounded by his 108 most notable associates — the associates represented by the 108 japa beads.
