# Vaisesika Pr Japa Workshop Part 02 2017 07 23

## Theme

This second part of Vaiśeṣika Dāsa's japa workshop builds a complete case for chanting the holy name and then turns it into a hands-on method. It opens with Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, who appeared 500 years ago to give this complicated age a simple spiritual process, and grounds that process in 'sonic theology': sound carries consciousness, and purifying what we speak and hear is the basis of Krishna consciousness. The speaker insists this is testable, not dogma — one begins with provisional faith, as with a doctor's prescription, and lets experience ratify it. The mantra is presented as itself spiritual, an authorized combination of syllables descending from Krishna, so chanting is direct contact with the Lord. A central epistemological argument follows: our material senses are imperfect, so what lies beyond them is known through sound received from authorities — demonstrated live by phone calls to distant cities — and hearing is therefore the most complete sense in bhakti-yoga, which is why the Vedas are called śruti. This reception must come through the transparent agency of the bona fide spiritual master (dīkṣā-guru as the one giving the medicine, śikṣā-guru as the pharmacist explaining its use); otherwise the chanting is only noise. The lecture then pairs the mantra with the pāñcarātrika system — regulated eating, sleeping, and worship — as two tracks of one railway: the mantra connects one to Krishna while regulation keeps body and mind fit to chant. Spiritual 'codes' must be kept exactly as given, like a phone number, and the genuinely spiritual mantras are for those who have concluded material prosperity is not life's goal; in Kali-yuga, the age of quarrel, the Pañcarātra is more important than the Vedānta because it bypasses argument and cuts to the essence. One must avoid both blind faith and blind doubt, accepting axiomatic truth with intelligence — and even doubt proves consciousness exists. The final movement is intensely practical: chanting is 'forced meditation' whose sound captures the attention by its own potency, the classical yoga methods being impractical today. Japa is a personal discipline — fixed time, fixed quota on beads, whispered audibly — and a two-way communication with Krishna, who guides internally as Paramātmā and externally through guru and scripture. The mantra acts equally on everyone like sunshine; differences come from one's own covering, the ten offenses, chiefly inattention. Everything reduces to concentrated hearing of the sound, through which Krishna's form, qualities, and pastimes are revealed automatically. Three elements — clear pronunciation, upright posture, and a genuinely prayerful mood — transform the chanting from mechanical repetition into immediate experience, and the medicine of the holy name, taken daily, works on its own, yielding knowledge and detachment as beneficial side effects and making the mind a friend that says 'chant now'.

## Practical application

Take up japa as a fixed daily vow: choose a set time, choose a quota of mantras counted on 108 beads (or finger segments), and chant that same number every day — steadiness is what gives traction. Whisper the mantra just loud enough to hear yourself, and receive it from a bona fide source, chanting under the direction of guru and śikṣā guidance rather than by speculation. During the session, apply the three elements: pronounce every syllable clearly with the tongue, sit or stand slightly straighter to signal seriousness to the senses, and approach the mantra as prayer — really mean what you say, because sincere feeling changes the sound itself and brings the result immediately, while mechanical chanting delays it. Concentrate the mind exclusively on the sound; when attention drifts, slow down and chant more deeply and deliberately, and commit to sitting somewhat longer than the mind wants. Guard against the ten offenses, especially inattentive chanting and criticizing other devotees. Support the practice pāñcarātrika-style: regulate eating and sleeping (not too much or too little), avoid bad habits and addictions, keep good association, and maintain regulated worship — rising early, bathing, āratis at temple or home — since an organized life keeps one steady in chanting. Expect the mind to propose postponement; persist anyway, testing the process with provisional faith and letting the felt result — purification, loosening of attachments, unexpected satisfaction — confirm it. Add hearing and study of Bhagavad-gītā to fine-tune discrimination (a newcomer should read it cover to cover), restate what you hear to make it stick, join congregational kirtan whenever possible and never miss a chance to dance, and treat daily japa as your appointed time to communicate directly with Krishna.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Soft, individual repetition of a mantra, counted on beads; in the lecture's mnemonic etymology, 'ja' eradicates rebirth and 'pa' removes sinful reactions.
- **mantra** — An authorized combination of syllables descending from Krishna through a bona fide source; itself spiritual, giving direct access to the spiritual world.
- **mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' — the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra, glorified as 'param akṣarākṛte', the supreme combination of syllables, prescribed as the yoga for this age.
- **Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — Accepted in this line as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who appeared 500 years ago in Bengal to distribute the chanting of the holy name.
- **Pañca-tattva mantra** — The mantra to Śrī Caitanya and His associates, chanted before the mahā-mantra because Caitanya opens the door to chanting the holy name.
- **bhakti-yoga** — The yoga of devotion to Krishna, in which hearing is the most complete sense and chanting the essential practice.
- **yoga** — From the Sanskrit root yuj, 'to connect' — coming nearer to the divine source, Krishna; chanting the mantra is itself this connection.
- **sādhana** — Regulated daily spiritual practice; japa is called a portable sadhana that can be taken anywhere.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present age of quarrel and duality, for which chanting the holy name is the prescribed spiritual method.
- **pāñcarātrika / Pañcarātra** — The authorized codes of devotional service — regulated living and worship transmitted through Nārada Muni as a practical summary of the Vedas — which support chanting like the second rail of a train track.
- **śruti** — 'That which is heard' — a name for the Vedas, reflecting that transcendental knowledge is received through sound vibration.
- **dīkṣā-guru** — The initiating spiritual master, likened to the doctor who gives the medicine (the mantra).
- **śikṣā-guru** — The instructing spiritual master, likened to the pharmacist who explains how to use the medicine properly.
- **ācārya** — A teacher in the authorized succession who teaches by example and takes the seeker by the hand.
- **Paramātmā** — The Supersoul — Krishna's presence in every heart, guiding the seeker internally while guru and scripture guide externally.
- **Viṣṇu** — The Supreme Lord; the name means 'he who enters within everything', indicating His all-pervading presence.
- **Vāsudeva** — A name of Krishna; the Bhāgavatam prescribes bhakti-yoga to Vāsudeva, which yields knowledge and detachment as automatic side effects.
- **mūrti** — A Sanskrit word carrying the twofold meanings 'form' and 'difficulty' per the Amara-kośa dictionary.
- **amūrtikam** — With the privative a- reversing mūrti, explained by Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura as 'without difficulty' — the transcendental form is experienced easily through chanting.
- **Amara-kośa** — A classical Sanskrit dictionary, cited for the double meaning of mūrti.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** — The principal bhakti scripture, cited for the mantra granting direct contact with the deity, the rejection of materially motivated religion, and doubt itself proving consciousness.
- **Bhagavad-gītā** — Krishna's core teaching; studying it fine-tunes discrimination and prepares one to receive spiritual sound — recommended cover to cover for newcomers.
- **Upaniṣads** — Texts at the end of the Vedas giving information from the spiritual world to those tired of adjusting material life.
- **Vedānta** — The philosophical conclusion of the Vedas; for the modern age the practical Pañcarātra is said to be more important than the Vedānta.
- **varṇāśrama** — The Vedic social-spiritual order; Brahmā attained his post by following it perfectly for 100 lifetimes, becoming a pure conduit of spiritual sound.
- **Brahmā** — The creator of the material world, who created through sound — illustrating the creative power carried by spiritual sound.
- **Nārada Muni** — The sage through whom most of the Pañcarātra was given, transmitting a practical summary of the Vedas.
- **Hayagrīva** — The form of the Lord who re-spoke the Vedas after they were stolen, the divine origin of the Pāñcarātrika restoration.
- **Uddhava** — Krishna's associate who said that even with the lifespan of Brahmā he could not offer enough gratitude for the Lord's guidance.
- **kīrtana / saṅkīrtana** — Glorification of the Lord through chanting, especially congregational call-and-response singing and public chanting; one should never miss a chance to dance in it.
- **bhāgavata path** — The path of hearing and chanting about the Lord (as in chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa), established alongside Pāñcarātrika deity worship, which keeps one pure enough to chant.
- **ārati** — A ceremony of regulated deity worship performed in the temple or at home as part of the Pāñcarātrika daily program.
- **param akṣarākṛte** — 'The supreme combination of syllables' — a bhakti saint's glorification of the mahā-mantra from 500 years ago.
- **ten offenses** — The traditional impediments to chanting (e.g., inattention, criticizing devotees) that constitute one's own covering and account for differences in the mantra's experienced effect.
