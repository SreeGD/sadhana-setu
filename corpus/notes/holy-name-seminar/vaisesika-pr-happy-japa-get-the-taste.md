# Vaisesika Pr Happy Japa Get the Taste

## Theme

The lecture centers on a single arc: the highest attainment in life is developing a genuine taste for chanting japa of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra. Vaisesika Prabhu opens by declaring that one who gains this taste needs nothing else, and that life should therefore be organized around obtaining it. He then establishes the supremacy of this taste — chanted as a heartfelt prayer to the Supreme Lord, it renders wealth, power, beauty, desirable association, and even liberation and self-realization insignificant by comparison. The obstacle is then named: our senses are covered by material dross, so the real nectar of the Holy Name cannot be tasted immediately, which is precisely why daily practice is required — the taste develops over time. This daily-practice method is not novel but is the consistent instruction of Krishna himself, all the acaryas, and especially Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu and his leading disciples. The lecture culminates with Bhaktivinoda Thakura's assurance: if one can even once get this taste for the Holy Name, one's whole life changes and becomes perfect, after which everything else falls into place.

## Practical application

A devotee should treat obtaining the taste for the Holy Name as life's organizing priority, arranging daily affairs around japa rather than fitting japa around other pursuits. Practically, this means chanting Hare Krishna on beads every single day as a heartfelt prayer to the Supreme Lord — not as a mechanical exercise — and continuing without discouragement when taste does not come immediately, understanding that the senses covered by material dross need time and steady practice to perceive the nectar. The devotee can anchor this commitment in faith that daily practice is the method taught by Krishna, the acaryas, and Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu, and in Bhaktivinoda Thakura's promise that even once tasting the Holy Name will transform and perfect one's whole life, with everything else falling into place afterward.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Private, meditative chanting of a mantra, typically counted on beads; here, the daily chanting of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra.
- **Hare Krishna maha-mantra** — The 'great mantra' composed of the holy names Hare, Krishna, and Rama, chanted as a prayer to the Supreme Lord.
- **Holy Name (Hari-Nāma)** — The name of God, considered non-different from the Lord himself; the object whose 'nectar' or taste the chanter seeks to awaken.
- **acarya** — A spiritual teacher who instructs by personal example; the lecture cites all acaryas as teaching daily practice to awaken the taste for chanting.
- **Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu** — The 15th–16th century avatar and founder of the sankirtana movement who, with his leading disciples, especially taught the practice of daily chanting to develop taste for the Holy Name.
- **Bhaktivinoda Thakura** — A prominent 19th-century Vaishnava acarya, quoted as teaching that gaining the taste for the Holy Name even once makes one's whole life perfect.
- **liberation** — Release from material existence (mukti); named in the lecture as insignificant compared to the taste of chanting the Holy Name.
