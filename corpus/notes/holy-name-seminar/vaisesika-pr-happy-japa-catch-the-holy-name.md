# Vaisesika Pr Happy Japa Catch the Holy Name

## Theme

This short lecture builds a single sustained metaphor: japa is like fishing. Just as an angler patiently casts a shiny lure past disinterested fish again and again until one finally bites, the chanter repeatedly brings the Holy Name past the disinterested mind, inviting it to look and catch on, until the mind is hooked by the Name. The lecture grounds this in scriptural glorification of Krishna's all-attractive beauty — a bluish lotus for the ear, ointment for the eyes, a necklace of indranīla gems for the chest, the universal ornament of the gopīs — to establish that the Lord, and therefore His Name (which is non-different from Him), is supremely attractive to every sense. From these two points the conclusion follows: since nothing is more attractive to the mind and senses than the Holy Name, the chanter's only real task is the angler's patience. Persistence, not immediate interest from the mind, is what distinguishes success, and for the persistent chanter the Name's capture of the mind is guaranteed.

## Practical application

In daily japa, take the role of the patient angler rather than expecting the mind to be interested from the first mantra. Each round, keep 'casting' the Holy Name past the mind — bringing the mind's attention back to the sound again and again without frustration when it wanders or stays indifferent. Do this day after day as a steady practice, trusting that because the Name is the most attractive object that exists, repeated exposure will inevitably hook the mind's attention. Success in Hari-Nāma is therefore reframed as happy, confident persistence: the chanter supplies the daily patience; the Name itself supplies the attraction that eventually captures the mind.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Soft, meditative recitation of the Lord's Holy Names, typically counted on beads as a daily practice.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The Holy Name of the Lord (Hari), understood to be non-different from the Lord Himself.
- **indranīla** — Sapphire; a deep-blue gem, here evoked in a necklace to describe Krishna's beauty adorning the chest.
- **gopīs** — The cowherd maidens of Vraja, Krishna's most intimate devotees, for whom He is described as the universal ornament.
