# Vaisesika Pr Holy Name Holy Name is Fully Potent 2017 07 08 UK

## Theme

The lecture builds a complete case for the holy name as the sole fully potent spiritual process of Kali-yuga, and then shows how to approach it. It opens with Jīva Gosvāmī's instruction that every activity in bhakti-yoga should begin and end with chanting, and grounds this in the example of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, who — though Kṛṣṇa Himself — enacts ācārya-līlā by taking initiation from Īśvara Purī and receiving the harer nāma verse, an absolute, unhedged scriptural declaration ('harer nāma' three times, 'eva', 'kevalam', 'nāsty eva' three times) that there is no other way in this age. The speaker frames this within the yuga system of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam: each age has its yuga-dharma, and for Kali-yuga — only 5,000 years into 432,000, with conditions already grim — it is chanting. The lecture then turns to epistemology: such knowledge comes not by the empiric, ascending method but by the descending Vedic method, which requires the humility of an open vessel and a concrete system — approaching a self-realized teacher with praṇipāta (submission), paripraśna (relevant inquiry), and sevā (unmotivated service), which opens the heart of a great soul so its contents flow to the servant. This is why kirtan follows a protocol: praṇati to Śrīla Prabhupāda (whose praṇati doubles as his mission statement — gaura-vāṇī-pracāriṇe, and the defeat of impersonalism and voidism in the West), then the Pañca-tattva mantra, and only then the mahā-mantra — because the mantra is not a symbol of the deity but the deity himself, so chanting is direct contact, and one's credential is one's line of teachers, never self-manufactured expertise. Against the impersonalist and voidist conclusion that spiritual life means no variety and no relationship, the speaker offers the number-line analogy: karmic life is living on credit, voidism only brings one to zero, while bhakti is life in the positive numbers, rich with unlimited, never-degrading spiritual variety. The lecture closes with the holy name's independent potency in public: the mahā-mantra is felt without being understood, acts regardless of the hearer's qualification (Rūpa Gosvāmī), and awakens bhakti even in the unprepared — demonstrated by harinama sankirtana in city centers, Prabhupāda's transplanting of Ratha-yatra to San Francisco where street dancing became police-escorted rather than police-dispersed, and the speaker's own testimony that a childhood glimpse of chanters near Berkeley planted an impression that later bore fruit.

## Practical application

Begin and end every spiritual practice — japa, worship, any sacrifice or devotional function — with chanting the holy names, so that the practice yields its full fruit. Follow the standard sequence when approaching the mahā-mantra: first the Prabhupāda-praṇati, then the Pañca-tattva mantra, then Hare Krishna, understanding this protocol exists because the mantra is the deity himself and chanting is direct audience with Him. Receive the name through the line of teachers rather than self-invention: cultivate the descending method by approaching realized devotees with submission (praṇipāta), questions that actually matter to your spiritual life (paripraśna), and service with no expectation of return (sevā) — and test in your own experience that such service opens a great soul's heart. Know your mission as Prabhupāda knew his, or you will meander. Do not wait to 'understand' the mantra intellectually — the name acts independently of qualification, so chant and let it be felt. Counter material disappointment by becoming intrigued with the unlimited spiritual variety described in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and Caitanya-caritāmṛta, rather than by negating variety altogether. And take the name public: harinama sankirtana, done properly (with permits, with genuine happiness), is a legitimate, welcomed experiment anyone can run — even brief, uncomprehending contact with the holy name leaves an indelible impression on hearers and passersby.

## Glossary

- **bhakti-yoga** — The path of devotional service to Kṛṣṇa; in this lecture, the framework within which every activity should begin and end with chanting the holy names.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present, fourth cosmic age — 432,000 years long, of which only 5,000 have passed — marked by degraded conditions; its prescribed spiritual method is chanting the holy name.
- **yuga-dharma** — The method of spiritual advancement scripture prescribes for a particular age; for Kali-yuga it is chanting the holy names.
- **ācārya** — A teacher who teaches by personal example; 'ācārya-līlā' is Caitanya Mahāprabhu's pastime of acting as such a teacher by accepting and strictly following His own guru.
- **harer nāma** — The verse Īśvara Purī gave Caitanya Mahāprabhu, repeating 'the name of Hari' three times and declaring — with the intensifiers 'eva' and 'kevalam' and the triple 'nāsty eva' ('there is no other way') — that chanting is the only means of advancement in Kali-yuga.
- **praṇipāta** — Submitting oneself to a self-realized teacher; the first of three practices for approaching such a teacher per the Bhagavad-gītā.
- **paripraśna** — Relevant inquiry — asking questions that actually matter for one's spiritual life, as opposed to idle curiosity.
- **sevā** — Rendering service; when unmotivated (wanting nothing in return), it opens the heart of a great soul so the secrets of devotional service flow to the servant.
- **praṇati / praṇāma-mantra** — Prayers of respectful obeisance offered before kirtan — here to Śrīla Prabhupāda — which in his case also state his mission.
- **gaura-vāṇī** — The teachings of Caitanya Mahāprabhu; described as the most valuable substance obtainable anywhere, distributed freely. 'Gaura-vāṇī-pracāriṇe' means 'preacher of those teachings,' from Prabhupāda's praṇati.
- **Pañca-tattva** — Literally 'five truths': Caitanya Mahāprabhu with Nityānanda, Advaita, Gadādhara, and Śrīvāsa; their mantra is chanted before the mahā-mantra.
- **gaura-bhakta-vṛnda** — The assembly of devotees of Lord Caitanya — invoked because God is never alone but always accompanied by His energies and devotees, like a king with his retinue.
- **eko bahu syām** — 'The one becomes many' — God expands Himself into many features for the joy of variety, especially the variety of relationships.
- **mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' — Hare Krishna Hare Krishna Krishna Krishna Hare Hare / Hare Rama Hare Rama Rama Rama Hare Hare — a prayer that can be felt without understanding its language and that acts independently of the hearer's qualification.
- **nirviśeṣa-śūnyavāda** — Impersonalism ('no variety/attributes') and voidism ('nothingness') — philosophies arising from projecting material disappointment onto spiritual life; per his praṇati, Prabhupāda's mission specifically defeats them as misleading.
- **karma** — Reaction accrued through material activity; in the lecture's number-line analogy, karmic life is living on credit — apparent prosperity while actually accruing debt.
- **harinama sankirtana** — Public congregational chanting of the holy names, taken into city centers as a legitimate, civically welcomed practice and an experiment anyone can run.
- **Ratha-yatra** — Jagannath Puri's ancient chariot festival enacting Lord Jagannatha's journey to Vrindavan, transplanted by Prabhupāda to San Francisco in the late 1960s.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** — The scripture that divides time into four yugas with their prescribed methods, and that describes unlimited spiritual variety in detail.
- **Caitanya-caritāmṛta** — The biography and theology of Caitanya Mahāprabhu, which elaborates the science and protocol for approaching the mantra and describes spiritual variety.
