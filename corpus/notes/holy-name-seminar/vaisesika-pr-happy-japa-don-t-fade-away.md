# Vaisesika Pr Happy Japa Don't fade away

## Theme

This lecture warns against 'japa fade' — the gradual, degree-by-degree diversion of attention that leads first to poor chanting, then to not chanting at all, and finally to losing one's grip on strict spiritual life. The remedy is daily vigilance: approach each morning's japa as a beginner who needs every second of chanting to stay spiritually strong, because all practices fade without proper attention. Attention itself is unpacked as giving special care and, from its root 'to stretch,' stretching the mind to hear the holy names. A concrete technique anchors this: deliberately moving the lips to pronounce each syllable of the mahā-mantra, which restores attention even when taste is absent — and taste will follow. The lecture culminates with Prabhupāda's purport to Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 2.1.12: by chanting the Lord's name one receives 'all the stipulated energy synchronized from all sources' — while chanting, one is in the right place at the right time and everything auspicious unfolds, so all the happiness the mind seeks across the universe is already available within the holy name.

## Practical application

Treat every day's japa as if you were a new chanter who cannot afford a single inattentive second — never assume yesterday's good chanting carries over, since fading happens by degrees and is easiest to stop early. During japa, actively stretch the mind toward hearing the holy names, and use the physical discipline of moving the lips to pronounce each syllable of Hare, Krishna, and Rama properly; do this even on days when taste for chanting is weak, trusting that attentive pronunciation restores attention and that taste will come. When the mind wanders in search of happiness elsewhere, tell it directly that everything auspicious and all the happiness it seeks are already present within the holy name, and that while chanting you are in exactly the right place at the right time.

## Glossary

- **japa** — Soft, meditative recitation of the holy names, typically the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra chanted on beads as a daily practice.
- **mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' — Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare / Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare — composed of the names Hare, Krishna, and Rama.
- **Hari-Nāma** — The holy name of the Lord (Hari); the practice of chanting God's names.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (SB)** — The Bhāgavata Purāṇa, a principal Vaiṣṇava scripture; SB 2.1.12 is cited here on the potency of chanting the Lord's name.
- **purport** — Śrīla Prabhupāda's explanatory commentary accompanying his translations of scriptural verses.
- **Prabhupāda** — A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda, founder-ācārya of ISKCON, whose commentary is cited in the lecture.
