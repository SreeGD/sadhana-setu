# Vaisesika Pr Japa Japa 2009 12 20

## Theme

Vaiśeṣika Prabhu builds the lecture as a complete case for making the chanting of the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra the deliberate 'plan' for one's spiritual health, just as one plans for physical health or wealth. He opens with what the holy name is and why it works: the sound descends from the spiritual stratum and cleanses the heart, and the rare human body — won like a lottery after millions of births — is specifically designed to receive it. He then qualifies how it must be received: the effect depends on who chants (Sanātana Gosvāmī's warning that the name from those outside pure devotion is like milk touched by a serpent's lips), so one chants in the association of pure devotees. Drawing on Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Rūpa Gosvāmī, he presents sankīrtana as the only means to extinguish the material 'forest fire,' the bestower of all good fortune and of knowledge and detachment, and the source of the only positive, ever-expanding happiness — Krishna personally present in His names — above both material pleasure and impersonal Brahman relief. The middle of the lecture turns intensely practical: karma-yoga (renouncing the fruits of work) frees the mind to hear; japa has a warm-up arc in which tongue, ear, and mind gradually coordinate until, by eleven or twelve rounds, the senses come under control; the name, invested with all of Krishna's energies, delivers the heart's actual desire that the mind has chased life after life. The proof is experiential — 'the proof of the pudding is in the tasting' — one hears God before seeing Him, and the unprecedented sound makes one want to dance, because the treasure of divine love descends from Goloka as the holy name. He prescribes a fixed daily time and place, gradual increase from even ten minutes, and daily Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam reading, framing chanting through Ṛṣabhadeva's teaching as the prescribed tapo divyam (divine austerity) for Kali-yuga, received through disciplic succession rather than self-invented, whose reward is release from the 'sentence' of material labor and the devotee's refusal to waste another moment (avyartha-kālatvam). The final movement integrates chanting with ordinary life: spontaneous remembrance of Krishna during japa is mercy, not distraction; what enters the ears is what surfaces in the mind, so the yogi guards his hearing; Bhagavad-gītā 8.7 shows duty and remembrance combined by working for Krishna and offering the fruits — even a portion of one's earnings — which pulls the mind toward Him all day. Chanting built into ordinary life becomes one's shelter in crisis (illustrated by a physician chanting while saving a dying infant), though, as Prahlāda teaches, no counter-measure works without the Lord's sanction. The lecture closes at the 'final examination' — remembering the Lord at death, possible only through lifelong practice — and with concrete beginnings: small weekly temple seva, bead bag, japa-mala and counter beads, and a personal vow of a manageable number of daily rounds increased gradually until one is firmly fixed in yoga.

## Key teachings

- **[00:00:30.260]** Just as health or wealth requires a deliberate plan, spiritual health requires a plan, and according to the ācāryas its core must be chanting the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra — the simple process for the highest benefit in Kali-yuga — done as frequently as possible, ideally constantly (kīrtanīyaḥ sadā hariḥ). It is easy and universal: chant with guests at home, chant publicly so others can hear, chant aloud when alone; chanting japa on beads takes more qualification and concentration, and renouncing the fruits of one's daily work is what lets the mind settle into introspection when one sits to chant (referencing Arjuna's question about how the person fixed in consciousness sits and walks).

- **[00:00:58.480]** Chanting the holy name produces many effects simultaneously, the first being cleansing of the heart, because the sound vibration descends from a different stratum — the spiritual world. The soul can feel this sound in any body (even in a coma, or as a tree, plant, or bug), what to speak of the human form, which is specifically designed to hear Vedic sound — and the essence of all Vedic sound is the holy name. This rare human body, awarded after transmigration through millions of species, is thus a 'lottery win' meant for contacting the holy name.

- **[00:02:48.400]** The quality and effect of the chanted sound differ according to who is chanting: hearing the name from someone with a wrong conception (that 'I am Krishna,' or that Krishna is the same as Durgā or Gaṇeśa) is entirely different from hearing it from one who knows himself as the Lord's eternal servant. Sanātana Gosvāmī therefore instructs not to hear chanting from those outside the line of pure devotion — as milk, though nourishing, becomes poisonous when touched by a serpent's lips — so one should chant in the association of pure devotees, following in their footsteps.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
- **[00:03:50.060]** Sankirtan is the only means to extinguish the great forest fire burning throughout the material world — our own bodies begin burning themselves out from birth, every cell passing through its lifespan. Caitanya Mahāprabhu teaches that sankirtan also invokes all good fortune (opening the 'white lotus' of auspiciousness) and is the 'wife of transcendental knowledge': by associating with sankirtan one automatically gains knowledge of the difference between matter and spirit and how to act, along with detachment — the principle that knowledge and vairāgya come as concomitants of serving Krishna.

- **[00:05:33.800]** Rūpa Gosvāmī's gradation of happiness (Nectar of Devotion): material happiness from sense contact is no happiness at all — it has a beginning and an end and is a source of misery, so the intelligent do not delight in it; the happiness of impersonal Brahman is only negative relief from material activity, with no positive taste; positive, ever-expanding bliss comes only from association with Krishna, who is personally present in His holy names. One who chants feels Krishna's protection and takes shelter of His lotus feet rather than of the material world, where every step holds danger.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 10

- **[00:07:26.680]** The sum and substance of bhakti is understanding that Krishna is the Supreme Personality of Godhead and that He reciprocates offered love: He accepts simple offerings — a leaf, flower, fruit, or water — when given with devotion. The best leaf is tulasī, glorified throughout śāstra (Hari-bhakti-vilāsa): whoever keeps, waters, sees, or speaks of Tulasī is benedicted, and Krishna accepts no offering without her. Through such constant loving offering — of service, food, and the fruits of one's work — Krishna, though far away, becomes very near, and this reciprocation is the real source of happiness that sankirtan revives.
    - BG 9.26: *patraṁ puṣpaṁ phalaṁ toyaṁ yo me bhaktyā prayacchati tad ahaṁ bhakty-upahṛtam aśnāmi prayatātmanaḥ* — If one offers Me with love and devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, I will accept it.

- **[00:10:00.000]** Acting in karma-yoga — giving up the fruits of one's work — is what frees the mind to actually concentrate and hear the holy name; on that basis one takes up the given standard of chanting 16 rounds, and simply sitting and chanting with nothing else is deeply satisfying.
    - BG 2.47: *karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana | mā karma-phala-hetur bhūr mā te saṅgo 'stv akarmaṇi ||* — You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but not to the fruits of action. - **[00:10:28.360]** Japa has a warm-up arc: the first rounds take longer because the mind, tongue, and hearing must become coordinated; around six rounds the tongue becomes engaged and 'happy', momentum builds, and the mind — seeing the tongue tasting something — becomes curious, attracted, and starts to listen. The name of Krishna contains all nectar, which is why Rūpa Gosvāmī, chanting, desired millions of ears because two are not enough to hear it.

- **[00:12:07.580]** Sitting and chanting is itself a very powerful yoga: by 11–12 rounds the senses come under control just by hearing the chanting. The mind travels place after place, life after life, through the whole universe thinking 'maybe I could be happy here', and the search never ends until one contacts Krishna — and the way to contact Him is chanting the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra.

The material body is an embarrassment for the soul, who is not of this world and not meant to be profaned by material energy; chanting Hare Krishna in sankīrtana clears that embarrassment, awakens realization of one's true non-material nature, begins one's separation from this world, and grants the heart's desire — the real fortune everyone is searching for, freely given by Lord Caitanya.

Anyone, in any place and time, who takes this even a little seriously makes great advancement.

## Practical application

Make chanting a planned, daily discipline rather than an occasional practice. Fix a specific time and place each day, sit alone (or with one or two serious chanters), keep the mind from wandering to the window or the computer, and chant japa just loud enough to hear the name — no imagination is required, simply let the sound enter the ear. Begin at a manageable level — even ten minutes — and take a personal vow of a fixed number of rounds, increasing gradually toward the standard of sixteen; use a bead bag, japa-mala, and counter beads, pulling one counter bead down per round to keep count. Expect the warm-up arc: the first rounds are slower while tongue, hearing, and mind coordinate; around six rounds the tongue becomes 'happy' and the mind starts to listen, and by eleven or twelve rounds the senses settle just by hearing — so don't judge the session by its opening minutes. Support the japa with the rest of the day: perform your prescribed duties while remembering Krishna (Bhagavad-gītā 8.7), and offer the fruits of your work — for example, sponsoring deity bhoga or flowers or donating a portion of earnings to the temple or outreach — so the mind, which follows its attachments, is pulled toward Krishna during working hours. Guard what enters your ears, since what you hear is what surfaces during chanting: read Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and Bhagavad-gītā daily instead of feeding on news media, and chant in the association of pure devotees rather than hearing the name from those with wrong conceptions of Krishna. Chant beyond the beads too — with guests at home, aloud when alone, and publicly so others can hear, since simply hearing the name begins others' attraction to Krishna. Take up even a small direct service (ten to fifteen minutes a week at the temple) to 'put your foot in the door,' and offer simple things — a leaf, flower, fruit, or water, best with tulasī — with devotion. When images of Krishna's pastimes or holy places arise during japa, continue chanting and remembering; that is mercy, not static. Chant continuously in ordinary life so that the habit surfaces in crisis, do your best in all endeavors while depending on Krishna for the result, and keep the ultimate aim in view: lifelong remembrance so that you can remember the Lord at the moment of death.

## Glossary

- **mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' — the Hare Krishna mantra, prescribed by the ācāryas as the core spiritual practice and simplest process for the highest benefit in Kali-yuga.
- **kīrtanīyaḥ sadā hariḥ** — 'The Lord (Hari) should be chanted always' — Lord Caitanya's instruction that the holy name be chanted as constantly as possible.
- **japa** — Soft, private chanting of the holy name on beads, done just loud enough to hear oneself; requires more concentration and qualification than congregational chanting.
- **japa-mala** — The string of chanting beads on which rounds of the mahā-mantra are counted.
- **sankīrtana (harinām-saṅkīrtana)** — Congregational chanting of the holy names; taught by Caitanya Mahāprabhu as the only means to extinguish the material 'forest fire,' the invoker of all good fortune, and the prescribed process for this age.
- **Kali-yuga** — The present degraded cosmic age, for which the chanting of the holy name is the prescribed and fully potent spiritual method.
- **ācārya** — A spiritual teacher in disciplic succession who teaches by example; the ācāryas prescribe chanting as the core of one's spiritual plan.
- **bhakti** — Devotional service to Krishna; its sum and substance is understanding that Krishna is the Supreme Personality of Godhead and that He reciprocates offered love.
- **vairāgya** — Detachment from matter; together with transcendental knowledge, it comes automatically as a concomitant of serving Krishna through sankīrtana.
- **Brahman** — The impersonal spiritual effulgence; its happiness is described by Rūpa Gosvāmī as merely negative relief from material activity, lacking positive taste.
- **tulasī** — The sacred plant most dear to Krishna; the best leaf to offer Him, glorified in śāstra — Krishna accepts no offering without her, and whoever keeps, waters, sees, or speaks of Tulasī is benedicted.
- **śāstra** — Revealed Vedic scripture, the standard by which genuine spiritual practice and austerity must be prescribed, as opposed to self-invented methods.
- **Hari-bhakti-vilāsa** — A devotional scripture (compiled in the line of Sanātana Gosvāmī) cited for the glorification of Tulasī.
- **karma-yoga** — Acting in the world while giving up the fruits of one's work; this renunciation frees the mind to settle into introspection and truly hear the holy name.
- **yuj / yoga** — Literally 'to connect'; being a yogi means connecting with Krishna daily, which the lecture identifies with attentive chanting and remembrance.
- **abhīṣṭa-doham** — The bestowing of one's actual heart's desire — possible through the holy name because all of Krishna's personal energies are invested in His name.
- **golokera prema-dhana, hari-nāma-saṅkīrtana** — 'The treasure of divine love from Goloka' — the song verse teaching that love of God descends from the spiritual world into this world as the chanting of the holy name.
- **Goloka** — The topmost spiritual planet, Krishna's own abode, from which the holy name descends as the treasure of divine love.
- **tapo divyam** — 'Divine austerity' — Ṛṣabhadeva's instruction (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 5.5.1) that human life is meant for purifying austerity leading to unlimited spiritual happiness, not sense gratification.
- **brahma-saukhya** — Unlimited, eternal spiritual happiness — the result of divine austerity, contrasted with temporary sense pleasure available even to animals.
- **avyartha-kālatvam** — The advanced devotee's symptom of not wasting time: regret at lifetimes already lost and refusal to waste another second, closing the senses to all but transcendental sound.
- **ante nārāyaṇa-smṛtiḥ** — Remembrance of the Lord (Nārāyaṇa) at the end of life — the 'final examination,' passable only through lifelong practice of remembering Krishna.
- **seva** — Direct devotional service, such as cleaning the temple or helping outreach; even 10–15 minutes a week helps one remember Krishna.
- **bhoga** — Food offerings prepared for the Deity; sponsoring bhoga or flowers is a way to offer the fruits of one's work and pull the mind toward Krishna.
- **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam** — The 'sublime literature' descended from the spiritual world; daily hearing of it, like chanting, gives direct experience of Krishna's presence.
- **Sanātana Gosvāmī** — Direct disciple of Caitanya Mahāprabhu who instructs devotees not to hear the holy name from those outside the line of pure devotion, as milk touched by a serpent's lips becomes poisonous.
- **Rūpa Gosvāmī** — Direct disciple of Caitanya Mahāprabhu; author of the gradation of happiness cited from Nectar of Devotion, who desired millions of ears to hear the nectar of Krishna's name.
- **Caitanya Mahāprabhu** — Krishna appearing as His own devotee, who freely distributed the chanting of the holy name; Karabhajana Muni directs meditation to His lotus feet.
- **Īśvara Purī** — The spiritual master from whom Caitanya Mahāprabhu received the teaching of harinām, illustrating that potent austerity comes through disciplic succession.
- **Karabhajana Muni** — One of the nine Yogendras of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, who directs meditation toward Lord Caitanya as the teacher of the chanting process for this age.
- **Prahlāda Mahārāja** — The great devotee who teaches that no counter-measure — a boat for a drowning man, treatment for a dying patient — can save anyone without the Lord's sanction.
- **Ṛṣabhadeva** — An incarnation of the Lord whose instruction to His sons (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 5.5.1) defines human life as meant for divine austerity rather than sense gratification.
