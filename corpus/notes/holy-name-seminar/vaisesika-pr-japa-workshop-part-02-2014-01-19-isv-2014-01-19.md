# Vaisesika Pr Japa Workshop Part 02 2014 01 19 ISV

## Theme

This second part of Vaiśeṣika Prabhu's japa workshop presents chanting the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra as a time-tested method to mold the mind to higher levels of perception, discipline, and contentment — the triad that frames the whole talk. It opens by contrasting the inherent limits of material research with spiritual science, which begins in humility and surrender to the Supreme Lord, and grounds everything in Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu's two-principle movement: chant Hare Krishna and teach others. The mind is diagnosed as a restless monkey that disintegrates when fed temporary pleasures (which Krishna defines as having a beginning and an end); the mantra — 'man' (mind) plus 'trāyate' (to deliver) — is the object that delivers it, likened to a banana that gives the monkey pinpoint focus, a radio frequency requiring a tuned receiver, and a seed or zip file holding unlimited potency that the chanter must water like a gardener. Drawing on Patañjali, the speaker shows that meditation requires an ethical foundation (yama and niyama — a liar cannot sit still) but that īśvara-praṇidhāna, surrender to the Supreme Controller in the helpless mood of Queen Kunti's akiñcana, is the stated shortcut to perfection; even yoga's own teacher names surrender to the Lord as its highest point. Following Krishna's instruction to Uddhava to purify a mantra by studying its meaning, the words Hare, Krishna, and Rāma are unpacked, and the coho salmon's unerring return to its birth stream illustrates the soul's innate homing attraction — currently misinvested in temporary relations (travelers sharing a restaurant table, in Hiraṇyakaśipu's image) — which the mantra magnetically redirects toward Krishna, the all-attractive source. Each element of the triad is then developed: perception (from percipere, 'to seize entirely') is remolded from 'this is mine' to seeing the world as a place of service, revealing the soul as the most valuable thing in the world; discipline (from the root 'learner') conquers restlessness and distraction — Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura identifying inattention as the root of all chanting offenses — through abhyāsa-yoga, adding one filament a day until it becomes a rope; contentment culminates in samādhi, boundless happiness from which one never departs, unshaken in the greatest difficulty. The talk closes with the theology and practice of the name: the holy name is absolutely non-different from Krishna — a living cintāmaṇi touchstone — yet, per Rūpa Gosvāmī, more merciful than Krishna Himself, following even the offender and leading him back by the hand; and the practical method of japa is simply attention — hearing oneself syllable by syllable so that chanting becomes simultaneous kīrtana and śravaṇa, chanted in the present moment like a baby crying for its mother, with genuine attentive japa being the one devotional activity that cannot be faked.

Spiritual science is a different kind of research: instead of trying to wring secrets from material nature, it begins with humility, pridelessness, and nonviolence, submitting to the Supreme Lord and praying to be led on the right path and freed from past sinful reactions.

Three diagnostic questions: have you found what you're looking for, is it eternal, and can you live and breathe in it every minute? If any answer is no, Caitanya Mahāprabhu invites you to take up chanting Hare Krishna — a yogic discipline that molds the mind to those higher levels.

Prabhupada goes further in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam: a common person who looks inward realizes the Supersoul beside him giving direction, which opens a whole new life — one can be right here, right now, and taste complete happiness, as Krishna promises in the fifth chapter of the Gītā that the inward-turned soul tastes unlimited happiness.

Just as a monkey gains pinpoint focus when shown a banana, the mind gains purpose and focus when given the mantra, which delivers it from restless, unproductive wandering (contrasted with the monkey mind kept jumping for years at a computer screen with no production).

Each mantra has its own frequency, and the Hare Krishna Maha Mantra is the 'omnibus' mantra — the highest frequency, containing everything one is looking for.

Following Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu's beloved gardening analogy, devotional service is gardening — one must become a gardener and water the mantra-seed with the right environment and nurturing for it to grow into something wonderful.

The monkey mind searches everywhere for something; when one finds the very source of all existence and directs love and attention there, one becomes satisfied — as Krishna states in the Bhagavad Gita that one who knows him as the source of everything gives his love to him.

- **[00:14:30.580]** Meditation requires an ethical foundation: Patanjali's eightfold (aṣṭāṅga) path begins with yama and niyama because a liar or cheater cannot even sit still — the guilty mind spins with agitation. One must clean up one's life, regulate the senses, and learn to sit and breathe, so the mind becomes still like a pond whose calm surface lets you see the bottom. The mind is otherwise the cause of saṁsāra, a reflective surface taking on vṛttis (impressions) through the senses, and by identifying with it like a movie screen our emotions become married to its scenes.

The starting point is 'there is a God, and I'm not him': giving up the intoxicating false conceptions of being rich, beautiful, or smart (Prabhupada's 'borrowed plumes' — all abilities are loaned to us) and, like Queen Kunti, feeling oneself akiñcana, helpless and possessing nothing. Surrendering this way and calling out with the mantra brings one very quickly to the perfection of meditation.

Applying this to the Maha Mantra's three words: Hare means the Lord's internal potency, Srimati Radharani, and per the acharyas also one who removes obstacles on one's true path of happiness; Krishna means the all-attractive one with magnetic attractive force. Hearing these definitions inspires the chanter.
    - cf. Srimad Bhagavatam
- **[00:24:18.540]** Just as the coho salmon, after years and thousands of miles in the ocean, is irresistibly drawn back to the exact stream of its birth (whether by smell or 'magnetic detection'), every living entity carries an innate homing attraction to its original home — and behind any mechanism, it is Īśvara within the heart who directs the creature, as He directs the soul.

- **[00:25:12.280]** We are all feeling that same magnetic pull toward our original home, but it is diverted into temporary people and events. Worldly busyness is, as Ernest Becker wrote, a distraction from having to face death; and as Hiraṇyakaśipu consoled his bereaved family, our relations are like travelers who share a table at a restaurant and then go their separate ways, never to meet again. What we lack is not attraction but direction — we misinvest emotional energy in relationships that disperse within a few years instead of in the eternal home of the soul.

    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 08

Śukadeva Gosvāmī's example is the opposite pole: he left the womb running, refusing to attach himself to anything.

- **[00:36:08.500]** Discipline (from the Latin root meaning 'learner'), when oriented toward pleasing the Supreme through acts of sacrifice, yields everything — knowledge, bliss, life. Discipline overcomes restlessness and distraction, two enemies of satisfaction that must be conquered to chant without offense. Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura teaches that inattention is the root cause of all offenses in chanting the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra; distraction means letting the mind drift from the valuable to the valueless, driven by asat-tṛṣṇā, the thirst for temporary things the mind is accustomed to chasing.

The violinist's maxim reinforces daily constancy: skip practice one day and you notice; two days, your teacher notices; three days, everybody notices.

This is actual freedom from all miseries arising from material contact.

- **[00:41:42.740]** The mahā-mantra — 16 words, 32 syllables, a balanced mantra — is prescribed in the Vedas, which are co-eternal with the Lord; the holy name itself is the absolute truth, with no difference between Krishna and His name. Śāstric evidence: the name is a cintāmaṇi (touchstone) — transformative, turning whatever it touches into spirit; it is fully alive (caitanya-rasa-vigraha) and eternally, completely perfect.

Even if one offends Krishna, the name follows that person; just by saying the name one is filled with transcendental bliss, brought back into devotional service, and taken personally by the hand back to Godhead.

- **[00:44:05.480]** The method of japa meditation: utter the mantra just loud enough that you can hear yourself, and make attention the whole practice — the mind will run away (even ~8 seconds of sustained focus is counted a very high attainment in the Yoga-sūtra), so expect it; your only job is to notice gently and bring the mind back to the sound of the mantra, without smashing or scolding it.
    - cf. Bhagavad-gita As It Is
- **[00:44:55.780]** Chant fully in the present moment: drop all considerations of time, place, and what others think of your chanting — you are in the best place in the universe with nowhere else to go. Giving your full attention to Krishna through the mantra is itself an act of love.
- **[01:03:40.720]** Mantra meditation molds the mind to a higher level of perception, discipline, and contentment — and this is verifiable within one sitting: a participant observed the initially hyperactive mind stabilize during chanting, perception sharpen, and satisfaction arise, which the speaker confirmed as exactly this molding effect.

Unlike neutral meditations on the breath or a candle flame, the maha-mantra carries acyuta-bhāva within it: there is reciprocation in the mantra because actual mercy descends from the name of God, undeniably experienced by the chanter.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 11
- **[01:11:37.220]** The essence of japa is simply paying attention — the basics are 'chant Hare Krishna and teach others,' and while chanting the most important part is attentive hearing, syllable by syllable. Deep attention makes japa simultaneous kīrtana and śravaṇa: the tongue and mouth create the mantra while the ear receives that same sound as nectar.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 09

- **[01:15:32.880]** Chant slowly and enunciate correctly first; like learning mridanga beats, whatever is practiced slowly and properly can later be sped up with full proficiency, since chanting is a mind-and-muscle connection. Practically: record yourself chanting on your phone and play it back — you may be surprised how you actually sound (one participant found every second mantra was wrong) — and this self-audit lets you chant 16 rounds accurately in well under three hours.
- **[01:16:44.240]** Śrīla Prabhupāda observed that a sahajiyā can imitate practically every sadhana activity except sitting down and attentively chanting japa — genuine japa cannot be faked.
- **[01:17:42.780]** While chanting, concentrate on the Supersoul in your heart. Even a brief moment of such connection produces a happiness that persists through the whole day and awakens spontaneous eagerness to chant beyond the prescribed 16 rounds — the same momentum reported when the rounds are chanted in one unbroken sitting: the mind, like the monkey shown one banana, wants more.

- **[01:20:29.400]** Physical stillness directly supports mental concentration in japa: even a slight unconscious rocking of the body disturbs attention, while sitting straight and motionless measurably improves it.

Cultivating this helpless, yearning mood transforms the quality of japa, making it easier and more intense; and this mood is accessible from the very beginning of one's chanting, not an 'advanced-only' topic.

- **[01:22:19.740]** Approach every japa session with the explicit intention of receiving at least 'one mustard seed of inspiration' — never go away empty-handed. Since the Name's blessing is descending mercy whose channel we cannot predict, our part is full receptivity: heart completely open, mind completely focused, 'leaning in,' with time set aside for undivided attention. This daily inspiration is the sustaining power of the practice.
    - cf. Srimad-Bhagavatam, Canto 05

## Practical application

Chant japa loud enough to hear yourself, and make attention the entire practice: expect the mind to run away (even ~8 seconds of sustained focus is a high attainment), and simply notice gently and bring it back to the sound of the mantra without scolding it — this repeated returning is abhyāsa-yoga, and daily repetition itself is the shaping force. Chant fully in the present moment, dropping all thought of time, place, and others' opinions, since giving Krishna full attention through the mantra is itself an act of love. Build the ethical foundation the practice requires — clean up your life and regulate the senses, because only a truthful person can sit straight and remain seated for long japa — and support concentration physically by sitting straight and motionless, as even slight rocking disturbs attention. Study the meanings of Hare, Krishna, and Rāma so the mind takes more interest in the mantra, and chant slowly with correct enunciation first (record yourself on your phone and play it back to audit your accuracy) before speeding up, which allows 16 accurate rounds in well under three hours; chanting the rounds in one unbroken sitting builds momentum where the mind wants more. Cultivate the helpless, yearning mood of a baby crying for its mother — accessible from the very beginning, not an advanced topic — and while chanting, concentrate on the Supersoul in the heart, since even a brief moment of connection produces happiness lasting the whole day and awakens eagerness to chant beyond 16 rounds. Approach every session intending to receive at least one 'mustard seed of inspiration,' with heart open and mind focused, never leaving empty-handed. Rather than vowing perfect chanting forever, commit to a fixed trial period (such as three weeks) of your best, most concentrated rounds so concentration becomes an established habit. Water the mantra-seed like a patient, practical gardener — with patience and determination success is assured — and when offenses make you feel there is no way up, keep chanting, because the name is more merciful than Krishna Himself and restores the fallen chanter. Finally, keep both halves of Caitanya Mahāprabhu's program: chant Hare Krishna, and teach it to others.

## Glossary

- **saṅkīrtana** — Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu's movement of congregational chanting of the holy name, resting on two principles: chant Hare Krishna and teach it to others.
- **mahā-mantra** — The 'great mantra' — Hare Krishna, 16 words and 32 syllables — described as the 'omnibus' mantra of the highest frequency, containing everything one is looking for.
- **mantra** — From 'man' (mind) and 'trāyate' (to deliver): sound that delivers the mind from restless wandering by giving it an object of meditation.
- **japa** — Individual meditative chanting of the mantra, uttered just loud enough to hear oneself, with attentive hearing syllable by syllable as the whole practice.
- **Hare** — Address of the Lord's internal potency, Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī; per the ācāryas, also the one who removes obstacles on one's true path of happiness.
- **Krishna** — The all-attractive one, possessing magnetic attractive force; the source of all existence toward whom chanting directs love.
- **Rāma** — The Lord as the source of life, power, and pleasure, addressed in the mahā-mantra.
- **aṣṭāṅga-yoga** — Patañjali's eightfold yoga path, beginning with ethical regulation (yama and niyama) as the foundation for meditation.
- **yama and niyama** — The moral restraints and observances that begin the eightfold path; without them (e.g., for a liar or cheater) the guilty mind is too agitated to sit still.
- **īśvara-praṇidhāna** — Surrender to the Supreme Controller — stated twice in the Yoga-sūtra as a shortcut past the arduous eightfold path to the perfection of meditation.
- **Īśvara** — The Supreme Controller, present within the heart, who directs the living creature — as He directs the salmon home and the soul back to Godhead.
- **akiñcana** — The mood of possessing nothing and being helpless, exemplified by Queen Kunti — giving up 'borrowed plumes' such as pride in wealth, beauty, or intelligence.
- **vṛttis** — Impressions taken on by the mind through the senses; by identifying with the mind like a movie screen, our emotions become married to its scenes.
- **saṁsāra** — The cycle of material existence, of which the unstilled mind is the cause.
- **asat-tṛṣṇā** — The thirst for temporary things; the driver of distraction, which lets the mind drift from the valuable to the valueless.
- **abhyāsa-yoga** — The yoga of repeated practice — again and again bringing the mind back to the mantra, like adding one filament a day until filaments become a rope.
- **samādhi** — The perfectional stage of yoga: the mind completely restrained from material activity, seeing the self by the pure mind and relishing boundless transcendental happiness, unshaken even in the greatest difficulty.
- **cintāmaṇi** — Touchstone — śāstric description of the holy name as transformative, turning whatever it touches into spirit.
- **caitanya-rasa-vigraha** — Description of the holy name as fully alive — the embodiment of conscious spiritual taste, eternally and completely perfect.
- **acyuta-bhāva** — Feeling for the Personality of Godhead; per Nārada Muni, self-realization without it 'does not shine' (na śobhate). Unlike neutral meditation objects, the mahā-mantra carries it within, so real mercy descends and reciprocates with the chanter.
- **kīrtana** — Glorification of the Lord through chanting; with deep attention, japa becomes simultaneous kīrtana and śravaṇa — the tongue creates the mantra while the ear receives it.
- **śravaṇa** — Hearing — the receiving side of japa; attentive hearing of the mantra's sound, syllable by syllable, is the most important part of chanting.
- **sahajiyā** — An imitator of devotional life; Prabhupāda observed such a person can mimic practically every sādhana activity except sitting down and attentively chanting japa.
- **sādhana** — Regulated devotional practice, of which attentive japa is the unfakeable core.
- **Supersoul** — The Lord situated beside the soul within the heart, giving direction; a common person who looks inward realizes Him, and concentrating on Him during chanting yields lasting happiness.
- **Māyā** — The Lord's illusory energy that recaptures the soul at birth, when the senses are externalized and a new false identity is imposed with a name.
- **percipere** — Latin root of 'perception,' meaning 'to seize entirely' — as we claim things as 'mine,' each claim entangles us in the sticky material world.
- **trāyate** — Sanskrit 'to deliver' — the root of the 'tra' in mantra, indicating that the mantra delivers the mind.
