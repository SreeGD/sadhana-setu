# Chapter 2 · Nāma-Uddhāra — Lives Transformed by the Holy Name

> *"Chanting the name of the Lord — even to indicate something else, jokingly, for music, or neglectfully — frees one from unlimited sins."*
> — **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 6.2.14** (Śukadeva Gosvāmī)

---

The power described in Chapter 1 is not abstract. Scripture records specific people whom the name
lifted from ruin — evidence that the name acts by *its own* potency, not by the qualification of the
chanter.

## 2.1 Ajāmila — the fallen brāhmaṇa (SB 6.1–3)

A learned brāhmaṇa wrecked by bad association, Ajāmila lived for years as a thief and debauchee. At
death, terrified by the Yamadūtas, he cried out the name of his youngest son — *Nārāyaṇa*.

```
patitaḥ skhalito bhagnaḥ sandaṣṭas tapta āhataḥ
harir ity avaśenāha pumān nārhati yātanāḥ   (SB 6.2.15)
```
"One who chants 'Hari' even helplessly — while falling, slipping, breaking a bone, bitten, burned, or
struck — is freed from all reactions."

The Viṣṇudūtas intervened; given a second chance, Ajāmila purified himself and attained liberation.
**Teaching:** even *sāṅketya* (indirect) chanting saved a sinful man at death's door.

## 2.2 Gajendra — the king of elephants (SB 8.2–4)

Intoxicated with strength and followers, Gajendra found every material asset useless against the
crocodile. After a thousand years he cried out to the Lord alone (SB 8.3) — and the Lord came on
Garuḍa, killed the crocodile, and gave him a spiritual form. **Teaching:** crisis becomes
opportunity when the soul finally calls out sincerely rather than relying on its own strength.

## 2.3 Prahlāda — the child devotee (SB 7)

Born in a demon dynasty, taught to hate Viṣṇu, Prahlāda simply kept chanting and remembering Kṛṣṇa —
"absorbed in the great nectar of Your glories, I fear nothing" (SB 7.9.43). Fire, poison, cliffs, and
elephants could not touch him; the Lord appeared as Nṛsiṁhadeva to protect His devotee. **Teaching:**
pure chanting is an impenetrable shelter.

## 2.4 Dhruva — the determined prince (SB 4.8–12)

A five-year-old sought a kingdom through austerity, chanting the mantra Nārada gave him. When the
Lord appeared, Dhruva said, *"I was searching for broken glass and You have given me a priceless jewel
— I want no benediction"* (Dhruva's prayer, *Hari-bhakti-sudhodaya* 7.28, cited in Prabhupāda's purport to SB 4.9.5). **Teaching:** the name purifies desire itself — even a material
motive matures into prema.

## 2.5 Mṛgāri — the hunter (Cc. Madhya 24)

A cruel hunter who half-killed animals for sport, taught by Nārada to chant, wept in repentance,
freed the animals, and became so compassionate he stepped over ants. **Teaching:** the most cruel
heart becomes the most tender through the name.

## 2.6 Jagāi & Mādhāi — the drunkards of Navadvīpa (Cc. Ādi 17)

The worst ruffians of the town — who even wounded Nityānanda Prabhu — were delivered by His causeless
mercy and never returned to sin. **Teaching:** the mercy of the devotees joined to the name can save
anyone.

---

## Summary

| Devotee | Contact with the Name | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Ajāmila | indirect, at death | liberated |
| Gajendra | crisis prayer | spiritual body |
| Prahlāda | constant remembrance | fully protected |
| Dhruva | guru-given mantra | desire purified |
| Mṛgāri | instruction | cruelty → compassion |
| Jagāi–Mādhāi | devotee's mercy | pure devotees |

> **From the lectures.** The corpus is itself a gallery of such lives: Bhūrijana Prabhu builds an
> entire seminar on **Ajāmila** as the supreme atonement; Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja retells
> **Haridāsa Ṭhākura** the Nāmācārya, **Kholāvecā Śrīdhara**, and **Nārada's** path from loss to
> longing; Vaiśeṣika Prabhu recounts **Vālmīki**, transformed from bandit to sage by chanting even
> "*mara*" ("death"), which became "*Rāma*."

**The through-line:** the name is not limited by the chanter's qualifications. It acts according to
its own power upon anyone who touches it sincerely — which is precisely why *my* case (Chapter 3) is
never hopeless.
