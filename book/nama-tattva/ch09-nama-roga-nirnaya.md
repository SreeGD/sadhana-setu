# Chapter 9 · नाम-रोग-निर्णय · NĀMA-ROGA-NIRṆAYA
## Diagnosing My Personal Blockage

> *"Be determined to hear one mantra. Just one mantra."*
> — **HG Bhūrijana Prabhu** (Japa Seminar, Day 03)

---

## 9.1 The Diagnostic Principle

Chapters 4–8 laid out the whole disease — the chain that runs **Guṇas → Ṣaḍ-Ripu → Anarthas →
Pāpa → Aparādha → Blocked Name**. But a chain is general; a patient is particular. The purpose of
this chapter is to move from *"chanting is blocked"* to *"here is exactly where **my** chanting is
blocked, and here is what to do about it today."*

The governing image is Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja's: **the holy name is always present, like the
sun; it is our own offenses that are the clouds hiding it.** The sun never fails. So when I feel
nothing, the honest question is never *"Why doesn't the Name work?"* but **"Which cloud is over my
sun right now?"**

**How this differs from Chapter 3.** Chapter 3 told one devotee's story (Kṛṣṇa Dāsa) so I could
recognize myself in it. This chapter hands me the instruments to examine *myself* — a self-diagnosis
I run on my own japa, not a narrative I read about someone else.

Mādhurya-kādambinī (3.19) gives the clinical basis:

> *bhagavan-nāmni saṁprāpte 'parādhe sati tat-prabhāvaṁ na darśayati* —
> "When an offense is present, the holy name does not reveal its full potency to the offender."

The Name withholds nothing; the *offender's perception* is what is curtained. Diagnosis, then, is
simply learning to read my own curtain.

## 9.2 Symptom → Cause Map

The fastest diagnostic is to notice **what my mind actually does during japa**, and trace it back.
Each surface symptom points to an active enemy (Chapter 4), which breeds a specific offense
(Chapter 7), which has a specific cure (Part Three).

| What I experience during japa | Active enemy | Offense it breeds | Turn to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planning pleasures, daydreaming, "what's next" | **Kāma** (lust) | #7 (sinning on the Name's strength), #10 (attachment) | **Ch 14** + **Ch 15** |
| Replaying an argument; rehearsing a reply; irritation | **Krodha** (anger) | #1 (offense to a devotee) | **Ch 11** + **Ch 14** |
| Rushing, watching the clock, "just finish the rounds" | **Lobha** (greed) | #8 (Name as a means to a result) | **Ch 13** |
| "Is this doing anything?"; doubt; forgetting the teaching | **Moha** (illusion) | #5 (Name's glory is exaggeration), #6 (concocting) | **Ch 2** (re-read the lives), **Ch 13** |
| "I chant better than them"; comparing favorably | **Mada** (pride) | #3 (disrespect to guru/senior), #9 (instructing the faithless) | **Ch 10** |
| Mentally criticizing a devotee; envy at another's advancement | **Mātsarya** (envy) | #1 (offense to a devotee) | **Ch 12** |

> **Read your tone, not just your thoughts.** As taught in the *Sonicate Your Life* seminar, the
> *tone* of chanting reveals the heart. If the inner voice of my japa is bored, resentful, or
> transactional, that tone is itself the diagnostic reading.

## 9.3 The Three Instruments

Run all three once for a full picture; thereafter, the one-minute Symptom Map above is usually
enough for daily practice.

### Instrument A — Ṣaḍ-Ripu Self-Assessment *(which enemy dominates me?)*

Score each line **0** (never), **1** (sometimes), **2** (often) for *this past week*.

| # | Statement | Enemy | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | My attention drifts to things I want to enjoy or acquire | Kāma | ▢ |
| 2 | I replayed conflicts or felt the urge to retaliate | Krodha | ▢ |
| 3 | I chanted fast, anxious mainly to be done | Lobha | ▢ |
| 4 | I doubted whether chanting really changes anything | Moha | ▢ |
| 5 | I felt superior to other devotees or to newcomers | Mada | ▢ |
| 6 | I found fault with a devotee, silently or aloud | Mātsarya | ▢ |

**My dominant enemy** = the line(s) scoring highest. That is the enemy to name in today's japa and
the chapter to study first. (Ties are common; treat the two together.)

### Instrument B — Ten-Offense Audit *(what am I actually doing?)*

A yes/no self-check. Honesty here is the whole value — this is the Kṛṣṇa-Dāsa dialogue of Chapter 3
turned inward.

| # | Nāma-aparādha | Honest answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Do I criticize or belittle devotees (even mentally)? | ▢ Y ▢ N |
| 2 | Do I equate Kṛṣṇa/the Name with the demigods? | ▢ Y ▢ N |
| 3 | Do I disregard my guru's or seniors' instructions? | ▢ Y ▢ N |
| 4 | Do I disparage scripture? | ▢ Y ▢ N |
| 5 | Do I secretly think the Name's glories are exaggerated? | ▢ Y ▢ N |
| 6 | Do I impose my own concocted meanings on the Name? | ▢ Y ▢ N |
| 7 | Do I sin on the strength of "I'll just chant it off"? | ▢ Y ▢ N |
| 8 | Do I treat chanting as one pious ritual among many? | ▢ Y ▢ N |
| 9 | Do I instruct the faithless who don't want to hear? | ▢ Y ▢ N |
| 10 | Do I chant inattentively, clinging to "I and mine"? | ▢ Y ▢ N |

Every **Y** is a named cloud. Offenses #1, #3, #9 trace to **mada/mātsarya** (→ Ch 10, 12);
#5, #6 to **moha** (→ Ch 13); #7, #10 to **kāma** (→ Ch 14–15); #8 to **lobha** (→ Ch 13).

### Instrument C — Anartha-Stage Locator *(how far along am I?)*

Following Mādhurya-kādambinī's four stages of *anartha-nivṛtti* (clearing), locate the honest
present stage — not to grade myself, but to set realistic expectations and avoid discouragement.

| Stage | Sanskrit | Felt reality |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Eka-deśa-vartinī** (~¼) | A few bad habits loosening; occasional clarity in japa |
| 2 | **Bahu-deśa-vartinī** (~½) | Noticeable behavior change; regular periods of peace |
| 3 | **Prāyikī** (~¾) | Others notice the change; steadiness under pressure |
| 4 | **Pūrṇā** (complete) | Anarthas gone; unshakeable absorption |

Most sincere practitioners live between Stage 1 and 2 for years. *That is normal, not failure* —
Bhūrijana Prabhu's counsel applies: the task is never "sixteen perfect rounds," only **this one
mantra, heard**.

## 9.4 The Chain Locator

Plot the findings on the chain. Circle where the blockage *originates* — treating a downstream link
while the upstream one runs is why chanting "doesn't work."

```
  GUṆAS ──► ṢAḌ-RIPU ──► ANARTHAS ──► PĀPA ──► APARĀDHA ──► BLOCKED NAME
 (lifestyle) (my enemy)  (my habits)  (my sins) (my offense)  (no taste)
     │            │            │           │          │
   Ch 15        Ch 14        Ch 5,15      Ch 6      Ch 10-13
```

- If my enemies flare mainly when I'm **tired, rushed, or overstimulated** → the origin is upstream
  at **Guṇas**; begin at **Ch 15 (lifestyle)**.
- If lifestyle is steady but one **enemy still dominates** → begin at **Ch 14**, plus the specific
  quality-chapter (Ch 10 humility / 11 tolerance / 12 respect).
- If the enemies are quiet but an **offense persists** (an unresolved Y in Instrument B) → go
  straight to the **remedy for that offense** and, where it involves a person, **make amends first**.

mantra, consciously choose to chant *with love* rather than mechanically).

## 9.6 The Re-Diagnosis Cadence

A diagnosis is not a verdict; it is a reading that changes. The heart is a moving target — "we are
not even the same person the next morning." So:

- **Daily (30 sec):** the Symptom Map (9.2) — name today's cloud before the first round.
- **Monthly (10 min):** re-run all three Instruments; compare with last month's Prescription.
- **After any fall or dry spell:** run Instrument B first — a persistent offense, not a weak Name,
  is almost always the cause.

The goal is not a clean scorecard. It is the shift Chapter 3 could only describe and this chapter
makes operational: **"The Name didn't change. I changed — by naming and clearing the one cloud in
front of my sun."**
