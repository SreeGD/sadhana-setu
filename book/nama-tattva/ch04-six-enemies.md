# Chapter 4 · Ṣaḍ-Ripu — The Six Enemies: Why They Arise and What They Are

> *"It is lust only, Arjuna, which is born of contact with the material mode of passion and later transformed into anger — the all-devouring, sinful enemy of this world."*
> — **Kṛṣṇa, Bhagavad-gītā 3.37**

---

Part Two traces the chain that blocks the name — **Guṇas → Ṣaḍ-Ripu → Anarthas → Pāpa → Aparādha →
Blocked Name**. This chapter covers the first two links together: *why* the soul is exposed to the
modes at all, and *what* the six enemies born from them are.

## 4.1 Why the Soul Can Be Covered — the Taṭasthā Position

The living entity is **marginal energy (taṭasthā-śakti)** — minute (*aṇu*), conscious (*cetana*),
eternal (*sanātana*), and Kṛṣṇa's part (*aṁśa*), able to associate with either the spiritual or the
material energy (BG 7.4–5, 15.7).

```
mamaivāṁśo jīva-loke jīva-bhūtaḥ sanātanaḥ
manaḥ-ṣaṣṭhānīndriyāṇi prakṛti-sthāni karṣati   (BG 15.7)
```

Because the soul is *minute*, it can be covered — as a spark can be put out though the sun cannot.
The mechanism is **guṇa-saṅga**, association with the modes (BG 13.22), switched on by **false ego**
(*ahaṅkāra*), which makes the soul think, "I am the doer, I am the enjoyer" (BG 3.27).

| | Jīva | Kṛṣṇa |
|---|---|---|
| Size | aṇu (minute) | vibhu (infinite) |
| Coverable? | yes | never |
| Modes | influenced by | controller of |

> **From the lectures.** Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja locates the root of bondage in *ahaṁ-mama* — "I
> and mine" — the proprietary illusion that binds the soul; and Bhūrijana Prabhu opens from the jīva's
> constitutional position: we are eternal parts of Kṛṣṇa whose very nature is to serve, so misidentifying
> with matter is the original wound.

## 4.2 The Six Enemies (Ṣaḍ-Ripu)

From guṇa-saṅga arise the six inner enemies. BG 3.37 names *kāma* as the root; when frustrated it
becomes *krodha*; the whole set are the "three gates to hell" and their kin (BG 16.21).

| Enemy | Sanskrit | Root guṇa | Core move | First remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kāma** — lust | काम | rajas | craving to enjoy/possess | higher taste (Ch 15) |
| **Krodha** — anger | क्रोध | rajas→tamas | frustrated lust erupts | tolerance (Ch 11) |
| **Lobha** — greed | लोभ | rajas | never-enough acquiring | contentment (Ch 15) |
| **Moha** — illusion | मोह | tamas | forgets the real | knowledge (Ch 13) |
| **Mada** — pride | मद | rajas+tamas | intoxication with position | humility (Ch 10) |
| **Mātsarya** — envy | मात्सर्य | tamas+rajas | can't bear others' good | respect (Ch 12) |

**The chain within the chain (BG 2.62–63).** Contemplating sense objects → attachment → lust →
anger → delusion → loss of memory → loss of intelligence → ruin. Note the order: *krodha* is
downstream of *kāma*. Treat the root (lust), not only the symptom (anger).

### A parable — the six thieves in the heart
A king asks a sage why he has no peace despite owning everything. "You have welcomed six invisible
thieves who live in your heart," the sage replies. "*Kāma* steals contentment; *krodha* burns your
bridges; *lobha* clutches what you cannot keep; *moha* makes rope look like snake; *mada* makes you
think you already know; *mātsarya* turns others' joy into your sorrow. Until you evict them, no wealth
brings peace."

> **From the lectures.** Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja treats *false ego* as the chanter's constant
> disturbing companion (Harināma Cintāmaṇi seminar 04); Vaiśeṣika Prabhu traces suffering to
> "material intelligence" — the reflex of seeing every situation as something to enjoy or control,
> which yields the opposite of what it seeks ("Purify the Mind").

## 4.3 Why This Matters for Chanting

Each enemy, left active, breeds a specific offense (Chapter 7) and specifically curtains the name.
Conditioning and liberation are mirror processes:

| Conditioning | Liberation |
|---|---|
| desire to enjoy separately | desire to serve |
| "I am the enjoyer" | "I am the servant" |
| guṇa-saṅga | nāma-saṅga (association with the Name) |

The name is **śuddha-sattva** — transcendental to all three modes — so associating with it directly
weakens the guṇa-saṅga from which the enemies grow. This is why a sāttvika lifestyle (Chapter 15)
*supports* japa, and why the name itself is the ultimate weapon against the six.
