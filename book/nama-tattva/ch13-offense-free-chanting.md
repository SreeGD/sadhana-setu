# Chapter 13 · Offense-Free Chanting

> *"Chant attentively, chant with quality, and pray for mercy — each grows out of the last."*
> — **HH Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja** (as taught, Kirtan Course, Day 03)

---

Chapters 10–12 built the *inner posture* (humility, tolerance, respect). This chapter applies it to
the *act*: chanting that steadily clears the ten offenses (Chapter 7) and carries chanting from
*nāmāparādha* toward *śuddha-nāma* (Chapter 8).

## 13.1 The Ten Remedies at a Glance

| # | Offense | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | blaspheming devotees | serve them; find their good qualities |
| 2 | Lord = demigods | study tattva (BG 7.20) |
| 3 | disregarding guru | increase submission and service |
| 4 | blaspheming scripture | hear submissively, with faith |
| 5 | glories = exaggeration | re-read the transformed lives (Ch 2) |
| 6 | concocting meanings | accept the paramparā |
| 7 | sinning on the name's strength | genuine repentance; actually stop |
| 8 | name = ordinary piety | meditate on its supremacy |
| 9 | instructing the faithless | assess the hearer first |
| 10 | inattention; "I and mine" | develop taste; hear *one* mantra |

## 13.2 The Three-Fold Discipline

Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja's epigraph gives the working sequence, each stage growing from the last:

1. **Attentively** — hear the mantra; do not let it become background sound.
2. **With quality** — add the "one drop of sincerity"; it is not only the mantra but the *mood* of the
   chanter that counts (a mother knows her child's true cry from its ordinary sounds).
3. **Praying for mercy** — chant as a helpless soul calling the Lord, not as a technician producing a
   result. The name reveals itself by *its own* mercy; our part is the sincere call.

## 13.3 From Shadow to Substance

Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura's prescription for moving from *nāmāparādha* to *nāmābhāsa* and onward:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | identify which offenses are active (Chapter 9) |
| 2 | apply the specific remedy for each |
| 3 | increase association with pure devotees |
| 4 | chant more *attentively* — quality over quantity |
| 5 | hear about the name's glories to rebuild faith |
| 6 | be patient — transformation takes time |

> **From the lectures.** Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja teaches faith as the *entry* into offenseless
> chanting (Harinaam Cintamani 01) and attentive chanting as the *fountainhead* from which all further
> results flow (Harinaam Cintamani 05). Bhūrijana Prabhu ranks offenseless, attentive chanting above
> liberation, yoga, and solitary forest meditation ("Japa Retreat, afternoon").
