# Chapter 12 · Māna-Dāna — Respect (Amāninā Mānadena)

> *"amāninā mānadena" — "expecting no honor for oneself, yet ready to offer all respect to others."*
> — **Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, Śikṣāṣṭaka 3**

---

The third quality of Śikṣāṣṭaka 3 is a two-edged formula that dismantles **mātsarya** (envy) at its
root:

| Term | Sanskrit | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **amāninā** | अमानिना | expecting no respect for oneself |
| **mānadena** | मानदेन | actively offering respect to others |

It is not enough to *not* want honor (*amānina*); one must *give* honor (*mānada*). The living being
naturally craves respect — a perverted reflection of its true position as Kṛṣṇa's servant. By
*giving* honor, I satisfy that craving in others and drain the envy from my own heart.

## 12.1 Respect vs. Envy

| Situation | Respect (mānadena) | Envy (mātsarya) |
|---|---|---|
| others succeed | "wonderful — how can I help?" | "why them, not me?" |
| others are praised | "they deserve it" | "but what about *my* work?" |
| others are served | "they deserve even more" | "I should be served too" |
| effect on chanting | a peaceful mind | a disturbed mind |

Envy is the subtlest enemy because it disguises itself as discernment ("I'm just being honest about
their faults"). It is also the most dangerous, because it produces the first and gravest offense —
blaspheming a devotee (Chapter 7).

## 12.2 The Vaiṣṇava Vision

A devotee offers respect because he sees **Kṛṣṇa in every heart** (SB 3.29). Even at a beginner's
stage one can *practice* this vision: "everyone I meet has Kṛṣṇa within; I will find something to honor
in each." Every act of respect thereby becomes worship.

## 12.3 Practice

- **Daily:** sincerely appreciate one devotee — verbally if possible.
- **Transform envy on the spot:** when it rises, pray, "Kṛṣṇa, give me *that* devotee's good quality"
  — turning envy into *spṛhā*, holy aspiration.
- **Give credit freely, take blame willingly.**
- **Evening:** did I mentally criticize anyone? feel superior? offer respect actively?

> **From the lectures.** The three qualities of Śikṣāṣṭaka 3 — humility, tolerance, respect — recur
> as the through-line of Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja's teaching on prayerful chanting: the name comes
> alive only in a heart that has stopped competing for its own honor.
