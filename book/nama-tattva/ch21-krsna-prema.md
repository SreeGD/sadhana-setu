# Chapter 21 · Kṛṣṇa-Prema Attained

> *"Kṛṣṇa consciousness… can be attained only by paying one price — intense greed (laulyam) to obtain it."*
> — **Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī, Cc. Madhya 22.42**

---

Every cloud has now lifted — enemies controlled (17), anarthas destroyed (18), sins burned (19),
offenses forgiven (20). What remains is not an achievement but a *revelation*: the sun that was never
absent, now standing clear. This is **prema**, pure love of God — the goal (*prayojana*) toward which
the entire book has been the road.

## 21.1 The One Price

```
kṛṣṇa-bhakti-rasa-bhāvitā matiḥ
krīyatāṁ yadi kuto 'pi labhyate
tatra laulyam api mūlyam ekalaṁ
janma-koṭi-sukṛtair na labhyate   (Cc. Madhya 22.42)
```

Prema cannot be bought by pious deeds of even millions of births. It has exactly one price: *laulyam*
— an intense, aching greed for it. This is the great equalizer. Every chapter's discipline matters,
but none of it *purchases* prema; it only clears the way for the one currency that does — a heart
that truly wants Kṛṣṇa.

## 21.2 The Nine Steps of the Creeper (bhakti-latā)

Prema is the ripe fruit of a creeper grown through nine stages (Rūpa Gosvāmī):

```
śraddhā → sādhu-saṅga → bhajana-kriyā → anartha-nivṛtti → niṣṭhā
     → ruci → āsakti → bhāva → PREMA
```

*Faith → devotee association → practice → clearing the unwanted → steadiness → taste → attachment →
emotion → love.* Notice that anartha-nivṛtti (Chapter 18) sits early on the path, and *ruci* (taste,
Chapter 15) only after it — a map that quietly reassures the struggling chanter of Chapter 3: dryness
is not the end of the road but a stretch of it.

### The mango seed
A child plants a seed and next morning asks, "Where is the mango?" First the sprout, then sapling,
tree, flower, green fruit, ripening — perhaps fifteen years. "Don't demand fruit from a seed," says
the grandfather. "Tend the tree with love. The fruit will come."

## 21.3 The Signs of Prema

| External | Internal |
|---|---|
| tears while chanting | constant remembrance of Kṛṣṇa |
| the voice chokes speaking of Him | cannot bear separation |
| hair stands on end | nothing else seems important |
| spontaneous chanting and dancing | Kṛṣṇa seen everywhere |

## 21.4 The Return of Kṛṣṇa Dāsa

Recall the devotee of Chapter 3 — fifteen years, no taste, no tears. He did not chant *more*; he
cleared, one by one, the clouds this book has named. And the change, when it came, was not that the
name grew stronger. "The name didn't change," his guru had said. "**You** changed."

This is the whole of *Nāma-Tattva* in a sentence. My chanting was never failing because the name was
weak. It was blocked, and blockages have names, and named things can be cleared. The sun was always
full. The work was only ever to open the sky.

> **From the lectures.** Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja names the goal without flinching: the *prayojana*
> is to awaken love for Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, abandoning the material consciousness that shrinks us; and he
> traces how attentive, humble chanting ripens — at Ṭer Kadamba, at the feet of Rūpa Gosvāmī — into
> that very love. This is where the Name was always leading.
