# Chapter 18 · Anarthas Destroyed

> *Clearing of the unwanted (anartha-nivṛtti) comes in four degrees — partial, fuller, nearly complete, and complete.*
> — **Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura**, *Mādhurya-kādambinī* (paraphrased)

---

As the enemies lose their grip (Chapter 17), the residue they deposited — the anarthas (Chapter 5) —
begins to dissolve. This clearing (*anartha-nivṛtti*) is gradual, and knowing its stages protects the
practitioner from both impatience and discouragement.

## 18.1 The Four Stages

| Stage | Sanskrit | Extent | Felt reality |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *eka-deśa-vartinī* | ~¼ | a few habits loosening; occasional clarity |
| 2 | *bahu-deśa-vartinī* | ~½ | visible behavior change; regular peace |
| 3 | *prāyikī* | ~¾ | others notice; steadiness under pressure |
| 4 | *pūrṇā* | complete | anarthas gone; unshakeable absorption |

Most sincere practitioners live for years between Stages 1 and 2. **This is normal, not failure.** The
cup empties one measure at a time.

## 18.2 Signs of Progress

| Stage | External | Internal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | some bad habits reducing | occasional clarity in japa |
| 2 | noticeable change | regular peace of mind |
| 3 | others comment on the change | deep steadiness |
| 4 | complete transformation | unshakeable devotion |

## 18.3 The Agent of Clearing

Anarthas do not leave by force of will; they are dissolved by the name — the "universal solvent" —
and by hearing, which Prabhupāda compares to food digesting automatically once eaten. The
practitioner's only task is to keep taking the medicine (attentive hearing and chanting, Chapters
13–16); the digestion is Kṛṣṇa's.

> **From the lectures.** Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja frames the whole of progress as this emptying —
> shedding the false "I and mine" and awakening into *sambandha*, the living knowledge that "I am an
> eternal part of Kṛṣṇa, my only master." As that knowledge deepens, the unwanted simply has less to
> hold on to.
