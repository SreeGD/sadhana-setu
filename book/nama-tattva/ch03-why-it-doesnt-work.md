# Chapter 3 · Nāma-Vighna — Why My Chanting Doesn't Seem to "Work"

> *"The holy name is fully alive — Kṛṣṇa present as a person — yet our chanting is so often lifeless, done from habit rather than from a living, tasted experience."*
> — **HH Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja** (as taught in "The Living Name" retreat)

---

## 3.1 A Devotee's Story

*(A composite drawn from experiences common to serious practitioners.)*

**Kṛṣṇa Dāsa** had chanted sixteen rounds daily for fifteen years — regular in temple attendance,
strict in the four principles, faithful in his reading. Yet inside he felt empty.

"Why is this not working?" he asked his spiritual master, voice breaking. "Fifteen years. No taste,
no tears, no absorption. My mind still wanders the whole round. I still get angry at home. I still
criticize other devotees in my mind. What am I doing wrong?"

His guru answered with questions:

- **"When you chant, where is your mind?"** — "Planning the day. Replaying arguments. Sometimes I
  finish a round and don't remember chanting a single mantra."
- **"Are you calling to Kṛṣṇa, or completing a quota?"** — "Honestly? A quota. I'm relieved when it's done."
- **"When a devotee errs, what is your first thought?"** — Silence. Then: "That they're doing it wrong.
  That I know better. Sometimes I feel satisfied when they fail."
- **"Do you truly believe the name can transform you — or do you secretly doubt it?"** — "After fifteen
  years of no experience… maybe I wonder if it only works for special devotees."

"You have named four of the ten offenses," his guru said, "in a single conversation."

## 3.2 The Diagnosis

| Symptom he described | Offense | Underlying enemy |
|---|---|---|
| Mind elsewhere; chanting as a quota | #10 — inattentive, attached chanting | kāma |
| Criticizing devotees, even mentally | #1 — offense to a devotee | mātsarya |
| Secret doubt of the name's power | #5 — the glories are "exaggeration" | moha |
| "I know better; I've done everything right" | pride (mada) | mada |

The name had not failed him for fifteen years. **Offenses had curtained it.**

```
bhagavan-nāmni saṁprāpte 'parādhe sati tat-prabhāvaṁ na darśayati   (rendering the teaching of the Mādhurya-kādambinī)
```
"When an offense is present, the holy name does not reveal its full potency to the offender."

The name is Kṛṣṇa Himself (Chapter 1). When we offend, He simply withholds the *experience* of His
power — the chanting still purifies, but at reduced perception — while we persist, the cloud thins.

## 3.3 The Prescription — and the Turn

Over a year, under guidance, Kṛṣṇa Dāsa worked *one* cloud at a time: a daily meditation on
*tṛṇād api sunīcena*; finding three good qualities in every devotee; pre-japa settling and one slow,
fully-heard round; reading a transformation story daily to rebuild faith; and concrete service to
the very devotees he had criticized.

The change was not dramatic. But gradually: tears came unbidden in one attentive round; he found
himself praising devotees he had envied; work stopped colonizing his japa; he began to *want* to chant.

"The name didn't change," his guru observed. "**You** changed — by removing what blocked it."

> **From the lectures.** Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja names the same gap — the scriptures heap glory on
> the name, yet the practitioner tastes little; the "steel-framed heart" that sheds no tear is the
> diagnostic. Vaiśeṣika Prabhu warns of "japa-fade": attention diverts by degrees until the practice
> quietly dies. Both point past the symptom to a *nameable* cause.
