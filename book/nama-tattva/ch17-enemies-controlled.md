# Chapter 17 · Jitendriya — Six Enemies Controlled

> *"A person free from all attachment and aversion, able to control the senses, obtains the complete mercy of the Lord."*
> — **Kṛṣṇa, Bhagavad-gītā 2.64**

---

Part Four is the result — the chain of Part Two run in reverse as freedom is won. It begins where the
problem began: the six enemies (Chapter 4). Controlled, they do not vanish from view; they lose their
*grip*.

## 17.1 Transformation, Not Suppression

The cure does not delete the enemies — it *redirects* their energy toward Kṛṣṇa.

| Enemy | Before | After (transformed) | Sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kāma | consuming material desire | desire to serve Kṛṣṇa | enthusiasm for service |
| Krodha | destructive anger | righteous energy only | patience under provocation |
| Lobha | material greed | spiritual eagerness | contentment; generosity |
| Moha | illusion | clarity | quick, śāstra-aligned decisions |
| Mada | false pride | genuine humility | comfort in the lowest place |
| Mātsarya | envy | holy aspiration (*spṛhā*) | joy at others' success |

## 17.2 The Mark of Control

BG 2.64 names the state: moving through the world's sense-objects *without* being pulled by
attraction or driven by aversion. This is not withdrawal from life but **freedom within it** — the
senses engaged in Kṛṣṇa's service rather than commanded by the six thieves. The one who reaches it
"obtains the complete mercy of the Lord" (*prasādam adhigacchati*), and in that mercy the higher
results of Part Four unfold of themselves.

> **From the lectures.** Mahātmā Prabhu's "worst-enemy assessment" and Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja's
> shift from mind-control to mind-*absorption* both point here: the enemies are subdued not by
> wrestling them down but by filling the heart so full of the Name that they find no room.
