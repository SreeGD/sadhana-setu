# Chapter 14 · Practical Control of the Six Enemies

> *"Bring your mind back to the mantra itself."*
> — **HG Vaiśeṣika Prabhu** (Japa Workshop)

---

Chapters 10–13 built posture and offense-free habit. This chapter is tactical: what to *do* when a
specific enemy attacks in the middle of a round.

## 14.1 The Samādhāya Method (SB 8.3.1)

When Gajendra was seized by the crocodile, Śukadeva describes exactly how he chanted:

```
evaṁ vyavasito buddhyā samādhāya mano hṛdi
jajāpa paramaṁ jāpyaṁ prāg-janmany anuśikṣitam   (SB 8.3.1)
```

| Word | Application |
|---|---|
| *vyavasitaḥ* — determined | "I will chant with attention *now*." |
| *buddhyā* — by intelligence | "Which enemy is disturbing me?" |
| *samādhāya* — concentrating | gather the scattered mind |
| *mano hṛdi* — mind in the heart | chant from the heart, not the head |

## 14.2 Enemy-Specific Japa Strategies

| Enemy | Sign during japa | On-the-spot counter |
|---|---|---|
| Kāma | planning pleasures | "all pleasure is from Kṛṣṇa" |
| Krodha | replaying a conflict | "the tree gives wood when cut" |
| Lobha | rushing, clock-watching | deliberately slow down |
| Moha | "is this doing anything?" | "one Name saved Ajāmila" |
| Mada | "I chant better than them" | "lower than the straw" |
| Mātsarya | criticizing a devotee | find three of their good qualities |

## 14.3 Emergency Protocol (under 30 seconds)

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 Recognize | "This is anger / greed / pride." |
| 2 Stop | pause the beads a moment |
| 3 Breathe | three slow breaths |
| 4 Pray | "Kṛṣṇa, please help me." |
| 5 Reframe | apply the specific counter above |
| 6 Resume | continue with renewed attention |
| 7 Note | remember it for the evening review |

The measure of success is **the return of attention, not the absence of distraction.** The mind will
wander a hundred times; bring it back a hundred and one. As Vaiśeṣika Prabhu puts it, the chanter's
whole job is to keep *bringing the mind back to the mantra itself* — Kṛṣṇa supplies the attraction.

> **From the lectures.** Across his japa workshops Vaiśeṣika Prabhu trains this single reflex — chant,
> notice the wandering, return — and grounds it in *sonic theology*: the sound is non-different from
> Kṛṣṇa, so attentive hearing *is* contact with Him. Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja reframes the same work
> from mind-*control* to mind-*absorption* ("Chanting with Absorption"): a mind wholly present is not
> fatigued but enlivened.
