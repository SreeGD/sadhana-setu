# Chapter 6 · Pāpa — Sins (Branch)

> *"…he gave him permission to reside in four places — where gambling, drinking, prostitution and slaughter of animals were performed."*
> — **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.17.38** (Sūta Gosvāmī)

---

When anarthas (Chapter 5) are indulged, they branch outward into overt sinful acts — **pāpa**. In the
chain of bondage, sins are the visible foliage: the part everyone sees, but downstream of the enemies
and anarthas that produced them.

## 6.1 The Four Pillars of Irreligion (SB 1.17.38)

When Mahārāja Parīkṣit checked Kali, he confined him to four places. These became the four acts that
dismantle spiritual life — each one destroying a specific pillar of *dharma*.

| Sin | Sanskrit | Pillar of dharma destroyed | Root enemy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gambling | द्यूतम् | *satyam* — truthfulness | lobha |
| Intoxication | पानम् | *tapaḥ* — austerity | moha |
| Illicit sex | स्त्रियः | *śaucam* — cleanliness | kāma |
| Meat-eating | सूना | *dayā* — mercy | krodha |

The logic is exact. Gambling makes a life of deception (kills truth). Intoxication removes all
self-control (kills austerity). Illicit sex destroys inner and outer purity (kills cleanliness).
Animal slaughter hardens the heart to violence (kills mercy). Where all four are avoided, **Kali has
no residence** in that person's life.

## 6.2 Why the Four Principles Come First

The four regulative principles are not arbitrary rules; they are the removal of Kali's four seats.
For the chanter they matter because **sin committed on the strength of the name is the seventh
offense** (Chapter 7): "I will chant it off." That single rationalization keeps a chanter perpetually
blocked, feeding the very fire he is trying to extinguish.

## 6.3 The Name and the Sin

Here the mercy of Chapter 1 returns with a caution. The name destroys unlimited sins (SB 6.2.14) —
*but* deliberately sinning in expectation of that mercy is an offense that blocks the mercy itself.
The name freely forgives the sinner who is *struggling to stop*; it withholds its taste from the one
who *plans* to sin and settle accounts by chanting. Avoiding the four pillars is therefore not
pre-qualification for chanting — anyone may chant — but it is how a chanter stops re-clouding his own
sun.

> **From the lectures.** Bhūrijana Prabhu, on Ajāmila, shows the other side: even the man who *broke*
> all four principles was delivered when he called the name helplessly — because he was falling
> *toward* the Lord, not exploiting Him. The dividing line is the heart's direction.
