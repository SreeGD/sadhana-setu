# Chapter 1 · The Transcendental Power of the Holy Name

> *"In these transcendental names You have invested all Your transcendental energies, and there is no hard and fast rule for chanting them."*
> — **Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, Śikṣāṣṭaka 2**

---

## 1.1 The Supreme Statement — Śikṣāṣṭaka 2

**Devanāgarī**
```
नाम्नाम् अकारि बहुधा निज-सर्व-शक्तिस्
तत्रार्पिता नियमितः स्मरणे न कालः।
एतादृशी तव कृपा भगवन् ममापि
दुर्दैवम् ईदृशम् इहाजनि नानुरागः॥
```

**IAST**
```
nāmnām akāri bahudhā nija-sarva-śaktis
tatrārpitā niyamitaḥ smaraṇe na kālaḥ
etādṛśī tava kṛpā bhagavan mamāpi
durdaivam īdṛśam ihājani nānurāgaḥ
```

**Translation.** "O my Lord, Your holy name alone can render all benediction to living beings, and
thus You have hundreds and millions of names like Kṛṣṇa and Govinda. In these transcendental names
You have invested all Your transcendental energies, and there is no hard and fast rule for chanting
them. Out of Your causeless mercy You have made approach to You easy through Your holy names, but I
am so unfortunate that I have no attraction for them."

### Word-by-word

| Sanskrit | IAST | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| नाम्नाम् | nāmnām | of the names |
| अकारि | akāri | manifested |
| बहुधा | bahudhā | in many ways |
| निज-सर्व-शक्तिः | nija-sarva-śaktiḥ | all His own potencies |
| तत्र अर्पिता | tatra arpitā | invested therein |
| नियमितः … न कालः | niyamitaḥ … na kālaḥ | no strict rule of time |
| एतादृशी तव कृपा | etādṛśī tava kṛpā | such is Your mercy |
| दुर्दैवम् | durdaivam | misfortune |
| न अनुरागः | na anurāgaḥ | no attraction |

Two revolutionary claims sit inside one verse: **(1)** the Lord has invested *all* His potencies
(*nija-sarva-śakti*) into His name; and **(2)** there is *no restriction* (*niyamitaḥ na kālaḥ*) — no
qualification of time, place, caste, or purity is required to chant. Everything Vedic ritual gates
behind rules, the holy name throws open.

## 1.2 The Name Is the Person — *Abhinnatva*

**Padma Purāṇa**
```
नाम चिन्तामणिः कृष्णश् चैतन्य-रस-विग्रहः।
पूर्णः शुद्धो नित्य-मुक्तो ऽभिन्नत्वान् नाम-नामिनोः॥

nāma cintāmaṇiḥ kṛṣṇaś caitanya-rasa-vigrahaḥ
pūrṇaḥ śuddho nitya-mukto 'bhinnatvān nāma-nāminoḥ
```

"The holy name is a wish-fulfilling gem (*cintāmaṇi*), the very embodiment of consciousness and
bliss (*caitanya-rasa-vigraha*) — complete, pure, eternally liberated, because the name and the named
are non-different (*abhinnatvāt nāma-nāminoḥ*)."

This is the hinge of the whole book. An ordinary name merely *points* to its object — the word
"water" cannot wet the tongue. The holy name does not point; it **delivers the Person**. When the
tongue says *Kṛṣṇa*, Kṛṣṇa Himself is present there.

> **From the lectures.** Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja develops exactly this in "The Living Name": the
> name is not a symbol but *Kṛṣṇa present as a person* — which is why our chanting can be lifeless
> even while the name is fully alive. Vaiśeṣika Prabhu grounds the same point in *śruti-pramāṇa*: the
> name is spiritual sound descending from beyond the material universe, not an ordinary vibration.

## 1.3 The Name Acts Even in Its Shadow — SB 6.2.14

```
साङ्केत्यं पारिहास्यं वा स्तोभं हेलनम् एव वा।
वैकुण्ठ-नाम-ग्रहणम् अशेषाघ-हरं विदुः॥

sāṅketyaṁ pārihāsyaṁ vā stobhaṁ helanam eva vā
vaikuṇṭha-nāma-grahaṇam aśeṣāgha-haraṁ viduḥ
```

"Chanting the name of the Lord of Vaikuṇṭha — even to indicate something else (*sāṅketya*), jokingly
(*pārihāsya*), for musical entertainment (*stobha*), or neglectfully (*helana*) — destroys unlimited
sins. This is the verdict of the learned."

Even the *shadow* of the name (*nāmābhāsa*) burns unlimited sins. If the weakest, most careless
contact is that potent, the case for attentive, devotional chanting makes itself. (We return to
nāmābhāsa systematically in Chapter 8.)

## 1.4 The Seven Glories of the Holy Name

| # | Glory | Sanskrit | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Non-different | अभिन्नत्व | The name **is** Kṛṣṇa |
| 2 | All-powerful | सर्व-शक्तिमान् | Full potency invested |
| 3 | Unlimited | बहुधा | Countless names |
| 4 | Accessible | नियमितः न | No hard-and-fast rule |
| 5 | All-merciful | कृपा | Approach made easy |
| 6 | All-benediction | सर्व-मङ्गल | Gives every blessing |
| 7 | Self-manifesting | स्वयं-स्फूर्ति | Reveals itself to the willing |

**Four analogies for these glories.**
- **Sun and sunlight (abhinnatva).** To stand in sunlight is to feel the sun itself — not a symbol of
  it. To chant is to be in Kṛṣṇa's presence.
- **The touchstone (cintāmaṇi).** A material touchstone turns iron to gold; the name gives what the
  soul actually needs — love of God — like a king pressing the whole kingdom on one who asked for rice.
- **The ocean in a drop (sarva-śakti).** All of Kṛṣṇa's infinite potencies are compressed into the
  sixteen words of the mahā-mantra.
- **The mother's call (no restriction).** A child may cry "Mother!" at any moment, in any state; the
  Lord has made His name just that available — even to one who is falling (Ajāmila — Chapter 2).

## 1.5 Why This Chapter Comes First

The rest of the book is a rescue operation for a chanter who feels nothing. That entire project rests
on one fact established here: **the failure is never in the name.** The sun is always full; if we feel
no warmth, the sky is clouded on *our* side. Fix that firmly, and every later chapter — the enemies,
the offenses, the diagnosis, the cure — becomes hopeful work rather than despair.
