# Chapter 7 · Aparādha — Offenses (Fruit)

> *"The name is always present, like the sun; it is our offenses that are the clouds hiding it from us."*
> — **HH Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja** (as taught, Holy-Name Retreat 2014, session 08)

---

Offenses (*nāma-aparādha*) are the **fruit** of the chain — the point at which the enemies and
anarthas of the previous chapters directly touch the name and curtain its potency (Mādhurya-kādambinī
3.19, Chapter 3). This is the single most practical chapter in Part Two: name your offense and you
have found the cloud.

## 7.1 The Ten Offenses to the Holy Name (Padma Purāṇa)

| # | Offense | Root enemy | Direct remedy | Cure chapter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Blaspheming devotees | mātsarya | serve the offended devotee | Ch 12 |
| 2 | Equating the Lord with demigods | moha | study tattva (BG 7.20) | Ch 13 |
| 3 | Disregarding the guru | mada | increase submission & service | Ch 10 |
| 4 | Blaspheming scripture | mada | hear submissively, with faith | Ch 10 |
| 5 | Deeming the glories exaggeration | moha | re-read the transformed lives (Ch 2) | Ch 13 |
| 6 | Concocting meanings of the name | mada | accept the paramparā | Ch 10 |
| 7 | Sinning on the strength of the name | kāma | genuine repentance; stop | Ch 6, 14 |
| 8 | Treating the name as ordinary piety | lobha | know its supremacy | Ch 13 |
| 9 | Instructing the faithless | mada | assess the hearer first | Ch 10 |
| 10 | Inattention; clinging to "I and mine" | kāma | develop taste; hear one mantra | Ch 14–15 |

**The gravest is the first.** Offense to a devotee (*vaiṣṇava-aparādha*) is the "mad elephant" that
uproots the whole creeper of devotion in one stroke — even great power cannot survive it (Durvāsā
fleeing the disc after offending Ambarīṣa). This is why humility, tolerance, and respect (Chapters
10–12) are placed first among the cures.

## 7.2 The Sun and the Clouds

The governing image (from the epigraph) resolves the whole puzzle of Chapter 3. The name — the sun —
is never diminished. Offenses are clouds: they do not touch the sun; they block *my* view of it.
Therefore:
- The problem is never "add more sun" (chant more mechanically).
- The problem is always "clear the specific cloud" (name and remedy the offense).

One cleared cloud lets in more light than ten thousand rounds chanted through an overcast heart.

> **From the lectures.** Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja teaches that *studying* an offense is only
> half-finished work — the other half is learning to *overcome* it (Kirtan Course, sessions 14 & 16).
> He singles out the tenth offense — attachment to "I and mine" — as the one that most quietly leaves
> a person "unable to taste" (Kirtan Course 21).

## 7.3 From List to Diagnosis

This chapter gives the map; Chapter 9 turns it into a personal instrument — a **Ten-Offense Audit**
in which each offense becomes an honest yes/no question about *my* chanting. The Kṛṣṇa-Dāsa dialogue
of Chapter 3 was exactly that audit, spoken aloud.
