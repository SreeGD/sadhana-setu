# Chapter 5 · Anartha — Unwanted Things (Root)

> *"As soon as irrevocable loving service is established in the heart, the effects of nature's modes of passion and ignorance — lust and greed — disappear from the heart. Then the devotee is established in goodness, and he becomes happy."*
> — **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.2.19**

---

An **anartha** is literally a "non-value" — an unwanted thing the enemies of Chapter 4 deposit in the
heart. Anarthas are the *residue* of guṇa-saṅga: the enemies act, and what settles behind is a
tendency, a taste, a weakness that persists even after the immediate impulse passes. They are the
"root" in the chain because they are what make offenses (Chapter 7) feel natural rather than foreign.

## 5.1 The Four Kinds of Anartha (Bhajana-rahasya)

| # | Anartha | Sanskrit | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Illusion about truth | *tattva-vibhrama* | wrong ideas about the self, God, the world, and their relations |
| 2 | Thirst for the temporary | *asat-tṛṣṇā* | craving for material objects here and hereafter |
| 3 | Offenses | *aparādha* | offenses to the name, the deity, and devotees (Chapter 7) |
| 4 | Weakness of heart | *hṛdaya-daurbalya* | envy, deceit, hankering for prestige, fault-finding |

The most corrosive for a chanter is the fourth. As Haridāsa Ṭhākura teaches, the daily enemy of japa
is not gross sin but **pramāda** — inattention, restlessness, and the quiet indifference of "I don't
really care anymore." That is *hṛdaya-daurbalya* wearing an ordinary face.

## 5.2 Why Anarthas Block the Name

The name is fully potent (Chapter 1); anarthas do not weaken *it* — they occupy the *space* where its
taste would be felt. A cup already full of stale water has no room for nectar. This is why more rounds
alone rarely help a blocked chanter: the work is not adding water pressure but **emptying the cup**.

> **From the lectures.** Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja devotes whole sessions to *pramāda* — glossed as
> distractedness, restlessness, and "I just don't care any longer" — showing how this single anartha
> keeps chanting at the level of *nāmābhāsa* (Chapter 8). Vaiśeṣika Prabhu frames the same emptying as
> curing "material intelligence," the habit of viewing everything as an object to enjoy or control.

## 5.3 How Anarthas Clear

Anarthas do not leave by force of will; they dissolve through *sādhana* and, above all, through the
name itself — the "universal solvent." The clearing (*anartha-nivṛtti*) proceeds in stages, which we
map in Chapter 18. For now the practical point is diagnostic (Chapter 9): **name your dominant
anartha**, because you cannot empty a cup you refuse to look into.
