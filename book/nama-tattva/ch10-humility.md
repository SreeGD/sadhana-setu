# Chapter 10 · Humility — Tṛṇād Api Sunīcena

> *"One should chant the holy name in a humble state of mind, thinking oneself lower than the straw in the street."*
> — **Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, Śikṣāṣṭaka 3**

---

Part Three is the cure. It opens with humility because **pride (mada) is the root from which most
clouds form** (offenses #3, #4, #6, #9 all trace to it), and because Śrī Caitanya names humility first
among the qualifications for constant chanting.

## 10.1 The Verse

```
tṛṇād api sunīcena taror api sahiṣṇunā
amāninā mānadena kīrtanīyaḥ sadā hariḥ   (Śikṣāṣṭaka 3)
```
"Thinking oneself lower than the straw in the street, more tolerant than a tree, free of false
prestige, and ready to offer all respect to others — in that state one should chant the name of Hari
constantly."

The verse is not decorative; it is *prescriptive*. It names the inner posture in which the name
actually acts. This chapter takes the first quality (*tṛṇād api sunīcena*); Chapters 11 and 12 take
the next two.

## 10.2 "Lower Than the Straw"

| Property of straw | Humble chanter |
|---|---|
| stepped on by all | accepts the lowest place |
| never protests | demands no recognition |
| expects nothing | wants no honor |
| rises after being crushed | recovers from insult |

Three depths of humility: **external** (courteous behavior), **mental** (no felt superiority), and
**spiritual** (seeing oneself as utterly dependent on Kṛṣṇa's mercy — *akiñcana*, having nothing of
one's own). The *su* in *sunīcena* means "very": ordinary politeness is not enough; the realization
is "without Kṛṣṇa's mercy I am nothing."

## 10.3 How Humility Clears Offenses

| Offense from pride | Humility's answer |
|---|---|
| #1 criticizing devotees | "they are more advanced than I" |
| #3 disregarding guru | "I am nothing without his mercy" |
| #6 concocting meanings | "let me accept the paramparā" |
| #9 instructing the faithless | "am I qualified to teach anyone?" |

### The straw and the storm
In a gale the proud oak declared, "I will not bend" — and cracked. The blade of grass bent flat and,
when the storm passed, simply stood back up. Bending before a greater force is not defeat but
intelligence. *Tṛṇād api sunīcena* is spiritual survival.

## 10.4 Practice

- **Morning (3 min):** recite *tṛṇād api sunīcena*; resolve to be servant of all today.
- **Under praise:** "this belongs to Kṛṣṇa; I am an instrument."
- **Under criticism:** first ask, "is there truth here to learn?"; then, "can I tolerate the rest?"
- **Evening (2 min):** where did I feel superior, compete for recognition, or resist correction?

> **From the lectures.** Sacīnandana Swami Mahārāja frames the whole retreat's aim as recovering the
> "vertical," prayerful posture — the holy name becomes real only when the chanter turns to Kṛṣṇa in
> humble, helpless prayer ("Chanting with Humility," "Chanting Prayerfully").
