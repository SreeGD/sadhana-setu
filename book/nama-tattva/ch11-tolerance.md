# Chapter 11 · Tolerance — Taror Api Sahiṣṇunā

> *A devotee should be more tolerant than a tree — which never protests even when cut down, and offers its shade to everyone.*
> — **Śrīla Prabhupāda** (gloss on Śikṣāṣṭaka 3, paraphrased)

---

The second quality of Śikṣāṣṭaka 3 directly counteracts **krodha** (anger) — the enemy that does the
most damage in the fewest seconds, and the one most likely to produce the gravest offense (blaspheming
a devotee, #1).

## 11.1 "More Tolerant Than a Tree"

| What a tree endures | What a devotee endures |
|---|---|
| cut down — gives wood | insulted — gives blessings |
| scorched — gives shade | persecuted — gives shelter |
| stoned — drops fruit | harmed — offers service |

Tolerance here is not suppression; it is **strength** — the settled knowledge that Kṛṣṇa is the
controller, so an insult is His arrangement for my purification, not an emergency requiring
retaliation.

## 11.2 Prahlāda's Example (SB 7.9.43)

Thrown from cliffs, given poison, cast into fire, trampled by elephants — Prahlāda never grew angry.
His tolerance flowed from **absorption**: *tvad-vīrya-gāyana-mahāmṛta-magna-cittaḥ*, "my mind is
merged in the great nectar of Your glories." The deepest tolerance is a by-product of being full;
there is no room for anger in a heart already occupied by the name.

## 11.3 The P.A.U.S.E. Method

When provoked (10–30 seconds):

| Step | Action | Inner words |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Pause | "Stop — don't react." |
| **A** | Acknowledge | "I am feeling anger." |
| **U** | Understand | "What desire of mine was frustrated?" |
| **S** | Surrender | "Kṛṣṇa, please help me respond." |
| **E** | Engage wisely | respond, don't react |

Because *krodha* is downstream of *kāma* (BG 2.62–63), the durable cure treats the frustrated desire
beneath the anger, not just the flare.

## 11.4 Practice

- **Morning vow:** "Whatever comes today, I will respond, not react; I will see difficulty as mercy."
- **In traffic / delay:** "Thank you, Kṛṣṇa, for this patience practice" — chant during the wait.
- **Evening:** list any anger episodes; trace each to the desire beneath it; offer it to Kṛṣṇa.

### The jackfruit tree
Children threw stones to knock down its fruit — it dropped more. Travelers carved their names — it grew
more shade. A limb was cut — new branches sprouted from the wound. "That tree," an old farmer said,
"taught me more of spiritual life than all my years in the temple. It only gives; it never retaliates."
