# Chapter 19 · Sins Destroyed

> *"Ajāmila has already atoned for the sins of millions of births, because in a helpless state he chanted the holy name of Nārāyaṇa."*
> — **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 6.2.7** (the Viṣṇudūtas)

---

As the anarthas dissolve (Chapter 18), the pāpa they branched into (Chapter 6) is burned away. Here
the name's power from Chapter 1 pays its full dividend: the destruction not of this life's sins alone,
but of the accumulated karma of countless lifetimes.

## 19.1 The Reach of the Name

```
ayaṁ hi kṛta-nirveśo janma-koṭy-aṁhasām api
yad vyājahāra vivaśo nāma svasty-ayanaṁ hareḥ   (SB 6.2.7)
```
"He has atoned for the sins of *millions of births* (*janma-koṭi*), because helplessly he uttered the
all-auspicious name of Hari."

Note *janma-koṭi* — not the errors of one lifetime, but the debt of millions. And note *vivaśaḥ* —
"helplessly": Ajāmila's chanting was barely even intentional, and still the reservoir emptied. This is
the outer limit of the mercy established in Chapter 2.

## 19.2 Why It Is Not License

The very next verses (and Chapter 6 here) guard against the misreading: *deliberately* sinning in
expectation of this cleansing is the seventh offense, which blocks the cleansing itself. The name
runs to the sinner who is *falling toward* the Lord (Ajāmila), not to the one *calculating* on Him.
The heart's direction is everything.

## 19.3 The Cleansing Is Real and Perceptible

Purification by the name is not a legal fiction but an experienced fact — the "becoming unstuck"
Prabhupāda describes, as long-cherished material desires diminish "step by step." What jñāna and yoga
can only settle temporarily (like mud sinking in still water, stirred up again by the least
agitation), devotional hearing dissolves at the root, because the cleanser is the Lord Himself,
present in His name.

> **From the lectures.** Bhūrijana Prabhu builds his Ajāmila seminar to exactly this point: the name
> is the supreme atonement — and, beyond mere absolution, the means of capturing the Lord's attention
> and love. Erasing sin is only the doorway; what waits beyond it is the subject of the last two
> chapters.
